# Research and Rabbit publication rules

Write for journalists first, with methods and reproducibility in a technical annex.

Rabbit is a neutral public source catalog for journalists and public-policy researchers. Public source records describe what a collection contains, geographic and temporal coverage, access methods, join fields, update patterns and limitations. Cite publisher documentation. Do not include Graeme’s investigation targets, hypotheses, findings, editorial judgments, selected test cases or source-use history in public entries, exports or change logs.

Preserve research conclusions, successes, failures and detailed source-use history in this repository. Log supporting evidence and a file manifest in Graeme’s Google Drive, retaining source URLs, acquisition dates, checksums and limits. Do not describe this repository as private or change its sharing settings without explicit instructions.

When research finds a reusable dataset, corpus or new access route, compare it with existing Rabbit sources, methods and candidates. Submit a neutral, evidence-backed proposal, reusing the existing parent source where appropriate. A new proposal is not approval; publish only after explicit owner approval. Never enable collection merely because a catalog listing is approved.

Public methodological case studies are separate editorial items. Publish them only with explicit authorization. They may explain an approved example, but their conclusions must not be copied into general source records. Distinguish identity matches, reported actions, payments, legal applicability and proven violations. Treat a decision to park a lead as an editorial choice, not an exoneration or finding of wrongdoing.

The 16 November 2026 instruction approves 17 new collections and five access routes from the GitHub audit; it excludes the three personal usage notes. It also separately authorizes the concise sanctions/procurement teaching example. Preserve this boundary for future additions.
