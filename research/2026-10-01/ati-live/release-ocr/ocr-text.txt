323 - Mental Health Intervention

323.1 PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This policy provides Military Police (MP) with clear and practical guidance when MP are requested to
assist with the mental health intervention of a person in crisis within their jurisdiction and applies to all
non-commissioned members and officers who are appointed as MP under regulations made for the
purposes of section 156 of the National Defence Act and are employed in law enforcement duties and
functions.

323.2 POLICY STATEMENT

The Canadian Forces Provost Marshal (CFPM) and MP are committed to responding to mental health-
related incidents with professionalism, compassion, and respect for the dignity and rights of all
individuals. In all interactions, MP will prioritize the safety of the individual, the public, and responding
personnel, while seeking to de-escalate situations and facilitate access to appropriate medical and mental
health services. Military Police will operate within the framework of applicable laws, policies, and trauma-
informed practices, ensuring that responses to mental health crises are guided by empathy, cultural
awareness, and a commitment to minimizing harm.

323.2.1 DEFINITIONS
The following definitions are applicable throughout this policy:

a. apprehension: The act of taking a person into custody for their safety or the safety of
others under legal authority.

b. mental disorder: A clinically significant disturbance in an individual's cognitive, emotional
regulation, or behaviour.

c. mental health: A state of mental well-being that reflects an individual's ability to cope
with stress, to realize their potential, to learn, to work and to contribute to their
community.

d. mental health crisis. An event or experience in which an individual's normal coping

mechanisms are overwhelmed, causing them to have an extreme emotional, physical
mental, and / or behavioural response.

e. person in crisis. An individual—military or civilian—whose behaviour results in contact
with MP or other emergency services due to an apparent need for urgent intervention
related to mental health, emotional distress, or substance use. This includes situations
where the individual is exhibiting behaviour that is erratic, threatening, or dangerous,
posing a potential risk to themselves or others, and requiring an immediate response to
ensure safety. It may also include individuals who meet the criteria for assessment under
applicable provincial or territorial mental health legislation.

323.2.2 CONTEXT

Persons in crisis may present in various ways: as compliant or non-compliant, armed or unarmed,
Canadian Armed Forces (CAF) members or civilians. Situations may occur during regular hours when
medical support (i.e., the Base Hospital) is accessible or after hours when it is not. MP must respond with
empathy, caution, and in coordination with medical and mental health services.

323.3 GENERAL

Mental health care, including crisis intervention, falls under provincial and territorial jurisdiction in
Canada. Each province and territory have its own laws governing non-consensual intervention, such as
detaining individuals who may pose a danger to themselves or others. These laws must also comply

This provision grants MP the authority to detain and transport a person in crisis to an appropriate
medical facility. MP should always be prepared to articulate their reasons for the apprehension of a
detained person.

323.4 GENERAL MP RESPONSE PRINCIPLES
When responding to a complaint of a person in crisis MP will:

a. prior to arriving on scene if, possible, conduct a Person Query within the Canadian Police
Information Centre (CPIC) and the Security and Military Police Information System
(SAMPIS);

b. determine if the person in crisis owns, possesses or has access to weapons, firearms,

ammunition, explosives or the related authorizations, licences, certificates or permits;

(at request the attendance of additional MP and / or a supervisor when there is information
that a person in crisis is armed or may be armed with a weapon; and

id if background information reveals that the person in crisis has a history of violence or use
of weapons, the incident involves a barricaded person, the incident involves a person who
by their position has placed themselves or others in immediate jeopardy (i.e., person
located at height on a balcony, bridge, etc.), the MP supervisor will notify the Military
Police Duty Officer (MPDO) and request additional resources and guidance.

When encountering a person in crisis, MP must first assess the scene for immediate threats and
determine if there’s a need to immediately detain the individual under the applicable MHA or under MP
legal authority.

Use of force (UoF) must be proportional, necessary, and in accordance with MP Policy and the MP
National UoF Framework. For officer and public safety, MP may use discretion when determining whether
to handcuff an individual as it may not be practicable (e.g., due to a person's medical condition, age,
disability, pregnancy, or frailty) or necessary (i.e., person does not represent a danger to themselves or
others, and is consenting to being escorted) in all circumstances. If physical restraints are required, and
depending on the situation, MP will minimize their impact by hiding them.

MP will ensure that a person exhibiting signs of a mental health crisis is not left unattended. As first
responders, MP must intervene in a manner appropriate to the specific circumstances.

323.5 NON-EMERGENCY OPTIONS FOR RESPONDING MP

Upon contact with a person in crisis and if the situation is not urgent, meaning the individual is not
posing an imminent threat to themselves or others, MP should consider less intrusive measures to
obtain the individual's consent before initiating a mental health apprehension. MP are expected to
employ de-escalation techniques and make reasonable efforts to persuade the individual to voluntarily

seek medical assistance. If consent is obtained, MP will attempt to secure transport for the individual
through a family member, friend, co-worker, or any other person the individual is comfortable with.
Only as a last resort if all other options have been exhausted, and if resources permit, MP may
provide an escort to the appropriate facility.

When MP respond to a CAF member in crisis during normal working hours and have access to the
Base Hospital, but do not have reasonable and probable grounds to apprehend the individual yet
believe the member could benefit from seeing a mental health practitioner, the member's unit will be
notified. The chain of command (CoC) has the authority to direct the member to the Base Hospital and
provide assistance with escorting them as needed.

After hours, when there is no access to the Base Hospital, MP will attempt to convince the member to
voluntarily attend the nearest medical facility under escort from a close friend, family member, co-
worker, chaplain, or other religious or spiritual advisor. If the individual requests a support person, MP
will document the request and any related details in their notebook, regardless of whether the support
person can be reached or attends.

For civilians, if the MP does not have reasonable and probable grounds to apprehend but believes that
the individual could benefit from seeing a mental health practitioner, the MP should inform the
individual's family members of their options under the applicable provincial Mental Health act. This
should only be done in situations where the privacy of the individual is respected (e.g., when the call is
initiated by family members and is a recurring concern or with the individual's consent). Again, MP will
attempt to convince the individual to voluntarily attend the nearest medical facility under escort from a
close friend, family member, co-worker, chaplain, or other religious or spiritual advisor. As most
civilians residing on a defence establishment are dependants of a military member, it is incumbent on
that member to assist in providing all necessary support to the individual, including escorting and
transporting the individual to the appropriate medical facility.

323.6 EMERGENCY OPTIONS FOR RESPONDING MP

MP may apprehend (detain) a person in crisis under applicable legal authority when the individual cannot
be managed through less intrusive means, and MP have reasonable and probable grounds (RPG) to
believe that the individual has:

a. threatened, attempted, or is threatening or attempting to cause bodily harm to
themselves;
b. behaved or is behaving violently toward another person or is causing another person to

fear bodily harm; or
ie demonstrated a lack of competence to care for themselves;

and where MP reasonably believe the person is suffering from a mental disorder of such nature or
severity that it will likely result in:

a. serious bodily harm to the person;
b. serious bodily harm to another person; or
Ch serious physical impairment of the person.

If the apprehension is to stop or prevent the commission of an offence, MP should arrest in accordance
with the relevant legal framework. Upon confirmation they are equipped and staffed to deal with the
member in crisis, the member should be transported to the Base Hospital, and civilians will be transported
to the nearest civilian hospital.

If MP are required to escort the individual to a medical facility, they must remain at the medical facility and

323.6.1 EMERGENCY TRANSPORTATION OPTIONS

It is preferable to transport an apprehended person via ambulance service as mental health is foremost a
medical concern. However, there are instances where it is appropriate for a member to apply their
discretion and transport the person to a designated facility in a police vehicle when the wait time for an
ambulance is not reasonable in the circumstances.

Before choosing the option to transport an apprehended person in crisis in a police vehicle, the member
must weigh officer safety considerations, the needs of the apprehended person, and police resourcing
considerations. The behaviour of a person apprehended for a mental health crisis may vary significantly
and officer safety assessments should be conducted throughout the interaction.

In making an assessment whether to transport an apprehended person in a police vehicle, the member
must consider officer safety, the needs and dignity of the apprehended person, and resource availability.
MP must further establish that the following criteria exist:

a. the person is not suffering from any apparent physical, non-mental health related medical
conditions and / or distress that would require that they be seen by paramedics prior to
transportation;

b. there are no presenting hygiene, contagion or biohazard concerns; and

c. there are no apparent officer safety concerns.

A patrol member's decision to transport a person apprehended for a mental health crisis in a police
vehicle must be approved by the supervisor and documented (including how the person met the above
criteria) in the accompanying report.

If these criteria are satisfied, supervisor approval is required before transport. The decision and rationale
(including criteria met) will be documented in the General Occurrence (GO) report and MP notebook.

MP will only use a police vehicle equipped with a secure partition (silent partner) and ensure the vehicle is
searched before and after the transport. The apprehended person will also be searched prior to entering
the vehicle.

MP (preferably, the MP supervisor or MP Dispatch) should advise the applicable hospital of impending
arrival and pass along any relevant information to the medical staff. MP will ensure that the Base Hospital
is equipped and staffed to deal with such situations prior to attending.

323.7 OUTSIDE A DEFENCE ESTABLISHMENT

In rare cases, such as carrying out a welfare check, MP may apprehend and transport a CAF member
experiencing a mental health crisis who has consented or is assessed as being an imminent danger to
themselves or others, to the nearest designated civilian facility. If, during normal working hours and if
practical, CAF members will be taken to the Base Hospital. Prior to transportation to a Base Hospital,
concerted actions should be taken to call beforehand and discuss the availability of medical staff to treat
the CAF member.

323.8 CHARTER OF RIGHTS AND FREEDOMS

MP will inform the person in crisis who has been apprehended of their rights under the Charter of Rights
and Freedoms, specifically section 10(a). If the individual is unable to understand or exercise their rights,
document this information in your notes. MP will also explain that, although the person is detained, they

are not under arrest (if applicable) and the distinction between the two.

323.9 APPREHENSION AND DETENTION OF PERSONS PURSUANT TO A MEDICAL CERTIFICATE
DIRECTING A PSYCHIATRIC ASSESSMENT

MP may receive a call for service from CF Health Services for an intervention pursuant to an application
for a medical certificate, directing a psychiatric assessment based on medical observations issued by
authorized medical authorities or designated officials. Some provinces authorize “any person” to take into
custody and transport a person in crisis who is named in the form. In these cases, MP are considered
“any person” and are legally authorized to detain and transport the person to the mental health facility.
The medical certificate is a form that is completed by medical or mental health professionals, and not by
police. MP do not and will not have the authority to “form” a patient. This regime is currently in place in the
following provinces and territory:

a. British Columbia: [Ref B, s. 22 (6)];

b. Alberta: (Ref C, s. 4);

c. Saskatchewan: [Ref D, s. 18 (3)];

d. Ontario: [Ref F, s. 15 (5)];

e. New Brunswick: [Ref H, s. 7.1 (4)];

if Prince Edward Island: [Ref J, s. 6 (3)]; and
g. Yukon: [Ref L, s. 11 (1) (a)].

In other provinces and territories, MP will exercise their primary legal authority under Ref A to respond to
and assist when a call for service is received from CF Health Services.

However, MP will only assist if they, or the medical authorities, believe there is a realistic risk of violence
and ambulance transportation is not a viable option.

323.10 INTERVENTION UNDER JUDICIAL ORDER FOR PSYCHIATRIC EXAMINATION

MP are lawfully authorized to execute a judicial order, issued by a justice of the peace or a judge of a
Provincial Court, directing the detention of a person for psychiatric evaluation if they are named in the
order. This regime is currently in place in the following provinces and territory:

a. PEI: [Ref J, s. 7(5) (b)];
b. Nova Scotia: [Ref I, s. 13 (5) (b)]; and
ic: Yukon: [Ref L, s. 6 (5)].

3233.11 SEIZURE OF FIREARM AND PROHIBITION ORDERS

Firearms and other regulated items may be seized for public safety pursuant to the authority granted
under section 117.04 (2) of the Criminal Code (CC). In all cases, a return to justice will be submitted for
all seized items. MP are to follow the Searches and Seizures policy as per 370.

323.12 MP UNIT STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE

MP Detachment Commanders are to generate or update their local Standard Operating Procedure
(SOP) accordingly, including identification of the nearest medical facility equipped or designated to
handle psychiatric / mental health patients, contacts for their respective Base Hospital Mental Health
clinic and General Duty Medical Officer (GDMO), and any other requirements under their respective
MHA. Further, the SOP should include any local mental health support services that may be beneficial for
MP to pass on to affected individuals.

Nationally, 9-8-8 is the Suicide Crisis Helpline that should be provided to individuals when appropriate.
The helpline assists those struggling to cope, and dealing with suicidal ideation, and provides support,
understanding, and available resources. The helpline may also be used to assist MP when dealing with a
person in crisis when medical advice is not readily available.

323.13 NOTES AND SAMPIS ENTRY REQUIREMENT

AGO will be generated for all mental health interventions following an emergency call or officer on
scene. All pertinent information will be included in the GO as per 126 and in the MP notebook as per
301. The Articulation for Apprehension SAMPIS template will also be filled out when a person in crisis is
detained. MP must articulate in the GO the reasons for any measures taken, and the detention and
non-consensual transportation of the implicated person.

Mental Health Interventions following a request from CF Health Services for a medical certificate will be
detailed in a Street Check as per 122, to protect the confidentiality of the individual.
