# What the Canadian ATI experiment actually found

1 October 2026. Completed live run, exploratory results.

**The workflow ran end to end: 1,234 summaries ranked, ten editorial clusters checked, three lower-ranked audits, a known-covered control, and five follow-up questions across three leads. It produced two precise acquisition priorities—not two verified scoops. These are 1,234 request summaries scored, not 1,234 underlying documents read; only one seven-page release packet was obtained and inspected.** The strongest next steps are a ten-page Nanisivik compliance file and a 95-page biometric-training file. It also found an already-published seven-page release and several reasons to stop chasing apparently fresh stories.

The broad ranking cost about five cents in Jev input-token charges. Final totals: **Jev USD0.061954 estimated from observed tokens (1,402 calls); Exa USD0.119 provider-reported (17 searches)**. No failed or uncertain charges remain. These figures do not verify an invoice, taxes, exchange fees or cash-versus-credit settlement; `cost-summary.json` preserves exact usage and accounting. Combined usage accounting is **USD0.180954**, not an audited or tax-inclusive bill. No credits were purchased and neither USD10 usage cap was approached.

## The two most useful handoffs

**Nanisivik: what happened after the storage-tank compliance order?** DND request **A-2025-02587**, ten pages disclosed in full, names briefing note **IE2025-0009981** about an environmental protection compliance order for alleged storage-tank non-compliance. That is a specific document to obtain, not proof of environmental damage.

The searches found extensive reporting on the facility's delays and transition out of operational use, but did not establish coverage of the precise order or its resolution. A direct read of [DND's May 21, 2026 announcement](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/news/2026/05/department-of-national-defence-to-transition-nanisivik-naval-facility-out-of-operational-use.html) supplied a useful check: DND cites ship endurance, seasonal access, construction problems and jetty repair costs. DND describes entering non-operational caretaker status. A formal divestment process is conditional on a later determination that the site is no longer required; that process would include required environmental assessments and necessary remediation. **The announcement does not connect the tank order to the transition out of operational use.** That tempting causal claim was rejected.

Human next step: obtain the ten-page release and the underlying order, inspection findings and completion record. Establish what corrective work was required, whether it was completed, and what obligations remain before disposal. A routine technical correction is a plausible explanation. Further generic web searches would not answer those questions.

**Biometric training: what were the rules for collecting Canadian personnel's data in 2025?** DND request **A-2025-00181**, 95 partly disclosed pages, labels the requested material as a technical concept of operations and standing authority for intentional collection of Canadian-citizen data from DND/CAF personnel during biometric training. The summary alone cannot establish whether the file records a proposal, a granted authorization, or actual collection.

A [public DND privacy assessment](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/corporate/transparency/access-information-privacy/privacy-impact-assessment/biometric-collection-die-program.html), dated September 2023, already acknowledges biometric privacy risks and a mitigation action plan. A [2018 directive published by NSICOP](https://nsicop-cpsnr.ca/reports/rp-2020-03-12-sr/09-en-annex-a.html) permits collection in specified mandated circumstances in §4.2. Section 5.3 supplies a general necessity requirement; §§5.4–5.6 address inadvertent collection, including during training. These are historical provisions, not a verified account of the policy governing the 2025 file. They undermine a blanket claim that no rules existed, but do not establish whether later authorization complied with applicable rules.

Neither source establishes what policy governed the 2025 matter, whether collection actually occurred, the conditions of any authorization, or completion of the mitigation plan. Human next step: first read the 95-page release to distinguish proposal, authorization and actual collection, identify the governing policy and precise authority, then examine consent, retention/deletion, sharing and supervision provisions and dated implementation evidence. Voluntary participation in controlled training remains a plausible ordinary explanation. **No unlawful collection or privacy breach was established.**

## A successful stop: an obtainable release was already covered

The high-ranked mental-health request **A-2025-01384** led to [an August 18, 2026 article](https://www.thecanadareport.ca/military-police-mental-health-detention/) naming that exact request and linking its release. The public link pointed, through a normal unauthenticated redirect, to a seven-page PDF. We downloaded it without an account or request. The file is image-only; OCR and visual checks were used rather than treating empty text extraction as an empty document.

The scan contains Military Police Policy Manual Chapter 323, *Mental Health Intervention*. Visual inspection confirms provincial/territorial jurisdiction on page 1 and conditions constraining emergency detention on page 3. Page 7 identifies the superseded July 2018 order. This supports the document identity and an ordinary policy explanation, not a new claim of blanket detention power. The publisher and filename associate the scan with the ATI number; no government response letter was independently obtained.

**Decision: already covered; stop unless a concrete implementation case supplies a new question.** This is a useful automation outcome: locating an existing release can save both acquisition effort and an unnecessary new assignment.

## What the wider checks rejected or left unresolved

- **Leopard tanks:** Reuters and an official announcement already describe the February 2023 package. The internal recommendation might add detail, but its title does not make the donation a fresh story.
- **Fifty armoured vehicles:** the programme was reported in September 2023; the request points to a December recommendation. Earlier programme coverage is not proof of what that later note says.
- **Israel exports:** the exact Maple article named in the request was recovered. Internal reactions could warrant a distinct question, but the published export claim itself is not a discovery.
- **China nuclear-related export permits:** an identifier query collided with unrelated Chinese standards. A broader query found rules, not the transaction. Obtain the five-page note **A-2025-01773** to identify the item, exporter, end-user and decision; “dual-use” does not establish a military end-use or approval.
- **GAC assignments:** staffing cuts are already reported, but the particular round-three eligibility decision remains unidentified. The nine-page **A-2025-02439** and eligibility bulletin are needed.
- **Ukraine mines:** reports of Canadians injured by or clearing anti-tank mines do not substantiate the separate allegation that Canadians deployed prohibited anti-personnel mines. The one-page release must be read before amplifying that claim.
- **Low-ranked audits:** Petawawa Q-103 produced a concrete balcony-repair listing, although not verified performance or a safety failure. This cautions against treating low scores as safe rejection. The other audits recovered ordinary evacuation context and existing medical-fairness guidance.

The C22 pistol control recovered the original CBC investigation in one Exa query. Alternate CBC pages, syndication and follow-ons were treated as shared lineage, not numerous independent investigations. One successful control does not establish general search recall.

## Scope, method and limits

The frozen source contains **441 Global Affairs Canada and 793 National Defence summaries** with closure year 2026. Six public API pages reconciled exactly; no duplicate IDs, institution/request pairs or exact duplicate rows were found. GAC closure months stop at July and DND at August. This is a complete snapshot for those filters, not a complete current-year publication feed.

Of 926 fully/partly disclosed rows, six list zero pages. One wholly exempted row lists 31 pages. Those inconsistencies were preserved. Year/month mean closure, pages mean pages released, and request wording does not verify its premise. Most underlying releases are still unavailable in this run.

A seeded 100-record departmental calibration preceded full ranking. All calibration responses were reused: 1,234 ranking calls total, not 1,334. Four score dimensions were preserved, with a fixed weighted composite. All rows were ranked; release-based shortlist eligibility required positive pages and full/partial disclosure. Semantic clustering merged repeated notes and closely related programme questions. Ten clusters, three seeded lower-half audits and the C22 control received initial searches; three decision-relevant expansion searches followed, including French for Nanisivik.

Jev also screened returned coverage and bounded public-source passages. These are aids to reading, not final editorial findings. A high-scoring “death penalty” request had a **no records** disposition, exposing how alarming wording can mislead. Claim-level review, date checks, ordinary explanations and accessible source passages mattered more than score alone.

No novel allegation was verified; no absence-of-coverage claim is conclusive. No matched baseline, validated precision/recall estimate, measured human review time or controlled improvement claim is offered. Nothing was sent to sources, no ATI request was filed, and nothing was publicly shared. The live research made no remote pushes; this sanitized report and evidence were subsequently authorized for archival in the existing private repository.
