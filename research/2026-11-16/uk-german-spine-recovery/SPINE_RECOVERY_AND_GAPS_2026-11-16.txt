UK: REAL ASSURANCE REQUIREMENTS, NOT PUBLIC VERIFICATION OF A PARTICULAR SYSTEM
JSP936 Part1 V1.1 (November 2024), especially §§91–105, sets lifecycle ethical-risk management. Unacceptable negative ethical risk calls for halting development/deployment or removing an out-of-bounds operational system until review. §99 requires annual executive-board AI ethical-assurance statements supported by auditable evidence. Accountability must be transferred into operational use. §105 recommends independent ethics assurance for larger/significant-risk or repeated-use systems; it uses SHOULD, not MUST, and does not establish independence from government or public access. The publication presents an evolving assurance framework and allows organizations to plan routes toward compliance. The record therefore rebuts an assertion of no policy, but supplies no project-specific compliance proof. Ask for the named assurance statement, risk owner, operating bounds and evidence underlying acceptance.
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6735fc89f6920bfb5abc7b62/JSP936_Part1.pdf

The February 2024 government response to the Lords AI-in-weapons report describes governance before, during and after use and says Article36 review is not simply a one-off release hurdle. Material changes can require review; adaptive systems pose challenges. The response discusses exploring supplier transparency into models, data and training, not a guaranteed public right to inspect them. Distinguish committee recommendations from the government's actual replies.
