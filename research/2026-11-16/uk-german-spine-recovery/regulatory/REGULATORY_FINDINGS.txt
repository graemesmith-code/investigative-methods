BOTTOM LINE
The strongest accountability finding here is not that safeguards are absent. UK policy sets lifecycle assurance obligations; the German government says humanitarian law and human engagement authorization apply. But the recovered public records do not expose the technical evidence that would let an outsider check those assurances for a named weapon. Germany's 10 November answer provides a particularly concrete, narrowly defined disclosure refusal. Meanwhile, public descriptions connect simulated AI tactics to intended physical platforms, but do not prove a patented safety feature has been installed on any specific weapon.

UK: REAL ASSURANCE REQUIREMENTS, NOT PUBLIC VERIFICATION OF A PARTICULAR SYSTEM
JSP936 Part1 V1.1 (November 2024), especially §§91–105, sets lifecycle ethical-risk management. Unacceptable negative ethical risk calls for halting development/deployment or removing an out-of-bounds operational system until review. §99 requires annual executive-board AI ethical-assurance statements supported by auditable evidence. Accountability must be transferred into operational use. §105 recommends independent ethics assurance for larger/significant-risk or repeated-use systems; it uses SHOULD, not MUST, and does not establish independence from government or public access. The publication presents an evolving assurance framework and allows organizations to plan routes toward compliance. The record therefore rebuts an assertion of no policy, but supplies no project-specific compliance proof. Ask for the named assurance statement, risk owner, operating bounds and evidence underlying acceptance.
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6735fc89f6920bfb5abc7b62/JSP936_Part1.pdf

The February 2024 government response to the Lords AI-in-weapons report describes governance before, during and after use and says Article36 review is not simply a one-off release hurdle. Material changes can require review; adaptive systems pose challenges. The response discusses exploring supplier transparency into models, data and training, not a guaranteed public right to inspect them. Distinguish committee recommendations from the government's actual replies.
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65cb77caa7ded0000c79e526/Government_response_to_the_House_of_Lords_AI_in_Weapon_Systems_Committee_Report.pdf

GERMANY: A SPECIFIC PARLIAMENTARY DISCLOSURE LIMIT
Bundestag printed paper 21/9054, government answer dated 10 November 2026. Q1/3/8 invoke existing humanitarian law, including stopping attacks against surrendering or incapacitated persons. Q7/9 say a human authorizes engagement regardless of how a target proposal originates. Q4 asks for unclassified minimum sensor/image/data-rate requirements to detect a target's changed status: the answer says none of the requirements within that question are unclassified. Q10 asks about tests/simulations/evaluation distinguishing combatants, surrendering people, wounded people and civilians under varied conditions; government declines to disclose the requested information even classified, citing military capability, operational secrecy and state welfare. This is a precise public-verification gap, NOT proof no tests exist, no oversight exists or all parliamentary access is blocked. The questioners' preamble is not an admission by government. Q11 centres human decision-making for aborting and traceability.
German Q10 excerpt: "auch nicht in eingestufter Form". Translation: "not even in classified form".
https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/090/2109054.pdf
