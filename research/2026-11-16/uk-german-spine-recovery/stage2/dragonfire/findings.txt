BOTTOM LINE
Primary records establish procurement, the intended Type 45 platform and several suppliers' roles. They do not establish that BAE's patented collateral-risk condition has been incorporated into DragonFire, nor identify a BAE CMS-1 deconfliction module as the relevant safety gate. No named-system ethical assurance statement or public Def Stan 00-055/00-056 safety case was located in this bounded search. This is not proof that one does not exist or that assurance was not performed.

ESTABLISHED
1. Royal Navy 20 November 2025 describes £316m MBDA work to develop DragonFire and install it in Type45 ships within two years, working with QinetiQ and Leonardo. This establishes platform/industrial context, not the internals of firing authorization.
2. Commons answer 26254, 6 October 2026, specifies £316m EXCLUDING VAT for TWO units, within £490m directed-energy investment. Its refusal to disaggregate technology/program spending is justified on commercial/value-for-money grounds. Do not reframe this as a refusal to disclose safety tests.
3. QinetiQ 6 January 2026 identifies its £67m MBDA subcontract: developing/producing DragonFire's laser source, plus safe testing. This positively establishes a specific role; BAE's role remains less well evidenced.
4. Commons answer80542 (17 October2025) says externally acquired AI should receive the same standards/assurance as internally developed systems; it describes intent to leverage third-party assurance. It does not identify an independent auditor of DragonFire.
5. Commons answer16183 (13 July2026) distinguishes real-time supervision from human control through operational parameters. It reiterates policy and RAISO oversight, not named-weapon audit results.

JSP936 PRECISE READING (V1.1 Nov2024)
§91 requires safe halting of development/deployment where unacceptable negative ethical risks arise, until managed. It does not specify a particular physical interlock.
§92 requires lifecycle ethical risk management.
§93 requires RAISO management of ethical/operational trade-offs.
§95 requires evidence and appropriate communication, transparency within security constraints.
§99 mandates annual TLB Executive Board statements to 2PUS supported by auditable evidence. These are ORGANIZATIONAL statements, not a requirement for each weapon to publish its own public statement.
§101 requires appropriate assurance across accountable AI projects.
§102 addresses accountability transfer and receiving RAISO acceptance of risk.
§104 requires AI projects to identify their RAISO and medium/high-risk projects to inform that officer.
§105 says there SHOULD be an Ethics Manager and Independent Ethics Assurance mechanism in large projects, significant-ethics-risk projects, or regular AI-producing teams. It does not say MUST, does not automatically require an external contractor, and does not mandate public release of a platform safety case.
Applicability to an individual system requires establishing which functions count as AI and who has responsibility; laser automation alone does not answer this.
