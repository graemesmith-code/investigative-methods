STAGE 2 GAP 1: RIGBY, UNCERTAIN LINKS, RSIC AND PYRAMID
Research date: 16 November 2026. Public unauthenticated sources only.

BOTTOM LINE
This pass materially narrows two gaps. There is a strong candidate for the unnamed Rigby uncertain-link patent, and the UK has published a detailed reference architecture that explicitly separates permission to act, safety interlocks, and store-release scheduling. Neither discovery establishes that a particular operational weapon implements the patent or a particular configuration of those components.

1. PATENT IDENTIFICATION
US9156552B2, Apparatus for use on unmanned vehicles, names Isobel Louise Freeman and Keith Antony Rigby, assigned to BAE Systems. Priority GB1110820.6, 24 June 2011; PCT/GB2012/051416; WO2012/175953; US application publication US20140222249A1; grant 13 October 2015.
Primary PDF: https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/e6/af/64/531ec203e9aa2e/US9156552.pdf

The description discusses the problem of uncertain communications between unmanned aircraft and their controllers. Claim 1 requires validation of a remote activation instruction by checking whether its age is below a predetermined threshold; claim 2 addresses preventing or opposing invalid activation; claim 12 expressly makes the payload a weapon. Claim 16 covers the apparatus and claim 21 a validation-code comparison. These are claim summaries, not statements of field performance.

This is a much closer match to the 2025 RAeS Rigby biography's uncertain-link functionality than US11422549B2. The present search did not find a source expressly saying that the biography refers to US9156552B2. Treat identification as a strongly supported candidate, not a completed attribution. Rigby is not an inventor of US11422549B2. Do not substitute either patent for the other.
A claim about fresh authorised commands is not proof of ethical target discrimination, law-of-war compliance, or a universal requirement to abort when a link fails.

2. OFFICIAL PYRAMID STANDARD: THE ARCHITECTURE IS PUBLIC IN MUCH MORE DETAIL THAN A BIOGRAPHY
UK MOD publication page: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/pyramid
Retrieved complete 2025 technical standard V1.0, 1513 pages, and guidance V1.0, 571 pages.
Standard PDF:
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67dbe7ec0f38d929e57ee310/20250224-PYRAMID_Technical_Standard_V1-O.pdf

Exact anchors:
- Section 5.4.2.3 Authorisation, printed/PDF p117: policy identifies necessary approval and authorisers; the component requests it and monitors continuing validity. Weapon release and entry into restricted zones are examples.
- Section 5.4.2.29 Interlocks, p612: checks defined conditions and enables/disables functions. A store-release example combines the master armaments safety switch, aircraft state, bay-door state, authorisation, release envelope and requested release power.
- Section 5.4.2.58 Stores Release, pp1179–1181: selects stores and schedules release; checks continuing feasibility. Its exclusions are important: this component does not itself control release equipment, transmit mission data/cryptography, validate mass and balance, or prepare the store/verify targeting data.

This is genuine documentary evidence of conceptual separation of authorisation, interlocks and scheduling, not merely the journalist guessing how a system might work. But a reference component definition is not an equipment bill, deployed hardware safety circuit, source-code audit, safety case or acceptance-test result. The standard does not, in the passages reviewed, tie those functions to Rigby's patent or one named deployed weapon. Nor does it establish a single generic degraded-link autonomy policy. No inference of implementation or absence should be made.
