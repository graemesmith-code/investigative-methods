SPINE STAGE 2: TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE GAPS AND COMPLETE LEDGER
Research date: 16 November 2026
Public-source, bounded recovery and gap-filling run

READ THIS FIRST
We found more actual engineering than the earlier record showed. Britain publishes a detailed architecture separating authorization, interlocks and weapon-release scheduling. A Rigby patent provides a plausible match for safe control of unmanned weapons over unreliable links. RCH155 has an onboard ballistic computer, and existing British fire-control software does more than display warnings. These findings narrow the uncertainty; they do not connect a particular patented protection to a purchased weapon's verified configuration.

The remaining accountability question is concrete: what did the customer require, who tested the delivered build, and what evidence can an independent reviewer or Parliament inspect? Public policy and patents cannot answer that alone. Neither missing public evidence nor a patent withdrawal proves that a safeguard was omitted.

WHAT CHANGED
1. Rigby/Pyramid: US9156552B2 tests remote-command age; it is a strong candidate, not explicitly confirmed as the patent meant in the biography. The complete official Pyramid standard and guidance were preserved. Specific components define authorization, interlocks and release scheduling. No deployed configuration or patent-to-Pyramid implementation was established.
2. RCH155: manufacturer evidence distinguishes onboard ballistic computation from the external artillery command connection. CGI describes distributed fire-safety functions; DE&S describes a human safety-officer check. Calling this 'only a map warning' would overstate the gap. No reviewed acceptance record demonstrates the protected-area blocking mechanism in the British/German purchased build. ADLER must not be asserted to be the onboard computer.
3. Boxer: public architecture and SafeNGVA work supply useful institutional leads, but no recovered current contractual GVA standard or acceptance record proves the precise independent inhibit in the UK RS4 build. A hardware safety barrier does not automatically mean dedicated wiring for every signal.
4. DragonFire: QinetiQ's laser-source and safe-testing work is documented. BAE's precise production work package, and a link to its collateral-risk patent, remain unestablished. A refusal to disaggregate cost is not a refusal to release safety tests.
5. Task1: original German claims13–17 now close the translation-numbering problem; the EPO XML is bibliographic only. INPI records were substantive, with a French revocation entry for Naval Group EP3812690 distinct from FR3102239. The NPS-hosted Wu/Srivastava paper has five Raytheon-affiliated authors; it is not an NPS-authored paper. The BMVg public directive was recovered from a clearly identified public mirror.
6. Oversight: JSP936 paragraph99 requires annual organizational assurances, not a public statement per weapon; paragraph105 recommends independent assurance. Bundestag21/5159 Q52 permits authorized classified inspection;21/9054 Q10 withholds the specified test-method details even classified. These are different access decisions.

STAGE 2 GAP 1: RIGBY, UNCERTAIN LINKS, RSIC AND PYRAMID
Research date: 16 November 2026. Public unauthenticated sources only.

BOTTOM LINE
This pass materially narrows two gaps. There is a strong candidate for the unnamed Rigby uncertain-link patent, and the UK has published a detailed reference architecture that explicitly separates permission to act, safety interlocks, and store-release scheduling. Neither discovery establishes that a particular operational weapon implements the patent or a particular configuration of those components.

1. PATENT IDENTIFICATION
US9156552B2, Apparatus for use on unmanned vehicles, names Isobel Louise Freeman and Keith Antony Rigby, assigned to BAE Systems. Priority GB1110820.6, 24 June 2011; PCT/GB2012/051416; WO2012/175953; US application publication US20140222249A1; grant 13 October 2015.
Primary PDF: https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/e6/af/64/531ec203e9aa2e/US9156552.pdf

The description discusses the problem of uncertain communications between unmanned aircraft and their controllers. Claim 1 requires validation of a remote activation instruction by checking whether its age is below a predetermined threshold; claim 2 addresses preventing or opposing invalid activation; claim 12 expressly makes the payload a weapon. Claim 16 covers the apparatus and claim 21 a validation-code comparison. These are claim summaries, not statements of field performance.

This is a much closer match to the 2025 RAeS Rigby biography's uncertain-link functionality than US11422549B2. The present search did not find a source expressly saying that the biography refers to US9156552B2. Treat identification as a strongly supported candidate, not a completed attribution. Rigby is not an inventor of US11422549B2. Do not substitute either patent for the other.
A claim about fresh authorised commands is not proof of ethical target discrimination, law-of-war compliance, or a universal requirement to abort when a link fails.

2. OFFICIAL PYRAMID STANDARD: THE ARCHITECTURE IS PUBLIC IN MUCH MORE DETAIL THAN A BIOGRAPHY
UK MOD publication page: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/pyramid
Retrieved complete 2025 technical standard V1.0, 1513 pages, and guidance V1.0, 571 pages.
Standard PDF:
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67dbe7ec0f38d929e57ee310/20250224-PYRAMID_Technical_Standard_V1-O.pdf

Exact anchors:
- Section 5.4.2.3 Authorisation, printed/PDF p117: policy identifies necessary approval and authorisers; the component requests it and monitors continuing validity. Weapon release and entry into restricted zones are examples.
- Section 5.4.2.29 Interlocks, p612: checks defined conditions and enables/disables functions. A store-release example combines the master armaments safety switch, aircraft state, bay-door state, authorisation, release envelope and requested release power.
- Section 5.4.2.58 Stores Release, pp1179–1181: selects stores and schedules release; checks continuing feasibility. Its exclusions are important: this component does not itself control release equipment, transmit mission data/cryptography, validate mass and balance, or prepare the store/verify targeting data.

This is genuine documentary evidence of conceptual separation of authorisation, interlocks and scheduling, not merely the journalist guessing how a system might work. But a reference component definition is not an equipment bill, deployed hardware safety circuit, source-code audit, safety case or acceptance-test result. The standard does not, in the passages reviewed, tie those functions to Rigby's patent or one named deployed weapon. Nor does it establish a single generic degraded-link autonomy policy. No inference of implementation or absence should be made.

3. RSIC AND NATO LINK REMAINS PARTIAL

The direct raw-file fetch returned HTTP 403 and no wrapper was archived. https://generaldynamics.uk.com/businesses/mission-systems/air/airborne-mission-management/stores-management/

STAGE 2 GAP 2: RCH 155 / FC BISA / ASCA
Public-source research, 16 November 2026

BOTTOM LINE
Public documents show both network-level fire-safety functions and an onboard RCH ballistic computer. They do not establish whether the UK or German RCH configuration installs the protected-area blocking mechanism described in US20230392899A1 claims 13–14. Nor do they justify saying safety exists only as warnings on a commander's map. The unclosed link is the location, authority, configuration and independent acceptance of protected-area enforcement in the procured system, not whether any safety arrangements exist.

1. ACTUAL RCH ARCHITECTURE, NOT JUST ITS TRUCK-MOUNTED RELATIVE
KNDS's current RCH155 page (R08, original HTML saved) explicitly describes an onboard fire-control computer integrating ballistic calculations and a radio-data connection to the customer's chosen artillery-control system. This establishes that onboard computation and external artillery command are separate parts of the advertised architecture. It does not identify the selected British interface, current software configuration or no-fire-area checking behaviour. Automated laying, loading, firing and marketing claims of autonomy are not evidence that a patented civilian-protection gate is installed. The archived AGM page (R04) independently provides the same high-level separation for the related module; it must not substitute for a UK integration specification.

2. FC BISA IS MORE THAN A MAP DISPLAY
CGI's original two-page FC BISA brochure (R02) distinguishes a handheld Fire Control Application, the distributed FC BISA application and an interface unit for external/coalition systems. Both applications use a NATO ballistic calculation kernel. The handheld's features include safety arcs and fire-support coordination measures; FC BISA advertises distributed fire-safety functionality. These are supplier descriptions, not a published independent safety test or evidence about the future RCH build. Nevertheless they are direct counterevidence to portraying the existing layer as merely a map which warns an operator. The brochure's older AS90/light-gun/mortar references limit any inference about RCH integration.

3. ASCA AND A HUMAN SAFETY OFFICER
DE&S's official digital sensor-to-shooter article (R01) describes FC BISA's NATO ASCA interoperability upgrade and a safety officer checking firing information. Its formulation is: "before any shots are fired, the information is checked and verified by a safety officer first". It covers historical FC BISA deployment and existing gun systems, not an RCH-specific acceptance test. ASCA supplies multinational information exchange; the article does not specify an onboard protected-area enforcement mechanism.

4. PLANNED MFP LINK, WITH ACQUISITION LIMIT
A government procurement search result for Welsh notice JUL635764 describes planned FC BISA upgrades requiring interoperability with Mobile Fires Platform. It points toward a genuine procurement integration relationship, rather than a speculative company-name overlap. However, direct fetch of this notice and Find a Tender 064929-2026 returned 403; no original bytes were preserved. Treat this as a search-extract lead, not a verified executed integration specification or completed acceptance. The derivative query log retains the result separately. Do not describe the integration as delivered on this evidence.

5. DO NOT CALL ADLER THE RCH ONBOARD COMPUTER
The German official Bundeswehr search extract describes ADLER as a network linking reconnaissance and effectors, passing target and other information digitally to weapon systems. Original German phrase: "Zieldaten und andere Informationen übermittelt und digital an die Waffensysteme übergeben". Translation: target data and other information are transmitted and handed digitally to the weapon systems. Direct original-page download returned 404; the search extract is derivative evidence only. No recovered primary source identifies IFAB or ADLER as the RCH onboard ballistic computer. Preserve this naming uncertainty rather than propagating the brief's parenthetical identification. A secondary search lead identifies IFAB as a historical predecessor; that is not relied on as an established finding here.

BOUNDED GAPS / QUESTIONS FOR MOD, KNDS AND GERMAN BAAINBW
Q1. Which configuration/version is contracted for the UK RCH Mobile Fires Platform, and which verified interface links FC BISA/ASCA information to its onboard fire-control computer? Can the functional allocation and independent acceptance summary be released without operational parameters?
Q2. In that configuration, does a protected/no-fire-area check merely notify a commander, prevent a firing solution, or inhibit release onboard? Which public or releasable acceptance record establishes this? Please distinguish human review, higher-level software and onboard enforcement.
Q3. Does the procured configuration implement the protected-area mechanism in US20230392899A1 claims 13–14, a different mechanism, or neither? Patent publication, common inventors and product-family marketing do not establish implementation.

Q5. Do not infer that every automated ballistic function is AI or that an AI policy necessarily applies to every fire-control component.

JSP936 PRECISE READING (V1.1 Nov2024)
§91 requires safe halting of development/deployment where unacceptable negative ethical risks arise, until managed. It does not specify a particular physical interlock.
§92 requires lifecycle ethical risk management.
§93 requires RAISO management of ethical/operational trade-offs.
§95 requires evidence and appropriate communication, transparency within security constraints.
§99 mandates annual TLB Executive Board statements to 2PUS supported by auditable evidence. These are ORGANIZATIONAL statements, not a requirement for each weapon to publish its own public statement.
§101 requires appropriate assurance across accountable AI projects.
§102 addresses accountability transfer and receiving RAISO acceptance of risk.
§104 requires AI projects to identify their RAISO and medium/high-risk projects to inform that officer.
§105 says there SHOULD be an Ethics Manager and Independent Ethics Assurance mechanism in large projects, significant-ethics-risk projects, or regular AI-producing teams. It does not say MUST, does not automatically require an external contractor, and does not mandate public release of a platform safety case.
Applicability to an individual system requires establishing which functions count as AI and who has responsibility; laser automation alone does not answer this.
