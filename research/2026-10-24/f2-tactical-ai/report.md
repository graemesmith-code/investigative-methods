# AI enabled tactical robots and weapons in US defence procurement

Research findings and reporting leads from the F2 pass

Evidence cut off 24 October 2026

## Executive assessment

**Methods clarification added 24 October 2026.** The original Strong Operational Enabler threshold was overstrict for investigative journalism. Controlled, same-hardware ablations can establish a narrow claim of comparative causal superiority, but they are not required to establish strong practical evidence that an AI component is used in a consequential military capability. Credible technical descriptions, executed procurement, named physical tests and corroborating government or operator accounts can support that practical judgment. The original grades below are retained as historical outputs of the original rubric, not silently reassigned. Their use as overall judgments of practical evidential strength is superseded by this clarification. No Strong grades does not mean no strong evidence or practical value. Mechanism and use, maturity, comparative advantage and editorial novelty must be assessed separately. The contracts, test facts, source limitations and unresolved attribution questions are unchanged.

The clearest change in this public record is not a giant language model commanding a war. It is learned perception and control moving into physical military systems: terrain interpretation on unmanned ground vehicles, recognition and tracking in weapon stations, and coordination of small aircraft under disrupted communications. The procurement trail includes executed research contracts, prototype agreements and, separately, production orders. It does not establish widespread operational deployment of autonomous lethal decision making.

The strongest reporting opportunity is the gap between a successful demonstration and an accepted military system. Publicity often collapses three different propositions: a company has an AI capability; the military bought something from that company; and that exact capability passed military acceptance testing. This research keeps those propositions separate. It found concrete questions about configuration, funding lineage, human authorization, test denominators and transition criteria. It did not establish a concealed failure, an unlawful weapon, or a safety shortcut.

Three conditional main reporting dossiers are developed below, rather than padding the requested six to eight. The original rubric assigned all three Plausible Prototype Enabler and none Strong Operational Enabler. Those labels are historical scoring results under the now-qualified rule, not a finding that these systems lack strong practical evidence. The pass did not produce six leads clearing its original combined gates; no new comprehensive regrading has been performed. The three dossiers remain prioritized reporting hypotheses, not three proven unreported scoops. Their individual funding-to-test, maturity and coverage qualifications remain material even after correcting the overly strict comparative-proof requirement.

1. **WPN01 Overland and GAUNTLET:** fresh Army evidence of commander-approved ground and air robotic effects, alongside an exact funded lethal-effects prototype agreement. The critical contract-to-demonstration bridge remains a question, not a discovered fact.
2. **WPN02 Gambit NEURON:** the strongest specific project-to-physical-experiment bridge. A government test-host account names NEURON during flights under communications disruption. It does not isolate what learning, rather than conventional radio engineering, contributed.
3. **WPN03 Allen Control Systems Bullfrog:** executed Army weapon-station awards and a vendor-authored technical test account covering 13 targets. The reporting target is independent acceptance and results by human-authorization mode, not a falsely missing denominator or the already well-publicized product.

**HB01 OKSI/AimLock is a historical boundary, not a fourth main lead.** A 2024 government publication describes neural recognition on a small-caliber weapon, but the verified award ended in 2022 and the physical test date is unspecified. Without an exact newer Phase III bridge, it does not establish the required 2024–2026 transition.

Tiami and Deepnight are high-priority reserves, not weaker facts dressed up as main findings. Each combines meaningful physical evidence with actual funding, but the funded model and demonstrated configuration are not yet tied together. Other reserves concern aircraft-autonomy procurement, Scout's Army integration, and adaptive control on surrogate boats.

No item is presented as a verified scoop. Existing journalism is credited, including work in WIRED, Defense News, Aerospace America, DefenseScoop and Shephard. A bounded search finding of no independent exact-event article is not proof of worldwide absence. The editorial priority rankings concern where additional reporting may be worthwhile.

## Main shortlist

### WPN01 Overland moves from autonomous driving to commanded robotic effects

**Programme and editorial hook.** Overland AI's RACER heritage, the GAUNTLET prototype agreement and the Army's 15 October 2026 Fort Knox demonstration provide a concrete way to investigate where navigation software becomes part of a military sensor-to-effector chain. The novel reporting question is what the government tested and accepted at that boundary. It is not the existence of Overland or its partnership with Anduril.

**What changes on the battlefield.** Learned interpretation of unstructured terrain could let ground vehicles move without continuous driving instructions while carrying sensors, logistics or other payloads. Combining ground mobility with airborne sensing and effectors potentially changes how a small team controls dispersed robots. The public evidence does not show an autonomous system independently deciding to kill.

**Physical evidence.** The [Army's 22 October account](https://www.army.mil/article/296278/darpa_autonomous_vehicles_deliver_command_controlled_robotic_lethality_at_fort_knox_event) describes four RACER-derived UGVs at Fort Knox on 15 October, Anduril one-way-attack platforms, autonomous mobility and detection, and human approval of engagements against simulated targets. EUCOM requested the proof of concept. The account calls the event live-fire but also uses simulated and hypothetical elements; it is not evidence of an autonomous missile salvo or combat deployment. Exact payload configurations, aggregate error rates and intervention logs are not disclosed.

The [DARPA RACER programme page](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/robotic-autonomy-in-complex-environments-with-resiliency) separately documents heavy-platform breaching demonstrations with an M58 MICLIC and Army units in October and November 2025. Those physical demonstrations must not be confused with acceptance of six heavy trucks ordered later. An April 2026 [Overland ROGUE Fires account](https://www.overland.ai/news/overland-ai-demonstrates-autonomous-ground-capability-for-usmc-rogue-fires) reports autonomous movement on a prototype, not demonstrated autonomous firing or a ROGUE Fires production purchase.

**AI mechanism and counterfactual.** An [NPS-hosted JIFX quad chart](https://nps.edu/documents/104517539/152398987/JIFX%2B25-1%2BQuad%2BCharts.pdf/67dda27c-16af-5424-58bf-bcdcb0b2ee42?download=true&t=1731110835820&version=4.0), page 10, describes stereo-camera/lidar terrain interpretation and deep networks estimating terrain and obscured areas for driving costs. This is a credible deep-learning-era mechanism, not dependent on post-2020 generative models. Classical mapping, planners and teleoperation are real alternatives. The cited speeds and readiness are performer statements in an experiment proposal, not independently established Fort Knox results. The mobility architecture does not prove that every detector and payload in the combined system is neural.

**Verified procurement.** DARPA **HR0011269E197**, signed 4 June 2026, is explicitly a **prototype Other Transaction agreement**, titled Ground-Air Unmanned Networked Teaming and Lethal Effector Technology. Three FPDS action deltas reconcile to **$4,903,954 obligated**: $242,216, $3,827,229 and $834,509. Potential value is **$6,373,110**. Awarding and funding office is DARPA HR0011; current completion is 3 November 2026 and ultimate completion 3 January 2027. The [exact primary FPDS feed](https://www.fpds.gov/ezsearch/FEEDS/ATOM?FEEDNAME=PUBLIC&VERSION=1.5.3&q=PIID%3A%22HR0011269E197%22&s=FPDSNG.COM&indexName=awardfull&start=0) proves the agreement, **not that it funded the Fort Knox event**.

There is a separate, real production transition. **M678542690040** is an explicit Marine Corps production OT for complete military UGVs and support. The [official 26 June announcement](https://www.war.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/4528154/contracts-for-june-26-2026/) identifies its predecessor, **HQ08452390066**, and MADIS support. MADIS means Marine Air Defense Integrated System. It is not ROGUE Fires. FPDS's base action obligates **$17,444,750.05**, with initial potential **$19,744,499**. The announcement instead states an initial obligation of **$16,897,683**. Later feed aggregate fields do not reconcile to the two visible actions. These differences remain open, not silently harmonized. The predecessor's six visible actions do reconcile to **$6,262,295.57**. Appendix A preserves each figure and source.

**Original rubric grade and evidence limits.** Plausible Prototype Enabler. Funded RACER development and physical military integration are established, but the exact GAUNTLET-to-Fort-Knox bridge is not. The fresh event clears the physical-test threshold; a configuration-specific classical comparison, detection-error profile and full acceptance chain are missing. Coverage supports a bounded exact-event follow-up, not a claim that Overland weapon integration is undiscovered.

**Coverage audit.** The exact Fort Knox event was documented by the Army; no substantive independent exact-event feature was identified in the six dedicated Exa searches. However, [October 2025 vendor publicity](https://www.overland.ai/news/anduril-overland-ai-coordinating-autonomous-effects-across-air-and-land) already described air-ground coordination. The production transition is already covered by [DefenseScoop, 29 June 2026](https://defensescoop.com/2026/06/29/autonomous-ground-vehicle-marine-corps-overland-ai-contract/) and [GeekWire, 8 October 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/overland-ai-unveils-ibex-a-new-autonomous-ground-vehicle-selected-by-the-marine-corps/). Those are substantive prior disclosures, not an absence of reporting.

**Next reporting question.** Which GAUNTLET work packages and software configurations were used at Fort Knox, and what test or acceptance authority evaluated operator workload, false detections, aborted engagements and communications loss? Request configuration-specific test summaries and the transition/acceptance documents, not sensitive implementation details. Ask separately which vehicles were accepted under the Marine production order. No failure or shortcut is alleged.

### WPN02 Gambit NEURON keeps a drone experiment connected under disruption

**Programme and editorial hook.** The unusually solid bridge is between a named SBIR project and an identified government-hosted experiment, not merely between a vendor and a drone photograph. NEURON's recent funding increase and physical trials make acceptance and transition a tractable records question.

**What changes on the battlefield.** Small robot teams must receive mission changes and coordinate when their preferred communications channel is unavailable. Distributed communications and onboard autonomy could reduce dependence on one link and on constant operator input. That is not evidence that these drones can conduct unrestricted offensive electronic attack.

**Physical and technical evidence.** The [Naval Postgraduate School's 30 September 2026 account](https://nps.edu/-/energizing-the-technology-of-tomorrow) describes August JIFX flights using ModalAI quadcopters for detection, following, patrol and swarm tasks. The article reports that communications were maintained when jammed; its gallery captions identify **NEURON** and describe tests of alternate-band switching and mission updates under disruption. This is a primary institutional account of an experiment, not an independent controlled evaluation. It does not give the denominator of successful versus failed missions or isolate which behavior depended on learning.

The [NEURON SBIR abstract](https://www.sbir.gov/awards/216349) and [CORTEX abstract](https://www.sbir.gov/awards/216352) describe proposed networked autonomy and AI/ML adversary training. Related experiment material names decentralized reinforcement-learning autonomy and onboard recognition. The NPS article does not establish that CORTEX was the tested configuration. The April 2026 [GVSC announcement](https://www.gambit.us/news-and-blogs/gambit-secures-u-s-army-gvsc-ota-to-advance-coordinated-autonomy-in-c-uas-simulation) concerns a separate simulation effort without an identified award value in the checked source.

**Verified procurement.** **FA864925P0453**, Gambit Defense Inc., is an AFRL fixed-price NEURON purchase order signed 26 August 2025. The live record shows **$1,511,684.51 obligated and current all-options value**, including a June 2026 enhancement, rather than the earlier SBIR snapshot's $1,161,732. Current performance ends 29 April 2027. Awarding office is FA2385 USAF AFMC AFRL PZL AFRL RSKD; funding office F4FBEQ AFRL RGF. [Verified award record](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_FA864925P0453_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-/).

**FA864925P0493**, CORTEX, is a separate fixed-price AFRL purchase order, signed 25 August 2025, **$1,788,872 obligated and potential**, current end 1 June 2027. It funds AI/ML counter-UAS opposing forces, not proof of an accepted offensive swarm. [Verified award record](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_FA864925P0493_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-/). Do not add these values and call the sum the cost of the August trial.

**Original rubric grade and evidence limits.** Plausible Prototype Enabler. The exact NEURON programme name links the executed award and the government-hosted physical experiment, stronger than a same-vendor join. This does not independently establish which award deliverable paid for every sortie or component. Learned coordination and perception have a credible role, but frequency agility, mesh routing and hand-coded contingency control predate recent AI. No disclosed same-hardware ablation establishes that reinforcement learning caused the successful communications behavior. Claims of dramatic percentage efficiency gains are not treated as measured findings.

**Coverage audit.** Dedicated Exa searches located the NPS account and company/government disclosures rather than a substantive exact-trial technical investigation. This does not mean Gambit or its funding was unreported. [Laurence Darmiento's Los Angeles Times article, 19 January 2026, syndicated by Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/l-defense-industry-booming-federal-110000780.html), covered SBIR funding and its lapse; [Axios, 7 January 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/07/gambit-stealth-autonomy-drones-venture), covered the company and partnerships; [Defense Daily, 7 January 2026](https://www.defensedaily.com/autonomy-software-firm-gambit-exits-stealth-with-dod-contracts/advanced-transformational-technology/), covered contracts and capabilities. The remaining angle is the trial's configuration, evaluation and transition, not a startup profile or an announcement of SBIR money.

**Next reporting question.** What did NEURON's government deliverable require at JIFX, which test build ran, and how did mission completion, communications continuity and operator interventions compare with a conventional mesh/autonomy baseline? Ask whether the current award includes a formal operational-customer acceptance event. An FCC experimental-filing mirror suggests further tests were planned for November 2026; its underlying exhibit was not primary-verified here and the planned event is not counted as completed evidence.

### WPN03 Bullfrog brings neural recognition into a funded weapon station

**Programme and editorial hook.** Allen Control Systems has actual Army contracts for its counter-drone weapon station. A 2026 technical paper goes beyond a product announcement by naming tests, operating modes and a small, bounded test population. The story is independent acceptance and the limits of that evidence, not the existence of an AI-controlled gun or a claim that no denominator was disclosed.

**What changes on the battlefield.** A small-caliber remote weapon station that automatically detects and tracks small aircraft could reduce the attention and aiming workload of counter-drone defense. Tracking accuracy, authorization to fire and effectiveness are separate questions. The research does not provide weapon implementation instructions or infer that automatic tracking means unreviewed lethal authority.

**Physical evidence.** The vendor-authored SAE/NDIA GVSETS paper [Bullfrog AI Powered Counter Unmanned Aerial System, 2026-01-7519](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/bullfrog-ai-powered-counter-unmanned-aerial-system-2026-01-7519) identifies computer vision and machine learning, an M240B-based system, T-REX 26-1 testing, and autonomous versus human-in-loop modes. The [full public vendor manuscript, sections 3.1 to 3.3](https://ndia-mich.org/2026%20tech%20papers/AR/11%2040%20AM%20-%20Bullfrog%20GVSETS26_ACS_Bullfrog%20CUAS_Final.pdf), reports **13 of 13 targets defeated across ten sequences at T-REX 26-1 in December 2025**, and **99.45 percent availability over nine hours**. The short abstract alone omits these denominators; the fuller paper supplies them. These are **vendor-reported, bounded test metrics**, not independently verified performance across battlefield conditions. Independent acceptance, outcomes separated by control mode, false-alarm behavior and unengaged threats remain unverified here. The public indexed manuscript sections were inspected; its direct download returned 406, so no direct PDF rendering or independent raw-data validation is claimed.

A [16 December 2025 integration announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251216968418/en/Allen-Control-Systems-Selected-by-U.S.-Army-Applications-Lab-for-Combat-Vehicle-Integration-Readiness-Plan) concerns integration-readiness work for Army combat vehicles. It is not proof that all named platforms have accepted or fielded the weapon station. Europe's separate Project Bullfrog and Army reports about other counter-drone systems were excluded as name collisions.

**Verified procurement.** **W911NF25CA031**, Allen Control Systems Inc., signed 25 September 2025, is a firm-fixed-price definitive contract explicitly describing a Bullfrog AI-powered C-UAS remote weapon station feasibility study. **$1,551,553.53 is obligated and the current all-options value.** Performance runs 1 October 2025 to 30 November 2026. Awarding office W6QK ACC-APG Durham; funding office W0CU USA Futures Command. [Verified award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_W911NF25CA031_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-/). The release's potential additional options up to $4.5 million are not counted as awarded or obligated money.

**W5170126CA031**, signed 26 January 2026, is a separate fixed-price Army award titled Bullfrog Autonomous Weapon Station under A254-P030. **$1,993,735.42 is obligated**; performance 30 January 2026 to 30 July 2027. Awarding and funding office W27P USA Acquisition Support Center. [Verified award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_W5170126CA031_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-/). Neither award alone establishes that T-REX results were acceptance deliverables under that exact instrument.

**Original rubric grade and evidence limits.** Plausible Prototype Enabler. Executed contracts name Bullfrog, and the physical tested system is Bullfrog, but the sources do not explicitly allocate T-REX to either PIID or prove accepted vehicle integration. The programme-level physical gate is met; the exact work-package bridge and a novel results-led story are still open. Visual learned classification/tracking is technically credible and connected to physical tests. Classical radar/optical tracking and automated fire-control systems are longstanding alternatives. The public record does not provide a controlled comparison isolating the neural contribution, or show that the headline success rate applies to an adversarial operational environment.

**Media coverage audit.** [WIRED, 11 November 2024](https://www.wired.com/story/us-military-robot-drone-guns/), and [Janes, 17 December 2025](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/weapons/us-army-to-test-c-uas-auto-turret-for-combat-vehicles), provide substantive prior journalism. The product, lethal potential and vehicle-integration plan are not unreported. A new story requires independent acceptance evidence, results by control mode and configuration limits, not repetition of the company demonstration.

**Next reporting question.** Which mode and configuration did the government evaluate, how many targets and trials were attempted, what exclusions or interventions affected the reported success rate, and what conditions govern authorization to engage? Seek the government's releasable test summary and integration-readiness deliverables, comparing them with the vendor's disclosed 13-target population. Differences between a vendor result and an acceptance record must be established, not presumed; no improper testing is alleged.

## Reserves and boundary cases

### RES01 Tiami PolyEdge and SEMPRE base defense

**Why it is worth pursuing.** A [20 May 2026 participant account](https://tiaminetworks.com/polyedge-aion-c2-integration-courage-lethality-2026/) describes PolyEdge passive cellular sensing feeding Northrop Grumman's AiON command-and-control display during Courage Lethality live flying at Joint Base Lewis-McChord. Northrop is quoted on rapid integration. The demonstrated endpoint is tracks displayed and correlated with other radar, not interception or autonomous weapon release.

Separately, SEMPRE's **FA462526C0009** is a verified fixed-price procurement for a C-UAS Striker package and SEMPRE T network support: **$44,761,105 obligated and potential**, 6 March 2026 to 30 September 2027, awarding office 509 CONS, funding AFGS DPCC. [Federal record](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_FA462526C0009_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-/). SEMPRE's [28 January partnership release](https://sempre.ai/news/sempre-announces-contract-with-afgsc-to-deliver-counter-uas-protection-with-partners-dedrone-by-axon-and-tiami-networks) names Tiami and Dedrone and discusses Barksdale. It predates the March award and does not name its PIID. The whole prime value is not Tiami revenue, and a partnership is not a line-item allocation.

**Missing gate.** Product material describes local learned RF classification, but the exact model and accepted military configuration are not verified. Passive radar has non-AI alternatives. Tiami's original **HR001125PE006 SpectrumSense** remains a $149,760 Phase I diffusion proposal; no diffusion-to-PolyEdge transition was shown. **W5170125CA141** has the official title SATCOM connectivity slices, not PolyEdge; a separate Army experimentation reference cannot silently rewrite the award's purpose. **FA875024CB118**, $1,230,240, explicitly funds fusion learning but is not proof that its model produced the demonstrated tracks.

**Coverage and question.** Vendor publicity, syndication, a G2X contract item and a Fierce Sensors feature precede this research. The remaining question is which hardware and software builds were bought and accepted under which line items, at what subcontract amount, with what track-continuity and false-alarm results. Conditional Plausible Prototype Enabler; reserve until the model-to-procurement bridge is documented.

### RES02 Deepnight neural low light sensing

**Evidence.** **W5170125CA242**, Deepnight Inc., is a fixed-price Army contract with **$1,999,911.25 obligated**, signed 7 August 2025, performance 18 August 2025 to 18 August 2027, W27P USA Acquisition Support Center. The [SBIR abstract](https://www.sbir.gov/awards/215803) describes a neural low-light vision capability for autonomous UAS navigation and sensing, including future onboard optimization and recognition objectives. The [federal award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_W5170125CA242_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-/) verifies funding, not an achieved airborne engagement capability.

A separate [28 April 2026 Circle Optics account](https://circleoptics.com/circle-optics-and-deepnight-deploy-integrated-counter-uas-capability-across-u-s-air-force-installations/) claims base-sensor installations at Barksdale and the Barry M Goldwater Range. [Shephard's 20 May interview](https://www.shephardmedia.com/news/defence-notes/sof-week-2026-us-military-tests-ai-algorithm-to-support-missions-in-low-light-scenarios/) already discusses those trials. Only the accessible part of that article was inspected; no paywall was bypassed.

**Missing gate and question.** Low-light neural processing is a credible mechanism, but a fixed ground-camera trial does not establish the Army's airborne model, computational envelope, recognition reliability or autonomous-engagement result. Compare against improved optics, infrared sensors and classical enhancement rather than saying vision at night was previously impossible. Ask for the actual UAS integration milestone and configuration-specific low-light evaluation, including false positives and operator intervention. Conditional prototype reserve, not a deployment finding.

### RES03 Aircraft and collaborative weapon autonomy procurement

**AIR and VENOM.** Government sources establish a real flight transition. [AFRL's 16 July account](https://afresearchlab.com/venom-program-progresses-to-piloted-flights-autonomy-tests/) distinguishes **June checkout flights from successful July autonomous flight**. VENOM's interface permits AI control on modified F-16s while retaining a pilot and a control switch. The [DARPA milestone](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2026/darpa-us-air-force-fly-ai-controlled-f-16) does not identify the agent contractor. Aerospace America's [22 July interview](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/new-test-campaign-will-put-ai-pilots-in-most-realistic-flight-conditions-yet-darpa-official-says/) already explains the test's more realistic sensing and dynamics.

Episys/EpiSci's **HR0011269E025** is a current AIR prototype OT, signed 6 October 2025, **$7,085,366 obligated against $14,354,513 potential**, through 5 April 2028. Eight action deltas reconcile. It proves ongoing development in the flight-test period, not which agent flew. Earlier Lockheed, BAE and EpiSci awards are separately audited; they cannot all be assumed to supply the 2026 airborne software.

**EpiSci kits.** **FA228025C0010** explicitly buys SBIR Phase III UAV kits for **$4,725,000 obligated and potential**, signed 26 September 2025, ending 15 October 2026. **FA228025C0002** is a different **Phase II STRATFI**, with $12,434,000 obligated and $15 million potential, not Phase III. The [official STRATFI abstract](https://www.sbir.gov/awards/216103) names intended adaptations and hybrid deep-RL/deterministic control. It does not prove each aircraft flew the stack, or identify the kits' antecedent SBIR. The funding office's security-assistance association does not establish an export destination. [Phase III award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_FA228025C0010_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-/).

**Scientific Systems BLUE.** **FA865925CB002**, $1,999,633 obligated and potential, explicitly concerns Phase III BLUE AI and next-generation Colosseum evaluation. Scientific Systems separately claims [Rusty Dagger coordinated-arrival testing](https://www.ssci.com/what-we-do/collaborative-weapons-autonomy/). Its page says June 2025 while an image filename says June 2026; neither date was silently selected as definitive. No checked source connects that physical test to the BLUE contract. A digital test environment does not become a tested weapon through vendor identity alone.

**Question.** Which model, software build and physical platform were accepted under each instrument, and what predecessor documents establish the Phase III lineage? Ask for model-specific intervention and sim-to-real results, not another announcement of AI flight. AIR is a physical plausible enabler already deeply covered; the kit and BLUE bridges remain unproved.

### RES04 Scout Fury and Army acceptance

Scout has executed Army contracts and physical demonstrations. **W5170125CA057**, $2 million, explicitly names vision-language models and retrieval-augmented generation for ground vehicles beginning with counter-UAS. **W5170126CA024**, $1,999,698.90, is another Direct to Phase II award, not Phase III. **HR00112590205** is now primary-verified in FPDS as a RACER prototype OT IDV: $1 million obligated, $2.5 million potential, with a zero-dollar extension to 21 May 2027. Missing USAspending results did not mean the agreement was missing from the public record.

WIRED's Will Knight [reported the lethal orchestration demonstration in depth on 18 February 2026](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-lab-scout-ai-is-using-ai-agents-to-blow-things-up/). Breaking Defense [reported the Army's switch to 12 Polaris MRZR Alphas in August](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/08/army-now-using-polaris-mrzr-for-uxs-program-official/). A vendor Army-facing video showing a passenger and a driver taking hands off the wheel is not proof of an unoccupied mission. Plan approval in one demonstration is not proof of a universal per-engagement rule.

The useful question is which platform, payload and model the Army actually accepted under each award, with what safety-driver and intervention conditions. Original rubric grade: Plausible Prototype Enabler, but deep prior coverage and unresolved government acceptance keep it in reserve. The publicized potential $150 million follow-on is not invested money.

### RES05 LINC and FALCON adaptive boat control

LINC is not simulation-only. Aurora's [19 February 2026 release](https://www.aurora.aero/2026/02/19/aurora-successfully-demonstrates-ai-enabled-control-technology/) describes late-2025 trials on 1.5-metre and 5-metre USVs, with relative station keeping for a simulated replenishment mission and imposed disturbances. Human reference commands remained part of the setup. Reported 63/82/94 percent safe-zone occupancy and 61 percent faster recovery are vendor results without independent logs.

Aurora's **N6523623C8011** is a cost-plus-fixed-fee LINC research contract: **$6,195,797 obligated, $6,208,405.35 potential**, 8 February 2023 to 30 September 2026, NIWC Atlantic. Caltech and Peraton have separate research contracts; their amounts are not added to the cost of this demonstration. [Aurora award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_N6523623C8011_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-/).

Adaptive/introspective learned control plausibly helps with changed dynamics, but this is not proof of recovery from real battle damage or accepted control of a military replenishment vessel. Older model-based adaptive control is a serious alternative. Ask what full-size military hardware, service transition agreement and final acceptance results followed. Plausible Prototype Enabler in a surrogate setting; no verified tactical transition.

## What the causal and operational test ruled out

A funded award title can be a useful lead without being sufficient evidence of a modern-AI military capability. The following exclusions are substantive findings of this selection exercise, not claims that the programmes lack value or could not have undisclosed results.

## Appendix B Research method and selection audit

### Grading clarification dated 24 October 2026

The original rubric conflated four different questions. Its comparative-proof requirement was too strict as a general threshold for journalistic lead selection. In particular, absence of a publicly disclosed, controlled comparison against a classical system on the same military hardware should not automatically cap a capability at merely plausible. Such data would strengthen a specific causal or performance claim, but public procurement and weapons trials rarely disclose research-style ablations. Their absence is not itself a negative finding about a system.

The corrected interpretation separates four axes:

1. Mechanism and actual use. Is there credible, specific evidence that a modern learned component performs a meaningful function in the named physical system? Executed procurement, technical descriptions, government test accounts and corroborating operator or supplier evidence may jointly provide strong practical evidence without an academic ablation. A contract title alone remains insufficient.
2. Maturity. Is the capability proposed, integrated, demonstrated in a bounded exercise, accepted by a customer, or deployed? These are different stages. A well-evidenced prototype can be important journalism without being fielded.
3. Comparative causal advantage. What evidence supports a claim that learning improves performance, adaptability, cost or workload relative to alternatives? Distinguish a credible engineering explanation from measured comparison and a controlled causal test. Uncertainty on this axis limits the claimed advantage, not every other conclusion.
4. Editorial novelty and reporting value. What has already been reported, and what consequential, answerable question remains? Strong technical evidence can be well covered, while a less mature but concretely funded prototype can justify investigation. Neither a missing ablation nor minor trade coverage is an automatic veto.

All original case grades and selection records are preserved for audit and should be read as historical outputs of the original rubric. They are qualified by this clarification and must not be treated as current overall ratings of practical evidence. No retrospective mass reclassification has been performed, and no new searches or spending were undertaken for this amendment. This is an editorial correction to how the findings are interpreted, not a change to any contract amount, test result, source attribution, maturity fact or unresolved lineage.
