## Ranking

Scores are editorial triage, not measured likelihoods of wrongdoing. Consequence (1–5) estimates potential public-interest harm if the predicate proves true. Feasibility (1–5) measures route maturity: 1 is conceptual/limited documentation, 2 is documented components, 3 would require relevant records, 4 a demonstrated linked component and 5 an end-to-end reproducible test. Every current card is at most 2. Join route (1–3) distinguishes provisional person/name bridges, multi-field conditional routes and precise candidate identifiers; it never certifies an actual identity match. Burden (1–4) estimates increasing document, identity, access and historical-record effort. No dollar/time estimate is empirically verified.

The composite is `10 × consequence + 3 × feasibility + 2 × join route − 2 × burden`. Importance, feasibility and composite ranks are separate columns across all 16 cards, including the four labeled refinements. Filter `kind=new_mechanism` when seeking new ideas. Composite weights are subjective and editable. Small rank differences should not determine a newsroom investigation.

### X03 — Reported waste destination versus actual handling authority

Kind: new_mechanism. Composite rank 1; consequence 5/5, feasibility 2/5, join route 3/3, burden 3/4.

**If:** IF a TRI sender reports a chemical/year transfer to a specifically identified off-site receiver under a disposal, treatment, energy-recovery or recycling code.

**Then:** THEN resolve the actual receiving operation and compare the reported handling route with its historical permit/authorization and shipment evidence for the same waste, process and interval.

**Why it matters / basis:** A verified incompatible destination or management account can expose a waste oversight gap; a transfer is not necessarily a release or a hazardous waste.

**Join:** Sender TRIFID is not the receiver RCRA ID. Use the reported receiver identifier/address, validate the receiving site rather than a broker, and crosswalk chemical to actual regulated waste/process evidence.

**Scope:** US domestic routes with identifiable receiver; annual TRI totals may span permit changes, so dated shipment evidence is mandatory for event timing.

**Ordinary explanations / disqualifiers:** Nonhazardous or exempt stream; Permitted intermediary or onward transfer; Code or address error; Different permitted process/unit; Current permit status differs from historic authorization

**Required evidence:** Form R transfer record and revisions; Historical receiver site/permit and applicable waste classification; Dated manifests/receipts or equivalent transfer evidence; Actual downstream process

**Next cheapest check:** Inspect the transfer fields and one historical permit scope before considering any allegation; absence of a RCRA permit by itself is disqualifying as an illegality inference.

**Novelty:** C01 compares emissions estimates; C02/C05 are spatial exposure. This follows the sender-to-receiver waste-management chain and its permitted process.

**Access evidence:** Current sender guidance and receiver modules documented; no permit, manifest or transfer record sampled. No bulk RCRA files downloaded.

**Catalog provenance:** SYS-0218 US EPA Toxics Release Inventory (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:13764); SYS-0038 US EPA ECHO web services (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:2250)

**Documentation:** E04, E05, E06 in evidence/documentation.jsonl. All case applicability remains unverified.

### X11 — A synthesis treating overlapping participants as independent evidence

Kind: new_mechanism. Composite rank 2; consequence 5/5, feasibility 2/5, join route 3/3, burden 3/4.

**If:** IF a review or meta-analysis treats two papers as independent study populations, and explicit trial references suggest their enrolled cohorts may overlap.

**Then:** THEN reconstruct cohorts, arms, recruitment intervals and outcomes from registry history and full papers, and determine whether the synthesis double-counts the same participants for the same comparison.

**Why it matters / basis:** Inflated independent evidence can alter treatment or policy conclusions. Multiple papers from one trial are ordinary and not misconduct when handled correctly.

**Join:** DOI references → explicit NCT/protocol IDs → cohort/arm/recruitment overlap → synthesis study rows. One NCT is not automatically one identical participant set.

**Scope:** Matched trial versions and actual synthesis analysis; repeated follow-up, platform trials and subgroup populations need explicit handling.

**Ordinary explanations / disqualifiers:** Disjoint cohorts or arms; Synthesis combines dependence correctly; Different outcomes/timepoints validly analyzed; Secondary analysis clearly separated; Registry ID entered incorrectly

**Required evidence:** Full synthesis methods and included-study table; Both primary papers; Trial history and cohort/arm details; Reproducible participant/effect mapping

**Next cheapest check:** Read the synthesis’s exact inclusion rows and the papers’ cohort descriptions before counting shared trial IDs as duplicate evidence.

**Novelty:** H02 compares prespecified outcomes and P01 checks retractions. This tests independence of the evidence units used in a synthesis.

**Access evidence:** Registry and DOI metadata routes documented earlier; full texts/cohort records and synthesis calculation not tested.

**Catalog provenance:** SYS-0248 ClinicalTrials.gov API (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:15540); SYS-0250 Crossref REST API (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:15823); SYS-0249 OpenAlex API (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:15599)

**Documentation:** E15 in evidence/documentation.jsonl. All case applicability remains unverified.

### X01 — Direct industry payments versus an explicit article disclosure

Kind: existing_mechanism_refinement. Composite rank 4; consequence 5/5, feasibility 2/5, join route 1/3, burden 2/4.

**If:** IF an article expressly declares no relevant financial relationships, and a verified author received direct general consulting or honoraria payments from a manufacturer relevant to the article within the journal’s applicable window.

**Then:** THEN read the full declaration, supplements and corrections against the dated direct-payment records and exact policy to determine whether a material relationship is unaccounted for.

**Why it matters / basis:** An undisclosed relevant relationship can affect readers’ interpretation of clinical evidence; a payment alone proves neither bias nor policy breach.

**Join:** CMS physician/profile ID identifies the payment recipient; DOI identifies the article. Author identity requires an independent biographical/affiliation bridge. ORCID is supporting, not an automatic CMS-to-author key.

**Scope:** US-covered recipients; payment date versus declaration/submission date and applicable policy version. Product relevance must be established, not inferred from any industry payment.

**Ordinary explanations / disqualifiers:** Namesake or wrong author; Research/institutional payment wrongly allocated to the investigator; Supplement or correction discloses the tie; Window, relevance or policy excludes the relationship; Disputed/corrected CMS report

**Required evidence:** Original article and full disclosure; Journal policy at submission; Verified person identity; Direct general-payment category and payer/product; CMS corrections/dispute status

**Next cheapest check:** Read one article’s precise declaration and policy, then confirm a permissible direct-payment record and author identity; do not start by summing institutional research payments.

**Novelty:** Independent review correction: H04 already calls for published conflict declarations and payment taxonomy (journalists-guide section 14). This sharpens direct general-payment attribution and policy window; it is not a new mechanism.

**Access evidence:** CMS public routes documented; article/full supplement availability and author bridge unverified.

**Catalog provenance:** SYS-0247 CMS Open Payments (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:15481); SYS-0250 Crossref REST API (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:15823); SYS-0251 ORCID Public API (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:16047)

**Documentation:** E01, E02 in evidence/documentation.jsonl. All case applicability remains unverified.

### X02 — Fixed extractive-contract obligation versus covered receipts

Kind: new_mechanism. Composite rank 5; consequence 5/5, feasibility 2/5, join route 2/3, burden 3/4.

**If:** IF the operative original contract fixes a particular annual rent, community contribution or guaranteed minimum due for one project/payer/payee and period, and the payment trigger is evidenced.

**Then:** THEN compare that exact obligation with a demonstrably complete matching receipt stream, reconciling any payer disclosure and authorized amendment before describing a shortfall.

**Why it matters / basis:** A fixed amount supplies an independent contractual benchmark for public revenue. A low or absent aggregate does not prove nonpayment.

**Join:** Contract/project identity → legal payer → exact government/community payee → payment stream → period and currency. Company-level EITI totals cannot substitute for a project receipt.

**Scope:** Only countries/projects with granular EITI coverage; contract effective dates, trigger, payment deadline, fiscal basis, waiver and amendment history must align.

**Ordinary explanations / disqualifiers:** Amendment, waiver or suspension; Trigger not reached; Payment in another covered period or stream; In-kind or netting provision; Incomplete receipt population or confidential amount

**Required evidence:** Original signed PDF and all operative amendments; Evidence of trigger and due date; Receipt coverage and accounting basis; Payer/project/payee crosswalk

**Next cheapest check:** Find a simple fixed clause first; stop if the actual receipt dataset cannot isolate that project and stream. Avoid speculative revenue estimates from commodity prices.

**Novelty:** D05/N01 reconcile two accounts of money paid. This derives the expected amount from a separately established obligation.

**Access evidence:** Contract access documented; project-level receipt completeness and amendments unverified. ESTMA is payer disclosure, not government receipt proof.

**Catalog provenance:** SYS-0111 ResourceContracts (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:7042); SYS-0107 EITI API and open data (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:6873); SYS-0295 Extractive Sector Transparency Measures Act data portal (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit3.txt:1603)

**Documentation:** E03 in evidence/documentation.jsonl. All case applicability remains unverified.

### X13 — Voluntary deregistration despite a contemporaneous property interest

Kind: new_mechanism. Composite rank 6; consequence 5/5, feasibility 2/5, join route 3/3, burden 4/4.

**If:** IF an Australian company is confirmed to have used voluntary deregistration, and contemporaneous title evidence identifies a valuable English/Welsh property interest held by that same legal entity.

**Then:** THEN establish the company’s beneficial/economic interest and valuation at the application date against the applicable voluntary-deregistration asset condition, distinguishing other deregistration routes.

**Why it matters / basis:** Current ASIC guidance separately requires assets below A$1,000 and no outstanding liabilities for voluntary deregistration. Subtracting a mortgage is not automatically a valid asset-condition test; establish the applicable valuation treatment and test liabilities separately. A verified contradictory asset position warrants scrutiny; a stale title entry is not proof of an invalid application.

**Join:** Exact Australian ACN as recorded/verified for the overseas proprietor → ASIC company and deregistration method → title and interest history. ABN, name similarity and registration dates are not substitutes.

**Scope:** Australian corporate process and England/Wales property; application-date assets and historical rule version, not today’s company status or property asking price.

**Ordinary explanations / disqualifiers:** ASIC-initiated deregistration; Prior disposal with land-register lag; Trustee/nominee rather than economic asset; Different legally established asset interest/valuation; a mortgage is not an automatic offset; Reinstatement or incorrect company identifier

**Required evidence:** Deregistration application/method and date; Contemporaneous title/transfer record; Nature and value of company interest; Separate outstanding-liabilities evidence; no automatic net-equity substitution; Applicable historical rule

**Next cheapest check:** First confirm the deregistration was voluntary. Then check exact ACN and historical interest; stop before paid title or ASIC documents without an authorized free route.

**Novelty:** F04 asks whether a supplier existed; S03 traces sanctioned assets. This tests the factual asset condition of a specified deregistration process.

**Access evidence:** HMLR account gate documented; historical title/ASIC records may cost. No account or paid record requested.

**Catalog provenance:** SYS-0047 HM Land Registry Overseas Companies Ownership Data (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:2798); SYS-0007 ASIC company dataset (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:948)

### X04 — Explicit foreign-entity declarations that cannot both fit the same facts

Kind: new_mechanism. Composite rank 7; consequence 5/5, feasibility 2/5, join route 1/3, burden 3/4.

**If:** IF a dated LD-1 explicitly reports no qualifying foreign entity for a legal client, while a contemporaneous FARA Exhibit A identifies ownership/control of that same client that appears to meet the applicable LD-1 criteria.

**Then:** THEN establish the exact legal entities, registration scope, activity, ownership/control facts and filing-date rules, and compare amendments before calling the declarations inconsistent.

**Why it matters / basis:** Inconsistent public descriptions of who directs a policy campaign can be consequential. FARA registration alone neither triggers every LDA field nor proves a false declaration.

**Join:** LDA registrant/client IDs and FARA registration/principal IDs are distinct. Bridge exact legal client and disclosed control relationship with dated original documents.

**Scope:** US disclosures, same legal client and relevant dates/activity. Apply filing-date thresholds; an old semiannual instructions PDF is not current quarterly guidance.

**Required evidence:** Actual LD-1 and amendments; Actual Exhibit A and referenced ownership evidence; Applicable current or historic LDA criteria; Specific client/activity and date crosswalk

**Next cheapest check:** Read the exact yes/no declaration and applicable rule before joining names; confirm the client is the same entity described in Exhibit A.

**Novelty:** I01 adds ownership context; this tests mutually inconsistent affirmative disclosure facts within carefully matched legal scope.

**Access evidence:** Public search/forms documented; field interpretation and cross-regime identity need manual review. No filing sampled.

**Catalog provenance:** SYS-0117 US Senate Lobbying Disclosure data (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:7734); SYS-0118 FARA eFile and bulk data (references/rabbit/originals/Rabbit2(1).txt:7776)

### X09 — A filed mining-right claim versus that exact instrument’s status

Kind: new_mechanism. Composite rank 14; consequence 4/5, feasibility 1/5, join route 3/3, burden 3/4.

**Why it matters / basis:** A materially unsupported assertion of project rights can affect investors and public subsidy decisions; current map absence is not evidence of historical nonownership.

### X06 — Foreign-principal money behind a specific advertised campaign

Kind: new_mechanism. Composite rank 15; consequence 4/5, feasibility 1/5, join route 1/3, burden 3/4.

**If:** IF a FARA agreement/supplement itemizes a particular advertisement or campaign financed for a named foreign principal, with a sufficiently specific placement or creative reference.

**Then:** THEN trace that placement to the archived ad and its payer/disclaimer, establishing which principal financed the message rather than treating the displayed intermediary as the ultimate sponsor.

**Why it matters / basis:** A documented financing chain can reveal whose interests a public campaign serves even when it is lawful and all disclosures are technically present.

**Join:** FARA principal/registrant/document → exact campaign/creative/placement reference → Meta ad identifier. Similar slogans, domains or spend totals are not a stable join.

**Scope:** Actual campaign dates and covered archive geography/category; receipt, agency fee and advertising spend are different quantities.

**Ordinary explanations / disqualifiers:** Different campaign with similar creative; Agent paid from unrelated funds; Disclaimer already names the principal; Agency amount includes nonadvertising services; Archive lacks the relevant ad class

**Required evidence:** Original principal agreement and itemized campaign spending; Exact ad/creative/placement crosswalk; Full disclaimer and archived ad metadata

**Next cheapest check:** Look for an explicit creative or placement identifier in one public FARA document; defer if the bridge is only a generic campaign description.

**Novelty:** I04 reconciles campaign spending; S05 screens sanctioned principals. This establishes message-specific financing through an intermediary without a sanctions premise.

**Access evidence:** FARA disbursement purpose/recipient fields do not guarantee a Meta creative ID. An explicit placement reference or equivalently exact documented creative bridge is mandatory; Meta archival access/coverage remains unverified.
