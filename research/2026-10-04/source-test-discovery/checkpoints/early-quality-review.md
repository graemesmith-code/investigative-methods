# Early defect-finding checkpoint

79 conditional source-pair designs; 86 expansions; seven quarantined candidates. Zero record-level executions and zero confirmed executable tests.

The model reviewed 40 deduplicated candidates using eight highest-ranked, eight from the largest template cluster, eight risky identity/temporal/legal, eight sparsely represented mechanisms and eight seeded-random remaining. Selection seed: 20261004. This is a purposive defect-finding gate, not statistical proof. Reviewer is the same model session that designed the mechanisms; no independent human or model review is claimed.

The initial 73 designs exposed a broader ownership defect: corporate officer fields and leaked relationship labels were being used as an entry point for ownership inferences. The affected ownership paths were quarantined at rule level for every downstream expansion, not only sampled rows. GLEIF accounting consolidation was also barred as ownership-share evidence. A distinct governance-role mechanism preserves legitimate officer questions without inferring ownership. The final checkpoint has 79 designs after these corrections.

Other gates: registry/incident context is not vessel trading; funding approval is not disbursement; registration is not asset use; annual reports cannot establish a day-level post-designation event; no absence or legal violation claim is admitted. Each selected row records its residual check in early-review-sample.csv. Non-SDN scope, licenses, identity, pre-designation activity and ownership-edge errors have synthetic negative fixtures.

Validation: 16 automated tests passed, including source byte-span hashes, stable SYS/AP provenance, name collisions, namespace collisions, many-to-many inflation, pre-designation and delisted dates, non-SDN restrictions, control versus ownership, accounting parents, minority interests, terminations and authorizations.

Sources remain catalog-only except selected official documentation reads. Those reads do not confirm record fields or coverage. The early gate authorizes conditional design expansion only; it does not authorize bulk execution or allegations.
