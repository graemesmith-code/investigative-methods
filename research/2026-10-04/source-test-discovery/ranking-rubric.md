# How to read the ranking

Public-interest consequence is a model editorial judgment (0–100), not a probability of wrongdoing or a prediction of headlines. Highest priority goes to public money and restrictions, civilian harm, industrial hazards and evidence affecting health. The basis column explains each mechanism's rationale.

Separate dimensions: testability 1–3 (concept only, direct identifier route, or direct route with both official docs checked); join confidence 1–4 (provisional name/bridge/aggregate, spatial conditional, domain, or direct named identifier); cost tier 1–3 (likely short documentation check, substantive bridge/manual work, or catalog mentions paid access). These are design estimates, not measured success rates or verified prices. Testability never substitutes for availability. Unverified direct-ID sources can still fail completely.

Sort formula: 0.65 × public interest + 5 × testability + 3 × join confidence − 4 × (cost tier − 1). Stable test ID breaks ties. The formula is transparent editorial triage, not a fitted or calibrated statistical model. A documented low-cost route is useful, but weaker-source high-consequence ideas remain visible. Cost strings preserve Rabbit's claims as unverified; no money is spent.

Names, addresses and employer strings remain provisional. Identical numeric IDs from different registries are incompatible without a crosswalk. Direct-ID scores describe a plausible join route, never a confirmed match. Spatial overlap needs resolution, timing and boundary checks. All comparisons need entity type, actual record granularity and many-to-many controls.

A distinct recipe requires a different source-role pair, evidence path, substantive predicate, temporal relationship or inference. Wording, arbitrary dates, thresholds and harmless direction swaps are not new tests. Mirrored ENAAT/EU arms series are collapsed. Source-pair implementations remain correlated alternatives and are reported separately from mechanism counts.
