# Mining disclosures across governments: ten evidence pairs, no proven contradiction

Research completed and archived 12 October 2026. This study found ten mining projects with accessible company statements in both Western disclosure systems and host-country reporting records. It offers a practical way to compare claims about production, payments, ownership and tailings facilities. **No material cross-government contradiction has yet been established to publication standard.**

The original study reports **88 direct access requests: 85 HTTP 200 responses and three failed routes**. Those are access outcomes, not 85 verified contradictions. Brazil and Peru provide the clearest direct-regulator filings; the other eight pairs use company returns in national EITI processes. Their precise legal submission obligations still need country-specific checking.

## What was found, and why a reporter might care

Peru's molybdenum figures agree after adding the two mines, converting pounds to tonnes and allowing for rounding. Kinross Tank XII's construction method and liner specimen also agree. Ity and Senegal royalties fall within the disclosed currency-conversion bounds. These controls show why a raw difference or translated label cannot be treated as a scoop.

Fekola dividends are the strongest unresolved numerical lead: the host amount converts to approximately **US$51.211–55.071 million**, below the amended ESTMA amount of **US$59.06 million**. Gross/net withholding, shareholder entitlement and cash scope could explain the difference. It remains **REVIEW REQUIRED**.

The potential reporting stakes are public revenue, state profit participation, production reporting and tailings oversight. A reporter should establish that two affirmative company assertions describe the same entity, time, fact, scope and measurement convention before calling them incompatible. Ordinary company-payment versus government-receipt reconciliation is a different comparison.
