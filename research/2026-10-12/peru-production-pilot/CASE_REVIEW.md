## Gold priorities

La Arena's 2023 ministry gold total is 2,980,749.193425 g. Pan American reports 97.1 thousand troy ounces, or 3,020,147.59728 g. The difference is -39.3984 kg (-1.3045%). All 2020–2023 years have the same direction, totaling -274.5194 kg.

Shahuindo's 2023 ministry total is 4,443,887.768592 g. The 140.1 thousand ounces in the English disclosure convert to 4,357,597.09968 g: +86.2907 kg (+1.9802%). All four full years have this direction, totaling +263.4246 kg. Across the two mines the four-year net is approximately -11.0947 kg. Offsetting totals do not prove either mine-level statement correct.

The company's 2019 disclosures start at the 22 February acquisition; the ministry totals cover the calendar year. Those two 2019 comparisons are parked. The Shahuindo technical report describes doré assays and refinery arrangements but does not reconcile the observed annual differences. Ministry annual editions and detailed monthly totals repeat the relevant values, which reduces one transcription/version hypothesis without resolving original-return history. Public company-specific return exhibits or a leach-to-doré-to-final-assay reconciliation would be needed to advance.

Yanacocha uses 100% consolidated production rather than attributable ownership, which changed in 2022. Four gold years are within coarse reporting precision; 2019 differs by about -105.815 kg. All five retain the same stage gate. The five silver ministry observations are now available from the bulk archives, but a matching English mine-level annual silver series was not established.

Gold Fields illustrates why language cannot establish the theory by itself. Its Spanish company communication gives 121,982 ounces of gold and 26,739 tonnes of copper for 2023, consistent with its rounded English headline figures. The technical-report plant table instead gives 127.0 thousand ounces and 27.8 thousand tonnes. The ministry largely resembles the latter. Commercial payability is discussed in technical documentation, but applying an assumed percentage is not a demonstrated reconciliation of every historical year. All ten remain blocked.

Ares requires a year-specific plant-to-mine map before gold or silver aggregation. Company reports distinguish Inmaculada, Pallancata and the Argentina mine San José; the latter is excluded here. Ministry aliases/process labels change and the 2019 two-mine subtotal omits Arcata. Buenaventura's direct-mine gold sum excludes other subsidiaries/associates; ministry labels, particularly in 2020, do not establish a complete stable bridge. A dash for Julcani 2022 was preserved as missing, not zero.

## Base-metal priorities and controls

Antamina: Teck's output is explicitly 100%, so multiplying by its ownership stake would create an error. Ministry zinc is 527,979.4192523 tonnes in 2023 versus Teck's 463,100 tonnes: about +64,879 tonnes (+14.01%). Copper also differs in all five years. A possible explanation is metal counted across all concentrate products versus the principal product only; this has neither been established nor ruled out. Use Teck's direct 2019 filing (448,500 tonnes copper; 303,300 tonnes zinc), not the coarser streaming-company figures initially inspected.

Nexa: the bulk ministry files identify Cerro Lindo as the plant for the Nexa Resources Peru zinc row in every sampled year. Adding Atacocha or El Porvenir is therefore not justified. Ministry quantities exceed the company's contained-zinc table by 4.66%–8.31%; the 2023 gap is 6,501 tonnes. Cerro Lindo makes separate copper, lead and zinc concentrates. Whether both series include identical byproduct-metal coverage is the remaining measurement question.

Southern: the filing explains that Cuajone's copper solution is sent to Toquepala's SX-EW plant. That supplies a textual bridge for the differently grouped SX-EW rows; it is not merely inferred from a numerical match. Copper gaps for 2020–2023 are approximately +9.38, -4.76, +78.35 and +14.00 tonnes, respectively. Molybdenum agrees in all five years. These are useful controls; small residuals still need revision/measurement evidence before any claim of falsity.

Hudbay: Peru operations tables specify contained metal before refining losses/smelter deductions. Constancia/Pampacancha feed the Constancia operation; Canadian output and payable metal sold are excluded. The seven raw gaps are 2019 copper (+85.271 tonnes), gold (+0.490 kg), silver (+55.769 kg); 2022 molybdenum (+2.210 tonnes); and very small silver gaps in 2020, 2022 and 2023 (roughly -0.095, -0.185 and -0.111 kg). The tiny silver residuals could reflect conversion/assay practice, but no exact public reconciliation was located. Their existence is not a strong standalone news finding.

Freeport: 2023 Cerro Verde copper is 985 million recoverable pounds versus 466,463.44 fine tonnes in the ministry series, a difference near 19,675 tonnes after conversion. The explicit recoverable-metal wording blocks a literal identity comparison. The same rule blocks the coarsely rounded molybdenum agreements. The filing's commercial discussion of unrecoverable-metal allowances is relevant context, not a verified mathematical bridge for these annual production figures.
