# Peru mining disclosures: what the pilot found

**We can find and compare these records at useful scale. We have not yet proved that a mining company gave incompatible facts to two governments.** The experiment found repeated differences worth investigating, including gold. It also found several reasons why a simple number-matching system would produce misleading accusations.

We fixed a batch of **100 company–metal–year checks before doing the arithmetic**, covering 11 operator groups in 2019–2023. It included **35 gold checks**. Every selected record stayed in the experiment, including the failures.

Peru's free ministry downloads supplied figures for all 100 checks. We found Western figures that allowed arithmetic for 94. Of these, 32 fell within the published figures' reporting precision and 62 differed. **Those 62 are raw alerts, not 62 false statements:** they include cases with different measurement definitions, incomplete periods and incomplete mine coverage.

A narrower group of **45 checks** had sufficiently clear company, calendar-year and contained-metal descriptions for an initial production screen. Nineteen were consistent within reporting precision; 26 had unexplained differences. Even these 26 are **investigative leads, not established contradictions**. We retrieved ministry compilations based on company declarations, not the original signed company returns and their correction history. Concentrate-product coverage also needs to be pinned down before asserting that both statements cannot be true.

The original goal was at least 80 defensible, reusable comparisons. **This pilot did not meet that goal.** Downloading and arithmetic are easy; deciding exactly what each number measures remains the expensive part. Matching numbers does not prove truthful reporting either.

## Leads worth a focused follow-up

These examples identify documents and questions. They are not allegations against the named companies. Percentages below use the English figure as the denominator.

| Case | What the records say | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| **La Arena gold, Pan American, 2020–2023** | Peru's annual gold figures are lower in all four years. In 2023: **2,980.75 kg** in ministry records versus **3,020.15 kg** after converting the company's 97,100 ounces: **39.40 kg lower**, or 1.30%. | Whether both count the same gold at the same point between leaching, pouring bars and final assay; corrections and original returns. |
| **Shahuindo gold, Pan American, 2020–2023** | Peru's figures are higher in all four years. In 2023: **4,443.89 kg** versus **4,357.60 kg**, or **86.29 kg higher** (1.98%). | The same measurement questions. This is the opposite direction from under-reporting to Peru. |
| **Antamina copper and zinc, 2019–2023** | The ministry and shareholder Teck differ in every year. For 2023 zinc: **527,979 tonnes** versus **463,100 tonnes**, about **14.01% higher** in Peru. | Whether one counts metal in all concentrate products and the other counts only selected products. The discrepancy is real in the published numbers; that explanation has not been established or ruled out. |
| **Cerro Lindo zinc, Nexa, 2019–2023** | Peru's figures exceed Nexa's by **4.66%–8.31%**. In 2023 the gap is **6,501 tonnes**. The detailed ministry file confirms the row is Cerro Lindo, not a sum with Atacocha or El Porvenir. | Product-stream coverage, original company returns and assays. This is a specific, testable measurement question. |

The La Arena and Shahuindo differences nearly offset over 2020–2023: approximately **274.52 kg lower** at La Arena and **263.42 kg higher** at Shahuindo, leaving about **11.09 kg lower across the two**. That does not resolve either mine-level discrepancy. It does show why their differences cannot responsibly be presented as a large, one-directional disappearance of gold.

Other alerts are less promising immediately. Hudbay's clearer production tables largely reconcile; several remaining differences are tiny. Freeport explicitly reports recoverable metal, which is not automatically the same as Peru's fine-metal measure. Gold Fields has different headline and technical-report production figures; its Spanish company communications repeat its English headline figures. A language difference, by itself, is therefore not a convincing explanation in that case.

## What we learned about export records

**Peru has useful, free, company-level customs records.** A live query for La Arena returned three December 2023 declarations with its tax identifier, declaration numbers, dates, destination countries, commodity code, weights, values and descriptions containing gold and silver percentages. That is a concrete route for further reporting.

It did **not** establish a matched import declaration abroad. The Canadian bulk file we downloaded lists company names, products and origin countries but supplies no shipment quantities. The UK page supplies trader/product/month information, not the corresponding shipment's fine-gold quantity. China's public statistics route did not yield a company-level match. We therefore stopped the mirror-trade comparison. No national Peru–China total was used to accuse a company.

An export weight is not automatically a production figure. Bars can contain several metals; declarations can split products or allocations; exports can draw on inventory or purchased material. Our sample does not establish the meaning of every customs weight field, so we have not multiplied its assay percentages into a purported missing-gold total.

## What an editor should do with this

**Continue narrowly; do not publish a false-reporting accusation from this pilot.** The next useful reporting work is to obtain publicly accessible, company-specific production-return exhibits or metallurgical reconciliations for the four leads above and test the exact product and measurement definitions. Some original production returns are publicly embedded in environmental files, but this run did not locate matching originals for these companies and years. A case that cannot be resolved from public evidence should stay parked.

The technology is already useful for collecting documents, translating labels, tracing company names and spotting persistent differences. The saved code deliberately keeps unexplained differences separate from proven contradictions. It includes the failed comparisons so a future system cannot make its success rate look better by dropping them.

Finally, this sample does **not** test or explain the reported national production–export gap. [CooperAcción's article](https://cooperaccion.org.pe/la-brecha-entre-la-produccion-y-la-exportacion-de-oro-en-el-peru/) reports gaps of 73.9 tonnes in 2023, 98.5 tonnes in 2024 and more than 100 tonnes in 2025. That is the reason to investigate, not evidence that the Western-reporting companies in our sample caused it. Informal miners and gold buyers outside this sample may be central to that separate question.
