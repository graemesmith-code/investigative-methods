# Product licences and operator responsibility in the F6 corporate controls audit

Research cutoff and retrieval date: 26 October 2026. Lanes 1 and 8. Public sources only. No paid calls, licence acceptance, accounts, outreach, purchases or permission changes. This is documentary research, not a legal opinion or a qualified-lawyer review.

## What the evidence establishes

Some public product terms impose real obligations beyond an ethics promise: human review, restrictions on particular uses, compliance with warnings, audit rights and suspension or termination. But the correct contract and hosting environment determine their reach. A public SDK agreement is not an operational weapon licence; a trial agreement is not a production agreement; a vendor's statement that software can be disabled is not evidence that an IHL control was implemented or exercised in a military deployment.

Three useful extensions survived this lane's checks:

1. **Contract precedence can change the apparent force of a public restriction.** The actual Texas Skydio framework preserves licence duties but gives the government agreement precedence over conflicting clickwrap and linked additions. A Northrop Grumman Ridgeback supplier addendum supplies a stronger contrary example: for the specified government licence breach, the government need not stop using or uninstall the items, with the stated remedy limited to money in one paragraph. Neither is a universal power to disregard vendor restrictions.
2. **The relevant public terms change by date and deployment environment.** The supplied Palantir AIP seed was effective in February–April 2025, not current at the cutoff. The current 5 October 2026 addendum has distinct Anthropic, AWS, Azure and Google rows. Skydio's public archive does not show a simple, permanent relaxation of its weapon-use language: an early-2026 page reverts to an older formulation before exceptions appear again. Archive/CMS error remains a plausible explanation.
3. **The strongest new technical statement is still bounded.** The archived Skydio software EULA expressly describes communicating deactivation controls, and the X10D INTL manual identifies that EULA URL. Dynamo's evaluation agreement also declares disabling functionality. These are vendor feature assertions, not independent observations of an IHL lock or an exercised military veto.

The disputes lane has separately retrieved an actual signed Anthropic–Palantir government rider. That record is stronger for its historical negotiated relationship than generic public AIP terms. It must remain separate: its referenced master terms and a later successor are not fully established, and this lane has not independently reprocessed the court exhibit. See ancillary source WEB10 and the disputes memo.

### Skydio has both restrictions and exceptions

The [current enterprise terms](https://www.skydio.com/legal/terms-and-conditions-enterprise) contain a government-action exception in the general conditions of use and a lawful-government-action exception to weapon-system/hazardous-use restrictions. Section 5.1 separately requires compliance with applicable manuals and safety guidance. Section 9.4's suspected-breach suspension differs from section 9.5(a)(iii)'s for-cause termination and deactivation; otherwise perpetual licences can survive. LC02–LC03 retain these distinctions.

Seven official archived enterprise pages were compared with the live page. The 2022 page prohibits use as a weapon and targeting a weapon, without the article in the phrase used by an early narrow search. The 2023–June 2025 and June–September 2025 texts also contain a weapon prohibition without the later express government exception. September–December 2025 adds an exception, January–early February 2026 reverts to older language, and February–April 2026 restores an exception. April–June 2026 and the live page use a lawful-government-actions formulation. These are observations about public archive pages, not proof of the terms accepted by any military customer or the date any operational restriction was waived. See LC04 and `version_comparison`.

The [archived software EULA](https://www.skydio.com/legal/eula), last updated 20 September 2023, is a distinct instrument. Section 8.3 declares programmed technological controls that communicate with Skydio and permit deactivation on specified licence-status grounds; it also requires prompt reactivation on reinstatement. Section 6 requires installation of designated safety updates. Section 12 limits amendment effects on existing perpetual and renewed term licences. The offline-product exception in section 10 concerns the mobile-app section: it is not proof that the rest of the EULA is inapplicable, nor proof of actual connectivity for a particular offline system. LC23 is evidence level 2 plus level 3 **vendor assertion**, with no independent test or exercise established.

An actual public [Texas DIR contract record](https://dir.texas.gov/contracts/dir-cpo-5863) names Skydio and DIR-CPO-5863. In its contract PDF, sections 10–11, pages 8–9, licence compliance remains the customer's responsibility, but conflicting/additional clickwrap and linked agreements do not outrank the framework; changed additional terms have purchase-order and notice limits. The exact purchased product and software licence version were not identified. This is a state procurement countercheck, not a military order. LC05.

### Palantir's current form is not the supplied seed or a universal military rider

The [official version centre](https://palantir.pactsafe.io/aip-legal-3791.html) maps the supplied seed to 3 February–12 April 2025. All 22 listed AIP PDFs were retrieved. The [current PDF](https://palantir.pactsafe.io/versions/6abf8606f3b5be6e77c772a9.pdf), effective 5 October 2026, combines natural-person evaluation of output before decisions/actions/omissions with customer responsibility and risk allocation in section 2.1. Section 4 provides qualified suspension grounds. Those are contractual provisions, not merely aspirations, but no named military customer's incorporation of this public form was established. LC06.

Exhibit A treats environments separately. Palantir's Anthropic environment and AWS Anthropic repeat standalone weapon and battlefield-management restrictions. Azure Anthropic incorporates the Anthropic AUP but does not repeat that list. Omission of the repeated list does **not** establish permission. Google-hosted model use has its own dangerous-activity restriction and incorporated Google/Vertex terms. LC07 and the chain register preserve the distinctions.

The live Anthropic AUP retrieved at the cutoff is headed effective **12 November 2026**, a future date. It cannot silently substitute for the [15 September 2025 archive](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/archive/22742366-2ef0-4c7a-a833-6523f10d3944). That archive expressly contemplates tailored contracts with government customers. Its accordion bodies were not exposed in the web extraction and its native fetch was refused; no complete negative reference claim is made for it. The dated 2023 Bedrock agreement and March 2024 Vertex agreement add downstream warning, evaluation and provider-chain obligations, but a dynamic URL does not establish which policy version or negotiated exception governs a named customer. WEB01–WEB05, LC16.

A [Carahsoft-hosted Palantir licence/services form](https://static.carahsoft.com/concrete/files/5316/6206/2785/Palantir_License_and_Services_Agreement.pdf) has government provisions. It does not itself prove incorporation of the AIP addendum. The Army's 2025 enterprise-agreement announcement likewise does not disclose the governing licence stack. LC08 and WEB09.

### New trial and catalogue terms cannot be transferred to weapon deployments

[Dynamo's non-production agreement](https://www.dynamo.ai/non-production-license-agreement), identified on the site as updated 20 June 2025, incorporates its [AUP](https://www.dynamo.ai/acceptable-use-policy) and requires an executed order. The AUP covers enumerated weapons-related activities and human final decisions for legally significant automated decisions. The evaluation agreement declares disabling functionality at expiry/termination, while expressly excluding production use. No production agreement or Army deliverable was shown to incorporate those terms. LC10–LC11. The financing lane must not attribute this policy to an investor condition without separate evidence.

[Auterion's 10 November 2022 PDF](https://auterion.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/2022.11.10.-EULA-US.pdf) and its [undated live EULA](https://auterion.com/eula/) are not assumed to form a known supersession sequence. The former includes warnings/policy compliance, suspension grounds, technological termination language and qualified export/end-use restrictions. No Skynode or other military order establishes their precise applicability. LC09.

Helsing Limited's G-Cloud 14 terms have **October 2024 on the cover**, despite May 2024 in their URL. The [AI Infrastructure listing](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/515112760122066) links a terms PDF byte-identical to the initially located PDF. The terms require scope-of-use compliance, permit licence audit, contain a safety/laws/treaties catchall and put the G-Cloud framework first in a conflict. No signed call-off or combat-product contract was obtained. These are not evidence of a lawyer's targeting veto. LC12.

### A program-specific contrary clause and an actual commercial customer

The [Northrop Grumman Systems Corporation Ridgeback addendum](https://cdn.northropgrumman.com/-/media/Supplier-Documents/Program-Addenda/PA_3051.pdf?rev=6a5e52e0ffe4456c93a9370bba1170f2), dated 15 December 2021, contains commercial-software licensing precedence at x52.227-008, pages 15–17. Paragraph (a)(13) states a no-uninstall/no-stop-use consequence and monetary-remedy limit; (b)(9) contains the corresponding flowdown, without simply reproducing every phrase of (a)(13). Scope, transfer conditions and federal-law priority matter. Historical statutory citations inside the clause were not independently validated as current law. No AI system or actual invocation was established. LC13.

Applied Intuition's [2018 Kodiak master](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1747286/000119312525152053/d948047dex1027.htm) and [2024 order/SOW](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1747286/000119312525152053/d948047dex1028.htm) are actual customer documents, but concern commercial simulation. The 2024 document is executed by Applied's Head of Legal Aref Wardak and Kodiak's CLO Jordan Coleman. That establishes their execution role, not veto authority over autonomous weapons. The support addendum includes incident review/post-mortem and service-level termination conditions, with substantial redactions. Its natural-person licence-seat definition is not human review of target decisions. LC14–LC15.

DFARS [227.7202](https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/227.7202-1-policy.) is the relevant commercial-software framework; additional rights require negotiation and enumeration under 227.7202-3. Section 227.7200 expressly excludes software/documentation acquired under GSA schedule contracts, so this subpart must not be projected automatically onto a GSA example. [252.227-7015](https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/252.227-7015-technical-data-commercial-products-and-commercial-services.) defines technical data separately from computer software. Neither creates a universal vendor veto or universal government override. LC19–LC20.

## Operator-facing evidence and its limits

The [X10D INTL Operator Manual](https://support.skydio.com/hc/article_attachments/28469210603035) was downloaded: document version 3.0, 27 June 2024, vehicle 34.1.125 and controller 34.1.81. The 176-page PDF was text-screened in full; governance passages were independently read at PDF pages 1, 7–8, 13 and 175 (printed pages 1–2, 7 and 169 where applicable). They allocate flight-law and warning responsibilities to the pilot and identify the software EULA. No firing procedures, parameters or technical operating instructions are reproduced in this package. LC17.

The [live Safety and Operating Guide](https://support.skydio.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000586653-Skydio-Safety-and-Operating-Guide) says updated 2 September 2026; a search preview's older April date was rejected. Its opening warning preserves user judgment and warranty consequences. That live guide is not silently substituted for the 2024 manual. Enterprise section 5.1's incorporation, the existence of an instruction, completed training and actual practice are four different propositions. Only the first two have bounded support here. No new named target-verification instruction or training-completion evidence was established. LC18.

The F5 Dutch SMASH material was treated as baseline, not a scoop. This lane did not locate a new approval or instruction beyond that prior record, and did not infer that a time-limited 2024–2025 approval continued through 2026.

Recall checks were purposive, not statistically representative: Anduril SDK and Helsing terms tested generic catchalls despite no direct IHL string; Applied's commercial order tested misleading human-person language; Skydio's oldest archive tested the narrow weapon-phrase nonhit; the operator manual tested lawful-flight and pilot-responsibility wording. The preliminary v1 probe was exploratory, not a retained complete corpus run. No false claim of a measured recall percentage is made.

Limits remain material. This is full **extracted-text lexical** coverage of the stated set, not exhaustive manual semantic adjudication of every generic occurrence, not certified OCR of every image, and not full processing of every linked instrument.
