# Corporate legal controls and liability limits

## Verdict

The strongest evidence in these four lanes is **an enforceable export-compliance governance arrangement, not an independently imposed autonomous-weapons IHL veto**. RTX’s 2024 consent agreement specifies legal-department support, an independent compliance official, board escalation, pre-export classification checks, audits and financial consequences. It does not establish that RTX counsel stopped a named autonomous release or that its automated export-compliance system remotely disables a weapon. Conversely, the Lafarge plea record shows why the presence of lawyers is not enough: the admitted facts describe both a payment-suspension request and conduct designed to avoid records and backdate documentation. These are bounded, substantially different cases. [RTX consent §§ 9,12–22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101829/000010182924000031/a2024-08x30exhibit991.htm); [Lafarge plea Attachment A §§ 92–101 and Attachment C § 7](https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2022/10/19/us_v._lafarge_-_plea_agreement_and_attachments_ecf_0.pdf).

Two recent legal changes materially improve the premise audit. On 25 February 2026, the Supreme Court held that **Yearsley is a merits defence, not immunity from suit**. Separately, the enacted December 2025 NDAA added insurance-market input and a 90-day final-decision requirement for specified DoD extraordinary-indemnity requests, with a narrow advanced-nuclear delegation rule. Neither rule automatically shields AI suppliers or strips them of a defence. [GEO Group v Menocal, majority pp 1,7–12](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-758_2dp3.pdf); [PL119-60 §§ 801,876](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ60/html/PLAW-119publ60.htm).

This memo covers lanes 2, 3, 7 and 12 through **26 October 2026**. It is primary-source legal research, not a qualified legal opinion. F5’s pinned legal-review dossier was used as a baseline, not republished or presented as a new discovery. The source register contains 49 records: 37 complete extracted documents, two scoped sections of one additional enacted act, and 11 failed/unprocessed records. The text sweep covers 1,597,667 characters and 389 PDF pages, without OCR; that is not a claim to have checked every image or exhaustively interpreted every semantic implication. The appendix and code explain that limitation.

## Government contractor defence and actual warning records

### Boyle supplies conditions rather than a universal immunity

Boyle concerned a military helicopter and a state-law design-defect claim. The Supreme Court identified three requirements: reasonably precise specifications approved by the United States, conformity, and warning of dangers known to the supplier but not to the United States. The Court vacated and remanded. Do not turn this into immunity for every contract claim, negligence theory, criminal offence or international-law breach. Nor does a board approval, an OTA label, the presence of AI, or an opaque model itself settle the test. The missing evidence for an AI-product investigation is the actual specification-approval record, conformity evidence and communicated hazards—not a general statement that the contractor works for government. **LRE01.** [Boyle, 487 US 500,512–514,27 June 1988](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-487/pdf/USREPORTS-487-500.pdf).

Getz provides a concrete counterexample to a categorical software-defence theory. The Ninth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Boeing, Honeywell and Goodrich in litigation involving an Army helicopter’s digital engine-control system. Its opinion describes substantive Army review of specifications and the government’s role in choosing operator warnings. Failure-to-warn claims needed a distinct adapted inquiry; satisfying design-defect elements alone was not enough. The manufacturers and government had equal awareness of the disputed risks on the record. ATEC’s separate dismissal was for lack of personal jurisdiction. This is a historical aircraft/software case, **not an autonomous-targeting decision**. No technical operating details are reproduced. **LRE02.** [Getz, No 10-15284,2 August 2011, printed pp 9966–9967,9972–9976](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/08/02/10-15284.pdf).

Sawyer is narrower still. The Fourth Circuit held that the asserted government-contractor warning defence was sufficiently colorable for federal-officer removal; it reversed a remand. It did not grant final merits immunity. The underlying Navy/asbestos warning record is useful precisely because claim type and procedural threshold are visible. **LRE03.** [Sawyer, No 16-1530,22 June 2017, pp 12–17](https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/Opinions/Published/161530.P.pdf).

### A 2026 correction to immunity shorthand

Menocal rejects describing Yearsley protection as immunity from having to litigate. A district court’s denial is not immediately appealable under the collateral-order doctrine, although the opinion distinguishes another statutory certification route. The detention-contract dispute does not decide a weapon’s legality or replace Boyle’s three conditions. Campbell-Ewald had already rejected the proposition that contractors receive the sovereign’s blanket immunity; the alleged combination of violating federal law and express government instructions was outside the protection asserted there. Read its older terminology with Menocal. **LRE04–05.** [Menocal,25 February 2026](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-758_2dp3.pdf); [Campbell-Ewald,577 US 153,166–169,20 January 2016](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-577/pdf/USREPORTS-577-153.pdf).

No named AI-provider safety case, hazard-disclosure schedule or counsel-approved model-limitation notice was obtained in this lane. That is an access/coverage gap, not proof that such documents do not exist. Boyle’s knowledge-based warning condition is not a free-standing obligation to disclose every model error.

## Indemnification and insurance

### Separate the request from the approved and incorporated coverage

FAR 50.104-3 requires a contractor request identifying the contract and hazardous risk, insurance information and financial-protection details. The contracting officer, assisted by legal counsel and program staff, reviews it and may deny it. An approval pathway includes a signed memorandum and incorporation of the defined risk and appropriate clause. Counsel’s documented role here is advisory support to a governmental decision; it is not proof of a vendor general counsel’s independent product veto. **LRE07.** [FAR 50.104-3, current FAC 2026-01](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/50.104-3).

The current FAR’s greater-than-$150 million congressional notice and 60-continuous-session-day rule expressly does **not** apply to indemnity agreements authorized under 50.104-3. This narrow exemption should not be paraphrased as “no reporting.” Recordkeeping, contract-specific conditions and other duties remain. The FAR’s $90,000 delegation provision also must not be confused with the $500,000 statutory approval provision printed in the 2024 US Code. Full preliminary Code pages were unavailable; indexed official preliminary text corroborated the latter provision but is not a substitute for full current text. **LRE06.** [FAR 50.102-1 and 50.102-3](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-50.1); [50 USC 1431–1435,2024 edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title50/html/USCODE-2024-title50-chap29.htm).

A late primary-law check prevented an important overstatement. **PL119-60§ 876**, enacted 18 December 2025, provides:

- practicable DCMA input, including insurance-market and coverage-availability reviews;
- a final approval **or denial**, with executed memorandum, within 90 days of the request;
- mandatory military-secretary delegation for requests concerning advanced nuclear energy systems/components.

The last provision qualifies older FAR language reserving hazardous/nuclear approval to agency heads. It is not a general AI delegation or automatic approval rule. No actual AI request, approval memorandum, incorporated coverage or payment was established in the bounded search. **LRE09.** [§ 876,139 Stat 1005](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ60/html/PLAW-119publ60.htm).

### Coverage can transfer losses without controlling use

FAR 52.250-1(c) limits coverage to losses from the contract-defined unusually hazardous/nuclear risk that are uncompensated by insurance or otherwise. Insurance deductibles are excluded; reducing insurance does not increase government liability. Paragraph(d)’s principal-official willful-misconduct/lack-good-faith provision excludes government claims except subrogation and contractor-property losses in the stated circumstances. It is **not** a blanket exclusion of every third-party claim. The clause also requires notice/evidence and permits government direction of the defence. These are important financial and procedural conditions, not in themselves targeting restraints. **LRE08.** [FAR 52.250-1(c)–(h), clause April 1984, currentpublication 13 March 2026](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.250-1).

The December 2025 NDAA also added **10 USC 3864** for a different risk: work in process under specified classified fixed-price product contracts entered after enactment, where classification prevents commercial insurance or claim processing. It excludes managerial willful misconduct/lack of good faith and workmanship error. It is not third-party AI-injury coverage. **LRE10.** [PL119-60§ 801,139 Stat 939–940](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ60/html/PLAW-119publ60.htm).

Two further boundaries matter. FAR 28.305’s employee war-hazard context defeats a universal claim that all war-related loss is uninsurable, but does not establish a weapons-product policy. DFARS 250.104-3-70 identifies separate 10 USC 3861 and 85-804 treatment for mixed R&D/other work; historical renumbered 2354 references should not be silently treated as current citations. **LRE11–12.** [FAR 28.305](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/28.305); [DFARS 250.104-3-70, current 7 May 2026](https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/250.104-3-70-indemnification-under-contracts-involving-both-research-and-development-and-other-work.).

Indexed historical Boeing/Lockheed litigation and a 2024 Lockheed supplier flowdown were found, but raw full-text access failed for the latter and the ASBCA file. They remain discovery records, not evidence of autonomous-weapons indemnity. Grouped company searches had poor precision, so they are not a reliable company-level negative denominator. Actual policy exclusions, endorsements and loss histories remain unavailable.

## Export conditions and corporate decision authority

### RTX is a real governance case with a limited normative reach

The 29 August 2024 RTX consent is an exact publicly filed instrument applying to RTX Corporation and specified divisions, subsidiaries and business units, with acquisition/transfer provisions. It requires an external Special Compliance Officer initially, written authority approved by DTCC, CEO/board reporting and access to relevant records. It expressly preserves attorney-client/workproduct protections. Section 22 requires Legal Department support across divisions. Sections 13–14 require jurisdiction reviews, including specified supplier items before export/reexport/retransfer. Section 12’s automation tracks **export decisions**, not weapon behavior. **LRE13–14.** [Consent§§ 4–5,9,12–17,22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101829/000010182924000031/a2024-08x30exhibit991.htm).

The $200 million headline comprises $100 million cash and $100 million conditionally suspended for approved remediation, with accounting and potential payment of unused amounts. The agreement did not impose administrative debarment for the resolved charges and expressly did not promise future licensing outcomes. It was a civil settlement, with an opening nonadmission provision; allegations should not be converted into litigated findings. The document establishes legal/governance authority and financial consequences. It does not establish a general counsel’s veto over a named autonomous system, an IHL condition, completion of every remedial task, or a particular refused export. The strongest next records would be releasable monitor delegations, audit conclusions and a particular licence’s conditions. **LRE13–14.** [Consent§§ 18–24,28–29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101829/000010182924000031/a2024-08x30exhibit991.htm).

### Do not collapse classification licensing and monitoring

A BIS classification is not itself an export permission. ITAR 22 CFR 123.9 deals with changes of end user/use/destination and transfer approvals, subject to exceptions. EAR 736.2(b)(9) concerns terms of licences/orders/authorizations under a different regime. Neither proves that remote disablement or geofencing is routinely required. **LRE15–16.** [BIS classification guidance](https://www.bis.gov/licensing/classify-your-item); [ITAR 123.9](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-M/part-123/section-123.9); [EAR 736.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-VII/subchapter-C/part-736/section-736.2).

Blue Lantern covers direct-commercial export channels and brokering, using checks at several licensing/shipment stages. Golden Sentry concerns FMS and other government-to-government transfers. A monitoring program is not an autonomous-product licence condition; the exact assurance or LOA note matters. **LRE17–18.** [Blue Lantern FY 2025, pp 1–2](https://www.pmddtc.state.gov/sys_attachment.do?sys_id=f7c320619774cb14fe5c39b0f053af72); [SAMM chapter 8, C8.2–3](https://samm.dsca.mil/chapter/chapter-8).

Current SAMM chapter 4 also expressly links specified transfer support/training to partners’ LOAC obligations and sets governmental/interagency inclusion decisions. This is a government transfer gate, not a documented vendor veto. No operational methods are reproduced. **LRE29.** [C4.4.18.2–4](https://samm.dsca.mil/chapter/chapter-4).

The expanded indirect-reference sweep did find a genuine IHL chain. Chapter 8 refers to a UAS Principles-of-Use agreement. DSCA 20-31,13 July 2020, contains mandatory military-UAS FMS/BPC template notes with explicit IHL and human-rights language, lawful-force conditions, modification/integration consent, and appropriate operator training. The 2020 memo removes earlier references to strike-enabling technologies; this is a scope change, not disappearance of all use conditions. However, the current public Appendix 6 listing no longer contains that named note and points to additional CAC-only notes. We did not access or request access to the restricted annex. Thus the established finding is a **historical government-recipient template**, not proof of its exact current application to every sale or of an independent corporate veto. No repeal is inferred either. **LRE19.** [DSCA 20-31 attachment](https://samm.dsca.mil/policy-memoranda/dsca-20-31); [current Appendix 6](https://samm.dsca.mil/appendix/appendix-6).

## What the lawyer and board records actually show

- **RTX:** institutional legal support and independent monitoring/escalation duties in an executed settlement; no named GC product veto proven.
- **FAR indemnity:** counsel assists a governmental contracting officer, and an authorized official approves/denies financial protection. The 2025 statute adds decision timing and a narrow delegation change.
- **Lafarge:** the admitted corporate facts record a regional senior counsel’s suspension request, but also avoidance/backdating conduct. The plea then requires senior compliance officials with direct independent/board reporting and autonomy. Neither side of this evidence should be omitted. No individual guilt is inferred from the corporate admission. The stated plea term must not be assumed still active in October 2026 without a completion/extension record.
- **Getz:** an actual government specification/warning approval record, not a vendor GC sign-off or IHL assessment.

The hypothesis that long-lived companies retain safeguards is plausible, but survival incentives also support liability allocation, confidentiality and continued commercial activity. These sources do not establish a general causal story that counsel independently prevents unlawful autonomous-weapon use.

## Ranked reporting leads and exact next documents

1. **RTX’s external oversight and legal-department remit.** Strongest current corporate instrument in this lane. Novelty is an accountability question, not discovery of the already-public settlement. Counterevidence: export compliance rather than IHL; no named refusal. Seek a releasable implementation delegation, audit finding and a matching product/customer licence condition.
2. **The 2025 indemnity decision clock and insurance-market input.** A precise recent legal change, not proof AI suppliers requested or obtained coverage. Counterevidence: nuclear emphasis, decision may be denial, work-in-process rule is narrower than liability cover. Seek a public request, dated memorandum, delegation and incorporated risk definition for an identified autonomous/AI contract.
3. **The historical UAS IHL template and unresolved current incorporation.** Concrete explicit language is stronger than generic export-policy inference. Counterevidence: governmental template, current public-list omission, no deployed contract. Seek the releasable exact current note, applicable LOA/version and any authorized amendment/waiver. Do not seek restricted annex access in this task.

Draft interview questions only, not sent: Who has written authority to refuse the specific release/export? What exact requirement can be waived and by whom? Which dated risk definition, licence note or contract amendment binds this version? Does insurance require a behavioral safeguard or merely change who pays? What record establishes an actual refusal, condition or override? What can be released without privilege, security or commercial restrictions?

## Method and publication boundary

Public official statutes, cases, regulatory texts, issuer filings and agency/court records were used. No paid search, docket purchase, outreach, account creation or access change occurred; cost for this lane is US $0. Raw copies remain private and outside the whitelist. Public files contain authored analysis, normalized metadata, short extracts and source links. The occurrence register retains low-confidence indirect/generic language and false positives without converting them into findings. It preserves offsets, pages, dates, hashes and duplicate links. It is complete for the stated regex matches, **not an assertion of complete semantic recall**. See coverage-method.md and editorial-log.md for the independent review and corrections.
