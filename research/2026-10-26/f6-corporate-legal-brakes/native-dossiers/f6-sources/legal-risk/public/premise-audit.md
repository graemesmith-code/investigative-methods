# F6 legal premise audit

Research cutoff and retrieval date 26 October 2026. Lanes 2, 3, 7 and 12. This is legal research, not a qualified legal opinion. Each authority is linked and versioned in the source register.

| Premise tested | Disposition | Operative primary authority and correction |
|---|---|---|
| Government contractors are immune from product liability | Incorrect as a general proposition | Boyle v United Technologies, 487 US 500, 512–514 (27 June 1988), LR01: the military-equipment design-defect defence requires reasonably precise government specifications, conformity, and disclosure of supplier-known dangers unknown to the government. The Court vacated and remanded; it did not adjudicate every possible tort or confer criminal immunity. |
| An OTA, AI model or software update automatically destroys that defence | Unsupported | Neither Boyle nor the subsequent cases reviewed supplies that categorical rule. Getz v Boeing, Ninth Circuit No 10-15284 (2 August 2011), LR33, involved digital engine-control software and affirmed summary judgment for the manufacturers on the record. It was not an AI weapon case. Different claim types and circuits require separate analysis. |
| Yearsley protection is immunity from having to litigate | Incorrect under the 2026 holding | GEO Group v Menocal, No 24-758 (25 February 2026), LR34, majority pp 1,7–12: merits defence, not immunity from suit; denial is not immediately appealable under the collateral-order doctrine. The case concerns detention contracts and does not rewrite Boyle's elements. |
| Suppliers must disclose every model error to retain protection | Unsupported | Boyle's third element concerns dangers known to the supplier but not to the government. Sawyer v Foster Wheeler, No 16-1530 (22 June 2017), LR02, addresses a colorable warning defence at removal, not final merits immunity. Contractual warning duties may be additional; no named AI hazard-disclosure contract was obtained in this lane. |
| PL85-804 automatically covers unusually hazardous defence AI | Incorrect | FAR 50.104-1 and 50.104-3, LR03/LR16: request, defined risk, insurance disclosure, agency-head approval, signed memorandum and incorporated clause are separate steps. No named AI/autonomous-system approval was established in the bounded query set. |
| Indemnity over $150 m always requires the FAR 60-day congressional wait | Incorrect | FAR 50.102-3(b)(4), LR03, expressly excepts indemnity agreements authorized under 50.104-3 from this threshold/wait. This does not establish absence of every other record/reporting obligation. |
| The same monetary threshold applies to every extraordinary-relief decision | Incorrect | Current FAR 50.102-1(b) uses $90,000 for its specified delegation limit; FAR 50.102-1(d) reserves hazardous/nuclear approval regardless of amount, but newer PL119-60§ 876(c) requires military-secretary delegation for advanced nuclear systems/components. 50 USC 1431(a), LR05 (2024 edition), has a different statutory $500,000 approval provision; indexed OLRC preliminary text corroborates it but full preliminary page was unavailable. Do not substitute one threshold for another. |
| FAR indemnity protects every loss even where managers knowingly disregard risks | Incorrect | FAR 52.250-1(c), LR04, limits coverage to contract-defined hazardous/nuclear risks uncompensated by insurance/otherwise; deductibles are excluded. Paragraph(d) treats principal-official misconduct differently for government claims and contractor property; it is not a blanket exclusion of all third-party claims. |
| War or autonomous software is universally uninsurable | Unsupported | The actual policy wording controls. FAR 28.305, LR24, provides war-hazard employee compensation insurance context, not AI-product cover. No panel-wide product-liability policy/exclusion universe was obtained. |
| Export classification is the export licence and routinely mandates a kill switch | Incorrect/unsupported | BIS classification guidance LR36 distinguishes classification and licensing; ITAR 22 CFR 123.9 LR30 regulates changes of end user/use/destination subject to exceptions. EAR 736.2(b)(9) LR31 governs licence/order conditions. None establishes routine remote disablement. |
| Export monitors prove an IHL safeguard | Incorrect without the substantive condition | RTX's 29 August 2024 consent, LR06, imposes genuine legal support, classification reviews, audits and board escalation. It enforces AECA/ITAR compliance, not a demonstrated autonomy/IHL use restraint. |
| ICC liability attaches to corporations or nationality alone determines ICC reach | Incorrect | Rome Statute arts 12,13,17,25(1),30,58,89,98, LR17; UN participation record LR18. ICC jurisdiction over natural persons is distinct from domestic corporate liability. Jurisdiction, admissibility, participation, mental element and arrest/surrender procedure are separate questions. |
| European/NATO criminal law is uniform and airport travel produces automatic arrest | Incorrect | Germany VStGB§ 1/StPO§ 153f, LR11/LR12; England and Wales ICC Act§§ 51–52, LR13/LR14; US 18 USC 2441(b), (f), LR37 (2024 edition, preliminary indexed text corroboration only), have materially different jurisdiction and procedural gates. Other states not fully audited here. |
| Lundin is already a conviction | Incorrect at cutoff | Stockholm District Court case B 11304-14, LR09: hearing concluded 28 May 2026; judgment scheduled 3 December 2026. Company fine/forfeiture claims are not an ICC prosecution of a corporation. |
| Lafarge was convicted of complicity in crimes against humanity | Incorrect at cutoff on available primary evidence | US 2022 corporate plea LR08 concerns material support to terrorist organizations. Holcim 2026 H 1 report p 30§ 15, LR38, reports French 13 April 2026 terrorism/customs convictions, appeals 15/17 April, and a separate still-pending complicity-humanity investigation. Full 2024 French original judgment inaccessible in this pass; translated 2021 decision LR27 is expressly unofficial. |
| Criminal/export analogies demonstrate an autonomous-weapon release was stopped by counsel | Unresolved | RTX and Lafarge establish bounded governance obligations; FinFisher prosecutor release LR20 establishes 2023 indictment allegations only. No named autonomous-product release veto was established from these records. |

## Late primary-source correction incorporated before final review

Enacted PL119-60 (18 December 2025), § 876,139 Stat 1005, LR47, requires practicable DCMA insurance-market input and a final approval or denial with executed memorandum within 90 days. Its advanced-nuclear delegation rule qualifies older FAR head-only language. Section 801,139 Stat 939–940, adds 10 USC 3864 for specified classified fixed-price product contracts entered after enactment: work-in-process risk uninsurable because of classification cannot be forced onto contractors, subject to managerial-misconduct/good-faith and workmanship exclusions. This is not an autonomous-weapon indemnity or third-party product-liability policy. Only these two sections of the entire act are within this lane’s screening scope.
