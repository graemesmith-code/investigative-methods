# F6 — Capital, counsel and corporate decision gates

**Historical cutoff and retrieval: 26 October 2026. Lanes 4–6 and corporate governance. Public/free research; spend $0.**

## Findings first

The strongest evidence is not a uniform industry veto over autonomous targeting. It is a mixture of **company-described review processes, actual investor exclusions, a non-autonomy product withdrawal, and resistance to additional shareholder scrutiny**. These are meaningful but different controls. None of this lane's documents establishes a named autonomous-weapon release stopped by a company's General Counsel. That bounded finding must not become “no safeguards exist.”

1. **Saab has a real described sales-review process, but its own report contradicts itself about the trigger.** Its collective management Ethics and Compliance Board has a responsible-sales decision remit; its General Counsel is a member, not a demonstrated sole veto-holder. Eight reported assessments are not eight refusals. [Saab 2025 annual report, pp60,70,124–126](https://www.saab.com/globalassets/corporate/investor-relations/annual-report/2026/saab-ar25-eng.pdf). Record **CAP01**.
2. **Storebrand actually excluded Palantir**, and the exclusion remains on its Q3 2026 list. The reason is the investor's human-rights/international-law assessment—not a demonstrated LAWS-specific decision. No transaction amount or product restraint was established. [Q3 2024 review, pp56–57](https://www.storebrandam.com/globalassets/storebrand-asset-management/documents/sustainability/quarterly-sustainable-investment-reviews/sustainable-investment-review-q3-2024.pdf); [Q3 2026 list, p1](https://www.storebrandam.com/globalassets/storebrand-asset-management/documents/sustainability/exclusion-list/2026/q3-2026-exclusion-list.pdf). **CAP03**.
3. **Rheinmetall disclosed cancellation of most acquired white-phosphorus illumination contracts and termination of production/deliveries in July 2024.** Storebrand subsequently removed observation. This is a concrete product/contract consequence, but not autonomous-weapons evidence or proof investor pressure caused it. [Issuer presentation, 8 August 2024, p11](https://irpages2.eqs.com/Download/Companies/rheinmetall/Presentations/20240808-Rheinmetall-Conference-Call-Q2-2024.pdf). **CAP04**.
4. **Norway's old GPFG exclusion framework cannot be represented as the current new-exclusion mechanism.** The operative interim framework uses ownership follow-up and permits revocation of existing exclusions; it does not automatically reinstate excluded companies. The October 2026 review remained a consultation at the cutoff. [Authentic consolidated instrument](https://lovdata.no/forskrift/2025-11-07-2231); [15 October Ministry release](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/finansministeren-mottok-etikkutvalgets-utredning-om-statens-pensjonsfond-utland/id3177352/). **CAP05**.
5. **Pure military investment eligibility does not mean an absence of ethical restrictions.** Darkstar's current public page declares human-in-the-loop and IHL/Geneva investment screening, while its state-backed anchor investor welcomed single-use military eligibility. A public commitment is not a retrieved LP covenant or a named screened/refused investment. [Darkstar, section05](https://darkstar.ee/); [SmartCap, 9 July 2025](https://smartcap.ee/smartcap-invests-e10m-in-darkstars-defence-focused-venture-capital-fund/). **CAP22**.

## 1. Who can require what?

### Saab: bounded governance, unresolved internal wording

The 2025 report names **Annika Bäremo**, Senior Vice President/Head of Group Legal Affairs, General Counsel and Board Secretary (p60). The management Ethics and Compliance Board includes the GC, Deputy CEO, CFO and head of Communication/Sustainability. Its p70 diagram includes responsible-sales decisions. It is **not the corporate Board of Directors**.

The Responsible Sales process places assessments with Business and Human Rights within Group Sustainability and informed decisions with business areas; complex cases may be escalated for guidance, with outcomes reported to the corporate board (pp124–125). Assessment may reopen on material new information and must be refreshed when at least twelve months old at closure (p126).

**Unresolved contradiction:** pp31/124 use a conjunction of low customer index, military-equipment classification and non-follow-on delivery. The p126 accounting principles instead say any criterion suffices. Both are retained; neither is silently selected as operative. The reported eight assessments in 2025 and sixteen in 2024 count assessments at the specified decision point, not refused sales. The p131 IHL reference concerns Swedish ISP export assessment, not an independently proved company IHL veto. **Next decisive evidence:** the operative policy, CRM flag rule and delegated authority/decision record. This is an extension of a known seed, not a scoop. **CAP01**.

## 2. Investor exclusions: operative scope, decisions and consequences

Storebrand's **9 February 2026** exclusion policy is a voluntary asset-manager policy with portfolio/mandate application, not a general law banning investment or weapons. It expressly includes LAWS among controversial weapons and IHL among conduct criteria. Conduct exclusions go to its Sustainable Investment Committee. Its p10 execution process gives managers approximately twenty days to sell excluded holdings and describes daily compliance controls. The approved-ETF exception can permit up to ten percent excluded-company exposure; selected funds have additional restrictions. These exceptions prevent an unqualified “all exposure eliminated” claim. [Policy, pp3–5,7–10](https://www.storebrandam.com/globalassets/storebrand-asset-management/documents/sustainability/policy-and-guidelines/exclusion-policy/exclusion-policy_2026.pdf). **CAP02**.

The **Palantir decision** is separately dated to the Q3 2024 review; the current policy does not retroactively establish sale timing. The investor's allegations and its ICJ sidebar are not judicial findings of Palantir liability. The Q3 2026 list's multi-column placement was visually checked: Palantir is in **Human Rights and International Law**, not Controversial weapons. **CAP03**.

For **Rheinmetall**, investor and issuer documents converge on an actual discontinued activity. But July completion differs from the earlier expected end of Q2. Neither document proves that Storebrand caused the decision, that a specific amount was divested, or that all white-phosphorus illumination uses are unlawful. **CAP04**.

### GPFG version trap

The old 18 December 2014 guidelines were repealed when the interim instrument entered force on **7 November 2025**. The authentic Norwegian consolidated text is **FOR-2025-11-07-2231**, amended **5 October 2026** by **FOR-2026-10-05-2004**. Original and amending texts were downloaded and compared: §3(1)(a)'s detected change is spelling/punctuation around non-detectable fragments, not a demonstrated LAWS reversal. Neither retrieved list expressly names LAWS.

Sections1–4 identify companies for possible ownership engagement; §§5–6 retain review/revocation of existing observation/exclusion; §10 maintains publication/list obligations. The Ministry's explanation confirms that new recommendations and decisions on observation/exclusion are paused. The English translation is explicitly informational; Norwegian controls. The 15 October 2026 commission report went to consultation, with government assessment aimed at spring 2027, not immediate adoption. [Amending instrument](https://lovdata.no/forskrift/2026-10-05-2004); [original promulgation](https://lovdata.no/LTI/forskrift/2025-11-07-2231); [Ministry explanation](https://www.regjeringen.no/en/topics/the-economy/the-government-pension-fund/responsible-management/ethical-guidelines/id447009/). **CAP05**.

ABP's 2020 explanation and PFZW's current page allow ordinary defence investment while excluding specified controversial weapons. The dated 2025 PFZW policy PDF was inaccessible; these sources do not establish a current exhaustive Dutch mandate or a named LAWS exclusion. **CAP14**.

## 3. Counsel and shareholders can resist more oversight

Palantir's board opposed two 2026 reporting proposals; the **3 June 2026** meeting rejected them, as filed on **9 June**. The register preserves each voting column. These are voting-power totals, not shareholder headcounts or equal-vote shares. Defeat demonstrates resistance to additional reports, not absence of existing controls. [2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000132165526000019/pltr-20260423.htm); [results, Item5.07](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000132165526000033/pltr-20260603.htm). **CAP07**.

General Dynamics's outside counsel **Davis Polk, Ning Chiu**, sought to omit a human-rights impact-assessment proposal. On **25 March 2025**, SEC staff declined to concur with the asserted misleading-statement and ordinary-business/micromanagement grounds. The proposal appeared in the proxy, with board opposition. This is an actual procedural outcome, **not an SEC finding that the proponents' war-crime or nuclear-law allegations were true**. [Correspondence, p1 and January2 letter](https://www.sec.gov/files/corpfin/no-action/14a-8/franciscangeneral32525-14a8.pdf); [2025 proxy, pp92–93](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40533/000130817925000291/gd013194-def14a.htm). **CAP08**.

### Darkstar: a useful counterexample, carefully bounded

## 5. Coverage, recall and publication limits

* **30 named firms received discovery queries.** The coverage register distinguishes primary-document examination from discovery-only results and unresolved legal entities. No denominator implies complete securities, investor or LP files for all thirty firms.
* **51 source records; 45 downloaded text bodies swept: 724 PDF pages and 32 HTML bodies.** Six failed direct retrievals are registered; one SEC landing page is separately distinguished from its actual correspondence PDF. BAE web-only reading is not counted as a downloaded sweep.
* **3,892 lexical occurrences**, retaining 3,759 context groups, plus **two manually located multi-column supplements**. After contextual review, **1,142** rows are marked context-reviewed and **2,750** machine-only. These are occurrence/page-context classifications, not 1,142 operative safeguards. **35** IHL false positives remain visible as generic rights/privacy references.
* The lexicon searches explicit, substantive-concept, indirect, generic and ambiguous references without requiring autonomy/weapon co-occurrence. It includes Norwegian, Dutch and selected other language anchors. It is a transparent high-recall candidate pass, **not complete semantic recall** or a certified translation.
* Recall tests found real misses: Norwegian ethics stems in the initially zero-hit Ministry article; white-phosphorus withdrawal on a zero-hit issuer slide; and multi-column interrupted IHL strings. Terms were expanded and the corpus rerun; manual supplements preserve the interrupted locations. Employment discrimination/UDHR distinction and GDPR proportionality are not battlefield concepts.
* All promoted passages were contextually checked; Saab pp70/126, Storebrand's listing column and Rheinmetall's slide were visually verified. General OCR was **not** performed. Image-only text, extraction order and web-only material remain gaps. No “no IHL reference” claim is made for an inaccessible or unscreened document.
* Full copyrighted source copies and raw extracted paragraphs remain **private working material, not whitelisted for GitHub or Drive**. Public records contain stable links, hashes, exact locators, common matched phrases, limited reviewed extracts and authored context. This is a rights-aware full audit trail, not permission to rehost entire reports.
