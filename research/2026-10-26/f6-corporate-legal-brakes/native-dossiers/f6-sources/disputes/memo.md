## Findings worth carrying forward

### D-C01. A signed, historically applicable restriction—not merely public AIP marketing

An actual **Anthropic PBC–Palantir Technologies Inc.** agreement appears in the public appellate appendix in **D.C. Circuit 26-1049, document 2169955, filed 22 April 2026**. The U.S. Federal Government Usage Policy Addendum and Restricted Use Addendum (RUA) are at **App.391–407 / PDF99–115**. Both signature pages were visually checked because text extraction garbles the signature dates. Palantir's **Ryan Taylor, CRO and CLO**, signed on **15 August 2025**; Anthropic's **Thiyagu Ramasamy, Head of Public Sector**, signed on **16 August 2025**. This proves a legal officer executed a bounded agreement, not that he personally drafted it, vetoed a deployment, or conducted an IHL weapons review. [Public filed appendix](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cadc.42923/gov.uscourts.cadc.42923.01208843409.4.pdf#page=99).

RUA §2(a), **App.405 / PDF113**, excludes using the services for making:

> any final determinations about a potential target of or directly tracking Kinetic action, as compared to informing human decision makers

That 20-word extract is the substantive finding. The same page allows wide military/intelligence planning, commercial defence-industrial support and operational facilitation. **§2(b) expressly allows human OR machine oversight for intelligence collection**; this is not a blanket meaningful-human-control obligation. §3 limits amendment or termination **of this RUA** to legal/court requirements or agreement of both parties. It does **not** establish that the unreviewed master terms contain no suspension, breach or other remedies. Indeed, the attached Usage Policy (**App.394 / PDF102**) generally reserves throttling, suspension or termination for violations and describes case-by-case government exceptions conditioned on relevant safeguards. Those provisions remain subject to the rider’s priority and actual deployment constraints; no exercise is established.

The government addendum modifies an attached usage policy and narrows the weapons prohibition: its definition excludes items not themselves intended to be lethal or cause grievous harm; it expressly permits specified intelligence and government activities. The RUA controls a conflict with the government usage policy; the government addendum controls inconsistent usage-policy/master provisions within its scope. This combines substantive restraint **A**, a negotiated exception, and commercial allocation—not a universal vendor veto.

**Applicability and unresolved chain:** the addendum identifies master Commercial Terms executed **10 July 2025** and provision through Anthropic, AWS Bedrock/Sagemaker and Google Vertex. Its replacement clause is effective at the last signature. The attached usage policy’s effective date is **unstated in the exhibit**; it is not independently proven equivalent to a June 2025 version and must not be replaced by today's live URL. Ramasamy's June 2026 declaration describes further **January 2026** government permissions; this audit has not established the executed successor text or whether it superseded this precise Palantir agreement. The agreement therefore establishes an **August 2025 historical contractual boundary**, not every customer's current restriction. It is distinct from the separate July 2025 direct CDAO arrangement and contemplated GenAI.mil renegotiation.

The licence lane independently locates public AIP terms effective **5 October 2026**, including natural-person output review and model/hosting-specific terms. No obtained customer contract makes those public standard terms the successor to this signed government rider. Cross-reference that lane rather than silently combining the instruments.

**Rights/provenance:** this is a lawfully accessible public RECAP court exhibit, not private access. Its inherited **Confidential** marking is retained as a provenance warning; it is not a licence to redistribute the agreement. The appendix, agreement, signature images and private exact-context extracts are excluded from the publication whitelist. Publish only this minimal quote, authored analysis, public link, locator and hash.

### D-C02. Refusal is documented, but the classified deployment lacked a live vendor enforcement switch

Kaplan's sworn declaration (**ECF6-1**, §§21–22,29–39) distinguishes partner-platform arrangements, the July 2025 direct engagement and later negotiations. It describes refusing the proposed unqualified all-lawful-uses expansion because Anthropic considered the model insufficiently reliable for fully autonomous lethal warfare, with risks to soldiers and civilians. Those are company testimony and scientific/mission judgments—not a court's ruling that a particular weapon is illegal. [Kaplan declaration](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.465515/gov.uscourts.cand.465515.6.1.pdf).

Ramasamy's later sworn declaration (**ECF166-4**, §§22–27,31–33,65,67) is important contrary evidence: Anthropic says it cannot access, shut down or alter models once deployed on the classified systems, receives no classified user data or intervention capability, and cannot technically enforce the relevant contractual restrictions there. Most usage restrictions were negotiable, according to his account; the two persistent exceptions concerned lethal autonomy and mass domestic surveillance. The declarant gives his role and personal-knowledge/reviewed-record basis. This supports a **contractual/negotiating brake**, not verified runtime enforcement. [June declaration](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.465515/gov.uscourts.cand.465515.166.4.pdf).

The government disputed model/wrapper/update reliability and trust in Emil Michael's April declaration (**appendix App.408–422**). The September opinion reports the Secretary's June reconsideration decision clarified it did not rely on Anthropic being able to control a classified model after delivery. Earlier government accusations are not proven sabotage. The opinions record the refusal, independently corroborating the existence of the dispute, but do not adjudicate the legality of an autonomous-weapons use. Company estimates of losses and contract ceilings are not demonstrated revenue losses or causal financial outcomes.

### D-C04. NDIA advocated both humanitarian guardrails and fewer defence-use restrictions

NDIA's **22 July 2024 submission**, pages2–3,5,10,12–13, is unusually useful contrary/mixed evidence. It asks for risk-calibrated human involvement, verification and termination frameworks; on **page12** it expressly invokes **global humanitarian law** and human oversight/governance of AI-enabled weapon behaviour. It also urges DoD to negotiate removal of defence-use licensing restrictions (**p5**), and proposes copyright/liability/indemnity risk allocation (**p10**). These are **industry requests**, not adopted law, executed indemnities, proof all vendors ban military use, or a company counsel's actual release decision. [NDIA submission](https://www.ndia.org/-/media/sites/ndia/policy/documents/ndia-comments-on-dib-adoption-of-ai-for-defense-applications-2024-11195.pdf).

NDIA's **3 April 2026** comments distinguish discretionary output restrictions from safeguards required by law/security standards; they also object to liability for upstream actors, excessive notices and broad government-specific improvement obligations. Its **3 August 2026** comments ask to narrow material-change notices and allow urgent security/safety/reliability/abuse-prevention changes with later notice, while preserving commercial IP and acquisition practices. This is more nuanced than industry uniformly opposing safeguards. [April comments](https://www.ndia.org/-/media/sites/ndia/policy/regulatory-submission-tracker/2026/gsa-ai-clause.pdf), [August comments](https://www.ndia.org/-/media/sites/ndia/policy/ndia-comments-on-gsar-552239-7001-ai-llm-clause--noticemvac202601.pdf).

The June 17 revised GSA proposal says industry comments prompted revisions. The later consolidated **RGO-2026-01 class deviation** contains operative LLM provisions with an October 19 effective date. **Do not call every comment adopted, attribute causation to NDIA alone, or call a class deviation a universally incorporated contract clause.** The licence lane independently handles final clause wording and scope. AIA and PSC leads were located but full local text/access is incomplete; no nonexistent industry white paper is asserted. ABA-specific search did not locate an accessible adopted defence-AI safeguard duty.

### D-C05–08. Real protest outcomes are not proof of failed weapons safety tests

* **Mistral Inc. (Bethesda, not French Mistral AI), GAO B-422905/B-422905.2,13December2024:** denied challenge to an Army sole-source loitering-munitions award to AeroVironment. Initial factual premises were dismissed and supplemental merits rejected. The Army's assertions about missing certification/facility requirements are **agency positions**, not proven test failures; GAO rejected a standing dismissal in light of the Army's own viable/technically-mature market research (**pp7–8**). A **20 September CICA stay override** is a procurement-stay override, not disabling a weapon safety interlock. The $990m figure is an award ceiling, not expenditure. [Decision](https://www.gao.gov/assets/880/874529.pdf).
* **Anduril Industries Inc., B-419420,22February2021:** ABMS competition/task-order dispute denied in part, with jurisdictional limits on other allegations. It does not establish an autonomy safety defect or IHL veto. [Decision](https://www.gao.gov/assets/b-419420.pdf).
* **Anduril B-424504.2:** official docket records filing12August2026 and dismissal4September2026. No public reasoning was retrieved; do not convert dismissal or press reports of reevaluation into an adjudicated technical defect. [GAO docket](https://www.gao.gov/docket/b-424504.2).
* **System Dynamics International Inc. v United States and Strata-G Solutions LLC, COFC23-431, ECF61**, filed21 September/public-redacted6October2023: the court actually **enjoined the SUAS engineering-support award**. Its decisive analysis concerned minimum educational/personnel qualifications and the agency's improper weakness-versus-deficiency treatment (**pp18–22,30**), not a failed autonomous-targeting test. This is a documented procurement enforcement event with engineering relevance, not a corporate IHL restraint. [Public redacted court opinion](https://ecf.cofc.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/show_public_doc?2023cv0431-61-0).

Altavian's2019 decision is an antecedent only. Blue Water Autonomy/Saildrone COFC26-936/26-944 is an open lead: an August 26 docket snapshot lists redacted filings, but no current merits opinion or full redacted pleading was retrieved here. No claim about an actual failed fly-off or waived safety requirement is promoted.

## Novelty and hypothesis assessment

F4 already reported the two courts, procurement-risk tension and rehearing-response order; those are **baseline**, not new scoops. F6 adds the signed historical Palantir–Anthropic agreement, the narrower intelligence-oversight wording, the contractual-versus-technical-enforcement distinction and exact NDIA mixed proposals. Targeted exact-clause/RUA/RyanTaylor searches did not establish prior press treatment of the agreement; this is **new documentary detail in this audit, not a warranted exclusivity claim**. The restriction's existence is strong evidence for a bounded corporate contractual brake; broad permitted uses, negotiability, uncertain successor text, no demonstrated classified runtime enforcement and pressure to remove licence restrictions materially weaken a simplistic durable-veto thesis.
