# The available data on US international security capabilities

**Source map checked 16 October 2026.** Scope: investments supporting US international engagement and overseas national-security missions, across military and other security institutions. This describes available data before choosing investigations, methods or story pitches. It builds outward from the existing roughly 10,000 Pentagon contract-announcement paragraphs covering September 2023–September 2026.

## The central finding

There is a much larger public record than the announcements: budget projects, transaction histories, research awards, requirements, consortium disclosures, laboratory projects and evidence of testing or use. The strongest collection would preserve these as connected records with different meanings. No public source provides a complete census of internationally directed US security capabilities or a reliable single total for “novel capabilities.”

Coverage extends beyond AI to quantum computing and sensing, drones and autonomy, cyber, electronic warfare, communications, space, advanced sensors, power and logistics. The broad budget and award sources can contain all of these. Technical descriptions and mission evidence are needed to distinguish them. Domestic policing or administrative AI is peripheral unless the record establishes a relevant international mission.

Two scales are visible. Individual projects can emerge in detailed budget narratives, small research awards, solicitation attachments and consortium project tables. Broader spending patterns can be examined in repeated budget lines and award transactions. Both require a separate prior-reporting record before making claims about originality.

## 1. What was sought: budgets and requirements

**Core corpus.** Defense R-1/P-1 summaries provide the annual program structure. Service and defense-wide justification books go further: R-2/R-2A project narratives, R-3 cost/performer tables, and P-40/P-5/P-5a procurement exhibits. Named subefforts can sit below the program-element total. O&M narratives add operating and sustainment costs. DOE/NNSA has its own congressional budget justifications. DHS and DOJ/FBI books are conditional additions where an international mission is evidenced. These are mostly PDFs with recurring identifiers rather than one clean database. [Budget inventory, families 1–3 and 13–15](sources/budgets.md)

SAM Contract Opportunities supplies notices and technical attachments. Grants.gov supplies grant forecasts and opportunities. DARPA, IARPA, DIU and Tradewinds add program-specific descriptions. Requirements explain what agencies wanted, often more clearly than a contract's short description. Estimated funding and solicitation ceilings remain plans. [Procurement inventory, families 6, 8, 11–12](sources/procurement.md), [research inventory, families 2–4](sources/research.md)

## 2. What Congress provided and agencies reallocated

**Core corpus.** Appropriations laws, adjustment tables and joint explanatory statements identify approved funding and additions. An authorization bill is a separate record. DD 1414 gives a defense program baseline; DD 1415 documents reprogramming actions; DD 1416 reports the changing program position. These can expose investments beyond the annual request. As checked, FY2026 DD 1416 workbooks through June 30 were listed, with September marked forthcoming. [Budget inventory, families 4–6 and 8–9](sources/budgets.md)

**Context.** OMB account spreadsheets, NCSES federal R&D tables and NITRD supplements provide wider denominators. They have different definitions, reporting lags and institutional exclusions. Neither an account total nor an AI-themed crosscut is an additional pot of money to add to underlying projects. [Budget inventory, families 12, 17 and 21](sources/budgets.md)

## 3. What was awarded or obligated, and to whom

**Core corpus.** USAspending award and transaction records are the principal structured expansion beyond press releases. They include smaller awards, modifications and negative deobligations. SAM Contract Awards, incorporating former FPDS records, supplies another view of the procurement record and explicitly documents Other Transactions. These overlapping sources must not be counted as independent spending. Public DoD procurement data have a 90-day delay. SAM's current award search/API requires an account, and the API requires a key. [Procurement inventory, families 1 and 4](sources/procurement.md)

SBIR/STTR adds small research awards and abstracts across technologies. USAspending File C links some awards to Treasury accounts and financial reporting, including outlays, with linkage gaps and historical limitations. ForeignAssistance.gov adds obligations/disbursements for security-assistance context. [Research inventory, family 1](sources/research.md), [procurement inventory, families 3 and 17](sources/procurement.md)

**Partial but important.** First-tier subcontract reports and consortium-manager disclosures can name technology firms obscured by prime or umbrella awards. ATI publishes a rolling 12-month project table with member names and ceilings. Other consortium material is uneven. None supplies a complete chain to ultimate performers. [Procurement inventory, families 2, 5 and 10](sources/procurement.md)

## 4. What the money produced, tested or put into use

**Context and corroboration.** DARPA/IARPA program pages, DIU transition catalogs, AFWERX/SpaceWERX disclosures, DOE/NNSA internal laboratory-research reports, OSTI publications, GAO assessments and DOT&E testing reports supply project names, performers and progress. A prototype, successful test and fielded system are different states. [Research inventory](sources/research.md), [procurement inventory, families 8–9](sources/procurement.md)

Federal AI inventories are a peripheral source: their reporting scope excludes defense, intelligence-community and National Security System uses. Agency privacy assessments describe system design and safeguards, without necessarily establishing spending or operational deployment. Domestic-only uses fall outside this investigation. Patents, SEC filings, NASA projects and ARPA-E records are selective extensions where an international security connection is evidenced. [Research inventory, families 10–17](sources/research.md)

## 5. What has already been reported

Keep a separate article/reference layer: Defense News, Breaking Defense, C4ISRNET, Defense One, SpaceNews, procurement/IT reporting, national investigations and local company/laboratory coverage. Publisher pages are available unevenly; this map does not assume historical bulk feeds.

Media Cloud documents exports of article URLs and aggregate results, with source/date gaps and no downloadable article full text. GDELT provides discovery metadata, but current query limits and historical retrieval were not tested. Nexis and Factiva are licensed candidates; no project subscription, API entitlement or reuse permission was verified. [Media inventory](sources/media.md)

An unmatched record is a lead. It is not proof that an investment was unreported. A new spending-pattern finding can also be original even when its component contracts have been covered.

## Institutional coverage

- **Military services and defense agencies:** strongest combination of public budgets, procurement and selected testing records, with classified and delayed records missing.
- **Intelligence community:** public NIP/MIP toplines and selected IARPA/program disclosures; no complete public agency/project ledger. Intelligence totals can overlap department budgets.
- **DHS and DOJ/FBI:** conditional coverage for evidenced overseas/international missions, including relevant maritime or cross-border operations. Domestic policing and routine administrative AI are outside scope.
- **DOE/NNSA and laboratories:** budget decision units, lab allocations, internal research reports and outputs. Large management contracts obscure project-level expenditure.
- **State, foreign security assistance, NASA and ARPA-E:** aid and transfer records cover international engagement; civilian research is a selective extension. Proposed foreign sales, US aid and foreign-funded purchases retain their separate meanings. This map does not yet establish comprehensive State intelligence, diplomatic-security or technology-program budget coverage.

## Access and limits that change the picture

This is a verified source map, not a downloaded dataset. The register distinguishes retrieved records/pages, documented or listed downloads, indexed-only material, and access requiring authentication. Many current defense budget files were indexed but not retrieved. DTIC public searches report being offline. SBA's API warns of maintenance. TechPort and patent-data routes have authentication requirements. Full Tradewinds buyer access and some technical attachments are restricted.

Critical gaps are classified intelligence activity, ultimate OTA performers, subcontract tiers, and the missing universal link between budget projects and contract IDs. Changing thresholds matter: Pentagon announcement coverage moved from $7.5 million to $9 million on 1 October 2025. Amendments, fiscal vintages, program transfers, parent/order relationships and cumulative values create double-counting risks. The dollar measure must stay attached to every record: request, budget authority, allocation, obligation, outlay, ceiling or lifecycle estimate. [Procurement inventory](sources/procurement.md), [budget inventory](sources/budgets.md)

## Appendix A. How to read the source register

[SOURCE_REGISTER.csv](SOURCE_REGISTER.csv) is the filterable inventory. Stable IDs identify source families rather than individual downloaded records. Each entry gives the official or first-party entry point, companion links, unit, institutional/time coverage, access format, possible joining identifiers, verification status, limitations and priority.

- **Core:** direct money/requirement records central to establishing the public investment universe. Core does not mean complete, downloadable anonymously or already ingested.
- **Contextual:** interpretation, capability, entity or prior-reporting evidence that supports the money records.
- **Limited-access:** useful candidates with a material access barrier, outage or unverified entitlement. An access-limited record may still be important.
- **Retrieved** always specifies what was retrieved. Opening documentation is not a successful data query. **Listed/documented** means a publisher describes or links the resource. **Indexed only** means an official result was visible in search without successful direct retrieval. None is a bulk-ingestion certification.

USAspending award records, SAM/FPDS-derived actions and daily announcements overlap. The register retains each access route because they expose different fields; its source-family grouping is not permission to add their money. DARPA's program/opportunity entry is consolidated across inventories. GAO weapons assessments are consolidated in the budget/oversight entry; DOT&E remains separate. OMB's consolidated AI inventory and agency originals remain separate source versions in the same family.
