# What happened to the Civilian Protection Center of Excellence team?

**Research cutoff: 16 October 2026.** This report separates the centre itself, the wider civilian-harm mitigation and response (CHMR) enterprise, specific technical products, and individual careers. They have different headcounts and trajectories.

## Bottom line

The best-supported account is **severe institutional contraction and dispersal of expertise, with some work surviving inside the remaining centre, operational commands, and independent research organisations**. There is no verified wholesale transfer of the team or its technical programme to one successor.

- The centre's statutory mandate remains in [10 U.S.C. §184](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t10/s184/). A proposed repeal and a proposed Army-to-Joint-Staff transfer are not evidence that either happened.
- A [13 October 2026 congressional letter](https://crow.house.gov/media/press-releases/crow-presses-pentagon-over-civilian-casualties-during-trump-s-iran-war) reports that CP CoE had fallen to **seven** staff. This is the newest official public statement located, but not an independently audited roster. It supersedes nine as the latest reported number.
- Named former staff are now publicly associated with NYU's Reiss Center, the Institute for Defense Analyses, independent civilian-protection work, and investigations. Their expertise continued; their new employers did not thereby inherit CP CoE's statutory responsibilities, government data, or software.
- The most concrete technical lineage is civilian-environment work in **Maven**, plus a separately reported **AskSage-based prototype**. Neither public report establishes the October 2026 maintainer, operational deployment status, or measured protection outcomes. The Army's discontinued **CHMR Data Management Platform (DMP)** is another system and should not be conflated with them.

## Evidence labels

**High:** directly readable official record or employer/author biography, for the proposition it actually establishes. **Medium:** credible investigative reporting, self-reported professional affiliation, or indexed primary-document text where the full document could not be retrieved. **Open:** a plausible lead without an execution order, roster, deliverable, or other adequate confirmation.

“Official” does not make a congressional allegation a verified personnel record; “primary” does not make a vendor's claim independently evaluated.

### What the proposed “reboot” actually establishes

The August investigation says eleven command leaders were consulted in early 2025: nine preferred a reduced but operational enterprise, while STRATCOM and CYBERCOM sought preservation. It reports that CENTCOM originally sought sixteen staff, with nine a minimum. The April proposal would restore regional-command teams as well as reposition the centre. Command support, a proposal, and restoration are three different stages. No public approval memorandum, effective transfer date, receiving billet list, or funded staffing table was located. It would be premature to say “the Joint Staff took over.” [ProPublica / Defense One, 25 August 2026](https://www.defenseone.com/policy/2026/08/hegseth-civilian-protection-axed/415621/)

The inspector general's report matters separately: its indexed text says a formal departmental decision was still outstanding as of April 2026. Its management-response section gives a 31 December 2026 estimated completion date for a standardisation recommendation, which is a future administrative commitment, not proof that the reform was already delivered. [DOWIG-2026-084](https://media.defense.gov/2026/May/14/2003930527/-1/-1/1/DOWIG-2026-084_REDACTED%20SECURE.PDF)

## 3. Who continued specific functions and technical work?

### A. Maven civilian-environment work: a concrete operational lineage

The FY2024 official report's indexed text identifies **XVIII Airborne Corps, Warfighter Exercise 24-5** as a specific setting where the centre supported civilian-environment integration in Maven Smart System and Civil Affairs operational planning. It also describes preparatory academic briefings for **I Corps and III Corps**. Two contracted technology analysts supported experimentation, but the indexed material does not name them. [FY2024 report](https://policy.defense.gov/Portals/11/Documents/CHMR/CP_CoE_FY2024_Report-508-Compliant.pdf?ver=MbjJrGoKDeoOGU28JJ0gPw%3D%3D)

This makes XVIII Airborne Corps' exercise/Maven integration staff a concrete institutional breadcrumb. It does **not** identify the present software owner or prove that any feature remained enabled in operational deployments after centre staffing cuts.

### B. FY2025 Maven layer and AskSage prototype: product survival is possible, maintenance is unknown

The July 2026 investigation, based on the FY2025 report, describes a civilian-environment layer in Maven and an AskSage-based prototype to help use CHMR information. It reports a **97% time reduction in one Army exercise**, with other Army units requesting access, and describes continued training and operational studies. This establishes reported experimentation during the period covered, not independent validation of accuracy, lawful outcomes, or production readiness. [Nick Turse, The Intercept investigation, republished 20 July 2026](https://www.rsn.org/001/hegseths-war-department-slashed-civilian-protection-staff-brought-in-ai-assessments.html)

An [Opinio Juris analysis, 17 August 2026](https://opiniojuris.org/2026/08/17/testing-the-limits-ai-enabled-targeting-model-evaluation-and-international-humanitarian-law/), links the official report and discusses the prototype. Summaries differ in describing the 97% task, so do not upgrade it into a precise validated benchmark. **AskSage is the underlying platform; the CP CoE application/prototype is not synonymous with the entire AskSage product.** No named government product manager, technical contractor, repository, support contract, evaluation dataset, current deployment list, or October maintainer was verified.

### C. Army CHMR Data Management Platform: a distinct disrupted programme

The IG describes DMP usability/data/interface problems and the Army's suspension of funding. This was not simply deletion of a fully mature, proven national database. The IG's discussion of Objective 6 supports a serious interruption to data-management reform, while its Objective 5 discussion also records losses affecting red-team training. [DOWIG-2026-084](https://media.defense.gov/2026/May/14/2003930527/-1/-1/1/DOWIG-2026-084_REDACTED%20SECURE.PDF)

**Contractor breadcrumb:** QBE's [own CHMR award announcement](https://qbe.net/chmraward/) names **Thomas Mastal, Senior Director**, as contract lead and says QBE staffed combatant-command and front-office positions. The undated page was already cited in an [October 2024 CIVIC/Stimson report](https://civiliansinconflict.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CIVIC_CHMRAP_Draft.pdf); that report identifies Army **DAMO-SO** as the DMP contracting office and QBE as its builder (see discussion around p.31 and endnote 77). The vendor primary source establishes the CHMR relationship and lead; the specific DMP attribution here comes from the civil-society report. It does not establish an active 2026 contract or prove QBE built the separate Maven/AskSage features.

### D. CENTCOM / Joint Staff: some duties remained in operational organisations

Cooper's Senate testimony said the people removed from dedicated CENTCOM CHMR jobs were retained in other capacities, while describing a wider targeting process involving many people. This is the strongest directly attributable answer to “where did the command team go?” available here. It leaves unanswered whether their new jobs retained protected time, independence, reporting responsibilities, or civilian-environment expertise. The centre and CENTCOM are different organisations. [Official transcript publication, 15 May 2026](https://www.kelly.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/watch-in-sasc-hearing-kelly-demands-answers-on-cuts-to-civilian-protection-staff-propaganda-funding-gap/)

### E. CNA / INDOPACOM: identifiable analytical continuity, with a date caveat

CNA published [Civilian Harm Mitigation and Response in a Large-Scale Contingency Operation Wargame](https://www.cna.org/quick-looks/2026/civilian-harm-mitigation-and-response-in-a-large-scale-contingency-operation-wargame) on **12 May 2026**. The event itself took place **18–21 February 2025** in Honolulu with INDOPACOM, components and CP CoE participation. Authors are **Larry Lewis, Andrew Olson, Kate Lea, Sarah Agyapong, Sabrina Verleysen, Tess Martin and Chris Steinitz**. This preserves a concrete analytical output and collaborator network; a 2026 publication does not establish uninterrupted 2026 operational staffing or funding. [Report PDF](https://www.cna.org/quick-looks/2026/05/Civilian-Harm-Mitigation-and-Response-in-a-Large-Scale-Contingency-Operation-Wargame.pdf)

The primary [USAspending award record](https://api.usaspending.gov/api/v2/awards/CONT_AWD_N0001424FT171_9700_N0001422D7001_9700/) confirms **CNA task order N0001424FT171**, titled *Civilian Harm and Responsible AI*, funded by **CDAO**, awarded by **ONR**, with **$500,000 obligated / $550,000 potential**, for **1 July 2024–31 October 2025** under N0001422D7001. The [retrieved award snapshot](evidence/N0001424FT171.json) is preserved with this report. This establishes a distinct funded research activity and a deliverable/ownership lead. It does not establish transfer of CP CoE staff, software or authority; do not attribute the task to CP CoE or call it a successor without the statement of work or deliverable.

### F. NATO, UK, Netherlands: the policy network continued internationally

The official NATO account of the September 2026 contact-group meeting identifies NATO, the UK and Netherlands as conveners, with military and civilian participation including ICRC, CIVIC and PAX. Subjects included exercises, critical infrastructure, coalition operations, maritime issues and national reporting/investigation practices. The group dates to 2022. It demonstrates an international professional network continuing the subject matter, not receipt of the U.S. centre's personnel, software or legal mandate. [NATO, 18 September 2026](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2026/09/18/nato-hosts-international-contact-group-on-civilian-harm-mitigation-and-response)

## 5. What remains genuinely unknown

1. **Current organisation and roster.** Who are the seven, which billets are filled, who directs the centre, and what is its present reporting chain? The latest congressional number does not answer these questions.
2. **Reboot decision.** Was the April proposal approved? If so, when did an Army-to-Joint-Staff transfer become effective, with which funds and authorised positions? No approval order was located.
3. **Software custody.** Who owns the Maven civilian-environment layer and AskSage-based assistant today? Are they experiment artifacts, deployed capabilities, or archived prototypes? Public product marketing for Maven or AskSage is insufficient evidence.
4. **Transferred versus diffuse duties.** Which CENTCOM positions retained CHMR responsibilities after reassignment, and at what level of effort? Generic legal review or targeting duties are not a demonstrated replacement for a dedicated civilian-environment team.
5. **DMP records and disposition.** What was accepted from QBE, what was stopped, and where do data, source code, test results and rights reside? The DMP's problems and funding cut do not answer disposition.
6. **Named contractor analysts.** The FY2024 report supplies functions but no names for its two technology analysts. No reliable public source in this research identifies their later assignments.

## 7. Source-access limitations and safeguards

- The **FY2024 report** and **May 2026 IG report** were visible through search-indexed official text, but full direct PDF retrieval returned errors/access failures. Their excerpts are labelled accordingly; no full-document reading is claimed.
- The **FY2025 CP CoE report** is linked on official and credible reporting pages at [this official URL](https://policy.war.gov/Portals/11/Documents/CHMR/TAB%20B%20-%20DoW%202025%20CP%20CoE%20Annual%20Report%206.15.26-508.pdf?ver=tEr0PWd4eLNHXneLp3UNUA%3D%3D). Direct retrieval failed. Alternate legitimate public links checked did not yield a readable complete copy. Detailed FY2025 technology claims remain attributable to reporting, not directly verified quotation from the PDF.
- The **1 October 2026 New York Times report**, [Military Leaders Will Examine Ways to Limit Civilian Deaths in War](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/01/us/politics/military-civilian-deaths-in-war.html), could not be read directly. Its existence/title is corroborated by [Brown's Costs of War news archive](https://costsofwar.watson.brown.edu/news/archive/2026). Leads about an October CENTCOM conference and a withdrawn reassignment memorandum are not treated here as verified events. The latest seven-person figure instead rests on the directly readable congressional letter.
- Public biographies and professional postings identify careers, not a complete chain of command. No private contact details, data-broker records, or personal-life information are included.
- Funding proposals, actual obligations, headcount targets, reported staffing and legal duties are separate evidence types. No dollar estimate here purports to be an AI-for-IHL total.

**Overall assessment:** No clean handover to a successor is established by the public evidence located. A legally surviving but greatly reduced centre, partially reassigned command personnel, extant technical experiments, and dispersed experts form a fragmented continuation. The crucial remaining test is whether that continuation has accountable owners, usable data, operating budgets and maintained tools, not whether the same subject appears in another institution's name or publications.
