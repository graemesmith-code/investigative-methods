# Public evidence of funding for IHL/LOAC-related military AI

Checked 16 October 2026. This is a targeted public-source check, not a complete spending census. Amounts below are not added together: a program budget, a total award, a subcontract increment, and a solicitation ceiling measure different things.

## Short answer

Yes. There is concrete public evidence of U.S. money for legal/ethical reasoning related to autonomous weapons, including an Air Force-funded model explicitly informed by the Laws of War and rules of engagement, its Army-funded training successor, and DARPA research on translating rules into machine-readable logic. But the evidence chiefly establishes research, training, and evaluation. It does not establish that an operational targeting system now implements, or reliably complies with, IHL.

The best specific current-period award is **Army → Perceptronics Solutions, TEELAWS, $1,899,999**, with a listed performance period ending 29 October 2026. It funds training and collateral-risk feedback, explicitly not engagement decisions. Its earlier **Air Force AFWERX CETLAWS** project is more directly about Laws-of-War-informed strike/no-strike decision support, documented in a 2024 research paper. **DARPA CODORD** is the strongest recent rules-to-logic program lead. **DARPA ASIMOV** was a funded IHL/ROE-related benchmarking program, but its current official page says it is complete.

## Interpretation and data cautions

- The strongest strict match is the CETLAWS research paper joined to its funding acknowledgement and the Army's TEELAWS successor record. These show actual funded attempts, but not accepted legal doctrine embodied in a deployed targeting system.
- ROE, ethical preferences, civilian-harm mitigation and IHL overlap but are not interchangeable. A software constraint can encode policy without covering distinction, proportionality, precautions, or context-sensitive legal judgment.
- No defensible aggregate dollar total for “IHL encoded in targeting algorithms” emerges from these records. A whole military-AI award or program budget should not be counted as an IHL-specific amount without work-package evidence.
- Commercial contract mirrors provide additional promising IDs: CODORD RTX BBN HR00112590088, APL task HR001125F0028; ASIMOV STR FA865125CB003 and Lockheed FA865125CB018. Amounts/periods were not promoted into the verified table because primary award transactions were not retrieved and some mirrors conflict or include generated summaries.
- The FY2026 and FY2027 official budget PDFs were available in indexed search extracts but returned access errors on full open. Budget values above therefore retain exact book/date/page labels. The FY2026 ASIMOV value of $23.205m was disambiguated against a [mirror of the government XML](https://github.com/handriss/defense-budget-justification-data/blob/main/fy2026/RDTE_Vol1_DARPA_MasterJustificationBook_PB_2026.xml), because the search-extracted page-break table misplaces the value. This is a request, not verified expenditure. No program-specific FY2027 successor amount was verified. Check original downloaded pages before use in a quantitative corpus.
- The Civilian Protection Center of Excellence is a separate institutional/civilian-harm track; this note does not establish its current staffing or whether a particular activity stopped. Its report and oversight record need to be read separately.
