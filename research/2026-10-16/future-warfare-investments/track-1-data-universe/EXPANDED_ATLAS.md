# Expanded public-data atlas: US international security investment beyond AI

Verified 16 October 2026. This research pass proposes **19 new source entries** and **seven refinements** to the initial 60-entry register. The consolidated register reconciles these with the parallel overseas-security inventory; these pass-level counts are not the final deduplicated totals. The accompanying `expanded_bulk_register.csv` marks each row `NEW` or `AMEND`, preserves links to existing source IDs, and records download scope, access conditions, file sizes and limitations. These are additions to a research universe, not 26 independent spending ledgers.

## What the larger universe contains

US investment in overseas security capabilities leaves records well beyond contracts containing “AI.” The relevant landscape includes basic research, laboratory operations, university teams, prototype solicitations, procurement, exports, facilities and the papers and patents produced along the way. Quantum sensing, navigation without GPS, autonomous aircraft and underwater systems, communications, electronic warfare, advanced materials, directed energy, resilient power and biotechnology appear under different program names and accounting categories.

The mission boundary remains important. A military sponsor, security program, relevant award or stated overseas mission can establish relevance. A quantum paper, foreign university affiliation or NIH award cannot do that alone. The sources below make those connections more discoverable without treating all US science as military investment. Nor do they make requests, obligations, expenditure, export permissions, property values and research outputs interchangeable.

### Research institutions and funding that ordinary contract searches miss

The strongest small download is an expanded use of **NCSES Federal Funds for R&D**. Its current release offers 99 Excel tables in one verified **1,167,352-byte ZIP**. Besides national totals, the collection includes obligations to non-US performers by country and agency, foreign R&D plant, defense RDT&E, and UARC/FFRDC categories. This is unusually direct evidence of the international research footprint, although it remains aggregated rather than project-level. [Federal Funds tables](https://ncses.nsf.gov/surveys/federal-funds-research-development/2024-2025/)

Two additional institutional datasets open another layer. **FFRDC public-use files** describe research expenditures by center and funding source. The verified FY2025 ZIP is only **29,809 bytes**, containing CSV and SAS files; its CSV has 862 long-form records. All FFRDCs are covered from 2001, with a narrower earlier series. **HERD** provides university research expenditures by source and field, including federal-agency detail. Its FY2024 annual ZIP is advertised at 7.1 MB. These are institutional expenditure surveys, not award registers. [FFRDC microdata](https://ncses.nsf.gov/explore-data/microdata/ffrdc-research-development), [HERD microdata](https://ncses.nsf.gov/explore-data/microdata/higher-education-research-development)

**Federal Science and Engineering Support** adds agency obligations to universities, consortia and nonprofit institutions. The FY2024 release has 37 tables and an advertised 1.1 MB Excel bundle. DoD’s **MURI award rosters** supply more specific project titles, sponsoring offices and university teams. They expose technical themes and collaborating institutions that disappear inside institutional totals, although announced multiyear funding may remain contingent on future appropriations. [Federal institutional support](https://ncses.nsf.gov/surveys/federal-support-survey/2024), [MURI announcement and roster](https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2953234/department-of-defense-announces-university-research-funding-awards/)

A less obvious source is the **Federal Audit Clearinghouse**. Its free CSV tables cover auditees, federal programs, reported expenditure, findings and pass-through relationships. Current downloads cover 2016 onward; separate legacy downloads cover 1998–2015. A sampled public CSV was live, with a full size of 561 MB, so annual slices are more manageable. The pass-through table identifies upstream entities; downstream subrecipient expenditure totals occur elsewhere. It is not a complete subaward network, and audit thresholds exclude many recipients. [Current audit downloads](https://www.fac.gov/data/download/current/), [historical files](https://www.fac.gov/data/download/historic/), [field definitions](https://www.fac.gov/data/download/current-dictionary/)

### Technical needs, older procurement and research provenance

**Grants.gov’s daily full XML extract** is a genuine free bulk opportunity: the 16 October 2026 ZIP returned a verified size of **78,292,394 bytes**. It collects opportunity metadata and synopses across agencies. **DSIP and service SBIR archives** add detailed defense research needs, often in downloadable topic documents. These describe desired capabilities and proposed funding, not completed awards. [Grants.gov extract](https://www.grants.gov/xml-extract), [DoD topics](https://www.dodsbirsttr.mil/topics-app/), [Navy historical solicitations](https://www.navysbir.com/previous.htm)

For optional context outside the September 2018-onward main window, **GovInfo’s Commerce Business Daily archive** provides annual ZIPs for 1996–2001. The 2001 archive was verified at **59,086,615 bytes**, with HTML notice files inside. It is a substantial historical procurement-text collection, but not a complete archive of solicitation attachments. GovInfo also offers free XML sitemaps and selected budget CSV/XLS files. Sitemap XML describes documents; it does not mean every budget book is available as structured financial XML. [CBD bulk archive](https://www.govinfo.gov/bulkdata/CBD), [budget formats](https://www.govinfo.gov/help/budget), [developer access](https://www.govinfo.gov/developers)

**OSTI and NASA NTRS** expose searchable technical-report metadata, abstracts, sponsor information, report identifiers and links to documents. Small live API responses were verified. An NTRS quantum record contained both a NASA funding number and an Air Force contract number, illustrating why actual funding fields are more useful than assuming relevance from the repository’s name. Public metadata and downloadable full text are different products, with different rights. **SERDP/ESTCP** supplies a further defense project library for installation resilience, energy, water, protective materials and related technology, although no bulk export was verified. [OSTI API](https://www.osti.gov/api/v1/docs), [NTRS API](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/openapi/), [SERDP/ESTCP projects](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects)

Government-interest patent statements can identify agencies and grant or contract numbers behind inventions. This strengthens the existing PatentsView entry, but its **2026 migration to USPTO’s Open Data Portal** matters: a tested legacy government-contract ZIP returned 403. Do not advertise old anonymous download URLs as working. Patents establish disclosed invention and rights relationships, not funding amounts or operational deployment. [Current USPTO PatentsView information](https://www.uspto.gov/ip-policy/economic-research/patentsview)

### The international transfer and physical-infrastructure footprint

**Census ECCN export files** are a particularly useful expansion. Official downloadable TXT tables associate export-control classification, commodity, destination, license type and value, with historical releases listed from 2009 through 2025. Payload retrieval was not verified. These describe controlled technology flows, not federal spending or confirmed military end users. **DDTC Section 655 reports** complement them with direct commercial defense-export authorizations and separately reported actual shipments. The latest located report is labeled 2025; preserve the original reporting-period definitions when extracting each table; its tables are PDF, not a verified clean bulk dataset. [Census ECCN files](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/data/ECCN.html), [DDTC report releases](https://www.pmddtc.state.gov/ddtc_public?cat=Report&id=ddtc_public_portal_news_and_events&timeframe=12month)

Facilities evidence deserves equal weight. DoD **Base Structure Report workbooks** and GSA’s **Federal Real Property Profile** describe the estate supporting capabilities. They have important exclusions: property stock is not new investment; DoD public detail is limited; national-security records can be withheld; and GSA’s State/USAID map shows US properties only. The FY2025 Base Structure Report URL was identified but returned 502. [DoD property library](https://www.acq.osd.mil/eie/imr/rpid/library.html), [GSA public property data](https://www.gsa.gov/policy-regulations/policy/real-property-policy-division-overview/asset-management/federal-real-property-profile/federal-real-property-public-data-set)

**Navy/NAVFAC environmental libraries** provide rich public documents about proposed construction, utilities, hardening and training infrastructure, including Yap and Guam. Technical appendices can be more revealing than short award descriptions. **EPA’s EIS database** has metadata since 1987 and electronic EIS documents since October 2012. These are document collections rather than verified bulk datasets; overseas environmental reviews also have different coverage from domestic NEPA reviews. Proposals and alternatives should not be counted as completed investment. [Yap documents](https://www.nepa.navy.mil/Current-Projects/Yap-EIS/Documents/), [NAVFAC Pacific library](https://pacific.navfac.navy.mil/About-Us/National-Environmental-Policy-Act-NEPA-Information/), [EPA EIS database](https://cdxapps.epa.gov/cdx-enepa-II/public/action/eis/search)

### Free scholarly graphs, with a clear metadata boundary

**Crossref** makes an annual public metadata file available free through Academic Torrents. The March 2026 release contains nearly 180 million records and is **208 GB compressed**. Deposited fields can include funder identifiers, award numbers, author affiliations and abstracts. A small free API sample confirmed funding fields. Completeness and accuracy vary, and this is not article full text. Most metadata is freely reusable, while abstracts can retain copyright. The AWS mirror is **requester-pays**, and monthly snapshots are paid, so neither belongs under an unqualified “free download” label. [2026 public file](https://www.crossref.org/blog/2026-public-data-file-now-available/), [access routes](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/bulk-downloads/)

**OpenAlex** provides anonymous, CC0 quarterly metadata snapshots in compressed JSONL or Parquet. The October 14 funder manifest was verified: **41,799 records totaling 11,155,921 compressed bytes**. A 404-byte shard downloaded without authentication. The complete graph is much larger: September documentation puts JSONL around 745 GB. Works can carry funding links, affiliations and abstract indexes; award records can include source-provided identifiers, dates and nullable amounts. These require provenance checks and are not independent official spending totals. The free snapshot is distinct from paid full-text/content services. [Snapshot access](https://help.openalex.org/access/snapshot/), [award fields](https://help.openalex.org/data/awards/)

Two small reference layers help describe this universe: the CC0 **Open Funder Registry** and the federal **PSC classification workbook**, verified at 462,762 bytes. Neither contains investment amounts. NSF and NIH research exports remain contextual: NIH’s project ZIP endpoint worked, while NSF’s current JSON-download page explicitly displayed **“No export files available at this time.”** [Funder registry](https://www.crossref.org/services/funder-registry/index.html), [PSC files](https://www.acquisition.gov/psc-manual), [NIH ExPORTER](https://reporter.nih.gov/exporter?index=1&sid=0), [NSF download status](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/download-awards/)

| 2 | FFRDC / HERD / institutional support | Annual CSV/SAS and XLSX bundles | FFRDC checked; other exact links advertised; institutional totals |
| 3 | Federal Audit Clearinghouse | Current CSV tables and historical ZIPs | Live CSV sampled; use year slices rather than large full tables |
| 4 | Grants.gov | Daily full opportunity XML ZIP, 78.3 MB | Header/ZIP sample checked; opportunities, not awards |
| 5 | Census ECCN exports | Historical TXT by period | Official files listed; payload still unverified |
| 6 | OSTI / NTRS | Free programmatic metadata | Small records checked; not a universal full-text download |

| 9 | PSC / Open Funder Registry | Small classification/identity files | PSC payload checked; OFR official free-download offer verified |

Commerce Business Daily is a separate optional historical source: its checked 1996–2001 holdings fall outside the September 2018-onward collection target.

**Status discipline:** `FREE_BULK_VERIFIED` means an actual payload or bounded payload sample worked. `FREE_BULK_ADVERTISED` means the official publisher documents the download, without a successful payload test here. `FREE_PROGRAMMATIC_METADATA` is a collection API, not a ready-made archive. `FREE_DOCUMENT_COLLECTION` is public document access without verified bulk export. Failed, migrated and temporarily unavailable routes retain explicit separate flags. Sizes describe full advertised files or HTTP headers unless a complete small file was downloaded. No paid retrieval or large corpus download was performed.
