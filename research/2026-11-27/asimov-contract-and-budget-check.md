# How to tell whether a federal research program ended early

## Finding from the ASIMOV records, 27 November 2026

The Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) described ASIMOV as a two-year research effort. It bought one year of work from its main research teams and reserved the right to buy another. Its own budget request made the second stage more than a theoretical possibility. In June 2025 it requested money to finish the benchmark and check simulated scenes against field data. In April 2026 it reported an initial framework. The seven performer awards show no exercise of a second-stage option. The reason for stopping, the date of the decision and the disposition of the planned field validation remain unknown. [Original solicitation, sections 1.1.1–1.1.2](https://research-authority.tau.ac.il/sites/resauth.tau.ac.il/files/DARPA_ASIMOV_HR001124S0011.pdf) · [June 2025 budget, printed p. 273](https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/Documents/defbudget/FY2026/budget_justification/pdfs/03_RDT_and_E/RDTE_Vol1_DARPA_MasterJustificationBook_PB_2026.pdf#page=296) · [April 2026 budget, printed p. 310](https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/Documents/defbudget/FY2027/budget_justification/pdfs/03_RDT_and_E/RDTE_Vol1_DARPA_MasterJustificationBook_PB_2027.pdf#page=334)

A budget request shows what the agency wanted to do. A contract shows how much it committed to a particular team. A final report and its acceptance can show what that team delivered. Those records can all be accurate even when they tell different parts of the story. A contractor can finish every task it was paid to do while the agency ends the larger effort short of its ambitions.

## Technical annex

**Scope.** The January 2024 solicitation called for an initial 12 months of feasibility work and a separately priced 12-month option for selected performers to refine the benchmarks. The February amendments retained that structure. The clock for contract deliverables started at award. DARPA announced seven performers in December 2024. Searching the Federal Procurement Data System by solicitation HR001124S0011 on 27 November 2026 returned seven award identifiers and 43 actions. Other contracts or government in-house work could carry related research. USAspending and the transaction feeds draw on the same underlying procurement reporting. The seven originals, 25 funding actions, ten within-scope agreements and one administrative modification total 43. No entry has a negative obligation. No modification is coded as an option exercise, termination, legal cancellation or closeout. Obligations and base-plus-exercised values both total $20,495,054. **Dates that change the interpretation.** Kitware's first and last actions carry the same base end date. CoVar's P00005 extended the current end date from 14 September to 27 October 2025 without adding money. Saab's P00006 moved the current end from 31 October 2025 to 30 April 2026. P00007 then set both the current and ultimate end at 31 March 2026. Both were zero-dollar within-scope agreements. The last obligation, the last day someone worked, the end of the performance period and the first public completion notice can each fall on a different date. The archived DARPA page lacks the completion banner on 16 February 2026 and carries it on 17 June. **Budget cross-check.** The June 2025 fiscal 2026 book distinguishes development of an initial framework in fiscal 2025 (printed p. 332) from completion and field validation in fiscal 2026 (p. 273). The request for the latter year was $23.205 million under program element 0603467E, project DAT-01. The older project, 0603766E/NET-01, already showed a dash for fiscal 2026 because the agency proposed moving the funding. In April 2026 the ASIMOV entry under the older account carried $22.789 million for fiscal 2025 and dashes for fiscal 2026 and 2027. An entire-book search found no separate named ASIMOV entry under the receiving account. The fiscal 2026 base for reprogramming shows that the receiving account's appropriation was $115.365 million below the request, including a $97.545 million undistributed reduction. The public table does not allocate a cut to ASIMOV. The $23.205 million requested for the program, $18.980 million in potential contract value and account-wide reduction cannot be substituted for one another. **Publication cross-check.** Duality named REBAR as Kitware's ASIMOV effort in May 2025. The public preprint appeared on 18 May 2026. RTX's MAGNET paper appeared on 5 July and identifies the RTX contract, which ended the previous November with no priced option difference. The Lockheed Martin team manuscript identifies CB018, and CoVar described reuse of its ASIMOV software in a separate DARPA award. These are evidence of research outputs and reuse. Publication alone does not tell us who paid for the work that month or whether the agency accepted a completed second-stage benchmark.
