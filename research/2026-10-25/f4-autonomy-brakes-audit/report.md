# The brakes audit: ASIMOV's completion does not establish that safeguards disappeared

**Research cutoff: 25 October 2026. Complete consumer archive sweep; bounded live and primary-source verification.**

**Verdict, as of 25 October 2026: do not publish the proposed universal claim.** The public record contains funded normative evaluation and instruction outside ASIMOV, research intended to support legal/rule compliance, explicit vessel-distinction work, a demonstrated human-constraint mission-planning system, and continuing institutional oversight. These findings do not establish a comprehensive, effective or universally deployed legal governor. They do disprove the inference that ASIMOV's completion, by itself, ended meaningful public technical work on autonomous-weapons safeguards.

The clearest weapons-linked normative training counterexample is **TEELAWS**, with $1,899,998.92 obligated. In the selected primary-A case set, TEELAWS and CODORD support have disclosed periods overlapping the cutoff and **$4,381,839.92 cumulative obligations** in the selected standard-contract primary-A view. Their functions are training/scoring and normative rule reasoning; none is established here as a universally deployed legal governor. Navy vessel-classification work contributes to distinction but is counted under C with secondary A tags. HAMLET, EpochGeo's civilian-harm context tool and other broader research remain in a separate view until weapons-specific application/integration is verified. These views must not be combined into an allocated safeguards budget. [TEELAWS](https://www.sbir.gov/awards/215327), [Army implementation](https://www.army.mil/article/291926/cadets_work_with_software_using_lethal_autonomous_weapons_systems_that_analyzes_tactical_decisions_provides_ethical_score), [CODORD](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/codord), [EpochGeo](https://www.sbir.gov/awards/209584).

There is also a defensible story of weakened capacity and opaque implementation. The Inspector General documented erosion of civilian-harm work; GAO documented gaps in Responsible AI staffing and collaboration. The evidence supports investigating those failures alongside surviving safeguards, rather than claiming an institutional vacuum. [IG](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2026-05/DOWIG-2026-084_Redacted%20SECURE.pdf), [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-107837).

**An important blind spot applies throughout:** FPDS and USAspending disclose a 90-day delay for DoD contract and related subcontract data; visible assistance actions must be assessed separately. At this cutoff, much of the newest quarter can be invisible. “No continuation verified” cannot mean “no continuation exists.” [FPDS notice](https://www.fpds.gov/common/html/public_welcome_text.html), [USAspending notice](https://www.usaspending.gov/download_center/custom_account_data).

## 1. What exists, what ended, and what the dollars mean

The categories overlap. A is explicit normative/legal/civilian-harm testing or enabling work; B concerns human authorization, constraints and abort; C concerns reliability, robustness and assurance; D concerns governance and institutional oversight. A component can support A without solving every legal judgment. A C tool can implement a lawful restriction when the relevant rule, tests and response are specified. Reliability alone does not establish legality, and technical reliability is not legally irrelevant.

ASIMOV was a research/benchmark program, not demonstrated here to be an acquisition **program of record**. An active research award, a public tool, a prototype, training use, an operational deployment and an acquisition program of record are separate statuses. Applying the acquisition denominator only to possible replacements would create a false disappearance.
