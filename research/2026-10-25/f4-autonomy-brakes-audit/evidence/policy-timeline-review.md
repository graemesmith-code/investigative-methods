# Policy timeline and institutional safeguards: primary-source review

Reviewed 25 October 2026. No paid API, Exa, source outreach, or email was used for this review. This note distinguishes an issued document, an internal submission, public availability, implementation, and effectiveness. They are different propositions. Department of War is the administration's secondary title; statutory Department of Defense authorities continue.

## Findings that change the hypothesis

The proposed claim that the government prohibited all vendor safety guardrails is too broad. NSPM-11 restricts unapproved disabling or modification of mission systems; its text also calls for lawful operation and assurance. OpenAI's public description of its agreement says that it retained technical safeguards. That statement is a vendor account, not independent inspection of the entire contract or operational enforcement.

The 120-day TEVV provision is an internal submission requirement, not an unconditional public-publication deadline. Public silence does not prove noncompliance. The 90-day directive-update requirement uses different language and must be audited separately. Continuing Responsible AI infrastructure exists alongside documented implementation and staffing weaknesses; a policy remaining online does not establish that its institution retains capacity.

## Litigation: two different statutory routes

The [27 August Northern District of California order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-3_26-cv-01996/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-3_26-cv-01996-5.pdf), case 26-cv-01996-RFL, granted Anthropic substantial relief concerning the broad directives and 10 U.S.C. §3252 designation. Its record describes the January 9 mandate and negotiations. This is stronger than a party allegation but is not the latest decision across every statutory route.

The [25 September D.C. Circuit opinion](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2026/09/26-1049-2194984.pdf), No. 26-1049 consolidated with 26-1162, denied Anthropic's petitions challenging exclusion under FASCSA, 41 U.S.C. §4713. Judges Katsas and Rao formed the majority; Judge Henderson dissented. The majority upheld this supply-chain action and rejected the statutory/constitutional challenges before it; the dissent rejected that reading of FASCSA. This parallel review must not be described as simply reversing every aspect of the district judgment. The [district final-relief order, document 251](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.465515/gov.uscourts.cand.465515.251.0_2.pdf), permanently enjoins the defined challenged actions and vacates the §3252 designation and broad contractor boycott; it expressly does not require the government to purchase Anthropic services. [Document 252](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.465515/gov.uscourts.cand.465515.252.0_3.pdf) enters judgment and directs closure of the file. These are final district relief, not merely the March preliminary injunction.

The [September 25 appellate judgment](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cadc.42923/gov.uscourts.cadc.42923.01208891903.0.pdf) denies the petitions. A [separate same-day order](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cadc.42923/gov.uscourts.cadc.42923.01208891915.0.pdf) withholds the mandate until seven days after disposition of timely rehearing requests; this is not a blanket stay of all underlying agency actions. The [October 15 order](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cadc.42923/gov.uscourts.cadc.42923.01208898343.0.pdf) requests the government's response to a panel-rehearing petition within 15 days (October 30); it does not grant rehearing. Thus the located October record closes part of the earlier status gap, while a completed rehearing disposition or Supreme Court outcome was not established. Avoid a blanket claim that Anthropic won or lost the entire dispute.

## CHMR: documentary evidence of institutional erosion

The May 13, 2026 [Inspector General report DOWIG-2026-084](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2026-05/DOWIG-2026-084_Redacted%20SECURE.pdf) is accessible through the federal Oversight.gov mirror. It finds incomplete implementation of all 11 CHMR-AP objectives by FY2025 end; initial progress followed by lost/reassigned staff, discontinued platform funding and halted steering meetings. Printed page 12 says proposed reductions had not received a formally announced Secretary decision as of April 2026, while components had nevertheless stopped activities. It distinguishes operational erosion from formal cancellation and identifies continuing statutory requirements. Recommendations were unresolved at issuance; that is not automatically their October status. The report does not establish that CHMR staff were specifically excluded from DAWG, Agincourt or AUTOWARCOM.

The [July 5 senators' letter, publicized July 6](https://www.warren.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Letter-from-Senator-Warren-Colleagues-to-Secretary-Hegseth-on-CHMR-AP-IG-Report.pdf) is a congressional challenge to the department, not an independent adjudication of unlawful conduct. The original IG findings should carry the reporting. A FY2025 CP CoE annual report (filename includes June15,2026; cover/publication date unverified) is linked from the [official CHMR index](https://policy.defense.gov/Offices/Civilian-Harm-Mitigation-and-Response/); its body was not successfully retrieved here, so no claim about its substantive conclusions is made.

## Surviving RAI apparatus and its limits

[GAO-27-107837, released October 14](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-27-107837.pdf), documents RAI Toolkit use and integration into Maven Smart System in December 2025. Its review of 10 selected use cases is not a department-wide effectiveness census. GAO reports a working-council lapse from late 2024 to August 2025, lack of a charter, and three of ten RAI Division positions filled **as of February 2026**. These support a finding of continuing work with substantial capacity/governance weaknesses; February staffing must not be presented as an independently measured October headcount. Neither toolkit integration nor administrative review proves automatic enforcement of LOAC in a weapon.

## FY2026 NDAA section 1533: an additional statutory assessment track

The [enacted Public Law 119-60, section 1533](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ60/html/PLAW-119publ60.htm), 139 Stat. 1159–1162, establishes a CDAO-led cross-functional AI model assessment and oversight team. Its required assessment framework includes ethical-principles compliance alongside performance, testing, documentation and security. The exact milestones differ: establish the team by June 1, 2026; designate Department organizations as functional leads for AI applications by January 1, 2027; finish the framework/governance structure by June 1, 2027; assess defined major AI systems by January 1, 2028. Congress must receive briefings after the milestones. The section is identified as a 10 U.S.C. §4001 note.

This is a Category D statutory safeguard and a potential route to technical assessment. Enactment and deadlines do not prove that the team was actually constituted, briefings occurred, or a weapons-LOAC test was implemented. No allocable obligations or implementation evidence were established here. Do not confuse the June 2026 team deadline with the June 2027 completed-framework deadline or NSPM-11's separate October 2026 TEVV submission.

## Acceleration organizations and current status

The [July 1 reorganization announcement](https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4531728/department-of-war-establishes-direct-reporting-portfolio-manager-for-unmanned-s/) places DAWG among activities subject to a direct-report unmanned-systems portfolio. The [October 21 Agincourt release](https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4630269/department-of-wars-project-agincourt-accelerates-autonomous-warfare-command-to/) and [October 23 official account](https://www.war.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/4632827/project-agincourt-leaders-reach-out-to-industry-with-path-forward/) identify Owen West and Max Strasiser as Agincourt leaders. The latter says AUTOWARCOM is expected operational October 1, 2027. This substantiates acceleration and a command-building process, not an already fully operational command as of October 25, 2026. Separate Southcom's regional autonomous-warfare initiative from the proposed department-wide combatant command. None of the inspected releases establishes the full organization chart, all legal-review arrangements, or affirmative exclusion of civilian-harm expertise.
