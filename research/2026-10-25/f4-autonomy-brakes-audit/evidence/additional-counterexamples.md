## HAMLET and FY2026 MURI ethical reasoning

The main audit’s independent award research identifies UT Austin grant **FA9550261B207**, [USAspending full key](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_FA9550261B207_097), with $2,999,976 obligations and a 1 September 2026–31 August 2031 period. The provided award description names HAMLET—Humans Autonomous Machines Learning to Effectively Team—and design/TEV&V for adaptive ethical reasoning. These award facts should be reconciled in the main grant register; this reviewer’s direct web retrieval returned only the USAspending application shell, not an independently readable award abstract.

There is substantive primary technical context beyond that title. The [official FY2026 MURI solicitation](https://www.onr.navy.mil/assets/2025-03/N0001425SF002.pdf), printed pp56–58, Topic 2, **Ethical Constructs and Adaptive Learning Systems**, seeks methods to incorporate, interpret and evaluate ethical constructs in adaptive systems for DoD human-machine teaming. It calls for ethical metrics, normative procedures, context adaptation and implementation on compact autonomous platforms. Its references include Arkin’s lethal-action ethical governor and an IHL text. It also warns that validation may not be fully completed within proposed research. The 2025 topic chiefs are Brett Pokines and Laura Steckman (AFOSR) and Gregory Ruark (ARO); those are dated solicitation affiliations.

**Classification:** A-enabling research primary; B/C secondary possible. This is a strong challenge to an absence-of-funded-normative-research claim if the main register confirms identity and scope. A topic-level solicitation does not establish HAMLET’s precise work plan, acceptance tests, civilian-harm scenarios, LOAC validation or operational deployment. Searches did not resolve a UT Austin award-specific technical announcement or abstract; no particular PI, consortium or test result is invented. Do not count the solicitation’s average annual award ceiling as obligations, or call this basic research a program of record.

## Switchblade 600: operator selection and strike wave-off

[Army/DVIDS coverage dated 25 September 2025](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/549283/1st-cavalry-division-conducts-first-switchblade-600-live-fire-fort-hood), posted 26 September, reports a 15 September live-fire exercise. It describes operators identifying, selecting and engaging targets and retaining the ability to wave off a strike during flight. This is evidence of a reported capability on a system being fielded and exercised, rather than only an ethical-policy promise. The article’s collateral-harm benefit is an attributed Army claim, not a civilian-harm evaluation independently established here. It does not document a wave-off stress test or behavior after communications loss.

The manufacturer’s [Block 2 datasheet, version 250923](https://www.avinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2025_Switchblade-600b2_Datasheet.pdf), separately advertises wave-off/recommit capability. The PDF’s internal 2025 version and its 2026 hosting path are different dates; neither is silently substituted for a release date. Its general capability information supports a B classification but does not prove the feature succeeds under every battlefield condition.

The [26 February 2026 company release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260226483479/en/AV-Receives-%24186-Million-U.S.-Army-Delivery-Order-for-Next-Generation-Switchblade-Systems) announces a $186 million mixed Switchblade order under an existing LUS IDIQ. The main audit associates that vehicle with W91CRB24D0011; verify the particular order before counting any financial transaction. **Do not label the whole order or vehicle as safeguard spending.** A munition contains many functions, and the public sources do not allocate costs to its human-control features.

**Classification:** B implemented/reported capability; A contribution possible through operator restraint, without explicit normative validation established. Autonomous navigation or recognition and human attack authority may coexist. This defeats “no technical brakes of any kind”; it does not establish an ASIMOV-equivalent benchmark or a fully autonomous weapon certified against LOAC.

## Hunter WOLF / wireless CROWS: demonstration and human lethal control

The [Army article of 21 August 2026](https://www.army.mil/article/294796/armaments_center_partners_with_industry_to_explore_concepts_for_integrating_lethal_payloads_to_unmanned_ground_vehicles) reports a CRADA with HDT Robotics, doing business as BLADE, and delivery of an armed remotely operated ground vehicle for a technical demonstration. It reports April experimentation by the 101st Airborne Division and describes an effort to add autonomy while retaining positive human control of lethal decisions. The article distinguishes current operator workload from a desired supervisory role. Andrew Wilson and Josiah Fay are named in their dated Armaments Center roles.

**Classification:** B demonstration and development; C secondary. Actual remote weapon control is a concrete capability. Future supervisory autonomy is a development direction, not an achieved universal veto guarantee. The source gives no separable safeguard appropriation and does not establish legal-compliance benchmarking or an ASIMOV follow-on. A CRADA is not by itself proof of a federal procurement payment. Keep this demonstration separate from a program-of-record funding claim.
