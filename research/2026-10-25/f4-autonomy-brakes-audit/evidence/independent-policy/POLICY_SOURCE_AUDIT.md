# F4 policy-premises audit — source memo

**Research cutoff:** 25 October 2026. **Scope:** Stage 3 policy and institutional premises; not an audit of ASIMOV transactions or a complete inventory of funded technical programs. Prepared for the coordinating investigator; no outreach or repository publication performed by this subagent. **Paid Exa requests: zero.**

## Executive finding

The policy-side version of the proposed story needs substantial qualification. Acceleration and weakened civilian-harm capacity are documented. Abolition of all safeguards, a blanket prohibition on every vendor safety mechanism, an already-operational four-star AutoWarCom, and a publicly missed 3 October TEVV issuance deadline are **not established**. The TEVV deadline is misstated in the hypothesis. Current oversight statutes and October 2026 GAO findings provide affirmative counterevidence to an institutional vacuum. An uninspected FY2025 Civilian Protection Center report is a high-priority potential technical civilian-harm counterexample.

No located public document in this pass establishes that a revised 3000.09 or the specific NSPM-11 TEVV submission was issued/completed by the cutoff. That is a bounded retrieval finding, not proof of nonperformance, especially where submission may be classified or publication conditional.

## 1. NSPM-11: what was actually ordered

**Primary:** [White House, 5 June 2026](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/national-security-presidential-memorandum-nspm-11/); [authenticated presidential-document PDF, DCPD-202600387](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202600387/pdf/DCPD-202600387.pdf). Both retrieved; PDF retained.

| Provision | Required act and owner | Computed deadline | Publication implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| §3(a) | Secretary of War issues updated DoDD 3000.09; annual review thereafter | 3 September 2026, 90 days | Issuance required; our search did not locate successor text |
| §5(f) | Secretary of War **through the NSA Director**, and DNI, submit standardized national-security AI TEVV methodologies to APST and National Cyber Director for review | 3 October 2026, 120 days | Review precedes publication **where appropriate**; methodologies at appropriate classification levels |
| §5(e) | DNI, Secretary of War and NSA Director develop joint risk-management/assurance strategy and baseline security guidance, with specified coordination, for White House review | 3 October 2026 | Not a requirement that publication occur by that date |

Section 2(c) addresses unauthorized vendor/adversary prevention, disabling, degradation or material modification of mission AI; it also expressly requires reliability, controllability and rigorous security/functionality TEVV. Section 3(f) replaces NSM-25 and associated guidance. This is not an express ban on all safety techniques. The national-security TEVV provision is not accurately reduced to a department-only public framework deadline.

**Editorial correction:** Replace “missed the October 3 deadline to publish TEVV” with “we could not locate the methodologies or confirmation of their submission; the order required submission for review by October 3, not necessarily public release.” Ask the named offices for submission dates, document identifiers, classifications, publication decisions and a releasable description. Do not treat a zero-result search as the answer to those questions.

Searches combined NSPM-11, 3000.09, standardized methodologies, TEVV, September/October 2026 and relevant terms across White House, NSA, DNI, War/Defense, ESD issuances, DOT&E, Federal Register and congressional records; broad searches also surfaced independent deadline trackers. No identified successor document was confirmed. The ESD index search result still displayed the 25 January 2023 directive, but live direct retrieval returned HTTP 403. Nonpublic completion, indexing lag and inaccessible hosts remain alternatives to missed deadlines.

## 2. Existing autonomy oversight was not erased from law

The [official 2023 directive source](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodd/300009p.PDF) was identified but its direct download was blocked. The independently accessible [Congressional Research Service page](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11150), whose retrieved text was **updated 2 January 2025**, describes human judgment, realistic testing, senior approval before development/fielding for covered systems, an urgent-need waiver and a supporting Autonomous Weapon System Working Group. It also describes Congress's 30-day notification requirement for modifications. The page is background, not evidence of October 2026 operational compliance. An indexed March 2026 CRS mirror exists, but the official page returned an older version; do not silently attribute that retrieved page to March.

**New statutory counterevidence:** [10 U.S.C. §130g, official code current through Public Law 119-120, 7 October 2026](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t10/s130g-1/), added by FY2026 NDAA §1061 on 18 December 2025, requires notification to defense committees within 30 days of **any** waiver under 3000.09 or a successor. Notifications must identify rationale, system/technology and expected duration; unclassified form may include a classified annex. This is Category D, not a technical program or proof notices were sent. Ask Congress for receipt dates and unclassified copies before alleging that waivers lack oversight.

**Classification discipline:** The directive and review bodies are D. A specific implemented authorization/abort control could be B. Neither a policy mandate nor a software-capability claim proves the control exists on a fielded system. Appropriate human judgment is not synonymous with a human approving every individual target engagement.

## 3. January procurement instruction and commercial guardrails

### The actual January document

The Secretary's memorandum is stamped **9 January 2026**, although the government PDF path is dated 12 January. The full six-page signed memo was read from an [IDA-hosted copy](https://www.dmi-ida.org/download-pdf/pdf/ARTIFICIAL-INTELLIGENCE-STRATEGY-FOR-THE-DEPARTMENT-OF-WAR.pdf), linked by [IDA's record](https://www.dmi-ida.org/knowledge-base-detail/AI-Strategy-DOW-Memo). Government host returned 403. Page 5 directs CDAO to establish objectivity procurement benchmarks within 90 days and USD(A&S) to incorporate standard “any lawful use” wording into AI-services procurement contracts within 180 days. Dates computed from signature: **9 April** and **8 July 2026**. It seeks models free of usage-policy constraints limiting lawful military applications and alignment of existing AI guidance. It separately targets barriers including TEVV and certification.

**Not established:** We did not locate an authoritative department-wide completed clause/modification register showing implementation in every contract by 8 July. An instruction to alter contracts is not evidence all alterations occurred. Do not confuse draft GSA-wide procurement clauses with executed DoW contract terms.

### Concrete counterexample to a blanket ban

[OpenAI's agreement explanation](https://openai.com/index/our-agreement-with-the-department-of-war/), published 28 February and updated 2 March 2026, says the 27 February deal retains OpenAI's safety stack, cleared personnel and cloud-only deployment. Its published contract excerpt ties restrictions on independent autonomous-weapons direction to cases in which law, regulation or policy requires human control. The March update adds domestic-surveillance restrictions and says intelligence-agency access requires another agreement. The company describes safeguards layered across law, contract and architecture.

**Caveat:** This is a contracting party's public explanation and excerpt, not an independently inspected complete signed contract or a compliance audit. Its broad description of an autonomous-weapons red line is stronger than the conditional wording of the excerpt. It nevertheless directly contradicts claiming that all vendor-side safety engineering was categorically forbidden. The narrower defensible account is a government push against vendor-imposed restrictions on uses the government considers lawful, with negotiated and contested boundaries.

### May classified-network agreements

The [DoW announcement, 1 May 2026](https://www.war.gov/News/releases/release/Article/4475177/classified-networks-ai-agreements/) names SpaceX, OpenAI, Google, NVIDIA, Reflection, Microsoft, AWS and Oracle, covering lawful operational use on IL6/IL7 environments. The indexed official text was read; direct hosting returned 403. [Oracle's same-day first-party announcement](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/the-department-of-war-announces-agreement-with-oracle-to-deploy-ai-capabilities-on-classified-cloud-networks-2026-05-01/) independently confirms its agreement for classified networks and lawful use, emphasizing infrastructure, decision support and model choice.

No complete contracts or award dollars were disclosed in the inspected releases. Classified-network eligibility is **not** autonomous-weapon release authority, proof of legal compliance, a safety certification, or evidence every named company accepted identical clauses. Ask for final signed terms, any red-line schedules, safety-stack ownership and subsequent modifications.

## 5. CHMR/CP CoE: severely impaired, not legally abolished

**High-weight primary evidence:** [DoW IG report DOWIG-2026-084, public redacted copy](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2026-05/DOWIG-2026-084_Redacted%20SECURE.pdf). The report itself is dated **13 May 2026**; online metadata varies between May 12/13/14. Use the internal date, with publication metadata noted separately. It found progress before February 2025 but no CHMR-AP objective fully implemented by the end of FY2025. Funding for the data-management platform stopped; steering meetings ceased; personnel were lost/reassigned, including center leadership. Management's proposed changes had no announced final Secretary decision at the audit's account. Two recommendations remained unresolved. IG warned of possible noncompliance with federally required civilian-harm policy and recorded commanders' concerns for readiness and operational risk.

[10 U.S.C. §184, official current code](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t10/s184/) still requires the center's operation and duties, including tools, training, research and reporting. A 2025 repeal proposal and a budget-request reduction are not an enacted repeal or an obligations ledger. The FY2026 [OSD OP-5 request](https://comptroller.defense.gov/Portals/45/Documents/defbudget/FY2026/budget_justification/pdfs/01_Operation_and_Maintenance/O_M_VOL_1_PART_1/OSD_OP-5.pdf), indexed but inaccessible directly, describes a **$9.567 million** CHMR reduction. Do not combine it with neighboring program reductions or label it verified spending removed.

## 6. Current CDAO Responsible AI evidence: a weakened but surviving apparatus

[GAO-27-107837, 14 October 2026](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-107837), [full report](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-27-107837.pdf), provides unusually current independent primary evidence. Its audit ran September 2024–October 2026, but individual observations have earlier dates. The RAI toolkit was integrated into **Maven Smart System in December 2025** and used by Army Special Operations Command, Navy, Army and NGA. GAO describes risk-assessment tools, an LLM benchmark/test harness and human-machine red teaming; that does not establish each tests LOAC.

The RAI Working Council did not meet from late 2024 until August 2025, then resumed, but lacked a charter. Only **three of ten** RAI Division positions were filled **as of February 2026**; do not relabel that number October staffing. GAO recommends formalizing collaboration and workforce planning. Its ten selected use cases are nongeneralizable, not the full defense-AI universe.

**Classification:** D for the toolkit/governance as the user's taxonomy specifies, with concrete software capabilities separately examined for A/B/C. This rebuts “no oversight remains” without establishing an ASIMOV successor or effective fielded normative restraint. Ask for Maven integration records, risk flags, corrective actions, named funding lines and independently evaluated outcomes.

- [Army's 15 October 2026 official report](https://www.army.mil/article/296131/hegseth_autonomous_warfare_command_has_always_been_end_state) describes the emerging national command and Agincourt under Owen West. It supports acceleration and planned organizational change, not proof that every proposed command is already operational.

Its two missions are to establish a four-star functional combatant command and continue the unmanned-systems direct-reporting portfolio manager's work. **AutoWarCom is expected operational 1 October 2027.** The performance framing emphasizes fielding speed, exercises, combat outcomes and scaling. No inspected text establishes CHMR's exclusion from all internal governance.

Primary documents identified but direct retrieval blocked: [30 September founding pathway memorandum](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Sep/30/2004009304/-1/-1/1/PROJECT-AGINCOURT-PATHWAY-TO-THE-AUTONOMOUS-WARFARE-COMMAND.PDF); [21 October industry letter](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Oct/21/2004017243/-1/-1/1/PROJECT-AGINCOURT.PDF); [22/23 October release](https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4630269/department-of-wars-project-agincourt-accelerates-autonomous-warfare-command-to/).
