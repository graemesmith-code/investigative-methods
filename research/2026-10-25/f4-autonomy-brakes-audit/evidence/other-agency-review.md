# Other agencies and FFRDCs: targeted primary-source review

Reviewed 25 October 2026. This review used free primary-web retrieval and direct HTTP requests; no Exa, other paid service, or outreach. It is a targeted check of the named organizations and promising results from the coordinator’s discovery register, not a complete census of their task orders, classified work, or internal projects. No matched October 2026 award funding for a new ASIMOV-equivalent benchmark was established here. That is a bounded research result, not proof that none exists.

## What the inspected record establishes

| Organization / initiative | Evidence and date | Classification and limits |
|---|---|---|
| NIST TEVV-Athlon | [Official framework page](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/ai-research/tevv-athlon-framework-evaluating-ai-systems): August 7, 2026 announcement; initial public draft NIST AI 200-2; comments through October 6. A customizable, four-stage assessment method applies across multiple AI technologies and organizational objectives. | D methodological guidance, with C evaluation applications. This is contemporaneous TEVV work, but the inspected page establishes neither a weapons-specific LOAC benchmark nor completion of NSPM-11’s separate national-security submission. October 6 is the draft’s comment deadline. The page is not a procurement record or evidence of funded weapons adoption. |
| NIST agent standards / monitoring | [February 17, 2026 initiative](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/announcing-ai-agent-standards-initiative-interoperable-and-secure) concerns interoperable agents, security and identity; [March 6 monitoring report](https://www.nist.gov/publications/challenges-monitoring-deployed-ai-systems-center-ai-standards-and-innovation), NIST AI 800-4, identifies gaps in monitoring methods. | C/D. These can support trustworthy deployment, but no explicit autonomous-weapons legal test criterion was established. The February document uses **Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI)**; this dated naming should replace an unsupported assumption that every 2026 activity belongs to the former AISI. Do not infer the latest name solely from that February document. |
| IARPA TrojAI | [Official program page](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/trojai) describes detecting backdoored models, multi-domain evaluation, public challenge results, and a final report. T&E partners listed include NIST, SEI, Sandia and Johns Hopkins APL. | C. The soldier/civilian classifier example illustrates malicious model manipulation; it is not evidence that the program validated legal combatant classification or proportionality. The solicitation is marked closed. Neither that label nor a live page establishes active October funding. The final-report web reader failed on a 14,873,556-byte content-length limit; its full body was not inspected in this subreview. |
| MIT Lincoln Laboratory Responsible AI Toolbox | [Official project description](https://www.ll.mit.edu/r-d/projects/responsible-ai-toolbox) identifies ASERT and RAIDEN work on modular tools for evaluation, robustness, explainability and reproducible experiments. | C/D. The description says broader ethical topics are planned; it does not establish an active weapons-LOAC benchmark, dedicated award dollars, or system acceptance requirements. Broader “responsible AI” wording should not be silently counted as A enforcement. |
| CNA ethical autonomy checklist | [January 24, 2022 primary announcement](https://www.cna.org/our-media/press-releases/2022/01-24) describes 565 engineering/command questions, including protected symbols, lost-command abort behavior, human authorization, LOAC training and inappropriate behaviors under red-team testing. It calls this an independent research effort. | D primary, A/B-relevant requirements and evaluation design. This is substantial historical normative guidance outside ASIMOV, but not proof of an actively funded October 2026 test program, mandatory compliance or implemented onboard restraint. The announcement was inspected; this subreview did not inspect all 565 questions. |
| CNA civilian-harm analysis | [Leveraging AI to Mitigate Civilian Harm](https://www.cna.org/analyses/2022/02/leveraging-ai-to-mitigate-civilian-harm), February 3, 2022, develops a civilian-protection life-cycle approach and potential technical/operational mitigation uses. | A-relevant research/D guidance, distinct from an operational benchmark or funded 2026 implementation. It is particularly useful against a claim that engineering capability and civilian protection are conceptually unrelated. The publisher summary was inspected; the 82-page report was not read in full here. |
| DHS S&T | [July 22, 2026 Genesis Mission announcement](https://www.dhs.gov/science-and-technology/news/2026/07/22/st-announces-new-genesis-mission-challenges-safeguard-americas-future) announces AI/formal-method software assurance for mission systems and critical infrastructure. The [AI/autonomous-systems research page](https://www.dhs.gov/science-and-technology/artificial-intelligence), updated September 23, describes performance assessment and adversarial resilience across DHS applications. | C/D, with no weapons-LOAC acceptance criterion established. A challenge announcement is not an award or an operational restraint. This review did not establish an ASIMOV continuation through DHS. |
| Sandia | [National-security autonomy overview](https://autonomy.sandia.gov/) describes advanced flight guidance, ISR sensing, cyber/EW and cooperative systems in adversarial environments. | General autonomy development; no dedicated normative benchmark established by this overview. It is not evidence that the laboratory lacks one. IARPA’s partner list separately establishes Sandia’s involvement in TrojAI T&E, without allocating dollars. |
| LLNL | [March 9, 2026 laboratory account](https://data-science.llnl.gov/advancing-ai-security-llnls-dsi-attends-technical-exchange-korea-ai-safety-institute) describes a January 26–29 CBRN AI-security exchange, including red teaming, evaluation and benchmarking. | C/D and CBRN-risk assurance. This is contemporaneous national-security AI work, but neither an autonomous-weapons LOAC benchmark nor proof of a funded ASIMOV successor. Avoid equating nuclear/CBRN safeguards with targeting safeguards. |
| ORNL | [Trustworthy-AI overview](https://www.ornl.gov/news/turning-ai-into-something-we-can-trust/) describes AI security, scientific reliability and energy efficiency; [July 2026 autonomous-science roadmap](https://www.ornl.gov/publications/toward-trustworthy-autonomous-science-a-two-year-community-roadmap/) concerns laboratories, verification, reproducibility and governance. | C/D; autonomous **science** is not autonomous weapons. Mentions of weapon misuse motivate security research but do not identify a normative weapons testing program. No relevant task-order funding was established. |

## Retrieval failures and unresolved scope

Two promising IDA links from the discovery results returned HTTP 404 both through direct HTTP and the web reader:

- `https://www.ida.org/assets/2026/06/18010641/P-22652.pdf`
- `https://www.ida.org/assets/2026/06/18010125/P-33087.pdf`

Their titles suggested ethical/legal machine-learning analysis, but neither their actual text, original publication dates, sponsor nor current funding was established. An `assets/2026/06/` URL component is not a publication date. These are unresolved leads, not excluded counterexamples.

Direct retrieval of NIST TEVV-Athlon and MIT Lincoln’s page returned HTTP 403; primary indexed page text was inspected through the web tool. TrojAI’s final-report web reader failed because the content exceeded its size limit. These are retrieval limits, not evidence of absence or substantive falsity. No access control was bypassed. Raw successful HTTP bodies remain private; only authored summaries, URLs and receipts are included here.

FFRDC and national-laboratory work can be embedded in broad sponsor vehicles, internal work packages and laboratory research portfolios. No whole laboratory management contract, broad IDIQ ceiling, general standards initiative or webpage was booked as normative-safeguards spending. The coordinator’s archive sweep and live award register are the separate funding/accounting evidence; this subreview adds no dollar total.

The defensible conclusion is that surviving evaluation and security infrastructure is broader than the ASIMOV label, while the inspected other-agency descriptions do not establish a new, actively funded equivalent autonomous-weapons normative benchmark. This review cannot establish an exhaustive government-wide negative.
