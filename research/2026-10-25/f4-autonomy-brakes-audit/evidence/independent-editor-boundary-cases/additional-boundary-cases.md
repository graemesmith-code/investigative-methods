## Golden Horde: preprogrammed ROE is real, but cannot inherit it into every new funded task

AFRL's program description explicitly limits weapon action to human-preselected targets/groups and a preloaded rule/play set, including what happens when permitted options are unavailable: https://www.afresearchlab.com/golden-horde-networked-collaborative-weapons/ . This is a substantive **B permission/engagement-envelope mechanism**, potentially A-adjacent, not simply a policy promise. The page is undated and its fact sheet is dated2022, so current page availability must not be equated with fresh funding.

AFRL's January7 2021 account of a December15 2020 flight demonstration describes applying planner-loaded constraints to choose target priorities. It also discloses a software-load failure and impact at a failsafe target location: https://afresearchlab.com/afrl-demonstrates-first-collaborative-weapon-technologies/ . This demonstrates a limited machine-constraint mechanism but **not a successful civilian-distinction test, legal reasoning, or blanket performance guarantee**. Target-priority ROE can serve operational intent without directly testing civilian protection.

JHU/APL's2025 annual report confirms continuing Colosseum testing/development, including maritime scenarios and integration onto classified systems: https://secwww.jhuapl.edu/2025-Annual-Report/countering-evolving-threats/ . Public program-level existence is not public disclosure of every test. Do not call the entire program unclassified merely because public summaries exist.

## Army LASSO budget: direct civilian-harm rationale for abort

A primary Army FY2026 missile-procurement budget indexed extract (June2025, printedVol1p198, line6872C91500) expressly relates LASSO's abort capability to dynamic situations involving human shields, as well as lower-collateral options. This reinforces the legally relevant purpose of **B** without showing autonomous legal reasoning.
