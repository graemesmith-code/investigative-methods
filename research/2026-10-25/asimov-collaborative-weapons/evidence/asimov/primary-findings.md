# DARPA ASIMOV: completion, funding and planned second phase

Evidence checked 24 October 2026. Public-source research note for editorial review. No outreach has been conducted. Dollar amounts are US dollars.

## Finding

DARPA publicly labelled its ASIMOV ethics-benchmarking programme complete by **17 June 2026**. The label is absent from the available **16 February 2026** snapshot. The original procurement contemplated a 12-month base phase and an optional 12-month second phase. The seven identified performer contracts show **$20,495,054 in obligations**, equal to their base-plus-exercised-option values, against **$39,475,202 including all potential options**. Their 43 publicly returned actions contain no option-exercise, termination, legal-cancellation or closeout reason codes. The funded contract values have not increased beyond their initial base values.

This supports an investigation into why the planned optional operational-refinement stage did not appear in these seven contracts, what the first stage achieved, and which outputs became publicly usable. It does **not** establish a formal early termination, a politically motivated cancellation, programme failure, a precise shutdown date, or the absence of other follow-on vehicles. Saab's records also show no-additional-obligation extensions into March 2026, so a literal programme-wide stop after one year is contradicted by the visible record.

## Corrections to the initial hypothesis

1. The seven performer identifiers are **FA865125CB001, CB002, CB003, CB004, CB006, CB007 and CB018**. Lockheed Martin is CB018. An exact CB005 query returned no FPDS actions and the tested USAspending award URI returned 404. Those negative results alone do not prove that no CB005 exists under any circumstances, but CB005 is not the identified seventh ASIMOV performer award.
2. **31 October 2026 was generally a potential option-inclusive end date**, not the funded/current completion date. RTX had no recorded option gap and an original ultimate date of 30 November 2025. CoVar's original potential date was 14 August 2026. Saab's ultimate date subsequently changed to 31 March 2026.
3. **Last funding and last activity differ.** The latest positive obligation in these records is 10 September 2025. Saab has zero-dollar scope modifications dated 3 December 2025 and 25 February 2026. CoVar's September 2025 administrative modification also changed its completion date.
4. **$18,980,148 is the difference between potential and base/exercised values**, not cancelled obligations, savings, lost funding or expenditure. All seven returned action sums reconcile to current USAspending award totals. There are no negative obligation transactions in these feeds.
5. **Publication of results continued:** the REBAR preprint appeared on 18 May 2026. A 5 July 2026 MAGNET conference paper explicitly acknowledges RTX contract FA865125CB002. A further full Lockheed Martin team workshop manuscript, dated August 2025 in an author's university CV, explicitly acknowledges CB018. These are research outputs, not official programme-wide final evaluations. See the [people/output source map](people-outputs.md) and [public tool access review](software-access-review.md).
6. **No personnel departure is established.** Current official and September 2026 host sources identify T. J. Klausutis as a DARPA programme manager. Removal of his card from the ASIMOV page is part of the page's completed-programme presentation, not evidence of his leaving DARPA.

## Planned structure and timeline

The [original DARPA BAA, HR001124S0011](https://sam.gov/api/prod/opps/v3/opportunities/resources/files/698f584a2531423fb4cd344dc81a69aa/download), dated 25 January 2024, specifies a two-phase, 24-month programme. Section 1.1.1 gives Phase I a 12-month base period for feasibility and decomposition. Section 1.1.2 gives Phase II a 12-month option for operational refinement. End-of-Phase-I down-selection could reduce performers or their scope. Proposals had to price the second phase as an option. Phase-I reporting included a final report by month 11. Phase-II reporting and public-community participation were contingent on that phase proceeding. The BAA also anticipated publication restrictions requiring DARPA permission, so the later public-access promise was not an unconditional promise that every underlying deliverable would be immediately public. These terms do not identify why any option went unexercised.

The [7 February amendment](https://sam.gov/api/prod/opps/v3/opportunities/resources/files/4b7de1675d444ac8b1cb921e29c39fdc/download) moved the abstract deadline to 14 February and corrected tables. The [9 February amendment](https://sam.gov/api/prod/opps/v3/opportunities/resources/files/1413e32acf2245dbb08f8f00088ce5f4/download) changed abstract-submission language, updated Attachment B and posted FAQs. Both retain the two-phase/24-month and 12+12-month provisions. The [first FAQ](https://sam.gov/api/prod/opps/v3/opportunities/resources/files/a7fba9ee399e45dbadfa8efc9be324eb/download), administrative question 3, clarifies an expected kickoff in Q4 calendar 2024, equivalent to Q1 FY2025. It explicitly says the schedule is an estimate. The final notice and attachment list can be reached through [SAM.gov](https://sam.gov/opp/908cbf15b903492f967e67cc80cdbb82/view).

There are 6, 6, 6, 6, 4, 8 and 7 returned actions respectively, 43 in total. Each feed contains fewer than the ten-entry page size and no next-page link. Each action sum equals its reported award aggregate and current USAspending total. The initial and latest base/exercised values are identical on each contract. The initial base was incrementally funded rather than fully obligated at signature. Recorded options therefore account for the **$18,980,148** potential-value gap across six awards; RTX's base and all-options values were already equal at inception.

FPDS [modification definitions](https://fpds.gov/help/Reason_for_Modification.htm) distinguish funding-only, within-scope agreement and administrative changes from option exercise (G), default/convenience/cause termination (E/F/X), legal cancellation (N) and closeout (K). None of G/E/F/X/N/K appears here. The [FAR option provision](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.217-5) makes evaluation of an option distinct from a commitment to exercise it. The [exercise rule](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/17.207) calls for written notice and a contract-file determination. Absence of an option exercise is not synonymous with a termination.

The [AFRL active-contract inventory, titled as of 22 September 2025](https://www.afrl.af.mil/Portals/90/Documents/SB/AFRL_Active_OTSB-SB_Contracts.pdf), is useful corroboration of identity, but its headings are **estimated ultimate completion** and **base and all options value**. Its old Saab date is superseded by the later modifications. Do not use it as evidence of a fully funded two-year commitment.

### Limits of the money evidence

- This is seven prime performer contracts, not a complete programme-cost accounting. Government staff, IV&V, ELSI, support costs and other vehicles may be outside the sum.
- Obligations are commitments, not payments or proof of delivered outcomes. Do not interpret the API's zero/null outlay fields as no money paid.
- Public DoD data can lag. [GAO documents the 90-day visibility issue](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-75). Retrieval on 24 October 2026 does not make this a real-time census. A year-old absence of optional funding is more informative than absence in the latest quarter, but still merits agency confirmation.
- Last-modified database timestamps differ from action dates. CoVar's API says last modified 20 January 2026 although its last signed action is 16 September 2025; Saab's is 19 March 2026 although its last signed action is 25 February. These timestamps must not be treated as new awards or funding.
- No written option non-exercise determination, contract termination notice, final acceptance document or programme closeout memorandum was located in this pass.

## What remains genuinely reportable

The strongest new reporting question is: **Why did DARPA mark ASIMOV complete without visible exercise of its planned second-stage options, and what independently usable ethical-evaluation capability emerged from the approximately $20.5 million obligated to performers?** The public record is sufficient to ask this precisely; it does not supply the answer.

Priority document/interview requests, not yet sent:

1. DARPA programme office: exact decision and completion dates; decision authority; whether Phase II occurred under other vehicles; outcomes against the base-phase objectives; reasons for non-exercise, separately from formal termination.
2. AFRL contracting office: contracts and all modifications, including relevant option CLINs and written decision records; confirm no missing actions and explain Saab's December/February date changes and RTX's one-stage financial structure.
3. DARPA and performers: accepted Phase-I final reports, independent evaluator findings, releases presented to the Technical Community Panel, available software/data licences and a usable public access route. Ask which work remains restricted and why rather than assuming suppression.
4. Government IV&V and ELSI: who served; what independent reviews occurred; which ethical claims proved measurable, which did not, and whether findings influenced a go/no-go decision.
5. Transition customers: whether any benchmark has actually been adopted in developmental or operational testing. A paper, prototype or promise is not operational adoption.

The ethics premise itself has substantial earlier journalism, including Rebecca McCarthy's [2 January 2025 New Republic feature](https://newrepublic.com/article/189236/inside-plan-teach-robots-laws-war). A fresh story should focus on documented disposition, results, access and unresolved decisions, not present the launch or ethical debate as newly discovered. Public absence does not establish exclusive novelty. The live DARPA programme page links its proposers-day slides but does not itself provide a final-results summary or a working benchmark download; that narrow observation should not be expanded into a claim that no results exist anywhere.

## Eglin and name-confusion boundaries

The award-office code links ASIMOV contracting to AFRL, not automatically physical experiments at Eglin. Recorded prime places of performance are Clifton Park NY, East Hartford CT, Woburn MA, McLean VA, Australia, East Syracuse NY and Cherry Hill NJ. A public contract-place field does not enumerate all work sites, either. The BAA describes government IV&V participants generically; this review has not established AFRL RW's substantive evaluation role beyond contracting.

Do not conflate this programme with AFRL's older **Autonomous Swarms for Information-aware Mission Operations with Verification**, also called ASIMOV, or unrelated civilian benchmarks bearing the name. Shared acronyms and shared contracting offices are not technical programme lineage.
