# ASIMOV: people, institutions, and public outputs

Research cutoff and observation date: 24 October 2026. Primary public sources and original interview reporting, distinguished below. This is a high-level public-record review of ethics benchmarking, not a technical guide to autonomous weapons.

## Most consequential findings

- Timothy (T.J.) Klausutis remains publicly identified with DARPA. The official biography says he joined its Strategic Technology Office in August 2022. A dated Ohio State agenda identifies him as a DARPA STO program manager for a 29 September 2026 keynote. Neither source establishes a departure.
- A substantive public research output exists: **REBAR: Reference Ethical Benchmark for Autonomy Readiness**, arXiv submission dated **18 May 2026**, from the Kitware-associated team. It acknowledges DARPA funding. It is not a DARPA final report or evidence that every program objective was achieved.
- Two further contract-attributable outputs exist: the **July 2026 MAGNET paper** and a **Lockheed Martin team workshop manuscript dated August 2025**, expressly acknowledging CB002 and CB018 respectively (details below).
- The currently accessible DARPA program page says the program is complete but gives neither a completion date nor reason. Nothing in the personnel evidence reviewed establishes early termination, political intervention, or a personnel departure.
- Public project/person records have different periods: one ASU CV lists October 2024–October 2026, another lists an empirical subproject ending October 2025, and a UNSW researcher describes her involvement as 2024–2025. These are not interchangeable with the whole program's actual end date.

## 3. Verified public research output

### REBAR: Reference Ethical Benchmark for Autonomy Readiness

- **Full paper publicly accessible**, submitted **18 May 2026**, arXiv:2605.18423v1, paper license CC BY 4.0. Metadata anticipates a WOROBET 2026 presentation. A later **1 June 2026 Duality news item** says it was presented at ICRA 2026: https://www.duality.ai/news/rebar-robot-ethics . This is the team's dated account, not independent attendance verification or government acceptance.
- Authors: **Jonathan Diller, David Barnes, Rebekah Bogdanoff, Rhett Collier, Roddy Collins, Keith Fieldhouse, Yonatan Gefen, Cameron Johnson, Anuriha Kodali, Brad Kriel, Varun Murali, James Niehaus, Mish Sukharev, Joseph VanPelt, Anthony Hoogs, Vijay Kumar, Arslan Basharat**.
- The demonstration separates task proficiency from bystander safety, with expert-labor and simulation limitations.
- It acknowledges **DARPA funding**, but does not identify ASIMOV or a contract number in the acknowledgement. Attribution to the ASIMOV effort is supported by the identical project name, PI, and partner organization in Duality's May 2025 announcement.
- **Software release not verified.** The paper does not establish an adopted standard or final program success.
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18423 and https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18423v1

### MAGNET: contract-attributable RTX-team paper

- **5 July 2026**, *What Could Possibly Go Wrong? A Hazard-Centric Approach for AI Test and Evaluation*, HCI International 2026 Posters, CCIS 3052, pp.448–456, DOI **10.1007/978-3-032-30836-8_46**.
- Authors: **Peggy Wu and Timothy Wang (RTX Technologies Research Center); Mary Nerayo (Virginia Tech National Security Institute); Ugur Kuter, Christopher Miller, and Helen Wauck (Smart Information Flow Technologies)**. Wu and Nerayo are corresponding authors.
- The abstract introduces **Mission Analysis Generalizable for Novel and Evolving Tests (MAGNET)**, a safety-oriented AI-evaluation framework.
- Its publicly visible acknowledgment names DARPA/AFRL/RWK support under **FA865125CB002**. The primary USAspending award record identifies that contract as RTX's ASIMOV award. This is a direct contract linkage, not acronym matching.
- Publisher abstract, authors, affiliations, acknowledgment, and bibliographic metadata are public. **Full text is paywalled and was not accessed.** No accompanying public code repository was established.
- Publisher: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-30836-8_46
- Primary award: https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_FA865125CB002_9700_-NONE-_-NONE-
- Conference organizer's poster list, last updated **30 July 2026**, lists the title, all six authors, and poster board 11.17: https://2026.hci.international/poster-presentations.html
- Wu's own public post says it builds on DARPA-funded AI-evaluation work and was presented at HCI International 2026. Its relative date varies across web renderings, so do not assign an exact post date from those labels: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/contactpeggywu_what-could-possibly-go-wrong-a-hazard-centric-activity-7502430688642740224-RMB9

## 5. Avoid these false matches

- **ASIMOV Benchmark / Generating Robot Constitutions & Benchmarks for Semantic Safety**, Pierre Sermanet and colleagues, March 2025, is a separate robot semantic-safety project. Its name is not enough to attribute it to DARPA ASIMOV; REBAR cites it as related work.
  - https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08663
- **neu-autonomy/ReBAR** is a different project's abbreviation: Relative Backward Reachability Analysis. It is not the verified Kitware REBAR source repository.
  - https://github.com/neu-autonomy/rebar
- Charles River Analytics has a separate page for a multicamera product also called ASIMOV. That alone is not a deliverable from this DARPA ethics program.
  - https://cra.com/projects/asimov/
- The Asimov synthetic-biology company and Menlo's Asimov humanoid releases are unrelated namesakes.
