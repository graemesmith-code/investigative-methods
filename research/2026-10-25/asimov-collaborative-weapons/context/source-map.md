## Novelty conclusion

The claim that network-enabled weapons, collaborative weapon concepts, or actual weapon-to-weapon communications have just appeared is untenable on these sources. The cleanest demonstrated precedent is Golden Horde in December 2020 and May 2021. Be exact that those tests used glide bombs. Gray Wolf defeats novelty of the *cruise-missile collaboration concept* by 2017, but its announcement should not be promoted into proof that operational missiles were already collaborating. A 2016 JSOW deployment proves a different, narrower capability: network-based updates and control.

## Evidence that would strengthen a results or transition claim

These are reporting questions, not mandatory gates for deciding whether funded research is newsworthy. Concrete contracts can establish a consequential research direction before deployment; stronger claims about results, integration or operational use require the corresponding evidence.

1. A verified programme/award relationship rather than a common administrative prefix or similar description.
2. Obligations, modifications and deliverables distinguishing sustained work from an unused ceiling or isolated study.
3. A named transition sponsor and funded integration or procurement path, not just a successful research demonstration.
4. Independent developmental/operational evidence, including whether testing covered representative uncertainty and degraded communications.
5. Clear evidence of what authority humans retain, what happens when communications fail, and who approves material software changes. Request public policy and assurance descriptions, not targeting/design details.
6. A documented change in operational availability, assurance, accountability, supplier access or integration that earlier programmes did not deliver.
