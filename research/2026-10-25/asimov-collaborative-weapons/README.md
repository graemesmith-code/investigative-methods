# ASIMOV and collaborative weapons: completion, coverage and context

**Report for 25 October 2026. Evidence cutoff: 24 October 2026, 21:49 UTC / 23:49 Europe/Rome (CEST).** This is a bounded public-source audit, not a claim to have read every related article.

The central finding is a narrower and more defensible question than an “ethics shutdown versus weapons acceleration” story: **what usable evaluation capability emerged from ASIMOV, why was it marked complete without visible exercise of the planned second-stage options, and how does that compare with the practical assurance needs of a real collaborative-weapons research pipeline?** The records do not establish a politically motivated shutdown or a newly deployed missile swarm.

## Provenance and scope

This extends, rather than replaces, the [24 October F2 investigation at its fixed published commit](https://github.com/graemesmith-code/investigative-methods/tree/e931fcbc6dc52c00b0565e853637a64fa95773b3/research/2026-10-24/f2-tactical-ai). The current selected cohort is not the whole Air Force portfolio. The seven ASIMOV awards and twenty-two FA8651 cohort entries overlap once, at Saab RESPECT; that evaluation award is not double-counted as a weapons capability.

The work is AI-assisted public-source research and source review. It separates obligations, current value and potential options; programme ambitions and demonstrations; current completion fields and real-time work status; award office and performance location; primary disclosure and independent reporting; historical roles and confirmed departures.

No outreach was conducted. Public files contain selected procurement fields, analytical paraphrases, source pointers, short necessary excerpts and methodology, not credentials, private correspondence, full copyrighted articles or weapon-building instructions. Source hashes identify the reviewed material; they are not claims that every raw source body is redistributed here.

The new Exa audit used 233 successful searches at a provider-reported cost of US$1.631, with zero pending or uncertain charges. See the [exact query/cost ledger](exa/query-cost-log.csv), [accounting and access limits](exa/methodology-accounting.json), and [transfer/editorial provenance](exa/transfer-and-editorial-audit.json). The ledger was received and hash-verified; the unreviewed all-results index and full article bodies are not included.

This package does not claim exclusive novelty or universal completeness of the public record. Its strongest remaining angle is an unanswered, evidence-backed reporting question, not a verified allegation of misconduct.
