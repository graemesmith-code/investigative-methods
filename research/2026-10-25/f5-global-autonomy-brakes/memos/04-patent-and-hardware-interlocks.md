# F5 — Patented safeguards: claims, controls and an evidence gap
**Research cutoff:** 25 October 2026. **Window:** 1 January 2022–25 October 2026, qualifying filing or publication (including grant publication). **Status:** integrated after the two lanes' documented domain editor rounds; accepted within the independent global editorial review scope on 25 October 2026, 23:05 UTC.

## What the combined public record establishes
The public patent record supports a narrower and more useful story than “there are no brakes.” The integrated register preserves **23 distinct publication records in 19 reporting clusters**, including direct weapon-governance disclosures, adjacent aircraft/range assurance, and explicit comparators. It combines a 12-cluster independent sweep with a 16-publication/13-family second lane, collapsing six overlapping clusters. These counts are inventory counts, not deployed safeguards, expenditure, or inventions first made in the window. National/family members listed only for genealogy are not silently counted as separately read claim documents.

The strongest reporting question is **which claimed constraint survives in a specific delivered configuration, under whose authority, and with what independent evidence?** No retrieved source explicitly mapped the reviewed claim sets to a fielded product/version. That is an evidence gap in this search, not proof of non-implementation. **A demonstrated safety control can count as evidence without a patent mapping; the missing mapping limits attribution to a particular claim set, not the existence of the control.**

**No current legal status is officially verified.** The register preserves aggregator status labels as leads, never as legal conclusions. Official public grant PDFs were subsequently retrieved and visually checked for Kongsberg US11946710B1, BAE US11768065B2 and Anduril US12282340B2; this successful document route is separate from a current-status, prosecution-history or ownership audit. Earlier record-specific status reads failed for native P01 and P11; other status checks were not attempted or completed. Application claims, granted claims and technical descriptions remain separate.

## Strongest patent-domain hook — a discovery lead, not yet a deployed-system finding
**Kongsberg’s families with first priorities in 2022 and publications in 2023–24—not merely recent grants on 2010s priorities—claim operator permission and loss-of-link disengagement, raising the concrete question whether a delivered weapon stops when permission or contact ends: the published claims establish the disclosed constraints, but no retrieved product-configuration or acceptance evidence yet establishes that operational consequence.**

The novelty established here is the dated public disclosure/claim record, not a finding that these safety ideas were first invented in 2022. On available evidence, this remains the strongest patent-domain reporting lead, not a sufficiently verified standalone revelation about operational behavior. The combined clusters must not be sold as new or deployed brakes. The source inventories preserve the original lane counts so the merge can be audited.

## Four direct leads from the independent sweep
- **Kongsberg: operator permission.** US11946710B1 claims 1 and 6 expressly require operator-confirmed authorization for semi-autonomous engagement; dependent claim 5 adds a withdrawal-related function. Priority/filing: 3 October 2022; grant publication: 2 April 2024. Ask for a configuration-specific assurance case, not an assurance that a product is merely “human supervised.” [Patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11946710B1/en)
- **Kongsberg: losing the connection.** In the separate US20230229794A1 family, dependent claims 5 and 17 require disabling the device when communications are lost. Publication: 20 July 2023. Google labels the US application abandoned; this remains unverified and says nothing by itself about product use. [Application record](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230229794A1/en)
- **Elbit: safety need not mean a human veto.** WO2025088600A1, published 1 May 2025, includes time-limited readiness and positional/separation constraints. Claims 9 and 10 offer human-originated and autonomous command alternatives. A safing device can therefore coexist with autonomous operation; do not report this as mandatory human approval. [PCT publication](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2025088600A1/en)
- **Tomahawk/AV: deliberate privileged actions.** US20250030576A1, published 23 January 2025, separates ordinary vehicle control from privileged commands and includes dependent constraints on incomplete or interrupted confirmation. This is a concrete accidental/unauthorized-command lead, not proof of autonomous legal judgment. [Application record](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20250030576A1/en)

These are high-level reporting summaries, not claim-construction opinions. Dependent-claim features are not mandatory in every claim or embodiment.

Safing can prevent accidental operation while permitting autonomous operation; it does not necessarily encode a human veto. Flight/range protection may be essential evidence for lawful employment without performing complete targeting-law judgment. No identified patent establishes a general model that decides every legal issue.

## Keep the other kinds of “brake” separate
Safety and restraint can overlap: a containment rule may implement a protected-area restriction, and a limited authorization may encode a normative boundary. The distinction is not “technical means cannot constrain violence”; it is that the source must show which restriction is encoded, who controls it and how it operates, rather than assuming a flight-safety function performs complete IHL judgment.

- **Containment:** AeroVironment US12205481B2 (P05) and NASA US12217615B2 (P12) address boundaries. A geographic constraint may implement a protected-area exclusion, but it does not by itself establish whether someone inside the permitted boundary is a lawful target. [AV](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12205481B2/en), [NASA](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12217615B2/en)
- **Accidental commands/manual takeover:** AV US11977380B2 (P06) and Boeing US20220194561A1 (P10) concern deliberate input and regaining control. Boeing's granted text was not retrieved, so only the A1 claim set is summarized. [AV](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11977380B2/en), [Boeing A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20220194561A1/en)
- **Range safety:** General Atomics US12175877B2 (P07) claims autonomous flight termination. Protecting a launch corridor is different from limiting harmful engagement decisions. [Grant](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12175877B2/en)
- **Assurance:** Collins US12087171B2 (P08) concerns a verified contingency; Aurora US12154441B2 (P09) evaluates path-planner safety. Neither establishes an IHL classifier or fielded independent engagement governor. [Collins](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12087171B2/en), [Aurora](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12154441B2/en)
- **Mode governance:** Rheinmetall EP4150284A1 (P11) distinguishes configurable safety modes from fixed protections. This supports asking who controls safety configuration, not an allegation of unlawful bypass. The reported April 2026 grant and its claim scope need official verification. No disabling instructions are reproduced. [A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP4150284A1/en)

Recent grant dates sometimes conceal much older priorities: AV P05 dates back to 2014 and NASA P12 to 2015. Count family clusters, not each national publication or continuation as a newly invented brake.

## Strong reporting next steps
- Obtain official prosecution/maintenance and assignment records for P01, P02, P03 and P11; reconcile the Elbit priority-date discrepancy.
- Seek a documented product/version-to-claim mapping and the delivered safety configuration. A supplier-wide statement is insufficient.
- Request independent results for loss of communications, stale authorization, operator withdrawal, boundary updates and unsafe-autonomy fallback. Ask for defined test conditions and failure/false-inhibition rates, not just successful demonstrations.
- Establish who can change constraints, with what authorization, logging and validation. Distinguish software release governance from mission-time authority.
- For fielded claims, corroborate acceptance records and actual operating procedures. A patent, demonstration, procurement notice, audit board or sales claim alone cannot establish effectiveness.
- For funding, join verified program/award evidence to the specific safeguard and avoid attributing an entire platform budget to “brakes.”

## Search limits and exclusions
Named-assignee coverage and rejected leads are in `search-coverage-and-acquisition-log.md`; exact preserved query batches are in `query-log.json`. This is a systematic targeted sweep, **not an exhaustive patent landscape or freedom-to-operate search**. Unpublished applications, secrecy restrictions, trade secrets, aliases, translations and indexing delays limit discovery. Negative results mean “not located in this search.”

The independent sweep used free public search and readable Google Patents reproductions. The second lane also checked leads from the central paid Exa discovery ledger; these are distinct from reporter-paid calls, of which there were none. The final cost total belongs to the central F5 ledger. Earlier USPTO/EPO/WIPO failures are retained in the historical acquisition log. The later three successful official USPTO grant-PDF checks above update document-access status without upgrading current legal-status verification. They do not establish that other records were attempted or blocked. The USPTO search landing page did not supply a completed current-status search. No access control was circumvented. No qualifying intentional defensive publication was verified in the targeted Technical Disclosure Commons queries; ordinary patent publication must not be relabelled as deliberate defensive publication.

A newly surfaced **WO2026217133** AV wave-off lead is quarantined: a third-party index lists a 15 October 2026 publication, but a primary publication was not retrieved. Do not count it in the 12 clusters or publish its asserted claims until verified.

## Combined inventory, provenance and scope

The two original inventories are retained unchanged in the data package, along with the combined deduplicated publication register and merge notes. The combined CSV uses one row per distinct publication document (23 rows); its `cluster_id` groups 19 reporting clusters. Six overlaps were joined on explicit publication/application/family relationships. The NASA A1/B2 are one cluster while their separately read claim versions remain separate document rows. Three second-lane national/PCT family rows remain visible without being counted as extra inventions. Family members mentioned only for genealogy remain a distinct metadata field, not promoted claim-read rows. The quarantined WO2026217133 lead is kept in a separate lead inventory; it is not counted among verified records.

The original native P01–P12 identifiers and second-lane source rows are preserved. Date, owner, claim and status fields keep their source basis; differing claim selections are retained rather than silently reconciled. Assignee names in a relevance query never override document identity. Class-guided discovery is not exhaustive native USPTO/EPO/WIPO pagination. No qualifying intentional defensive publication was verified; that is a bounded search result, not global absence.

The [USPTO's explanation of patent rights](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/manage) distinguishes exclusion rights from permission to practice. Even a verified grant is not military acceptance or legal authorization. Source quality, currentness, implementation, funding and novelty are tracked separately. Publication in 2022–2026 is not proof the underlying idea originated then.
