# Patents: concrete brakes, narrow guarantees

Research cutoff: 25 October 2026. This is a bounded public-source patent screen, not a global freedom-to-operate opinion, a certification audit, or a complete register of deployed systems. The companion CSV separates weapon-related disclosures, general vehicle assurance, and civilian comparators. Publication during 2022–2026 qualifies for the screen even when priority and original development are much older.

The strongest finding is architectural. Several patents locate authority to stop or withhold an action outside ordinary mission autonomy: a failsafe weapon-control boundary, deliberate authorization for privileged commands, abort authority, or a checked contingency plan. These are specific engineering propositions. They neither demonstrate compliance with international humanitarian law nor establish that a named product incorporates them. Conversely, they make a blanket assertion that industry has conceived no technical brakes untenable.

## Assurance concepts that must remain separate from weapon-law guarantees

[Aurora Flight Sciences US12154441B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12154441B2/en), granted 26 November 2024, concerns assessing a vehicle path planner's sufficiency for avoiding obstacles (claims 1–3 and 10–12). [Spleenlab US20230298474A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230298474A1/en), published 21 September 2023, uses independent position estimates and plausibility checks (claims 1, 2 and 9). These are relevant to assuring autonomy, but neither document alone establishes a weapons implementation or a legal-review result.

[NASA US20220366798A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20220366798A1/en), filed 15 July and published 17 November 2022, discloses onboard geographic containment (claim 1). Its listed assignee is NASA. Exa returned it in searches mentioning Shield AI, Northrop and General Atomics. Search context is not ownership. The page records a NASA-contract/government-work statement and an assignment involving Northrop, which likewise does not make it a Shield AI patent.

[Wing Aviation US20230316739A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230316739A1/en), published 5 October 2023, concerns aborting a civilian delivery after an obstacle check (claims 1 and 11). It is retained as a civilian comparator and a control against keyword overreach. [US11599125B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11599125B2/en), returned for an Aurora/Boeing query, is Toyota material-handling technology. It was excluded from military findings. So were the 2012 Raytheon safe-and-arm-robot publication and 2010 MBDA flight-abort publication: interesting background, outside the specified publication window.

## Search design and access limits

The screen combined assignee names with restraint terms, searched F41G/F42B/F42C/G05D1/B64U terminology, followed family links, and inspected claim text for promoted documents. The parent maintains the paid Exa query/cost ledger; this reporter made no paid requests. Free web discovery covered all named target groups, including searches for Shield AI/Allen Control Systems, Lockheed/Boeing/Aurora, General Atomics/Elbit/IAI, Saab/Kongsberg/Thales, MBDA/Rafael/Rheinmetall, RTX/BAE/Northrop, AeroVironment and Anduril. Broad Boolean queries had poor precision; a result-free or irrelevant page is not a negative finding about a company's portfolio.

This is a lead screen, not exhaustive native USPTO/EPO/WIPO database pagination. Primary patent text was read through Google Patents' public reproductions. Direct Espacenet/EPO register attempts failed in this environment; the PatentScope link for WO2025019462 returned HTTP 403. No login, account, purchase, or circumvention was attempted. Consequently, current legal status and chain of title are not independently certified. The [EPO identifies its Publication Server](https://www.epo.org/en/searching-for-patents/technical/publication-server) as the authoritative publication source; that remains the preferred follow-up for final claim/legal-status analysis.

The register's document hashes identify bytes retrieved at the research cutoff, not permanent canonical patent-office records. A source may later render differently. No raw full patent texts, images, schematics or PDFs are proposed for GitHub. Summaries are authored and intentionally functional.

## Story lead, not scoop claim

The defensible story is that rapid autonomy and assurance separation are being designed together. The BAE patent supplies an unusually explicit link between component churn, recertification cost and a proposed safety boundary. Tomahawk and AeroVironment supply different kinds of human-control and abort claims. A publishable implementation story would still need a product-to-patent mapping, evidence of the actual certified configuration, test limitations, and whether the relevant authority accepted the safety argument. None is inferred from a patent grant.

The [USPTO's description of patent rights](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/manage) confirms why this distinction matters: a patent establishes exclusion rights, not permission or certification to deploy the invention. There is no verified documentary basis here for claiming these patents prove operational legal compliance, effective protection of civilians, or deliberate circumvention of a safety board.
