# NATO and EU autonomy guardrails: promises, funding gates and evidence of implementation

**F5 / workstream 2 — research cut-off: 25 October 2026. Integrated revision from two reporting streams after three independent editorial rounds and subsequent source-specific addenda; accepted within the independent global editorial review scope on 25 October 2026, 23:05 UTC.**

## 1. Finding and limits

The strongest supported story is **fragmented, instrument-specific control**, not either universal certification or universal absence of safeguards. NATO's responsible-AI process, national acquisition controls, munition software standards, interoperability standards, accelerator funding, EU research eligibility and civilian AI regulation are different systems. None should be presented as a single permission to field an autonomous weapon.

Three particularly reportable seams emerge:

* NATO announced an end-2023 certification-process target, subsequently reported a toolkit pilot, and acquired an operational AI decision-support product. The public evidence reviewed does not connect a named product to a completed responsible-AI assessment, conditions or an approval signature. This is a bounded disclosure gap, not proof that no review occurred. [N02](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2023/02/07/nato-starts-work-on-artificial-intelligence-certification-standard) [N05](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/legacy-wcm/media_pdf/2025/4/pdf/sgar24-en.pdf) [N06](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/publications-and-reports/annual-reports/sgar25-en.pdf)
* The DIANA audit supplies an actual compliance finding about **financial evidence for testing expenditure**, with management's response and unqualified opinions on resubmitted statements/compliance. It must not be converted into a claim that testing did not happen. See section 4. [N11](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/iban/financial-audits/PO%282025%290336%20NU%20ENG%20-%20DIANA%202024%20FS%20-%20public%20disclosure.pdf)
* EU funding has substantive eligibility and proposal requirements, including human control, but a call, selection decision and public project description do not establish implementation or a weapons-release certificate. The best next reporting target is the grant's ethics conditions and their closure evidence, not another statement of principles.

**Evidence ladder used here:** legal/policy requirement → specified contractual condition → selected award → signed agreement → money paid → test conducted → result accepted → deployment authorized. An item proves only its own rung unless the source supplies the connection. No contacts, submissions, purchases or restricted-account access were undertaken. Sources are public; this dossier paraphrases rather than republishes their full text.

## 2. Which rule binds whom?

| Instrument | What is established | What is not established |
|---|---|---|
| DARB responsible-AI framework | NATO Enterprise-oriented process; national uptake distinguished explicitly | Automatic legal control over every Allied acquisition |
| STANAG and Allied Publication | A standard and agreement to implement it, potentially with national reservations | Identical national implementation or a product's conformity |
| DIANA programme and TEVV funding | Participation, progress and funding obligations | Blanket weapon safety certification or national procurement approval |
| EDF Regulation and grant conditions | Eligibility, ethics screening and enforceable funding conditions | A prohibition governing all nationally financed weapon development |
| AI Act | Scope-limited civilian regulatory duties | Coverage of exclusively military/defence/national-security AI merely because the developer also sells civilian products |
| Horizon/EIC after December 2025 | Specific dual-use/defence exceptions; EIC Fund excludes investment in persons conducting excluded activities | General military eligibility for every Horizon call; merely project-earmarked EIC exclusions |
| EDA/STO technical studies | Research findings, proposed frameworks, measurements and methods | A promulgated binding standard or actual certification decision |

These distinctions are the basis for the investigations below, not substitutes for procurement- and system-specific records.

## 3. DARB and ACT: from principles to an observable review gate

The 13 October 2022 charter gives DARB an advisory and coordinating role. Paragraph 6 envisages a responsible-AI **certification process standard** and practical tools for the NATO Enterprise, also available to interested Allies. Paragraph 8 envisages periodic project-owner self-assessment and Enterprise compliance once the standard is issued. Paragraph 9 makes national uptake voluntary. Paragraph 4 also says outputs should not unnecessarily impede adoption. It is therefore misleading to describe DARB as an independent supranational licensing authority. [N01](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2022/10/13/natos-data-and-artificial-intelligence-review-board)

NATO's February 2023 announcement expected the standard by year-end. ACT's 2023 solicitation sought contractor support to develop RAICS, including a process, deliverables and engagement with industry and civil society. This proves planned resourcing and an acquisition request, **not** a contract award, delivered specification, certification or rejected system. [N02](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2023/02/07/nato-starts-work-on-artificial-intelligence-certification-standard) [N03](https://www.act.nato.int/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/ifib023001_amdt2.pdf)

The July 2024 revised strategy called for assessment templates/review processes and an Alliance-wide testing landscape. It also identified the need to understand laboratory, accreditation and certification maturity and develop baseline recommendations. The 2024 annual report says DARB began piloting a Responsible AI Assessment and Toolkit; the 2025 annual report still describes pilot work. Those records demonstrate activity, but do not identify published pass/fail thresholds, reviewer independence, a product register or sanctions. The difference between the 2023 target and later pilot language merits questioning; it does **not**, by itself, prove a delayed weapons procurement. [N04](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2024/07/10/summary-of-natos-revised-artificial-intelligence-ai-strategy) [N05](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/legacy-wcm/media_pdf/2025/4/pdf/sgar24-en.pdf) [N06](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/publications-and-reports/annual-reports/sgar25-en.pdf)

GATE's own account confirms two acquisition/TEVV workshops on 10–11 December 2024 with ACT, NCIA, DARB and industry participation. It identifies Professor Sylvia Ilieva as GATE director and Bulgaria's DARB representative. Workshops are evidence of implementation effort and a useful professional reporting route; attendance is not certification. [N07](https://www.gate-ai.eu/en/news/gate-hosted-nato-workshops-on-artificial-intelligence-acquisition-and-testing/)

The [2021 NATO AI strategy](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2021/10/22/summary-of-the-nato-artificial-intelligence-strategy) already included governability, disengagement/deactivation for unintended behaviour and lifecycle reliability. These are meaningful commitments, not proof a particular mechanism was implemented or worked. The [25 June 2025 Rapid Adoption Action Plan](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2025/06/25/summary-of-natos-rapid-adoption-action-plan) aims generally at adoption within 24 months and incremental TEVV/integration within 12, with voluntary cross-recognition, badges and ranges. A compressed schedule or maturity badge does not itself establish waived national review or permission for lethal engagement.

### Named acquisition to test the process against

NATO reports that NCIA finalized acquisition of **Palantir Maven Smart System NATO** in 2025, that Allied Command Operations uses it, and that it was first tested during Steadfast Deterrence in May 2025 across the three Joint Force Commands. The report does not provide the responsible-AI assessment, procurement clauses, residual-risk owner, operating restrictions or certification decision. A decision-support acquisition should not be silently recategorized as an autonomous weapon. [N06](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/publications-and-reports/annual-reports/sgar25-en.pdf)

A separate [SHAPE article dated 30 June 2026](https://shape.nato.int/news-archive/2026/nato-maven-smart-system-achieves-full-technical-operational-capability-.aspx), retrieved as **official indexed text**, says Maven met full technical operational capability on 22 June and separately received security accreditation for NATO classified networks. Direct SHAPE/SACEUR body requests returned 403 in the second reporting stream. Preserve that access limitation: the indexed claim concerns technical/security gates on a C2 platform, not a publicly inspected DARB decision or autonomous-engagement approval. It can guide follow-up but should not carry a stronger headline.

**Priority questions:** What issued version replaced the RAICS development target? Was the toolkit mandatory at Maven's acquisition, acceptance and exercise dates? Who signed the assessment, what conditions were imposed, and did a later software/model update trigger reassessment? Does DARB receive project-level outcomes or only programme reporting? Which fields can be released without classified operational detail? Are requirements incorporated into an NCIA contract or only guidance?

## 4. DIANA: participation, tests and the audit trail

DIANA's public core principles link responsible innovation to NATO's principles. The actual scope of those commitments must be traced through the innovator agreement and test work order; the website alone does not demonstrate an ethical veto or a safety certificate. [N08](https://www.diana.nato.int/about-diana/core-principles.html)

A 4 September 2025 release says **14** firms advanced from a 72-company cohort, each to receive €300,000 and further support. Its URL still says 15, while the audit foreword says 73 selected for 2025. These inconsistencies do not establish that a company was expelled; ask for reconciliation. [N09](https://www.diana.nato.int/connect/nato-diana-announces-15-innovators-to-move-forward-to-next-phase-of-acceleration.html) [N11](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/iban/financial-audits/PO%282025%290336%20NU%20ENG%20-%20DIANA%202024%20FS%20-%20public%20disclosure.pdf)

The February 2026 NATO release gives 150 innovators, 200 test centres and a claim that more than 25 innovators had technologies **adopted or tested operationally**. That composite is not 25 adoptions, 25 certifications, or a safety-success rate. Selection and access to facilities also do not show how many tests a particular firm actually completed. [N10](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2026/02/03/kicking-off-nato-diana-s-2026-programme)

### Actual-event evidence: DIANA 2024 audit (precisely bounded)

IBAN's IBA-AR(2025)0016 gave unqualified opinions on **resubmitted** financial statements and compliance. Initial statements materially misstated loan proceeds; the resubmission corrected this. Separately, Phase 1/Grow funding lacked expenditure substantiation and audit clauses. TEVV files lacked required testing-centre invoices/payment evidence: innovators supplied invoices to DIANA instead. Management said accepted test reports evidenced completed activities; it accepted additional financial reporting/review and described existing Phase 2 milestones/tranches. It promised to link innovator status to reporting. The TEVV finding concerns financial substantiation, not failed/nonexistent tests or fraud. The audit records approximately €1m TEVV awards in 2024, not verified payments. Closure by the cutoff remains unestablished. [N11](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/iban/financial-audits/PO%282025%290336%20NU%20ENG%20-%20DIANA%202024%20FS%20-%20public%20disclosure.pdf)

**Current prospective implementation evidence:** DIANA's 25 June 2026 Challenge Q&A, addressing applicants for the incoming 2027 cohort, says the €100,000 is **contractual funding, not a grant**. Applicants must describe planned use, justify use in a final report and retain invoices/receipts for possible audit. The report must cover TEVV/OpEx results; further deliverables belong in the programme contract. This documents announced requirements beyond the older management promise, but does not prove retrospective repair of every 2024/2026 agreement, TEVV invoice or independent IBAN closure. The same incoming-2027-cohort FAQ expressly says Rapid Adoption does not automatically fast-track certification or standards processes; it also says DIANA does not take IP or require transfer of technology ownership, while later licensing/procurement belongs to separate agreements. That is not a guarantee that a later buyer will demand no proprietary evidence. [N30](https://www.diana.nato.int/connect/-nato-diana-challenge-call-qa-your-key-questions-answered-2.html)

**Priority questions:** Supply the actual 2026/2027 innovator/TEVV agreements, effective dates and audit-remediation status. Which invoices were subsequently obtained? Were repayments demanded or innovator statuses withdrawn, and for which non-sensitive reasons? Can DIANA publish counts of required/completed/accepted/repeated tests and responsible-use conditions separately from technology-readiness advancement? Which national authority takes over after accelerator testing, and what evidence travels with the product?

## 5. Standards: fix the identifiers before drawing conclusions

The NATO definition permits implementation in whole or part, with or without reservations. Czech defence-standardization guidance describes the national system as basically voluntary, while allowing specific mandatory commitments and separate implementation within NATO bodies. DLA warns that some standards and detailed national ratification/reservation data require registered access. This research did not obtain such restricted material; no inference of universal adoption follows from an active catalogue entry. [N12](https://www.nato.int/cps/uk/natohq/topics_69269.htm?selectedLocale=en) [N13](https://oos.mo.gov.cz/en/standardization-nato) [N20](https://www.dsp.dla.mil/Specs-Standards/International-Standardization-Documents/)

| Identifier | Verified title/status/version in official DLA catalogue | Proper interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **STANAG 4406** | **Military Message Handling System (MMHS)**; active; edition/revision 2, 26 October 2006 | Message interoperability, not a munitions safety standard. [N14](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsdocdetails.aspx?ident_number=275264) |
| **AOP-52** | **Guidance on Software Safety Design and Assessment of Munition-Related Computing Systems**; revisionB amendment 1, 29 November 2016; 208 pages | Relevant software-safety publication. Current amendment clauses not exhaustively inspected; a readable Edition B Version 1 copy is separately scoped below. [N15](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=278833) |
| **STANAG 4452** | Same subject, implementing **AOP-52 EditionB**; revision 1, 29 November 2016 | Correct agreement to investigate with AOP-52. [N16](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=281873) |
| **STANAG 4404** | **Safety Design Requirements and Guidelines for Munition Related Safety Critical Computing Systems**; cancelled 20 October 2011; superseded by 4452 | Historical predecessor, not a current independent requirement. [N17](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=278406) |
| **STANAG 4187** | **Fuzing Systems—Safety Design Requirements—AOP-4187 EditionA**; revision 5,21 June 2022 | Fuzing safety; cannot stand in for target-selection ethics, AI model assurance or every system-level hazard. [N18](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=97898) |
| **STANAG 4586** | **Standard Interfaces of UA Control System (UCS) for NATO UA Interoperability—AEP-84 EditionA**; revision 4,5 April 2017 | Control-system interface interoperability, not an autonomous-engagement approval. [N19](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=275263) |

Dates above are catalogue issue dates, not proof of national implementation on those dates. DLA separately records US ratification dates, illustrating the distinction. A claim that a product “meets NATO standards” needs the exact edition, invoked contractual clauses, assessment body, tested configuration and accepted deviations. Interoperability conformity may help safe operation without establishing software safety, legality or sufficient human control.

An official US-hosted [AOP-52 Edition B Version 1 copy, November 2016](https://www.cto.mil/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/AOP-52-EDB-V1E.pdf), was readable in the second reporting stream. Section 2.3 describes it as reference guidance rather than a compliance document; software-like devices and lifecycle safety are within scope. This does not erase the current DLA amendment entry or establish every current clause/national reservation. A programme's invoked requirements and implementing agreement determine the applicable duties. The separate [AOP-4187 catalogue record](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=284884) lists Edition A Amendment 1, 21 June 2022, fuzing safety; its full text was not accessed.

**Reporting test:** Select one munition-related AI procurement and request the contractual standards matrix and system safety case cover/approval sheet. Which AOP-52/4452 obligations were invoked? What national reservations applied? Did a learning-system update alter the baseline? Request only governance-level documents, not firing logic, schematics or instructions for defeating controls.

## 6. NATO STO: research evidence, not a certificate

### HFM-330 status caveat

The activity title **Human Systems Integration for Meaningful Human Control Over AI-Based Systems** is supported by the STO programme of work. A TNO-hosted pre-release indexed under TR-HFM-330 describes 17 candidate methods and notes not all were tested in real military operations; the live TNO copy now says REMOVED. One reporting stream could not read the final STO PDF directly and obtained co-author corroboration of title/year/ISBN from a [2026 article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41449-026-00527-3). [N21](https://publications.tno.nl/publication/34644895/mpMY7WNq/draper-2025-human.pdf) [N22](https://www.sto.nato.int/public/20250210_UC_IKM_NATO-science-and-technology-organization-2025-collaborative-programme-of-work.pdf)

The second stream retrieved **official indexed publisher catalogue and final-title-page text** giving **December 2025**, DOI **10.14339/STO-TR-HFM-330** and ISBN **978-92-837-2586-2**. Later direct body requests returned 403. This improves bibliographic corroboration but does not establish fresh full-final-PDF/page inspection. Treat the candidate methods and sociotechnical/lifecycle account as research findings, not implemented NATO acceptance conditions; do not quote uninspected final pages. Sources: [official catalogue](https://www.sto.nato.int/document/human-systems-integration-for-meaningful-human-control-over-ai-based-systems/); [official final PDF location](https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Technical%20Reports/STO-TR-HFM-330/TR-HFM-330-ALL.pdf).

A separate [STO PRU/URREF evaluation paper](https://www.sto.nato.int/document/evaluation-framework-natos-prus-and-urref-ontology-for-ai-assurance/), DOI **10.14339/STO-MP-IST-210-1A.04-PDF**, proposes 164 metrics/criteria. Its official indexed catalogue says loaded 17 February 2026; the accessible [proceedings cover](https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Meeting%20Proceedings/STO-MP-IST-210/MP-IST-210-Front-Cover.pdf) independently dates publication February 2026 and the symposium 21–22 October 2025. Direct catalogue requests later returned 403. This is a research proposal before the cutoff, not an issued DARB certification instrument.

### Verified adjacent reports and practical leads

* **STO-MP-HFM-322**, *Meaningful Human Control of AI-based Systems Workshop: Technical Evaluation Report, Thematic Perspectives and Associated Scenarios*, June 2023, ISBN 978-92-837-2425-4, is fully accessible through TNO. Its workshop synthesis offers a defensible route to the human/organizational dimensions of control beyond a last-second button. A workshop report is not a promulgated certification scheme. Public authors include Christopher Miller, Mark Draper, Jurriaan van Diggelen, Marlijn Heijnen, Robert Shively and others. [N23](https://publications.tno.nl/publication/34644896/6YFNMzmS/miller-2023-meaningful.pdf)
* **HFM-247**, *Human-Autonomy Teaming: Supporting Dynamically Adjustable Collaboration*, 2020; NTIS accession AD1183655,310 pages. The primary US catalogue describes ten national technical activities using common human–autonomy teaming patterns and metrics, and recommends continued work on meaningful control, calibrated trust and explainability. This is more concrete than a board's existence, but still not product licensing. [N24](https://ntrl.ntis.gov/NTRL/dashboard/searchResults/titleDetail/AD1183655.xhtml)
* **STO-TR-IST-164**, *Securing Unmanned and Autonomous Vehicles for Mission Assurance*: NATO's primary abstract describes a risk/security framework connecting mission, vehicle and component levels. Its stated focus is cybersecurity for individual medium-sized uncrewed vehicles with basic automated navigation. Expansion to safety and more comprehensive V&V is future work, so claiming that this report solves autonomous-weapon safety overstates its scope. [N25](https://www.sto.nato.int/public/20240223_UC_Book_%20of_%20Abstracts%202023_web.pdf)
* **STO-TR-SAS-146**, *Understanding the Cost-Related Implications of Autonomy—A System of Systems Perspective*,2020, ISBN 978-92-837-2298-4, is verified in the Slovak Armed Forces Academy catalogue. Jaan Murumets's public Estonian Defence Academy article explains that it is an adapted summary of the report. Use the public analysis for acquisition/lifecycle-cost questions; do not present it as the full report or as a certification standard. [N26](https://www.aos.sk/data/component_file/100/60/f2625.pdf) [N27](https://www.kvak.ee/files/2023/01/Jaan-Murumets_AUTONOOMSETE-SUSTEEMIDE-KULUMOJUD.pdf)
* **Fraunhofer NIMBUS** provides a national research bridge: the institute describes a 2024 requirements phase and 2025 interaction/concept demonstrator work for unmanned multi-domain systems. It is a route to measuring workload and human control, not evidence that the resulting controls were accepted for operational weapons use. [N28](https://www.fkie.fraunhofer.de/en/departments/mms/nimbus-interface-multi-domain-operations-unmanned-systems.html)

**Questions:** Which candidate methods became contractual acceptance criteria? What experiments measured operator intervention opportunity, automation surprise, workload and performance under degraded communication? Were negative findings published? Who owns the cost of recurrent testing after model or mission changes? What outcome would cause a programme to stop, restrict functions or remain in demonstration status?

## 7. EU law: funding conditions are real, but their reach is specific

### EDF

In the current 23 December 2025 consolidation, directly inspected in the official Publications Office PDF, Regulation 2021/697 Article 7 requires legal/ethical compliance, proposal self-assessment and Commission screening/assessment; resulting conditions belong in the grant arrangement and needed approvals precede the relevant activities. Ethically unacceptable proposals must be rejected. Article 10(6) excludes internationally prohibited products and lethal-autonomous development lacking the possibility of meaningful human control over selection/engagement for strikes against humans, while preserving defensive early-warning/countermeasure funding. This is an **EDF action-eligibility rule**, not a general ban on every national acquisition, all autonomous defensive functions or all anti-materiel systems. Article 9's foreign-control provisions have conditional safeguards, not an absolute all-foreign-ownership prohibition. [E12](https://op.europa.eu/o/opportal-service/download-handler?identifier=725a7cb0-1cbf-11f1-8c3a-01aa75ed71a1&format=pdfa2a&language=en&productionSystem=cellar&part=)

The Commission's interim evaluation provides actual process counts: **142 proposals screened in 2021–23; six ethics assessments**. The screened population is not all submissions: the evaluation describes funding/reserve-list proposals flagged for ethics issues. Screening outcomes include clearance, conditional clearance and escalation. It does not identify a named autonomous system rejected, restricted or delayed. The same report gives evaluation periods of 223,211 and 176 days for 2021,2022 and 2023: these are proposal-evaluation timelines, not weapons-certification delays. [E02](https://www.parliament.bg/pub/ECD/7506811_EN_evaluation_part1_v3.pdf)

The ethics self-assessment guide is an actual review instrument, unlike a mere board announcement. Nevertheless, the blank guide does not reveal how any named project's answer was challenged or closed. [E03](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/common/guidance/how-to-complete-your-ethics-self-assessment_en.pdf)

### AI Act and Horizon: avoid two common categorical errors

The **27 July 2026 AI Act consolidation, directly inspected in the official Publications Office PDF,** retains Article 2(3)'s exclusion for AI systems and uses exclusively serving military, defence or national-security purposes, irrespective of public/private operator. That is scope by purpose/use, not a blanket defence-company exemption. A separate civilian or law-enforcement deployment needs its own scope analysis. Do not use the AI Act's civilian conformity framework as proof of military certification; do not imply that its exclusion removes other law. [E13](https://op.europa.eu/o/opportal-service/download-handler?identifier=b1730fb2-8f1c-11f1-9262-01aa75ed71a1&format=pdfa2a&language=en&productionSystem=cellar&part=)

Regulation 2025/2653, effective 23 December 2025, creates targeted derogations through Horizon Article 48: specified EIC support may cover dual-use while advancing civilian applications, and a separate route covers critical defence technologies. It does **not** make every Horizon call eligible for military activities. Its EDF amendments do not remove the Article 10(6) control condition. The complete current Horizon consolidation, dated 24 December 2025, independently confirms these Article 48 derogations on p44. [E14](https://op.europa.eu/o/opportal-service/download-handler?identifier=23d5d1c5-0ce7-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1&format=pdfa2a&language=en&productionSystem=cellar&part=) [E05](https://strategic-technologies.europa.eu/document/download/f5e627b9-fc49-4873-a37b-458d4d53ccfd_en?filename=OJ_L_202502653_EN_TXT.pdf)

The August 2026 **EIC Fund Investment Guidelines, paragraph 1.67(t)** explicitly exclude the same category of human-targeting lethal-autonomous development without meaningful control, with the defensive early-warning/countermeasure qualification. **Section 1.66 bars investment in a person conducting any excluded activity**: this is investee/person eligibility, not merely an earmark restricting use of the EIC money. EDF Article 10(6), by contrast, is framed as eligibility of funded actions. Corporate-group, subsidiary and ongoing-monitoring questions require the applicable fund documents and implementation evidence. The Commission's update announcement confirms that defence-related activities were added alongside safeguards. Thus “Horizon is exclusively civilian” and “new EIC defence money has no weapons exclusions” are both inaccurate as blanket claims at this cutoff. [E06](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/document/download/6df458e7-fa47-4ace-93fa-86099606bdb0_en?filename=EIC_Fund_Investment_2026_guidelines_f.pdf) [E07](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/news/european-commission-updates-eic-fund-investment-guidelines-eic-accelerator-and-step-scaleup-2026-08-27_en)

The new EIC STEP defence scale-up route has a €100m announced allocation, €10–30m equity investments and a 28 October 2026 deadline; its factsheet anticipates results in early 2027. At 25 October it is not an executed 2026 award or disbursement. [E08](https://luxembourg.representation.ec.europa.eu/actualites-et-evenements/actualites/eu-research-and-innovation-funding-expands-defence-and-dual-use-technologies-new-call-offering-eur30-2026-06-17_fr?prefLang=pl) [E09](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/document/download/fd64a42e-ac92-45de-875d-5b487c126d5b_en?filename=EIC_factsheet_v40+4.pdf)

### EDA TAID: useful assurance recommendations, not binding certification

EDA labels the May 2025 TAID whitepaper a food-for-thought contribution. Its lifecycle/qualification discussion considers incremental qualification, testing, human factors, assurance and sovereignty. It recognizes limits of self-assessment tools and unresolved human-control questions. Its EICACS discussion allows a risk-based approach to non-European AI with independent monitoring; it does not establish an absolute foreign-model ban or compulsory national kill switch. The actionable lead is whether a Member State inserted these recommendations into a specific acquisition and what evidence it required. The paper itself cannot establish that transition. [E10](https://eda.europa.eu/docs/default-source/brochures/taid-white-paper-final-09052025.pdf) [E11](https://www.eda.europa.eu/publications-and-data/thematic-policy-reports/whitepaper-trustworthiness-for-artificial-intelligence-in-defence)

## 8. Money and status: named leads rather than an invented total

**EDF 2026:** the currently linked amendment download has the internal identifier C(2025)8719, amended by C(2026)690 of 4 February 2026. It sets a maximum €1,005,978,500 (€676,099,033 capability development; €329,879,467 research). The site also labels revised attachments amendment #1, with topic descriptions posted 30 April. Preserve both internal act identifier and attachment date instead of treating the upload label as a new legal decision. A claimed **€262m AI/autonomy total has not been reconciled to primary topic lines**; do not publish it as a verified spend. [F01](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/document/download/0e73a26d-659c-46c3-9496-378bf914dbc3_en)

The 2026 topics mix mandatory activities and functional expectations. **EMSAS**, €90m indicative, lists testing/qualification as mandatory but national-authority certification as an optional funded activity (printed pp133–134). Its functional requirements use **“should”**, including human-in-loop weapon engagements (p138); do not promote this to an automatic eligibility/rejection bar. ## Editorial hook test: consequence, novelty and scope

**Strongest supported one-sentence hook:** NATO and EU backing is not operational safety clearance: DIANA denies automatic certification fast-tracking, while the EDF’s indicative €90m EMSAS call lists mandatory qualification work, optional certification work and a “should”-level human-in-loop engagement expectation.

- **Named consequences actually evidenced:** incoming DIANA applicants face contractual reporting/record-retention requirements; EMSAS proposals must include qualification work, while human-in-loop engagements are a functional expectation expressed as “should”; EIC Fund applicants face person-level excluded-activity screening. These are conditions, not proof of a fielded system being stopped.
- **Novelty worth reporting:** the 2026 DIANA funding terminology/requirements, the person-versus-action distinction between EIC and EDF, and the distinction between EMSAS mandatory activities and functional expectations are more precise than treating all “NATO/EU-backed” systems as either certified or unregulated. The novelty claim is about this verified juxtaposition, not a claim that no journalist has reported any component.
- **Evidence:** N30 current prospective programme requirements; F02 actual call text and its mandatory/optional/“should” distinctions; E06 current investment exclusions. The DIANA audit independently establishes a past financial-control finding and management response, not a failed autonomous-weapon brake.
- **Investigative hypothesis still requiring reporting:** whether these conditions result in independently tested, configuration-specific restrictions—and whether a named procurement can be stopped, redesigned or suspended when they fail. No named autonomy-related rejection/delay is established in this dossier, so that stronger headline is not ready.

## Historical comparator: actual certification and closed follow-up

**For independent editorial review; not a claim about lethal AI or an autonomy certificate.**

A September 2020 article by the then NATO Alliance Ground Surveillance Force commander reports that Italy's Directorate of Air Armaments and Airworthiness (DAAA) awarded the RQ-4D a Military Type Certificate. This identifies a national certifying authority for a NATO system, rather than treating NATO membership or a STANAG as the certificate. The aircraft is a remotely piloted ISR platform: this does not certify AI targeting, meaningful human control of weapons, or all operating environments. [JAPCC commander account](https://www.japcc.org/articles/alliance-ground-surveillance-force/).

There is also documented follow-up closure. The official NSPO 2023 audit, IBA-AR(2024)0007, printed pages 51–52 (PDF pages 59–60 using one-based numbering), marks the AGS asset-control observation closed after physical configuration audits/inventory checks across July 2018–March 2024. The specific subrecommendation to include inventory-related continued-airworthiness risk in risk registers had been closed in IBA-AR(2023)0014. This proves a bounded audit finding was followed up and closed, not that every aircraft or AI function was proven safe. [IBAN audit](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/iban/financial-audits/2023-iban-nspo-en.pdf).

Reporting question: Can the responsible-AI toolkit furnish comparably concrete, configuration-specific authority, evidence and closure records for a named AI-enabled procurement? Keep the aircraft type certificate and the separate inventory/audit control in distinct columns; neither substitutes for autonomous-engagement assessment.
