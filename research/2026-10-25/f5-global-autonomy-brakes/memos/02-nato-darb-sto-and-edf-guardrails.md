# NATO and the EU: standards, research and funding gates do different jobs

F5 original reporting; public-source cutoff 25 October 2026. No paid Exa used. The investigation verified documents and their stated scope; it did not obtain private NATO certification decisions, confidential questionnaires or programme safety cases.

NATO and EU institutions have meaningful public guardrails, but the term certification hides several different activities: testing interfaces, assessing development processes, deciding national weapon safety, validating a human-control concept, and determining eligibility for European funding. One must identify the governing document and authority before treating any of these as permission to field an autonomous weapon.

## DARB: a process-standard mandate, not a supranational weapon licence

The [13 October 2022 DARB establishment text](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2022/10/13/natos-data-and-artificial-intelligence-review-board) defines a forum for translating six Principles of Responsible Use (PRUs) into practical tools. It describes a **process standard**, periodic project self-assessments, NATO Enterprise compliance once the standard is issued, and **voluntary national uptake**. The same document instructs the board to avoid unnecessary adoption barriers. The scope distinction is decisive: an Alliance process is not evidence every national autonomous weapon has been certified by DARB.

[ACT reported on 8 February 2023](https://www.act.nato.int/article/nato-starts-work-on-artificial-intelligence-certification-standard/) that work had begun, with completion due by the end of 2023. A projected completion date is not a verified issue date. Searches during F5 did not locate an authenticated public final questionnaire, issued-standard text or complete public register of DARB-certified weapons. This is a bounded access/verification gap, not proof that NATO never finished or uses no internal tools. Secondary articles asserting operationalization cannot replace the primary instrument or case evidence.

The [2021 AI Strategy summary](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2021/10/22/summary-of-the-nato-artificial-intelligence-strategy) includes explicit governability expectations, including disengagement/deactivation for unintended behaviour, alongside lifecycle reliability. Those are substantive commitments; they do not demonstrate the mechanism or effectiveness on a particular platform. The [10 July 2024 revision](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2024/07/10/summary-of-natos-revised-artificial-intelligence-ai-strategy) seeks a larger standards/tooling and TEVV landscape, using DIANA test centres. It acknowledges the need to understand available testing, accreditation and certification capacity. This is continuing institutional construction, not a clean shift from no controls to universal certification.

## Faster adoption includes explicit testing proposals

The [25 June 2025 Rapid Adoption Action Plan](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2025/06/25/summary-of-natos-rapid-adoption-action-plan) aims generally at adoption within 24 months and incremental TEVV/integration within 12. It proposes voluntary cross-recognition, innovation ranges and badges recording maturity/readiness. This creates a real story about the evidence that can be generated, reused and trusted within shorter schedules. It does not itself document skipped national legal review. A badge for testing or maturity must not be recast as blanket certification of safe lethal decision-making.

The [2026 DIANA-cohort challenge announcement](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2025/06/02/nato-diana-unveils-10-new-challenges-to-accelerate-dual-use-technology-breakthroughs) was published 2 June 2025; selected companies entered a January 2026 programme, including autonomy/unmanned systems among ten themes. The [June 2026 Q&A](https://www.diana.nato.int/connect/-nato-diana-challenge-call-qa-your-key-questions-answered-2.html) distinguishes acceleration from later procurement/licensing and says DIANA does not take IP or require technology ownership transfer. That is useful counterevidence to a blanket claim that allied assurance necessarily means surrendering proprietary models. It does not prohibit a later buyer demanding extensive evidence. The [June 2025 demo-days report](https://www.diana.nato.int/connect/diana-international-industry-investor-demo-days-2025.html) establishes demonstrations and access to test facilities; programme promotional language is not independent evidence of weapon safeguard performance.

The **indexed official text** of a [SHAPE report dated 30 June 2026](https://shape.nato.int/news-archive/2026/nato-maven-smart-system-achieves-full-technical-operational-capability-.aspx) states that NATO Maven Smart System met full technical operational capability on 22 June and separately received full security accreditation for NATO classified networks. Direct SHAPE/SACEUR page requests returned 403 in this run, so this remains an official-indexed-text finding, not fresh full-body verification. The stated gate concerns an AI-enabled C2 platform; it does not establish DARB certification or autonomous weapon engagement approval. It is useful counterevidence to an undifferentiated claim that allied buyers cannot accept any commercial AI, subject to that access limit.

## DIANA: an audited evidence gap concerns money, not failed tests

The [IBAN audit of DIANA's 2024 financial statements](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/iban/financial-audits/PO%282025%290336%20NU%20ENG%20-%20DIANA%202024%20FS%20-%20public%20disclosure.pdf), **IBA-AR(2025)0016, 26 August 2025**, provides an independent observation. Paragraphs **2.22–2.25** identify weak financial substantiation of Grant/Grow funding and missing testing-centre invoices or payment evidence for TEVV expenses; Grant/Grow agreements lacked audit clauses. DIANA's formal response says innovator invoices were accepted when test reports were submitted and accepted, demonstrating the agreed activity occurred (PDF p.29, printed p.25). The auditor's **financial-statement and compliance opinions were unqualified**; the findings still identify control improvements. This is a documented accountability friction point in funding assurance work. It is **not a finding that tests failed, never occurred, or that unsafe weapons were certified**. Technical test evidence and proof that an expense was properly paid answer different questions.

## HFM-330: concrete methods, with acknowledged limits

[STO-TR-HFM-330](https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Technical%20Reports/STO-TR-HFM-330/TR-HFM-330-ALL.pdf), **Human Systems Integration for Meaningful Human Control Over AI-Based Systems**, was published **December 2025**. The [publisher catalogue](https://www.sto.nato.int/document/human-systems-integration-for-meaningful-human-control-over-ai-based-systems/) confirms DOI **10.14339/STO-TR-HFM-330**, ISBN **978-92-837-2586-2**, open access. Its conclusion is not that one override button solves control. Meaningful control is a lifecycle property of a wider sociotechnical system; definitions remain contested and design guidance incomplete. It identifies 17 candidate assurance methods across design, evaluation, training and governance. This is substantive technical/human-factors research, not promulgated binding requirements or successful lethal-system certification.

A [2026 STO evaluation-framework paper](https://www.sto.nato.int/document/evaluation-framework-natos-prus-and-urref-ontology-for-ai-assurance/), DOI **10.14339/STO-MP-IST-210-1A.04-PDF**, maps the PRUs and URREF ontology to 164 metrics/criteria. The publisher lists it loaded **17 February 2026**; the [proceedings cover](https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Meeting%20Proceedings/STO-MP-IST-210/MP-IST-210-Front-Cover.pdf) dates publication **February 2026** and the symposium **21–22 October 2025**, all before this cutoff. Its authors propose a foundational evaluation resource. It should not be confused with a formally issued DARB certification instrument. The distinction preserves both genuine technical work and uncertainty about implementation.

## Standards identity check: one premise is wrong

|---|---|---|
| [AOP-52](https://www.cto.mil/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/AOP-52-EDB-V1E.pdf) | Edition B Version 1, November 2016 | Guidance on Software Safety Design and Assessment of Munition-Related Computing Systems. It expressly calls itself a reference, not a compliance document. It covers software and software-like devices throughout lifecycle; programme requirements still matter. |
| [STANAG 4187, DLA metadata](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=97898) | Revision/Edition 5, 21 June 2022; US ratification 23 June 2022 | Fuzing Systems — Safety Design Requirements — AOP-4187 Edition A. Public metadata verifies identity; login-required full text was not accessed. |
| [AOP-4187, DLA metadata](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=284884) | Edition A Amendment 1, 21 June 2022 | Fuzing Systems — Safety Design Requirements. Distinguish this current listed publication from historical AOP-16 guidance. No full-text purchase/access attempt. |
| [STANAG 4406, DLA metadata](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsdocdetails.aspx?ident_number=275264) | Revision/Edition 2, 26 October 2006 | **Military Message Handling System (MMHS)**. It is not a munitions software-safety standard; including it in that category was a false lead. |
| [STANAG 4586, DLA metadata](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/qsDocDetails.aspx?ident_number=275263) | Revision/Edition 4, 5 April 2017; US ratification 7 August 2020 | Standard Interfaces of UA Control System (UCS) for NATO UA Interoperability — AEP-84 Edition A. Interface conformance is not a weapon's IHL determination or comprehensive AI safety assurance. |

These dates are the editions evidenced, not an assertion that every nation's ratification/reservations or implementation has been verified. NATO's standards database and national implementing documents remain relevant to actual contractual force. Engineering safety standards can reduce specific failure risks while leaving intended-use lawfulness and human-control effectiveness to other processes.

## EDF: real eligibility gates, narrower budgets than the premise

The [EDF establishing Regulation (EU) 2021/697](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32021R0697), **Article 10(6)**, excludes actions to develop lethal autonomous weapons without the possibility of meaningful human control over selection and engagement when striking humans, while preserving specified warning/countermeasure uses. **Article 7** provides ethics assessment/screening. These are funding/legal gates, not proof of any particular hardware interlock. EUR-Lex flags a December 2025 consolidated version. The [December 2025 amending Regulation (EU) 2025/2653, Article 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/AUTO/?uri=CELEX%3A32025R2653) changes associated-country/eligibility arrangements and related EDF provisions; it does not replace Articles 7 or 10(6). A bot challenge blocked the consolidated URL, so the original plus published amendment were checked rather than claiming direct consolidated-text readback.

The Commission's [17 December 2025 announcement](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/commission-continues-strengthen-european-collaborative-defence-research-and-development-additional-2025-12-17_en) dedicates **€1 billion** to the 2026 programme and 31 R&D topics. It describes one quarter for a broad group of future technologies, not €262 million exclusively for AI/autonomy. This run did not substantiate the directive's €262 million label. Budget allocation is not obligations or disbursement.

The authoritative [2026 call-topic descriptions](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/document/download/2cd25753-d14f-4019-8fdf-68cca55b7f17_en?filename=EDF%202026%20Call%20Topic%20Descriptions.pdf), amended 4 February 2026, include **EDF-2026-RA-SIMTRAIN-MSAI**, indicative **€16 million**. Printed pp.42–44 require secure model-update pipelines, explainability and HITL/HOTL layers retaining final human decision authority in high-risk scenarios. This is simulation-supported military decision-making/training research; its activity table marks qualification and certification ineligible. It therefore demonstrates specified guardrails in a call, not completed weapon certification. The **AISAP/AISAO** tactical-awareness challenge budgets are **€23 million/€7 million**, with common evaluation environments and objective comparisons. No promoted funded-consortium or payment claim is made here; these are call conditions. Searching this document for kill-switch wording did not establish a disconnected national-control kill switch requirement.

The same document's **EDF-2026-DA-NAVAL-EMSAS**, indicative **€90 million**, is closer to weapon integration: printed p.139 specifies human-in-the-loop control for weapon engagements; p.132 describes human-on-the-loop missions including self-protection. These are scoped call requirements, not evidence a funded prototype delivered effective controls. The two formulations warrant clarification in the eventual design and evaluation record.

The Commission's [ethics tutorial](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2025-04/EDF%20tutorial%20-%20Ethics.pdf) documents ethics issue tables and self-assessment. EDA's [12 May 2025 trustworthiness whitepaper page](https://eda.europa.eu/publications-and-data/thematic-policy-reports/whitepaper-trustworthiness-for-artificial-intelligence-in-defence) and [paper](https://eda.europa.eu/docs/default-source/brochures/taid-white-paper-final-09052025.pdf) offer assessment concepts and annexes. The paper expressly describes a food-for-thought approach without commitments/obligations for Member States. This is useful preparatory work; it should not be reported as binding EU defence-AI certification.

## SecureAI is a cybersecurity call, not a disclosed weapons kill switch

The [ECCC call page](https://cybersecurity-centre.europa.eu/funding-opportunities/calls-proposals/cybersecurity-horizon-cl3-2026-02-cs-eccc_en) identifies **HORIZON-CL3-2026-02-CS-ECCC-02 SecureAI**, **€21.2 million**, opened March 2026 and closed 15 September 2026. It concerns adversarial robustness, poisoning/backdoors, privacy and secure AI, under Horizon Europe's Civil Security for Society programme. [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/programme/id/HORIZON_HORIZON-CL3-2026-02-CS-ECCC-02) confirms the topic's cyber-resilience scope. Neither topic page identifies a completed affordable-munition certification mechanism. Do not merge this civilian cyber call with EDF, or label its allocation as weapon-safety outlays.

## What is supported and what remains a reporting question

**Supported:** process-based NATO governance; public lifecycle/governability principles; practical human-control research; standards for different engineering layers; EDF human-control funding exclusions; explicit human authority and secure-update provisions in a 2026 research call.

**Not established:** a public final DARB vendor questionnaire, universal certification of member-state autonomous weapons, named US-vendor black-box failures, or systematic bypass of national safety/legal gates. Non-public review records are not evidence of absence.

The strongest editorial angle is the **portability of assurance under pressure for faster adoption**: when nations recognise one another's test evidence, what system/model version, mission limitations and human-control assumptions travel with it? A journalist should seek completed, versioned evidence packages and records of actual design/deployment changes. The institutional architecture is documented; operational outcomes need case-level corroboration.

Evidence quality is high for document identity and stated scope, lower for actual national implementation. Currentness ranges from 2006 messaging to 2026 calls. Funding evidence is allocation/eligibility only. Novelty lies in corrected categorisation and the gap between transferable evidence and specific operational authority; it is not an exclusive scoop about secret waivers.
