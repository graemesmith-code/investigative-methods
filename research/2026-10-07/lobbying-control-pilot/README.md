# Foreign control and lobbying disclosure: pilot findings

7 October 2026 · Private research, not a finding of misconduct

**The strongest lead is an apparent LDA ownership-disclosure inconsistency involving Stark Aerospace. This pilot does not establish deliberate concealment, a FARA violation, or an obscured campaign meeting its $1 million definition of “major.”** Two related follow-up questions emerged from original ownership records. Jev ranked all three, but its assessments required substantial correction and did not outperform the source-based rules consistently.

The work starts with the repository’s X04 source-pair card, recovered through the supplied inventory, and the user’s quoted item 10. Its $270,000 fee plus up to $100,000 reimbursable expenses is a contractual commitment, not verified receipts. Pages 11 and 18 describe government and media outreach benefiting IAI and Stark. GlobalPoint is a separate registrant with a separate engagement; it must not be substituted for either LDA firm.

The decisive chronological improvement came from [IAI’s 2024 financial statements, Note 11(c), printed page 54 / PDF page 55](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/41/4b/684080/IAI-FS-FY2024.pdf#page=55), an original company report available through Germany’s lobbying register. These snapshots bracket the registrations; they are not a daily share register. The 2024 table was also visually checked against the downloaded PDF.

**Best innocent explanation:** a filing mistake or misunderstanding of indirect ownership, rather than intentional concealment. Different services could explain the choice of LDA versus FARA, but do not by themselves resolve an LDA ownership checkbox. **Decisive missing record:** the contemporaneous intermediate share register/transaction chronology, followed by the filers’ explanation and any correction indexed under another alias. ## What the controls ruled out

Pillsbury’s [IAI North America LD1, posted 8 October 2024](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/a77c3c8a-af7b-4ac2-98f5-6effe2fba7b4/print/) names both IAI Ltd and the State of Israel at 100%, with zero contributions. Blank foreign-entity **additions** in subsequent quarterly reports are not evidence of nondisclosure. Its $480,000 reported 2025 income is separately attributed, not added to Stark’s campaign.

Haiti and Congo substring matches proved to be different named clients when original LDA client fields were retrieved. Harena’s Exhibit A and a [March 2026 contract amendment](https://efile.fara.gov/docs/6399-Amendment-20260302-69.pdf#page=1) supplied no contradictory same-client LDA pair. A mining company acquiring a project is not evidence that someone acquired the company. A [September 2025 GlobalPoint amendment](https://efile.fara.gov/docs/7518-Amendment-20250911-1.pdf#page=1) concerned a Côte d’Ivoire party, not Stark. Shared registrants, countries and similar names are inadequate joins.

## Coverage and limits

The deliberately bounded discovery cohort contains **19 indexed 2025 FARA Exhibit A/B documents**, discovered through corporate-ownership search phrases plus three government access controls; 15 originals were readable through browser PDF extraction and four were inaccessible. The corporate principals read were Stark and Harena. Ten government/government-intermediary and three individual records provided exclusion controls. A source-led expansion followed the US subsidiaries named in IAI’s ownership table rather than selecting famous names.

The pipeline completed **28 paginated LDA name/history queries**, retrieving **189 distinct catalog filings**, and downloaded **149 LDA originals**. Of these, **137 cover every result in the four corporate history queries** (Stark 13; ELTA 6; IAI North America 62; Israel Aerospace 56); 12 further originals test country-name joins. Two FARA amendments were read in addition to the frozen cohort. All figures describe acquired coverage, not the full lobbying universe. An initial unfiltered API access probe is excluded. Exact queries and every cohort disposition appear in the annex and machine-readable files.

“Major” means at least **$1 million in a calendar year of attributable, nonduplicated LDA outside-firm income or in-house expenses**, assessed separately. This operational threshold was chosen during the pilot, not preregistered. None of the three leads meets it on acquired evidence. Broader significance may warrant reporting, but procurement awards, full-term contract ceilings, group-wide spending and foreign funding are different measures. Overall campaign spending remains incomplete.

The declared search cohort and its ownership-led expansion have been screened. The stopping constraint is evidence access/completeness, not money: bulk FARA downloads and direct PDFs returned 403, four cohort originals remained unavailable, and exact-day private ownership records and a certified full FARA amendment history were not obtained. Search-engine indexing cannot certify completeness. Broader national coverage would require a new acquisition design; this is not an incidence estimate.

## What Jev added—and failed to add

Jev assessed every frozen-cohort dossier, all three leads and the real negative control, with source citations, identity, chronology, disclosure, materiality, innocent explanation, missing-record and abstention questions. Three evidence stages and comparative ranking are retained. Final ordering: **Stark, IAI/Emanuel, ELTA**.

Its useful contribution was cheap structured review and repeated identification of the dated share register as decisive. Its weaknesses were substantive: it called the initial later-only Stark evidence a mismatch; kept Haiti at medium even after rejecting the identity join; and assigned medium follow-up to the already-disclosed Pillsbury negative control. Final answers still combine “control not established” with “documentary mismatch” for two cases. Those are caution flags, not proof. The code-only baseline, using the same human-audited facts, was more consistent on the negative controls. This small, selected evaluation does **not** establish general accuracy or incremental discovery value.

Eight synthetic controls passed after one failed formulation was revised from a compound legal judgment to literal extraction; both attempts are retained. This is prompt calibration, not a clean held-out accuracy test. Eight offline budget/client tests passed. Source retrieval, joins, chronology, deduplication and spending enforcement used ordinary code; Jev neither browsed nor supplied new facts.
