# Foreign control and lobbying disclosure: the broader test

Private research, 7 October 2026. This extends the first checkpoint without changing it.

The broader search has not established that foreign control behind a major lobbying campaign was concealed. It has produced two concrete ownership-disclosure questions, a major reported engagement whose exact legal client remains unresolved, and several important false positives. Intent is not established in any case.

We started with a reproducible activity screen, not prominent company names: every official LDA API result for reporting year 2025 with a reported amount of at least $1 million. That returned 780 filings attached to 259 client IDs. These are candidate filings, not 780 separate campaigns. A client ID identifies a filing relationship; it is not a universal company identifier. The screen misses campaigns spread across smaller quarterly or multiple-firm reports.

The comparison corpus contains both complete DOJ reports to Congress for 2024. Their 1,598 extracted principal entries include repeated principals and termination annotations. They are historical leads, not a census of 2025 FARA registrations. We followed source-led matches into original Exhibit A/B filings and ownership documents. Download and amendment-reconciliation totals are in the accompanying coverage annex.

## Follow-up order

This is an editorial order based on documentary usefulness, not a probability of wrongdoing. New Jev legal rankings are withheld after its earlier negative-control failures.

**1. PPD / Mercury: strongest new cross-regime ownership question; the apparent million-dollar filing was corrected.**

Mercury's [LD-1, section 14](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/6ede88f5-9c9c-43d6-ba24-1dab72884c16/print/), filed 21 February 2025 and effective 15 January, checks No for qualifying foreign entities. Its client is PRVO PLINARSKO DRUTVO d.o.o., at Gospodarska zona Vukovar 13. The missing letter in the LDA spelling is bridged by the address and the legal name in the attached FARA contract, not treated as a different entity merely because of spelling.

Mercury's [FARA Exhibit A/B, received 6 May 2025](https://efile.fara.gov/docs/6170-Exhibit-AB-20250506-137.pdf#page=1), names Prvo Plinarsko Drustvo (PPD) Group at the same Vukovar address. Physical page 2 marks No to the ownership/control questions and leaves the separate owner-identification response blank. The attached agreement identifies the legal company, d.o.o., on page 10. The [audited 2024 company accounts](https://www.ppd.hr/storage/upload/web_struktura/PPD-FS-2024-HRV_144440.pdf#page=15), physical pages 15 and 42, identify Blue Horizon d.o.o. as sole owner at year end. They supply Croatian registry number 030070559 and OIB 58292277611. Page 45 says PPD left the ENNA group in August 2023; ENNA must not be carried forward as its current parent. These are year-end facts reported in June 2025, not a certified daily ownership history.

The [Q2 original](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/4012aaf7-8841-4f8a-90a6-db3a2a373e2a/print/) reported $1.2 million. The [23 July amendment](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/a11e2cd7-bedf-422d-98a4-6e2a31b3d4d0/print/) reduced it to $120,000. With Q1 $90,000 and Q3 $40,000, the acquired Mercury relationship reports $250,000 plus a Q4 amount below $5,000. The Q4 original checks both no lobbying issue activity and the under-$5,000 income box. A null API amount was not treated as zero.

**Strongest alternative explanation:** misunderstanding the form's private foreign-owner questions or a filing error, alongside a documented change in service scope. The original agreement concerned commercial energy and trade work; its amendment, effective 15 April, added arranging communications between the Croatian and US administrations and expressly addressed FARA compliance (physical page 20). The later FARA filing therefore does not by itself contradict the earlier LDA engagement. **Decisive missing records:** a certified ownership extract covering the registration dates, any owner-disclosure corrections, and the complete principal-specific FARA amendment history. The acquired record supports a question about identifying Blue Horizon, not concealed government control.

**2. Arm Limited / FGS Global: a clearer dated ownership chain, but no acquired FARA counterpart and no million-dollar amount in this relationship after correction.**

FGS Global's [LD-1, section 14](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/0a0eb801-ece9-445c-b137-93067243f611/print/), filed 30 September 2025 and effective 10 September, checks No. It identifies Arm Limited at 110 Fulbourn Road, Cambridge, and lobbying about artificial intelligence and semiconductors.

The original [Companies House PSC02, filed 24 August 2023](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/02557590/filing-history/MzM5MDcwMDA0M2FkaXF6a2N4/document?format=pdf&download=0), pages 1–2, identifies company 02557590 and records Arm Holdings Limited, company 11299879, as controlling at least 75% of shares and votes from 21 August 2023. The [PSC05](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/02557590/filing-history/MzQwMjA4MTM4MGFkaXF6a2N4/document?format=pdf&download=0) records the parent's change to plc. The [2025 accounts](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/02557590/filing-history/MzQ4MDYyNTMyNWFkaXF6a2N4/document?format=pdf&download=0), physical page 79, note 33, identify Arm Holdings plc as immediate parent and Japan's SoftBank Group Corp. as ultimate parent and controlling party. Original image pages were visually inspected; empty PDF text extraction was not taken as missing disclosure.

The [Q4 original](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/f44ab06b-02a3-4692-b80d-74f3cd211dd4/print/) reported $1.1 million. Its [22 January 2026 amendment](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/0ea84d9d-3c79-4ea3-af9d-536a797640e6/print/) reports $110,000. Together with Q3 $100,000, that relationship reports $210,000 for 2025.

**Strongest alternative explanation:** a filing mistake or confusion between Arm Limited and the publicly traded parent. Publicly available ownership does not establish an intent to hide it. **Decisive missing records:** the exact filing-date share chain and an explanation or correction of the No response. No FARA Exhibit A for this exact client was acquired; the case cannot be described as an established LDA/FARA collision. Relationship totals do not cap other firms' work for the wider Arm group.

A cross-registrant name search found Carmen Group's [2023 Arm Limited registration](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/0a90d026-2a37-4136-8f16-42fd298b50ef/print/), which already named SoftBank Group and SoftBank Vision Fund I. That earlier disclosure further weakens any claim of generally hidden ownership; it does not answer why FGS's separate 2025 registration checked No. Carmen's acquired relationship terminated in 2023. The same search also returned Armourshield Limited, which was excluded as a different client.

**3. KuCoin / A10 Associates: major reported lobbying, but legal identity and contemporaneous ownership remain unresolved.**

The [LD-1](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/18c0f855-8f6a-4804-ba43-d0cb8d74a4da/print/), filed 12 February 2025 and effective 1 January, identifies KuCoin at 2 Science Park Drive, Singapore, with no corporate suffix or legal registration number. Section 14 checks No. The specific activities include cryptocurrency regulation and congressional work concerning the company's New York attorney general settlement. The [Q1](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/b81ae0cb-af56-4133-b68c-9717340983b1/print/) and [Q2](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/295066f7-9a55-40d3-83ae-872ffa49489d/print/) originals each report $1 million income. The [Q3 termination](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/a2ada235-5a3f-4f67-be12-b7451da8e87b/print/) gives 1 July as termination date, checks no lobbying activity and income below $5,000.

The [New York settlement, opening pages](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/settlements-agreements/kucoin-stipulation-and-consent.pdf) distinguishes Mek Global, Phoenixfin and Peken. A [2024 federal indictment, pages 1 and 6–7](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/media/1345231/dl) also distinguishes multiple KuCoin entities and alleges earlier founder ownership. It is an indictment, not an adjudication or a 2025 share register. DOJ's [27 January 2025 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/kucoin-pleads-guilty-unlicensed-money-transmission-charge-and-agrees-pay-penalties) identifies Peken and describes the founders' withdrawal from management and operations. That does not establish a contemporaneous equity transfer or the identity of A10's contracting client.

**Strongest alternative explanation:** the brand spans different legal entities and an intervening governance change. **Decisive missing records:** A10's engagement identifying its contracting client, that entity's registration identifier and a dated ownership record. No same-client FARA original was acquired. The reported $2 million is lobbying income, not proof of $2 million foreign funding. The separate criminal proceeding supplies entity clues; it is not evidence of a lobbying-disclosure violation.

## What did not survive scrutiny

**Alibaba is a group relationship, not a same-client contradiction.** The [2016 LDA registration](https://lda.gov/filings/public/filing/76deddfa-3cdf-4380-98bf-96c1a5b0af76/print/) for Alibaba Group Holding Limited already identifies SoftBank at 32%. Its 2025 in-house expense total is $3.36 million after replacing the Q2 report with its amendment. Zeno's [2024 FARA Exhibit A, page 2](https://efile.fara.gov/docs/6652-Exhibit-AB-20240201-14.pdf#page=2) explicitly identifies Alibaba Group as parent of Alibaba.com Singapore E-Commerce Private Limited, despite its No checkbox responses. Its [February 2025 contract amendment](https://efile.fara.gov/docs/6652-Exhibit-AB-20250212-18.pdf#page=6) extends a PR engagement. These are distinct clients, registrants and services. The historical LDA shareholder disclosure is not a claim that SoftBank still held 32% in 2025.

**A foreign client is not automatically a company with a reportable foreign parent.** Nippon Steel and Barrick's No responses need ownership evidence, not nationality-based inference. Nippon's [2025 securities report, physical page 65](https://www.nipponsteel.com/newsroom/news/2025/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2025/09/10/nipponsteel_jp_br_2024_all.pdf#page=65), lists the largest registered holding at 14.8%, below the 20% equity trigger; nominee holdings are not a complete beneficial-owner analysis. Barrick's [2025 circular, physical page 12](https://s25.q4cdn.com/322814910/files/doc_downloads/agm/2025/Barrick_2025_Information_Circular.pdf#page=12), says management knew of no holder controlling 10% of voting rights as of 27 March. Neither source independently settles the alternative control or interested-affiliate tests. No contrary same-client FARA filing was acquired.

The stricter name join produced two historical edges linking Investment Company Institute with Future Investment Initiative Institute. The original names describe different entities. The wider 131-edge token screen also generated matches such as Lockheed Martin with Martin Fayulu and Nippon Steel with All Nippon Airways. All were reviewed as name leads; only Alibaba warranted a group-level follow-up. Rejected names were not sent to Jev as allegations.

## What Jev adds—and what it does not

The first checkpoint's model sometimes elevated negative controls and returned logically inconsistent identity and discrepancy judgments. Those results do not support unattended legal ranking. Before further calls, we froze a narrower test: ten previously unsubmitted original section-14 fragments and six new missingness/adversarial controls. Both Jev and an HTML parser scored 16/16. The repeated original fragments reduced the test to eight distinct request payloads; eight results were cache hits. This is a small, purposive test, not an independently annotated population benchmark.

Jev added no demonstrated accuracy over the parser on this task. It has not been shown to improve the decisive work here: retrieving amendments, resolving legal entities and establishing ownership dates. Passing checkbox extraction does not validate concealment judgments. The expansion therefore reports evidence-based editorial priorities and abstentions, without new model-generated suspicion scores.

Eight new paid calls cost an estimated $0.000177 at the verified input-token rate. Including the first checkpoint's 42 calls, the total under this authorization is **$0.003035**, not an invoice. Reserving the full conservative $0.003 for every one of the 50 calls gives a **$0.15 upper bound**, below the $15 authorization. No other paid service was used. The live test is closed against replay; the ledger is preserved.

The reusable result is an amendment-aware, original-source workflow that reduces false accusations. Its main limit is source coverage, especially a complete contemporary FARA principal/filing index and exact-date ownership records. The budget was not the limiting factor. The first checkpoint's findings and caveats remain separately available; this expansion does not silently replace them.
