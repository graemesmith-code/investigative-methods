Subject: Draganfly: the useful lead is the contacts around the award

Hi Graeme,

No breach is established by the public records checked so far. Your request was to scrutinize the award and the people around it, and the strongest lead is the lobbying close to the bidding and award dates. There is a concrete decision trail to pursue.

Draganfly consultant Andre Corbould disclosed contacts with defence ministerial adviser Quinn Rinke on August 30 and 31, during the bidding window, and with Canadian Army commander Lieutenant-General Michael Wright on September 8, 9 and 10. The government records the award on September 10. That is a concrete chronology to investigate. The filings don't tell us what was discussed or whether anyone influenced the competition.

Retired Lieutenant-General Michel Gauthier's own reports name National Defence assistant deputy minister Wendy Hadwen on June 23 and deputy minister Christiane Fox on July 21. They describe broader Canadian Armed Forces and Ukraine business; they don't identify the winning bid. We should keep those strands separate.

Michel Gauthier left the military in 2009. His disclosed lobbying began in 2025, well beyond the usual one-year and five-year periods on that service history. That makes the retirement-based restriction theory weak; his actual work and compensation still deserve scrutiny.

The headline numbers also need care. The government notice records C$439,851.55. Draganfly describes 100 firm systems with equipment and support, plus 4,900 discretionary options worth roughly C$24.25 million. Those aren't 5,000 guaranteed purchases. Licensing an Australian design for Canadian production is worth checking against the tender, but the government's announcement describes allied foreign designs for other suppliers too.

Corporate filings connect the older business to today's parent and subsidiary group. The current Controlled Goods directory names Draganfly Innovations Inc., while the award names Draganfly Inc. The tender's rules and actual performing entity would establish whether that distinction mattered; it does not demonstrate a breach by itself.

The next evidence that could make or break the story is the signed contract, the actual qualification and evaluation records, the licence and security decisions, and the briefing/follow-up trail around those contacts. We still haven't obtained those documents. The briefing-title search also does not establish that September records are missing: September titles are ordinarily due October 30.
