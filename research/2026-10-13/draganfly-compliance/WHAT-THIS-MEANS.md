# What this means for the story

Working assessment, October 13, 2026.

There is a reporting lead worth pursuing: Draganfly's registered lobbyists disclosed communications with senior defence officials while the company was moving towards a government drone order. The public record does not yet show that those contacts affected the competition. I would pursue the decision trail before writing an improper-influence story. We have a chronology that deserves explanation, rather than evidence that already supplies the explanation.

The strongest concrete lead concerns **Andre Corbould**, a Draganfly consultant with Integrated Strategic Partners Inc. He reported communications on **August 30 and 31** with **Quinn Rinke**, identified in the filings as a regional adviser for the West and North on the defence minister's staff. Both dates fall inside the **August 27–September 3** bidding period. Corbould also reported contacts with **Lieutenant-General Michael Wright, commander of the Canadian Army**, on **September 8, 9 and 10**. The federal procurement portal, CanadaBuys, records Draganfly's award on September 10. That puts identifiable officials and a company representative close to the decision. The reports give broad technology and research subjects; they do not identify the winning bid or tell us what was said. These are [disclosed communications](annexes/gauthier-and-lobbying.md), not a verified count of separate meetings.

**Michel Gauthier**, the retired lieutenant-general who formerly commanded Canadian Expeditionary Force Command, is a separate strand. His Draganfly consultant registration through Rubicon Strategy starts on August 25, 2025, before Draganfly appointed him to its military advisory board in February 2026. He reported a June 23 contact with **Wendy Hadwen, identified as a National Defence assistant deputy minister**, and a July 21 contact with **Christiane Fox, identified as National Defence's deputy minister**. The described subjects include Canadian Armed Forces applications and Ukraine contracts. Neither report connects him to this award. His disclosed military service ended in 2009, corroborated by official records from that year. A one-year or five-year restriction beginning then would have expired long before these contacts. The retirement theory is therefore weak on the history established so far. His actual role, compensation and communications remain legitimate reporting questions.

The money needs careful wording. The [government notice](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/tender-opportunities/contract-history/cw2467949-000) records **C$439,851.55** and a five-year term. Draganfly describes **100 firm systems**, associated equipment and support, plus **4,900 optional systems** worth approximately **C$24.25 million** if ordered. Those options need further authorization; they are not 5,000 guaranteed purchases. Another supplier publicly describes the same firm-and-optional quantities. A large possible follow-on order merits scrutiny of pricing and approvals, but the numbers alone do not demonstrate special treatment or an inflated aircraft price.

Licensing an Australian design is not inherently suspect either. The government's announcement describes allied foreign designs for several suppliers and says Draganfly will manufacture and support the Astiia drone under licence in Canada. The useful question is whether Draganfly held the rights and capacity required by the actual tender when it qualified and bid. We have not obtained a dated licence agreement or the controlling tender package. Canadian production claims deserve verification; they do not establish either compliance or deception by themselves.

Three sets of documents would change the judgment. The signed contract and bid papers would establish the promised work, prices and selection rules. Qualification, evaluation and security decisions would show how officials applied those rules and what evidence they accepted. Invitations, briefing materials and follow-up instructions for the named contacts would show whether lobbying intersected with requirements or selection. Evidence of privileged bidder information, an unexplained waived requirement or an instruction favouring Draganfly would materially strengthen the story. Records showing consistently applied criteria and shared information would weaken that concern. The [records plan](annexes/ranked-records-plan-draft-20261013.md) prioritizes those tests. That is where the investigation should concentrate next.
