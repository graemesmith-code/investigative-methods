## Concrete edits and additions

### 1. Add the surviving deemed compensation terms, rather than making all contract terms sound unknowable

GCR section 3(3) preserves section 18 even for contracts excluded under section 3(1). Section 18 supplies deemed contract and bid declarations about prohibited contingent fees, links compensation records to whatever accounting/auditing provisions the contract has, and specifies default consequences for the enumerated false declarations/non-compliance. This is distinct from Lobbying Act section 10.1. Neither source establishes Gauthier's actual compensation, or that any prohibited fee was agreed or paid.

Suggested addition to concern H: “The signed contract is missing, but GCR s.18 supplies deemed contingency-fee declarations that survive the NSE exclusion; s.18(1)(b) links relevant compensation records to the contract's actual audit provisions. No prohibited fee or false declaration has been established.” Do not imply s.18 alone grants a journalist access to the fee records, or invent the contract's audit machinery.

Primary: [GCR ss.3 and 18](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-87-402/FullText.html), current to September 21, 2026, last amended December 16, 2024; [Lobbying Act s.10.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/L-12.4/page-2.html).

### 2. Add the third relevant price-support trigger

The summary and card F correctly identify CAD10m competitive and CAD7.5m one-compliant-offer triggers. The same March 25, 2026 guide also mandates consultation for competitive procurement estimated at CAD7.5m or more, taxes included, where the contract provides for negotiated prices or prices are likely to be negotiated through an amendment. This is potentially relevant to option-pricing scrutiny and should not be omitted from a purportedly comprehensive rule card. It is not evidence this contract contains such a clause.

Suggested addition: “A separate CAD7.5m competitive trigger concerns specified negotiated-pricing circumstances; its applicability requires the actual price provisions.” Preserve the guide's different wording for each trigger rather than copying options/amendments language across all of them.

Primary: [Additional reviewers, pricing bullets](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/buyer-s-portal/buyer-s-guide/approve/prepare-approval-documents/additional-reviewers).

### 3. Make the NSE date test exact in the short summary

“Pre-award date” can be read as earlier on the same day. The statutory test requires the responsible ADM/equivalent's signed approval letter to be dated before the award day. If September 10 is confirmed as the actual award date, a September 10 letter would not satisfy that wording. Nothing recovered proves such a letter exists or has the wrong date. The detailed oversight annex already gets this right; carry it into the summary.

Primary: [CITT Procurement Inquiry Regulations s.10(3)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-93-602/FullText.html).

### 4. Repair a chronology label in matrix row 14

It calls the FAA source a “pre-award consolidation,” whereas card O says current to September 21, last amended September 1, and award metadata says September 10. A September 21 consolidation is not pre-award. Safer: “Primary consolidation current to September 21 read; relevant provisions and their September 10 applicability require version/commencement confirmation.” If the archived September 10 version or commencement record was independently checked, name that instead. A post-award consolidation is not automatically stale or substantively wrong; the issue is its label and the evidence chain.

### 5. Explain why a monthly lobbying ledger cannot be a census of lobbying

The existing count caveats are good. Add the legal reason: consultant monthly reports cover the prescribed oral, arranged-in-advance communications involving designated public office holders, with initiator/subject distinctions. They do not exhaust written communications, every spontaneous contact, or every public official. The registration itself also covers expected activities. No report therefore does not mean no lobbying; a registration is not proof every listed institution was actually contacted.

The current regulations were reopened: [Lobbyists Registration Regulations ss.4, 6–7](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2008-116/FullText.html), current to September 21, 2026, last amended April 27, 2012. Section 4 also defines receipt differently by filing method; a displayed posting date is not a sufficient independent lateness test.

### 6. Use the exact registry date label

The original Gauthier page says “Initial registration start date: 2025-08-25.” Prefer “the disclosed consultant registration dates from August 25, 2025” to asserting an independently established engagement-contract signing date. The page supports the earlier disclosed relationship and Rubicon Strategy affiliation, but does not provide the engagement contract. It reports military service ending May 2009; that remains a filed disclosure rather than the personnel instrument.

Primary reopened successfully: [Gauthier registration 973399](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/vwRg?cno=382133&amp;regId=973399). Fresh direct opens of his two communication bodies failed in this audit; their detailed fields remain supported by the inherited captures, not new retrieval here.
