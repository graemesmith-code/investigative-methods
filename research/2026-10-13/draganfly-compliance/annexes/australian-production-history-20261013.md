# Australian developer production history: a useful qualification test

13 October 2026, approximately 21:52 UTC. Three original Australian government releases corroborate a substantive military development and production history for AMSL Aero before Canada's competition. This weakens an assumption that the foreign developer had no relevant military production history. It does not prove Draganfly's qualification or licence rights.

| Date | Original source | Verified public statement |
|---|---|---|
| January 30, 2024 | [Defence Minister prototype challenge](https://www.minister.defence.gov.au/media-releases/2024-01-30/new-generation-drone-prototypes-take-part-strategic-capabilities-accelerator-challenge) | AMSL Aero was among 11 companies contracted for a prototype and production plan; the aggregate contract value was AUD1.21 million. No Canadian supplier role identified. |
| August 21, 2024 | [ASCA production contracts](https://www.defence.gov.au/news-events/releases/2024-08-21/asca-invests-66m-australian-drone-technology) | AMSL Aero, Boresight and Grabba Technologies each received an AUD2.2 million development-and-production contract for 100 general-purpose UAS following an April fly-off. The amount covers development and production; dividing by 100 would not establish an airframe unit price. |
| August 19, 2025 | [Delivery milestone](https://www.defence.gov.au/news-events/releases/2025-08-19/growing-sovereign-industrial-base-300-drones-delivered-defence) | The three suppliers' 300 units had been delivered to the Australian Army. Additional orders across them exceeded AUD1.5 million; neither the number nor supplier allocation of those extra units is given. |

These original releases name AMSL Aero but do not identify the product by the Astiia name. The product connection comes from the previously preserved Mission Systems September 16, 2025 developer announcement and a July 31, 2025 Australian Defence Magazine interview with AMSL's CEO. The latter is a secondary lead, not a new original contract capture: [article](https://www.australiandefence.com.au/defence/air/amsl-aero-displays-its-defence-credentials). It identifies Astiia and reports the first 100 deliveries. No full commercial article is redistributed.

## The precise Canadian test remains unresolved

Candidate, unverified RFSA Q&A suggests a qualifying recent military or government project, with the supplier's own experience or role documented rather than automatically inheriting a partner's history. If the authentic criterion says this, AMSL's records would be relevant to a cited partner project but would not independently show Draganfly's own role, an eligible joint venture, or the evaluators' accepted evidence. The governing RFSA and Draganfly's submission remain missing. No qualification failure or waiver is established.

The Australian history also supplies no executed Canadian licence, sublicensing permission, IP ownership chain, territory, exclusivity, grant date, Canadian production readiness or delivery acceptance. Canada's September 10 backgrounder remains the primary public statement about licensed Canadian production; it is not the agreement itself. The inquiry must identify rights to the developer's airframe and any separately owned embedded technology, without treating a multi-party technology chain as a defect by itself.

## Broad program statement versus stream-specific criterion

The already archived [July 23 DND backgrounder](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/news/2026/07/defence-drone-initiative.html), Competitive supplier process, says previous defence experience is not necessarily required and directs applicants to the formal RFSA requirements. Its Canadian sovereign capability section expressly contemplates licensed manufacturing and trusted-allied partnerships. A program spanning multiple streams may allow different experience routes; that broad statement cannot replace a specific S1-M1 clause or show that a specific clause was waived. Conversely, the unverified stream-specific Q&A must not be described as a universal bar across every DDI capability stream.
