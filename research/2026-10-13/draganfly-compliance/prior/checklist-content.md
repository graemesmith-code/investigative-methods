## What the evidence supports now

The public record supports a focused investigation of how Draganfly qualified, how its bid was evaluated, what Canada actually bought, and whether the promised Canadian production and security arrangements exist. It does not yet establish a procurement breach. The decisive next step is to obtain the signed contract, the operative solicitation packages and the contemporaneous decision records.

The first questions worth pursuing are concrete:

- Did the aircraft procurement's ground-vehicle description appear only in a public notice, or in the package bidders had to answer?

- Was Draganfly qualified in the correct supply-arrangement stream at the required time, and did the proper set of suppliers receive the same competition?

- What explains the notice's “no trade agreements” field, particularly under the Canadian Free Trade Agreement?

- Were all mandatory requirements met at their specified deadlines, including any foreign ownership, control or influence assessment?

- Were options included in the appropriate approval and pricing-review calculations?

- Does the Astiia licence and actual production plan support the specific Canadian-content and sustainment promises that Canada required?

A useful finding needs four things: the applicable rule and version; an authenticated action inconsistent with it; any valid exception or correction; and the consequence. Keep separate statutory breaches, trade-agreement breaches, procurement-policy failures, bid or contract non-compliance, poor value for money, and inaccurate public communications. One does not automatically establish the others. Record small technical departures too, but assess materiality and remedy separately: a breach can be real without changing the winner, and an available remedy may require additional proof of prejudice or loss.

## The factual starting point

Verified public government record. CanadaBuys identifies Draganfly Inc., contract CW2467949, solicitation WS5853743341, under W8703-270094. It lists C$439,851.55 and a 60-month term from 10 September 2026 to 10 September 2031. It describes a competitive selective request for proposal against Defence Drone Initiative Stream 1 supply arrangement W8703-270055. No applicable trade agreements are listed. The value's tax basis, line-item composition, treatment of options and relationship to the first delivery remain unresolved. [S01]

Verified notice-level discrepancy. RFP-A was published on 27 August 2026 and closed on 3 September at 14:00 EDT. Its title concerns uncrewed aircraft; its description concerns complex-terrain ground vehicles and tactical load carriage. The notice's last-amendment date is also 3 September. That date does not prove a substantive solicitation change occurred on closing day. The full distributed RFP-A, RFP-B and addenda remain essential. [S02]

Issuer claims requiring contract corroboration. Draganfly announced a firm order for 100 systems with associated equipment, documentation, spares and training; options for 4,900 additional systems worth approximately C$24.25 million if exercised; and discretionary written option authorization. Those quantities and option economics have not been checked against the signed instrument. They are neither guaranteed revenue nor proof of 5,000 committed purchases. [S03]

Official program description requiring execution evidence. The Prime Minister's backgrounder identifies the offering as Astiia UAS, using Australian technology licensed for Canadian production, with Canadian integration, support and sustainment. It refers to completing evaluation, including foreign ownership, control or influence assessments, before intended awards. It does not disclose the completed assessment or licence. The DDI expressly contemplates allied partnerships and licensed manufacturing. [S04, S05]

A separate eligibility lead. A CanadaBuys listing identifies Draganfly supply arrangement CW2465414 dated 24 August; an issuer announcement describes qualification as of 28 August. Establish issue, effective, acceptance and notification dates before treating this as a contradiction. A nominal C$1 supply-arrangement listing is not a drone purchase price. [S06]

## How to use the checklist

Priority means reporting value, not suspicion: P1 establishes the governing rules or could change the award's validity or price; P2 tests important integrity, security or performance obligations.

Each item states the governing test, the records to obtain, and an explanation that could resolve the concern. “Contract” means conditional on the actual RFSA, supply arrangement, solicitation or signed contract. “Policy” is not automatically legislation. A technical test is not a universal legal requirement unless an applicable law or clause makes it one.

Use the version effective at the decision being examined. Bid eligibility may be due at closing, certification at award, equipment capability at acceptance, and continuing duties later. Do not test all four against one date. For every item record: applicable clause/version; evidence and custodian; relevant date; status; benign explanation tested; consequence; next record needed. Status options are not assessed, not applicable, unresolved, supported concern, resolved concern, and verified compliance.

## Phase 1 Establish the complete record

### 1 Identify the transaction precisely

P1 | Test: Match legal supplier, solicitation, contract, lot and supply arrangement; do not merge different suppliers' awards or RFP-A and RFP-B. [S01, S02]

Evidence: Executed CW2467949, award recommendation, procurement index, legal name/business number, all associated instrument IDs and versions.

Possible resolution: Related notices use a common solicitation number but govern separate lots; the contract crosswalk explains the apparent mismatch.

### 2 Recover the controlling document hierarchy

P1 | Contract: Read the signed instrument's order-of-precedence clause and every incorporated annex, standard clause, certification and amendment, with effective version dates.

Evidence: Complete signature package, statement of work, specifications, basis of payment, delivery schedule, security checklist, bid incorporated by reference and acceptance terms.

Possible resolution: A provision that looks inconsistent in a public summary is correctly defined in a higher-ranking annex or authorized amendment.

### 3 Build a timestamped chronology

P1 | Fact check: Distinguish drafting, approval, signature, effective date, notification, public announcement, option exercise and payment.

Evidence: Native timestamps and time zones for RFSA submission, qualification, invitations, bid receipt, evaluations, security approval, award and notice publication.

Possible resolution: A date discrepancy reflects posting or notification after the legal event. A same-day announcement does not establish which event happened first.

### 4 Preserve originals and provenance

P1 | Evidence quality: Separate original government records, issuer filings, third-party mirrors and interview recollections. Never silently substitute a current RFSA round for the August version.

Evidence: Native downloads, URLs, retrieval dates, hashes, attachments, revision history and provenance log; independent corroboration for mirror-only amendment counts.

Possible resolution: A search snippet or aggregator is stale, incomplete or combines versions; the authenticated source removes the inconsistency.

### 5 Establish what a system contains

P1 | Contract: Confirm the unit of measure before calculating quantities or prices. “System,” “kit,” “aircraft” and “ground station” are not interchangeable. [S03]

Evidence: Line-item schedule showing airframes, controllers, payloads, radios, batteries, spares, software, freight, training and documentation; shared versus per-kit equipment.

Possible resolution: One package contains common support equipment or multiple airframes. Dividing C$439,851.55 by 100 would then misstate aircraft price.

## Phase 2 Determine the legal route and authority

### 6 Identify the Crown contracting authority

P1 | Law and delegation: Identify who could contract and who actually signed. CAF, DND, PSPC and DIA are not interchangeable legal authorities. [S07, S08]

Evidence: Signature block, statutory authority relied on, departmental mandate, procurement-service agreement and dated delegation to the individual signatory.

Possible resolution: PSPC or an authorized successor unit acted for DND under a valid delegation rather than DND's ordinary internal limit.

### 7 Test the correct competition route

P1 | Law and contract: The public record describes selective competition under an SA, not a sole-source purchase. Test that route before looking for sole-source justification. GCR sections 3, 5, 6 and 7 must be read together. [S01, S09]

Evidence: Approved procurement strategy, SA ordering rules, invitation records and any exception actually used.

Possible resolution: Competition among the properly selected qualified suppliers satisfied the applicable route even if only one responsive offer remained.

### 8 Verify any national security exception

P1 | Law and policy: A no-agreements field does not itself establish an NSE. Obtain the signed responsible-ADM-or-equivalent approval letter dated before the award day, with scope covering this purchase, and the required rationale/strategy record. [S10, S11]

Evidence: Approval or non-sensitive reference identifying authority/date, program coverage, rationale and secured record location.

Possible resolution: A valid program-level letter covered this procurement; classified details are properly held separately.

### 9 Apply the post 2024 GCR rule

P1 | Law: Since 16 December 2024, GCR 3(1)(i) excludes qualifying NSE contracts from the regulations, while 3(3) preserves section 18. Do not apply a pre-2024 argument requiring an additional section 6 exception automatically. [S09]

Evidence: Relevant NSE instrument, legal applicability determination and surviving terms.

Possible resolution: The procurement was properly excluded from bid-solicitation provisions, while remaining subject to other applicable financial, policy and contractual controls.

### 10 Test CFTA coverage separately

P1 | Treaty: Apply the August 2026 CFTA text, its entity, goods, value and exception provisions. Canada's Annex 519.1 lists no excluded entities or exceptions/notes; international defence-goods exclusions cannot simply be imported. National security is Article 901 in this version. [S12]

Evidence: Written CFTA analysis and precise alternative exclusion or NSE relied on.

Possible resolution: A valid Article 901 invocation or another actually applicable provision explains non-coverage; the notice alone does not.

### 11 Test international agreements separately

P1 | Treaty: CETA, CPTPP and revised WTO-GPA have entity schedules, defence-goods lists and other exclusions. Identify the real commodity and end user rather than inferring coverage from PSPC administration or one radio component. [S13-S15]

Evidence: Agreement-by-agreement analysis, FSC classification, goods/services allocation and applicable schedule versions.

Possible resolution: The purchase falls outside a defence positive list or within a valid exception even though its value exceeds the threshold.

### 12 Calculate the correct procurement value

P1 | Treaty and policy: Use each regime's valuation rule, including relevant options and duration; do not substitute the first invoice or public initial value. For 2026-27, federal CFTA goods threshold is C$34,700; CETA/GPA/CPTPP goods/services threshold is C$239,200. [S12-S16]

Evidence: Pre-solicitation estimate, option assumptions, taxes treatment and valuation date.

Possible resolution: A defensible exclusion applies despite the numerical threshold; different approval and reporting values legitimately use different rules.

### 13 Check contract approval and signing limits

P1 | Policy and delegation: Separate project approval, expenditure initiation, contract approval and signature authority. Apply the limits effective on the approval date, including the changes effective 15 December 2025. [S08, S17]

Evidence: Delegation chart, full approval valuation, risk/complexity assessment, special authority and Treasury Board decision if required.

Possible resolution: A valid higher-level or exceptional approval covers the option-inclusive amount. An old public limit is not the controlling ceiling.

### 14 Check commitment and payment controls

P1 | Law: FAA sections 32, 33 and 34 address different financial controls. Establish sufficient authority for amounts falling due in the fiscal year; do not assume all five-year options needed immediate appropriation. [S07]

Evidence: Commitment record, financial coding, authorized approvals, appropriation/availability clause and separation of certification/payment duties.

Possible resolution: Where consistent with fiscal-year obligations and applicable commitment rules, only the firm obligation was committed initially; later options required fresh funding and authorization.

### 15 Check procurement division and bundling

P2 | Treaty and policy: Test whether lots, prototypes, support contracts or staged awards were structured to evade an applicable threshold or approval rule; identify that rule first. [S12-S16]

Evidence: Original needs forecast, acquisition plan, related awards, combined estimates and documented reasons for packaging.

Possible resolution: Multiple awards, competitive experimentation, operational sequencing or genuinely separate requirements explain the structure without avoidance.

## Phase 3 Audit supply arrangement qualification

### 16 Recover the original RFSA round

P1 | Contract: Apply the first-round RFSA and amendments, not later Round 2 wording. The 23 July launch identified a first-round 14 August deadline. [S05, S06]

Evidence: Issued RFSA W8703-270055, original qualification matrix, questions/answers, closing rules and version history.

Possible resolution: A seemingly missing criterion was never required in the relevant round, or a later requirement did not apply retrospectively.

### 17 Confirm the qualified legal entity

P1 | Contract: Compare SA holder, bidder and contractor, including any permitted affiliate or joint venture. Draganfly Inc. and Draganfly Innovations Inc. must not be treated as interchangeable. [S01, S40]

Evidence: Executed SA, entity registration, bid signature, affiliate resource undertaking, assignment/novation consent where relevant.

Possible resolution: The same qualified contractor performs using disclosed, permitted affiliates; group-level publicity merely shortens the name.

### 18 Resolve qualification dates and conditions

P1 | Contract: Establish when qualification became effective and the stage at which it was required; reconcile the 24 August record with the issuer's 28 August description. [S06]

Evidence: Qualification decision, signed SA, acceptance/notification timestamps, conditional approvals and portal activation.

Possible resolution: Issue, acceptance and public announcement occurred on different dates, all before the relevant eligibility deadline.

### 19 Confirm the correct stream and product scope

P1 | Contract: Qualification in one DDI stream or for one capability does not automatically authorize every downstream competition.

Evidence: SA schedules, qualified capability/product list, stream definition, category restrictions and authorized users/order ceiling.

Possible resolution: Stream 1 qualification expressly covered Astiia or permitted a later specific product proposal; there was no requirement to qualify a named model initially.

### 20 Retest each qualification gate

P1 | Contract: Identify required experience, technical maturity, manufacturing capacity, Canadian business substance and certifications, and when they had to exist.

Evidence: Submitted qualification evidence, criterion-by-criterion evaluation, reference checks, demonstrations and verification notes.

Possible resolution: Allied partner experience or licensed production was permitted; previous CAF contracting or wholly Canadian intellectual property was not mandatory. [S05]

### 21 Examine cures and admissions consistently

P1 | Contract: Determine whether deficiencies could be corrected, additional evidence submitted or applicants admitted between refresh windows, and whether comparable applicants received the same opportunity.

Evidence: RFSA cure/refresh provisions, clarification correspondence, failed/pending applications and decision dates.

Possible resolution: The RFSA allowed the correction or rolling admission and officials applied the same rule consistently.

### 22 Reconstruct the eligible invitation pool

P1 | Contract: Test the SA's actual invitation rule: all suppliers, minimum number, rotation, capability subset or another method. Do not invent an all-holders rule.

Evidence: Dated roster, suspension/reinstatement record, invitation list, selection rationale and distribution logs.

Possible resolution: An expressly permitted capability-based subset received the RFP, while other holders were outside scope or not yet qualified.

### 23 Check access and continuing eligibility

P2 | Contract: Establish whether eligible suppliers could obtain the operative package and whether any suspension, expiry or certification change affected Draganfly before award.

Evidence: Ariba permissions, invitation delivery/bounce logs, access requests, help-desk tickets, SA validity and change notices.

Possible resolution: Access problems were corrected in time or arose outside the buyer's control; eligibility remained valid under the SA's continuing-obligation clauses.

## Phase 4 Audit the solicitation and equal treatment

### 24 Explain RFP A and RFP B

P1 | Contract: Determine whether A and B concern separate configurations, lots, phases or award paths, and which produced CW2467949. [S02]

Evidence: Both complete packages, cross-reference notices, number-of-awards provisions, evaluation plans and award mapping.

Possible resolution: Distinct competitions legitimately shared a parent number. A requirement from one did not govern the other.

### 25 Resolve the aircraft and ground vehicle mismatch

P1 | Fact and contract: Trace the UAS title/UGV body inconsistency into the materials bidders actually saw. Test whether it impaired access, understanding or competition. [S02]

Evidence: Original portal package, edit log, translations, bidder questions, correction notices and technical statement of requirement.

Possible resolution: A public-page template error was corrected or never appeared in the authoritative aircraft solicitation; no bidder was misled.

### 26 Test the response period under applicable rules

P1 | Contract and any covered treaty: Assess the 27 August to 3 September period against this SA, the requirements' complexity and any applicable permitted reductions. Do not import unrelated service-SA rules. [S02, S12-S15]

Evidence: Exact issue/access time, tender timetable, urgency rationale, extension requests and responses.

Possible resolution: Prequalified bidders had adequate preparation and the permitted downstream process did not require a longer period.

### 27 Authenticate every amendment

P1 | Contract: Establish what changed, when, who approved it, who received it and whether time to respond was extended as required.

Evidence: All addenda and Q&A, redlines, distribution timestamps, acknowledgement rules and closing extensions.

Possible resolution: The 3 September metadata reflects a minor public-notice edit rather than a material RFP amendment. Third-party amendment counts may combine different events. [S02]

### 28 Check equal answers and private briefings

P1 | Contract and fairness: Compare questions, answers, demonstrations and one-to-one briefings for materially unequal information.

Evidence: Q&A register including unanswered questions, meeting notes, help-desk communications and common clarification releases.

Possible resolution: Private exchanges concerned administrative access or genuinely proprietary details; all generally material information was distributed to eligible bidders.

### 29 Trace restrictive specifications to the need

P1 | Contract and any covered treaty: Identify brand, dimensions, interfaces, origin restrictions, experience thresholds or delivery dates that unnecessarily excluded alternatives. [S12-S15]

Evidence: Needs analysis, market research, drafting history, equivalency language, technical rationale and supplier input.

Possible resolution: A restriction was objectively needed for interoperability, safety, security or urgency and was clearly disclosed and consistently evaluated.

### 30 Identify supplier involvement in drafting

P2 | Policy and contract: Prior market engagement is not itself improper. Test whether a participant shaped specifications for itself or retained non-public information without effective mitigation. [S18, S26]

Evidence: Requests for information, demonstration invitations, consultant terms, specification metadata, contact logs and conflict mitigation.

Possible resolution: Officials independently validated the requirements and shared material information; the supplier's ordinary expertise created no unfair advantage.

### 31 Check complete evaluation instructions

P1 | Contract: Locate every mandatory gate, rated criterion, pass mark, weight, price formula, option quantity, tie-breaker, allocation rule and right to make multiple awards.

Evidence: Final instructions, evaluation matrix and locked evaluation plan approved before closing.

Possible resolution: The apparent discretionary award was expressly permitted by the published selection method; the winner was not required to have the lowest bare unit price.

### 32 Check bid submission mechanics

P2 | Contract: Apply the stipulated channel, deadline/time zone, signature, format, file-size, language and acknowledgement rules; distinguish mandatory requirements from conveniences.

Evidence: Bid receipt/opening register, original files and hashes, server logs, outage notices, late/replacement bid handling and amendment acknowledgements.

Possible resolution: A permitted correction, verified platform outage procedure or non-mandatory formatting defect explains acceptance without unequal treatment.

### 33 Check cancellation and negotiation reservations

P2 | Contract: Determine whether government reserved a lawful right to cancel, negotiate, seek best-and-final offers, waive specified defects or change quantities; test its use against the actual terms.

Evidence: Reservation clauses, approval/rationale, negotiation invitations and any relaunched solicitation.

Possible resolution: The process used a disclosed mechanism consistently and did not secretly replace an essential competed requirement.

## Phase 5 Reproduce the evaluation

### 34 Preserve the original bids

P1 | Evidence and contract: Work from the bid as received before closing, separately identifying every later clarification or replacement page.

Evidence: Read-only originals, attachment inventory, submission timestamps, audit trail and evaluators' working copies.

Possible resolution: A document thought to be a late improvement was already in the timely bid; later correspondence merely identified its location.

### 35 Reperform mandatory compliance

P1 | Contract: For each mandatory criterion, record the exact promised capability, evidence, compliance deadline and evaluator conclusion. Do not treat delivery-stage requirements as necessarily due at bid closing.

Evidence: Compliance matrix, original technical offer, verification material and rejection reasons.

Possible resolution: The bidder met the criterion in the prescribed way or valid equivalency applied; a preferred feature was not mandatory.

### 36 Distinguish clarification from bid repair

P1 | Contract: Identify whether later exchanges explained an existing commitment or supplied a new commitment necessary to become responsive; check any phased-compliance process.

Evidence: Every clarification, request, response, revised page and governing cure clause; comparison with other bidders' defects.

Possible resolution: The solicitation expressly allowed the cure, or the response clarified timely evidence without changing the offer.

### 37 Reproduce rated scores

P1 | Contract: Recalculate each weighted score and pass mark; trace material differences between individual and consensus ratings. [S19]

Evidence: Individual worksheets, reasons, consensus notes, spreadsheet formulas/version history and peer review.

Possible resolution: A reasoned consensus corrected an individual misunderstanding under the published rubric; a high-level summary omitted that explanation.

### 38 Test demonstrations and samples fairly

P1 | Contract: Compare configuration, payload, weather, route, operator skill, test opportunities, failures, retests and any waivers across bidders.

Evidence: Predetermined protocol, raw measurements, video if retained, calibration records, evaluator notes and exception approvals.

Possible resolution: Different conditions were normalized under the protocol or an authorized retest was offered consistently. A polished demonstration alone does not prove production compliance.

### 39 Look for undisclosed criteria

P1 | Contract: Identify factors used in ranking that were absent from the published method, including political desirability, relationships, extra features or an unannounced production preference.

Evidence: Score rationales, decision brief, meeting records and communications compared with the final evaluation matrix.

Possible resolution: The factor was genuinely within a disclosed criterion and supported by documented evidence rather than a new criterion introduced after bids arrived.

### 40 Check evaluator independence and information separation

P1 | Policy: Test conflict declarations, recusals and price-access controls against the applicable PSPC-led evaluation rules. [S19, S26]

Evidence: Team composition, signed declarations, access permissions, timestamps for technical completion and financial opening, substitute-evaluator records.

Possible resolution: Necessary access was segregated, a conflict was effectively mitigated or the approved process differed for a justified reason.

### 41 Recalculate evaluated price

P1 | Contract: Rebuild the financial comparison using the announced quantities, options, support costs, tax treatment, currency conversion, discounts, rounding and arithmetic correction rules.

Evidence: Original price schedules, locked model, exchange-rate source/date, formula audit and independent recalculation.

Possible resolution: A higher initial package price wins under a correctly disclosed total-cost or value-weighted formula.

### 42 Test multiple awards and allocations

P1 | Contract: Establish how award count, quantity allocation, technical categories, ties and reserve suppliers were determined.

Evidence: Published allocation method, comparative ranking, award recommendation and records for other relevant lots without conflating their prices.

Possible resolution: Draganfly's allocation followed a disclosed portfolio or capacity-based method, rather than winner-take-all lowest price.

### 43 Compare negotiated and evaluated offers

P1 | Contract: Track changes to price, product, quantities, delivery, liability and conditions after selection. Determine whether they were allowed and materially changed the competition.

Evidence: Negotiation record, bid-to-contract redline, approval of deviations and best-and-final requests where used.

Possible resolution: Clarifications or permitted negotiations preserved the essential basis of competition and required approvals.

### 44 Audit the signed recommendation and debriefs

P2 | Policy and contract: Match final scores, technical endorsement, financial review, approval and award notification. Reconcile regret/debrief explanations with the actual evaluation. [S18, S19]

Evidence: Signed consensus/recommendation, dissent or review findings, approval chain and unsuccessful-bidder correspondence.

Possible resolution: A debrief was abbreviated or protected competitor information, while the underlying record supports the same result.

## Phase 6 Reconcile price options and financial safeguards

### 45 Explain the published value

P1 | Fact and reporting: Reconcile C$439,851.55 to signed line items and the public field definitions. Establish taxes, currency, initial versus amended value and whether options are excluded. [S01, S20]

Evidence: Basis of payment, maximum liability, posting worksheet and amendments.

Possible resolution: The public amount is the authorized firm package while unit rates or future options remain confidential or uncommitted.

### 46 Authenticate the optional quantity and value

P1 | Contract: Verify the issuer's 4,900 additional systems and approximate C$24.25 million against the executed option language. [S03]

Evidence: Quantity bands, option windows, price schedule, training/support options, escalation and tax basis.

Possible resolution: The announcement accurately describes contingent capacity, not a government obligation or booked revenue; rounded publicity differs from precise contractual arithmetic.

### 47 Check the approval value includes what it must

P1 | Policy: Determine how each relevant authority values taxes, options, amendments and contingent liabilities; do not use the firm order automatically. [S17, S18]

Evidence: Approval worksheet, original estimate, exception/delegation and amendment forecasts.

Possible resolution: Required options were included in approval even though they were omitted from initial public reporting or annual commitment accounting.

### 48 Verify mandatory price support consultation

P1 | Policy: If PSPC's current rule applies, check consultation for competitive procurements of C$10m or more including taxes/options/amendments; C$7.5m or more with one compliant offer including taxes/options; and C$7.5m or more including taxes where prices are negotiated or likely to be negotiated by amendment. [S18]

Evidence: Applicable directive/version, estimate, referral, advice and documented pricing decision, or authorized exception.

Possible resolution: Consultation occurred or a genuine exception applied. The public C$0.44m entry is not automatically the relevant denominator.

### 49 Examine price reasonableness and assumptions

P2 | Policy and contract: Test the price analysis actually required, including supplier quotes, learning curves, exchange rates, profit assumptions and comparable package contents.

Evidence: Price validation, comparable buys, cost substantiation where required, negotiation brief and sensitivity analysis.

Possible resolution: Differences reflect payload, support, warranty, volume, delivery urgency or currency rather than excessive margins. A weak value-for-money case alone does not prove illegality.

### 50 Test volume pricing and escalation

P2 | Contract: Check whether option prices decrease, increase, remain fixed or require negotiation, and whether the evaluated model captured the permitted pattern.

Evidence: Quantity-band schedule, indexation formula, FX base date, economic-price-adjustment clauses, renewal rates and amendment rules.

Possible resolution: An apparent price increase follows an authorized index or a different configuration; nominal unit comparisons omit included services.

### 51 Check payment structure and advances

P2 | Law and contract: Distinguish advance payments, progress payments, accepted deliveries and reimbursable costs; test applicable authority, security and reconciliation requirements. [S07]

Evidence: Payment milestones, security/guarantee if required, invoices, holdbacks, liquidation of advances and audit rights.

Possible resolution: An authorized advance bought tooling or long-lead parts with adequate protection; payment before final delivery was expressly permitted.

### 52 Check hidden recurring and lifecycle costs

P2 | Contract: Identify subscriptions, data plans, software seats, licence renewals, batteries, spare parts, repair, training and disposal costs outside the headline price.

Evidence: Complete lifecycle-cost model, software/support agreements, option schedules and end-of-term obligations.

Possible resolution: Ongoing costs were disclosed and evaluated or knowingly budgeted as separate requirements; a low acquisition price was not represented as the whole lifecycle cost.

### 53 Check client concurrence and required reviews

P2 | Policy: Verify DND's written acceptance of specifications/evaluation criteria and required legal, security, risk or other reviews. [S18]

Evidence: Client sign-off, review checklist, comments, resolutions and approvals for deviations.

Possible resolution: A review was not mandatory for this risk category, or the required concurrence exists outside the central procurement file.

### 54 Audit reduced Crown protections

P2 | Policy and contract: Examine unusual liability caps, shortened warranties, disclaimers, indemnities, termination payments and reduced audit rights. Apply required legal review before treating a departure as unauthorized. [S18]

Evidence: Standard-to-final redline, legal review, client acceptance, insurance requirements and approval record.

Possible resolution: A negotiated allocation of risk was expressly approved and reflected in price; it was not an unexplained waiver.

## Phase 7 Examine integrity conflicts and corporate changes

### 55 Verify integrity screening

P1 | Policy and contract: Apply the incorporated Ineligibility and Suspension Policy version and its actual grounds, exceptions and procedures. An allegation or investor association is not disqualification. [S25]

Evidence: Declarations, required names, pre-award checks, relevant final decisions and any administrative agreement or public-interest exception.

Possible resolution: No applicable ground existed, the actor was outside the rule's scope, or a valid exception covered the award.

### 56 Check subcontractor integrity obligations

P2 | Contract: Determine which first-tier subcontractors required screening or approval and whether the contractor satisfied the clause. [S25]

Evidence: Actual subcontractor list, screening dates, declarations, substitution notices and approval records.

Possible resolution: A vendor was outside the clause's defined subcontractor scope or properly screened. Do not treat every ordinary supplier as a prohibited subcontractor.

### 57 Investigate concrete conflicts of interest

P1 | Policy: Look for decision-maker financial interests, family/business relationships, gifts, employment negotiations and privileged access that required disclosure or mitigation. [S26]

Evidence: Declarations, recusals, meeting/contact logs and decision participation, using relevant corroborated facts.

Possible resolution: The relationship was disclosed and effectively managed, or the person had no role in the decision. Mere acquaintance is not proof of favouritism.

### 58 Audit lobbying registration and reports

P2 | Law: Identify the actual paid consultant or in-house role, activity, officials contacted, subject and applicable registration/reporting rule at the time. [S27]

Evidence: Registry histories, mandates, communication dates and official meeting records; distinguish a 2025 registration from a 2026 communication.

Possible resolution: The contact was registered, reportable only under a different rule, exempt or outside lobbying activity. A registry entry alone is not evidence of improper influence.

### 59 Check former official restrictions precisely

P2 | Law and contract: Establish former role, departure date, designated-office-holder status, actual lobbying activity and applicable cooling-off/exemption rules. Treat pension-related procurement clauses separately. [S27, S28]

Evidence: Employment dates, duties, exemptions, relevant advisory mandate and the actual clause.

Possible resolution: The period expired, an exemption applied, no prohibited lobbying occurred or a corporate employee was outside a personal-services disclosure clause.

### 60 Test representations against contemporaneous evidence

P1 | Contract: Identify material statements about eligibility, capacity, origin, security or product readiness and whether they were accurate when certified.

Evidence: Signed attestations, substantiating records then available, verification questions, later corrections and continuing-notification duties.

Possible resolution: A statement was a clearly permitted future commitment rather than a present fact, or a material change was disclosed and approved. Later difficulty alone does not prove an earlier lie.

### 61 Examine post award investment triggers

P2 | Contract and conditional law: The 28 September issuer release describes a planned US$10m offering, not independently verified closing or control. Test actual voting, board, veto, information and technology-access rights. [S41]

Evidence: Closing documents, cap table, agreements and relevant change-notification clauses.

Possible resolution: Minority investment caused no regulated control/access change, or required notices and mitigation were completed. Shareholder nationality alone does not establish non-compliance.

### 62 Check assignment and unauthorized substitution

P2 | Contract: Distinguish a share investment, subcontract, asset transfer, licence change and legal assignment of the Crown contract.

Evidence: Corporate transaction documents, actual performing entity, novation/assignment clause, consent and payment-registration changes.

Possible resolution: The contractor remained unchanged and used permitted affiliates; no assignment consent was required. Separate Investment Canada Act questions require their own factual trigger.

## Phase 8 Test Canadian production rights and capacity

### 63 Establish the applicable Canadian preference

P1 | Policy and contract: Determine whether the strategic Buy Canadian policy applied, including its C$5m threshold for qualifying procurements published after 15 June 2026, SA transition rules and exceptions. [S23]

Evidence: Estimated value including relevant options, RFSA/RFP dates, policy clause, scoring method and exception approval.

## Reporting discipline before an allegation

For each potentially publishable finding, write a short evidence card: exact rule and effective date; who was obligated; triggering event; what happened and when; primary record/page; exception or benign explanation tested; effect on competition, expenditure, safety or performance; unresolved gap; and the subject's response when obtained.

A strong lead is an authenticated mandatory rule paired with a contemporaneous inconsistent action: an ineligible entity admitted without authority; a material criterion quietly waived; an undisclosed score changing the ranking; mandatory option-inclusive approval or price review omitted; an option ordered beyond authority; or payment certified contrary to the contract. A short timetable, a foreign shareholder, a public-page typo, unexercised options or an unsuccessful document search is not enough on its own.

Before publication, frame factual questions around the identified discrepancy and give the relevant institution and company a meaningful opportunity to supply the missing record or explanation. Treat records as potentially incomplete until the complete attachment/version history is checked. Preserve facts that weaken the hypothesis with the same care as those that support it.
