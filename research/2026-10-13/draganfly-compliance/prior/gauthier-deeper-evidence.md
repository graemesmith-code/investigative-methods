# Gauthier, Rubicon and pre-award activity: deeper public-evidence review

Prepared 13 October 2026. Supplemental research to the Gauthier role review. Public sources only; no outreach. This memo separates issuer claims, official proceedings and unresolved questions. It does not allege a breach or determine legal compliance.

## Bottom line

The additional sources establish a wider, public industry-engagement and procurement-advocacy setting. They do **not** yet identify Gauthier as an Astiia deal negotiator, technical-requirements author, demonstration organizer, bidder representative in this particular competition, or recipient of award-linked compensation. His declared consultancy and the two previously identified DND communications remain the strongest direct evidence of his activity for Draganfly.

The most useful additions are:

1. DND's industry consultations were underway before his February advisory appointment. That appointment cannot safely be treated as the origin of Draganfly's access, especially because his consulting registration already dates to August 2025.
2. Public corporate filings distinguish the advisory appointment from changes to employees or corporate directors. They disclose aggregate share compensation, but do not identify compensation paid to him or Rubicon.
3. Official Senate proceedings provide direct evidence of Chell advocating procurement reform in April, with a disclosed conflict precaution by one senator. They do not name Gauthier.
4. Astiia's Australian developers publicly demonstrated the product in September 2025. A subsequent partner statement corroborates Canadian production through Draganfly, but does not date the Draganfly agreement or identify Gauthier's role.

## 1. Corporate role and compensation: what filings actually establish

### February CSE monthly report

The report signed by CFO Paul Sun on 4 March records Gauthier's 20 February Military Advisory Board appointment. In separate reporting fields it says there were no employee hirings and no director/officer/committee-member changes that month. The securities section lists warrant exercises and financing securities, without a separately identified Gauthier advisory grant. Those fields should not be collapsed into an assertion that the appointment was unpaid or that he could not have held an existing consulting engagement. They support distinguishing an advisory appointment from a new employment or statutory board appointment. [CSE February 2026 Form 7](https://webfiles.thecse.com/Draganfly_-_CSE_Form_7_-_Monthly_Progress_Report_-_February_2026.pdf?3jX1coFV1VneDrAA8fWaJDMqtW98XyTE=)

**Evidence test:** Obtain the actual advisory engagement and any amendment to Rubicon's existing retainer, then compare effective dates, payer/payee, scope, compensation and termination provisions. A press-release date is not necessarily a contract-execution date. Publicly inspectable filings reviewed here do not supply those instruments.

### June-quarter financial statements

Neither “Gauthier” nor “Rubicon” appeared in the inspected financial statements. Note 14 reports no stock-option grants in the first six months of 2026, but separately reports 2,018,983 RSUs, including 1,295,279 performance RSUs. Of the total, 1,693,484 went to the CEO. Other grants are not individually attributed in that passage. Consequently, “no option grants” is not equivalent to “no equity compensation.” Note 16 reports aggregate first-half business-development expense of C$1,538,974 versus C$292,558 a year earlier; it does not identify Rubicon as its recipient. Note 18 names other consulting/related-party arrangements, which must not be reassigned to Gauthier. [Unaudited June 2026 statements, notes 14, 16 and 18](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1786286/000149315226036867/ex99-1.htm)

The accompanying MD&A also did not produce a Gauthier or Rubicon name match. This is a limited negative finding from these documents, not proof that fees were zero or that every arrangement required individual public disclosure. [June 2026 MD&A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1786286/000149315226036867/ex99-2.htm)

**Evidence test:** A compensation allegation needs a named agreement, invoice, ledger attribution or grant record. Aggregate expense increases and unnamed equity grants cannot supply that connection. A success-fee hypothesis specifically needs the remuneration formula and the event triggering payment. The publicly reposted vacancy for a different business-development job provides no evidence of Gauthier's compensation.

## 2. MINERVA and demonstrations: government context and access questions

### Official sequence

DND's events list records an industry orientation on 2 December 2025, a virtual industry engagement on 29 January 2026, and a general-purpose uncrewed-aircraft working group on 23 February. Subsequent groups addressed ground vehicles and enabling/support capabilities. Later demonstrations included Valcartier on 10 August and Petawawa on 19 August. The list is useful chronology, but is not a named attendee register and does not identify Gauthier or Draganfly participation in each event. [Army events and working groups](https://www.canada.ca/en/army/services/army-modernization/minerva-initiative/event-working-groups.html)

DND's orientation account describes a CADSI-supported engagement with large and small firms and participation by PSPC, ISED, NRC, DRDC and CAF. It presents early requirements discussion as a broad industry process. That creates a plausible ordinary explanation for supplier contact; it does not prove equal access, complete disclosure or fairness in the later individual procurement. [Army orientation account, 26 January](https://www.canada.ca/en/army/news-publications/2026/01/minerva-orientation.html)

A separate DND account traces Army capability work to a November 2025 Gagetown working group and describes synthesizing corps input before approaching industry. It weakens a simple narrative that one February adviser appointment originated the Army's drone requirement. It cannot exclude later supplier influence on detailed specifications. [DND, 9 February](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/corporate/news/regional-news/western-sentinel/2026/02/3-div-drone-en.html)

### Draganfly-specific participation

Draganfly announced on 19 February that it would attend the 23 February UAS working group, one day before announcing Gauthier's advisory appointment. His earlier consultancy means this chronology does not establish that he had no involvement. [Issuer working-group announcement](https://draganfly.com/press-release/draganfly-to-participate-in-canadian-working-group-for-uncrewed-aircraft-systems-in-support-of-government-of-canadas-newly-announced-defence-industrial-strategy/)

The company subsequently reported an 11 March Area XO demonstration for CAF, naming Commander 3XL, Overwatch, Apex ISR and FPV platforms. The inspected announcement does not identify Gauthier as an organizer or attendee and does not establish an Astiia evaluation. [16 March issuer announcement filed with CSE](https://webfiles.thecse.com/Draganfly_-_NR_Mar_16_2026.pdf?Qes.wb1yu1I4S_Y665AbvUTJqrGb3XUZ=)

**Evidence tests:** Invitations, eligibility criteria, attendance lists, presentations, questions and written answers would distinguish ordinary consultation from preferential access. Specification version histories and an audit trail linking proposed changes to decision-makers would test requirements influence. Demonstration records must identify the actual platform and evaluation purpose; similarity of drone categories is insufficient. A public photo album link was found, but photo contents were not successfully inspected in this pass. No claim about people pictured is made.

## 3. Public parliamentary advocacy and a disclosed safeguard

The official 13 April Senate committee transcript records Chell advocating faster procurement, contracts following successful pilots, operator-industry engagement and financing for production scale. He criticized the lack of an immediate contract at MINERVA's end. This is direct evidence of public policy advocacy before the award, not evidence that a private communication caused a procurement decision. The transcript contained no Gauthier name match.

Senator Marty Deacon disclosed that a relative was employed with Chell/Draganfly, stated that she had consulted the Senate Ethics Officer, and said she would not question him as a precaution. The disclosure is evidence of a stated safeguard, not evidence of misconduct or proof about the award. Do not speculate about the relative's identity or duties. [Official Senate evidence](https://sencanada.ca/en/content/sen/committee/451/secd/16ev-57596-e)

Official minutes list Chell as the Draganfly witness participating by videoconference. They do not list Gauthier as a witness. This does not rule out off-record preparation or advice. [Official minutes](https://sencanada.ca/en/content/sen/committee/451/secd/16mn-57596-e)

**Evidence tests:** Written submissions and hearing-preparation records could establish whose advice informed Chell's proposals. To connect advocacy to the awarded competition, one would still need the decision record, requirement amendments or contemporaneous procurement correspondence. A broadly similar policy objective is not a causal chain.

## 4. Astiia: product origins versus the Draganfly agreement

Mission Systems' 16 September 2025 account says Astiia was designed with AMSL Aero, demonstrated in PCC5 hyper-teaming trials, and shown at DSEI London on 9–12 September. It identifies Mission Systems director David Johnson as attending the exhibition. This is manufacturer evidence of a product and Australian development collaboration before the February 2026 advisory appointment. It supplies no date for Draganfly's later agreement and does not mention Gauthier. Performance statements remain manufacturer claims, not independent acceptance-test results. [Mission Systems announcement](https://www.missionsystems.com.au/post/astiia-the-world-s-longest-range-sub-2kg-drone-debuts-at-dsei-2025)

An Adriano Di Pietro public post, reposted by AMSL Aero, describes the awarded Astiia offering and production in Canada through its Draganfly partnership. It corroborates a partner's account of that relationship. The accessible page gives relative rather than dependable absolute posting times and does not provide the agreement's execution/effective date, licensing scope, exclusivity, subcontract structure or any Gauthier involvement. Do not convert “announced last week” into an exact contract-signing date. [Di Pietro's public post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/adrianodipietro_canada-and-ukraine-to-scale-up-drone-production-activity-7505115795673260032-DQeQ)

**Evidence tests:** The executed license/manufacturing agreement, amendments and bid team responsibilities would establish when Draganfly obtained production rights and who arranged them. Compare those dates with bid submission, technical demonstrations and any Canadian-content representations. A product's pre-existing foreign origin is not itself disqualifying; applicable tender definitions and the actual licensed/manufactured configuration matter.

## 5. What remains unresolved and how to avoid overclaiming

| Question | Evidence required | What would weaken the concerning interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Did Gauthier work on the specific winning bid? | Named bid-team role, instructions, deliverables or correspondence identifying this procurement | Retainer/deliverables limited to general policy or other platforms; absence from relevant bid work confirmed by reliable records |
| Did he influence requirements or evaluation? | Dated input, change log, decision-maker response and access to evaluation process | Publicly distributed inputs, independent scoring, unchanged pre-existing requirements, documented recusals |
| Was pay contingent on government success? | Actual contract/fee formula and payment trigger | Fixed retainer or time-based compensation without award linkage |
| Did he negotiate Astiia rights? | Executed agreement history, correspondence or named deal-team account | Independent commercial negotiations attributable to other parties |
| Were June/July contacts about this tender? | Agenda, briefing deck, minutes, correspondence and follow-up | Discussions limited to other platforms, general policy or Ukraine-related opportunities |

These are evidence requests for a future authorized review, not outreach undertaken here. The public sources inspected neither establish a procurement breach nor provide a complete compliance clearance. The most proportionate conclusion remains: scrutinize the specific contact-to-decision and compensation records, rather than treating military rank, an advisory title, chronology or ordinary industry engagement as proof.

## Search and coverage limits

This pass inspected issuer CSE/SEC materials, Army/DND consultation descriptions, official Senate proceedings and developer/partner statements. Searches combined Draganfly, Gauthier, Rubicon, Astiia, AMSL, compensation, consulting and event terms. Public search indexing and cached pages are incomplete. Absence of a name in a document is reported only for that document. No exhaustive attendee list, advisory contract, Rubicon invoice, shareholder grant register, Astiia license or procurement evaluation file was obtained. Broader lobbying-registry reconciliation is a separate workstream and should be joined before drawing conclusions about total contacts.
