(d) what security assessments, if any, have been done by the government in relation to its usage of the drones, and what were the findings of the assessments?

The drones were acquired and used primarily for research, testing, and technology exploration in controlled environments. Prior to their use, security considerations were reviewed to ensure that operations would present minimal risk and the activities were assessed as low risk. Mitigation measures were implemented to reduce potential data security risks. These included limiting drone activities to mapping publicly visible areas, operating drones using offline software so that data would not be uploaded to cloud services, and preventing the drones from connecting to the internet or internal networks. Additional safeguards were identified, such as enabling multi‑factor authentication, password‑protecting controllers, assessing payload connectivity, and confirming data would not be shared through manufacturer cloud services. Also, the drones were often used strictly in controlled research settings or as aerial targets, meaning they were not used to collect or transmit sensitive information. Standard National Research Council of Canada data security protocols were followed and no sensitive information has been collected through their use.

(e) what steps, if any, are in place to ensure that information gathered as a result of the drones' usage does not end up in the possession of government or government-connected entities in China?

Several operational and technical measures are in place to safeguard information gathered through drone usage from ending up in the possession of government or government‑connected entities in China.

Overall, the approach focuses on limiting connectivity, restricting data collection, and maintaining local control of any information generated during drone operations. In most cases, drones are operated offline and are not connected to the internet or National Research Council of Canada networks. Data is recorded locally on onboard storage (such as Secure Digital cards or dedicated camera systems), and offline versions of required software are used to prevent automatic uploads to cloud services, including manufacturer platforms.

Additional safeguards include disabling optional networking and location permissions on controllers, not registering drones to online manufacturer accounts, and encrypting flight plans or control data where applicable. Some controllers are password‑protected and access is restricted to authorized National Research Council of Canada personnel.

In certain cases, the drones are used only as aerial targets for testing and do not collect, store, or transmit information. Where imaging is used for research purposes, data is stored locally on isolated equipment. Information generated during drone operations is handled in alignment with data security protocols.

These combined measures are intended to keep operational data locally controlled and prevent transmission to external cloud services or foreign entities.

Here are some technical security requirements (originating from the Information Technology Security during the elaboration of a recent Unarmed Aerial Vehicles Special Operations Risk Assessment):

  •  The Unmanned Aerial Vehicle have the ability to be used in offline flight mode. Enforce Local Data Mode (no internet) and no account login.
  •  The Unmanned Aerial Vehicle have the ability to disable external services and all unnecessary network interfaces (e.g., WiFi or Bluetooth) when not used. In limited local mode disable Map Service, Network RTK, Third-party cloud.
  •  The Unmanned Aerial Vehicle have the ability to wipe (secure-erase) internal storage on the drone Embedded Multi-Media Card and clear RC Plus cache after each mission.
  •  The Unmanned Aerial Vehicle have the ability to only enable camera for flight safety and not have to video record while in flight.
  •  The Unmanned Aerial Vehicle have the ability to utilize offline updates only by being able to download installer packages from a vendor trusted official source to a secured standalone workstation (not a Correctional Service of Canada networked device) and apply during a scheduled maintenance window.

Additional best practices, where applicable, include:

  •  Using the offline flight mode;
  •  Disabling external services and all unnecessary network interfaces (e.g., WiFi or Bluetooth) when not used. Disabling map service, network Real-Time Kinematic positioning, and third-party cloud services;
  •  Wiping the internal storage on the drone (Embedded MultiMediaCard) and clearing the remote controller (RC Plus) cache after each mission. Not introducing additional storage outside the product;
  •  Only enabling the camera for flight safety and not using it for recording;
  •  Keeping and maintaining a document that identifies the software update version applied before and after given that vulnerabilities can arise when updates are not verified or when older versions are being used;
  •  Storing the drone, remote controller (RC Plus), and batteries in a locked storage and maintaining an asset log;
  •  Reporting incidents as directed.
