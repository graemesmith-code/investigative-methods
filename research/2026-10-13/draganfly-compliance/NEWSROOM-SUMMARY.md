# Draganfly: what the public record can support

Working report for Graeme Smith, 13 October 2026, updated at the 20:45 UTC checkpoint. Research continues; see technical log and later checkpoints for freshness. **No procurement breach or improper influence is established.**

The strongest new reporting lead is a disclosed lobbying campaign before the drone award, rather than a recent military retirement. Retired Lieutenant-General Michel Gauthier's Draganfly consultant registration began in August 2025, almost six months before the company announced his military advisory appointment. Two reports identify his undertaking's contacts with DND ADM Wendy Hadwen in June and Deputy Minister Christiane Fox in July. They describe CAF applications and, in July, Ukraine-related contracts. They do not identify the September winning bid. The records justify obtaining the meeting material and following any resulting instructions into the procurement file. [Registration](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/vwRg?cno=382133&regId=973399), [June report](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/cmmLgPblcVw?comlogId=691298), [July report](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/cmmLgPblcVw?comlogId=695650).

Companion filings sharpen that inquiry. Michele Cadario reports CAF/government contract discussions with Fox on the same July date. Andy Singh adds Acting ADM Materiel Robert Gray. These may be filings for one shared meeting or separate conversations; counting three meetings or attributing every topic/official to Gauthier would exceed the evidence. A separate consultant, Andre Corbould, reports contacts with Army commander Michael Wright before and just after bidding, including September 10, the recorded award date, and with ministerial adviser Quinn Rinke during bidding. The reports use a broad technology/R&D description, which neither proves nor excludes discussion of this award. [Cadario](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/cmmLgPblcVw?comlogId=696306), [Singh](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/cmmLgPblcVw?comlogId=697309), [Corbould August 31](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/cmmLgPblcVw?comlogId=699618), [Corbould September 10](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/cmmLgPblcVw?comlogId=701800).

The service-exit evidence weakens a cooling-off allegation. Gauthier's registry disclosure says May 2009; DND records his May 11 command handover and Parliament identifies him as retired by November 2009. His exact personnel release day remains unverified. A one-year or five-year period from that era cannot explain a restriction in 2025–26 without a later relevant appointment or return to service. Rank alone does not settle office coverage, and an advisory appointment does not restart a retirement clock. The useful continuing tests are actual confidential-information use, close relationships creating a sense of obligation, procurement involvement, and compensation terms. None is established by the contacts alone. [DND](https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2009/05/change-command-canadian-expeditionary-force-command-449469.html), [Parliament](https://www.ourcommons.ca/documentviewer/en/40-2/AFGH/meeting-16/notice), [dated office regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2008-117/20080702/P1TT3xt3.html).

The procurement itself presents a real document gap. CanadaBuys confirms Draganfly Inc., CW2467949, C$439,851.55, a September 10, 2026–September 10, 2031 term and selective competition against DDI Stream 1. Its RFP-A notice has an aircraft title but a ground-vehicle description, a seven-day published response window and closing-day amendment metadata. We have not obtained the actual package or change history. A public-page mistake is demonstrable; a defective bidder requirement or unfair deadline is not yet demonstrable. The page explicitly says no additional solicitation documents are downloadable there. [Award](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/tender-opportunities/contract-history/cw2467949-000), [RFP-A](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/tender-opportunities/tender-notice/ws5853743341-doc5853864815).

The public value and the potential business are different evidence categories. Draganfly's filed release claims 100 firm systems with equipment/support and approximately C$24.25 million in options for 4,900 more. The contract and price schedule have not corroborated those claims. Neither dividing the published value by 100 nor comparing peers' total values establishes a drone price. A new primary schema check finds the CanadaBuys contract-amount field defined as including taxes, but the exact CSV row and its mapping to the displayed value remain unrecovered. [Filed release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1786286/000149315226042365/ex99-1.htm), [official dictionary](https://donnees-data.tpsgc-pwgsc.gc.ca/ba2/ac-cb/achatscanada-canadabuys-dd.xml).

If the options are as reported and within the relevant procurement estimate, they could make pricing-review and Canadian-content policy tests material despite the smaller published value. PSPC's pre-award-dated guide makes a competitive C$10 million estimate, including options/taxes/amendments, a price-support trigger; C$7.5 million applies to a one-compliant-offer case. The Canadian strategic-procurement threshold fell to C$5 million before this solicitation. Actual application, exceptions, evaluation clauses and approvals remain missing. These are targeted document questions, not demonstrated omissions. [Price review, modified March 25](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/buyer-s-portal/buyer-s-guide/approve/prepare-approval-documents/additional-reviewers), [June threshold announcement](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/news-and-events/buy-canadian-update-strategic-procurement-threshold-lowered).

The trade-agreement field also needs the decision record. It says no agreements apply; it does not identify the rationale. The August 27 CFTA text lists no Canada-specific excluded entities or exceptions and uses Article 901 for national security. A proper security exception could change the governing competition rules. The 2024 GCR amendment expressly excludes qualifying NSE contracts while preserving section 18; therefore an older blanket claim that GCR competition provisions always survive is incorrect. The signed exception letter, scope and pre-award date are decisive missing facts. [CFTA, printed pages 56 and 99](https://www.cfta-alec.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/CFTA-Consolidated-Text-August-27-2026-1.pdf), [GCR](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-87-402/FullText.html), [CITT regulations s.10(3)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-93-602/FullText.html).

Officially, the proposed platform is Australian Astiia technology licensed for Canadian production. That is compatible with the DDI's stated allied-partnership model. The substantive test is the actual rights, production, integration, sustainment and acceptance commitments. Neither foreign design nor later foreign investment automatically proves disqualification. Public FOCI language gives no assessment date or conditions. [PM backgrounder](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/backgrounders/2026/09/10/canada-and-ukraine-scale-drone-production-build-canadas-defence), [DND program backgrounder](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/news/2026/07/defence-drone-initiative.html).

The next reporting move is to obtain a compact set of controlling records: the signed contract and pricing annexes; original RFSA/SA/RFP and addenda; qualification and evaluation decision; any trade-coverage/NSE instrument and security decision; and the identified contact agendas and follow-up instructions. These would establish whether the publicity, recorded procurement and actual official decisions align. They would also allow ordinary market engagement, legitimate licensed production and permitted exceptions to resolve apparent concerns.

The remaining annexes preserve exact links, chronology, disconfirming evidence and failed recovery routes. Quarterly disclosure for a September contract is not late on October 13: the ordinary Q2 deadline is October 30. September monthly lobbying reports are likewise not overdue before October 15. [ATIA s.86](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/A-1/section-86.html?wbdisable=true), [Lobbying Act s.5](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/L-12.4/page-1.html).

The recovery pass confirms three additional federal consultants: Hal Danchilla, Julie DeWolfe and Braedan de Bakker. The latter two official lists display seven further report rows, including older PMO, PSPC and GAC contacts. None establishes a connection to this winning bid. Their [dated review](annexes/recovery-verification-20261013.md) supplies source links and avoids adding filings into a meeting count. DeWolfe's February 16 posting dates also fail a simple lateness hypothesis: February 15 was Sunday, and the Interpretation Act provides a holiday extension. Filing receipt and report completeness remain separate questions.

A pre-award developer statement identifies Mission Systems and AMSL Aero as Astiia development partners. That sharpens the missing-licence question: who granted Draganfly which rights to manufacture, modify and sustain the airframe and embedded technology in Canada? The statement proves neither the Canadian licence nor delivered Canadian production. [Mission Systems, September 16, 2025](https://www.missionsystems.com.au/post/astiia-the-world-s-longest-range-sub-2kg-drone-debuts-at-dsei-2025).

The next substantive pass found a possible notice-revision trail: a search-indexed RFP-A mirror displays September 1 and an aircraft description, while its opened page displays September 3 and ground vehicles. Treat this as a lead for the original version/redline history, not an authenticated deadline extension or defective bidder requirement. [Dated procurement and production review](annexes/procurement-and-production-pass-20261013.md).

That review also narrows the foreign-origin concern: actual supplier status, operational rights and the applicable content method matter more than the technology's country of origin alone. Original June issuer statements add Canadian corporate/subsidiary evidence, but no signed Astiia licence or bid attestation. Public-value reporting, option-inclusive approval estimates and pricing consultation remain separate tests. Candidate RFSA Q&A uploads are explicitly unverified and cannot substitute for the issued qualification package.
