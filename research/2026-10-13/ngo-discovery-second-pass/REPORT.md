# Follow what happens after the permission, ban or sale

Second-pass NGO discovery review for Graeme Smith · 13 October 2026

The first report chose problems largely because two accessible documents could be compared. That made useful interpretation tests the centre of the review, while leaving the reporting question too small. The correction is to make **an unfolding obligation, asset, permission or economic right the unit of investigation**. Follow what happens next, including changes in the company, project, decision and source that matters.

The strongest questions concern whether a restriction actually constrained conduct, whether a banned business still obtained covered contracts, and whether an operating project fulfilled the conditions that permitted it. Mineral-title transfers and returned-money tranches could be equally consequential, but their public evidence paths are less complete. A neat table is neither necessary nor sufficient for a good investigation. Matching remains useful for finding events and identifiers; the harder work begins when an event changes the question and the next source.

This is a reporting agenda, not a findings dossier. No new breach, diversion or corrupt transaction was established. The supplied research includes targeted NGO reading and public-source probes; this synthesis independently rechecked selected official records. No pilot or AI benchmark was run. The [first report and audit](../ngo-discovery-opportunities/REPORT.md) remain unchanged. Two research memos were received and are preserved with their reading audits; a third transmission was cancelled and is not represented as incorporated.

## Editorial order and evidence position

This ranking combines potential news consequence with the strength of the public evidence path. “Ready for a bounded test” means a reporter can test a specified public question, not that a cohort has already been investigated or that every case is observable.

| Priority | Reporting question | Evidence position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did a former official perform an act forbidden by the conditions of their private-work approval? | Ready for a bounded test of public acts. Person-specific restrictions and dated event evidence exist; confidential clients remain outside scope. |
| 2 | Did an excluded business continue winning covered work through a successor or affiliate? | Public sanction/rule path is ready to test. Readiness of awards and business-transfer evidence must be established for the chosen jurisdiction. |
| 3 | Did a project start operating before a prerequisite was satisfied, or continue contrary to an operative condition? | Ready to test public permission histories in an accessible regulator. The Manaus probe supplies useful scope controls, not a breach. Environmental-exception beneficiaries are a conditional extension. |
| 4 | After a mine's sale or suspension, what happened to the title, retained economic rights and public obligations? | Conditional access bet. Public repositories and issuer filings are starting points; the complete project-specific chain is unproved. |

## 1. Test the permission against the former official's actual conduct

**The potential story:** a regulator approved private work on specific terms, but a later, independently documented act fell within a prohibited subject, contact or file. That would expose a failure of monitoring more directly than another list of people who moved between public office and industry.

TI-EU's 2021 Commission study explains why broad consulting descriptions make subsequent compliance hard to monitor. It also cautions against treating ordinary post-public employment as wrongdoing. This is an established institutional problem; the proposed addition is evidence of what happened under a particular approval. [TI-EU study, printed pp.54–55](https://transparency.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/EUIS2_EC.pdf#page=55).

The Commission's published 2024 annual report contains individual restrictions with dates, subjects and exceptions. A reporter can begin with a fixed set of 20 approvals, extract the actual clauses, and distinguish publicly testable acts from private advice that cannot be observed. The 2024 Alexis and Mueller restrictions concern historical windows, not automatically current restrictions. [Commission annual report, PDF pp.6–7 and 10–11](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/e8fc367d-1d98-47ca-8f77-dd0121be3d8e_en?filename=2024_Annual-report-activities-senior-officials-after-service_EN.PDF.pdf).

**What happens next:** an event programme suggests participation. Retrieve a recording or contemporaneous account to establish whether the person actually attended and in what capacity. If the clause concerns former responsibilities or contacts, retrieve the official dossier and the counterpart's role at that date. Then search for amendments, specific permission, substitutions or withdrawal. Only an act whose date, capacity and scope survive those checks should reach legal review.

The supplied Alexis probe illustrates a useful non-adverse control. A March 2023 foreign-subsidies panel is publicly described, but it does not establish activity within his restricted defence/space scope. Co-appearance with a Commission official is insufficient without checking the exact clause and responsibilities. Mueller appearing on a firm's trade practice page does not prove personal service to a prohibited client. These are rejected breach inferences, not declarations that all conduct was compliant. [Research detail and event sources](annexes/ti-monitoring-gaps.md).

**What an agent could add:** the spreadsheet can store names, dates and clauses. A search script can find name hits. The agent's testable contribution is to decide which missing fact would resolve a clause, follow the relevant dossier across languages and agencies, and look for the evidence that disproves the initial suspicion. A demonstrably prohibited act would be new reporting; finding a career move already mapped by TI would not.

**Abandon** an individual branch when personal activity, identity or applicable scope cannot be established publicly. Do not infer confidential client lists. Test the agent against the date spreadsheet and ordinary event search, counting verified new acts and total reviewer time, including cleared cases.

## 2. Follow the business after the exclusion

**The potential story:** the deterrent stopped a named company but not the business, and later covered work reached a successor to which the restriction applied. The question includes lawful continuation: an independent purchaser, expired restriction or non-covered contract can explain the same apparent pattern.

Spotlight's procurement and director-disqualification research describes fragmented records and weak visibility of indirect influence. Ownership/procurement/sanctions graphs already exist, including Open Ownership's 2023 BODS demonstration. The addition must therefore be a dated account of business continuity and legal scope beyond the existing graph, not a name-match warning. [Supplied research, source passages and prior work](annexes/enforcement-blind-spots.md).

The World Bank's official page identifies controlling/controlled affiliates and successors or assigns in its post-2021 grounds, while warning that project rules and cross-debarment coverage differ. Its dynamic list is a public starting point; opening the page is not evidence that a complete historical cohort or award join was downloaded. [World Bank list and notes](https://www.worldbank.org/en/Projects-operations/procurement/debarred-firms).

**What happens next:** a sanctioned contractor announces a sale. Retrieve the acquirer's filing and completion notice: were the business assets and contracts transferred, or just a brand? A subsequent award leads to the procurement recipient's legal identifier and the project's applicable financing rules. A shared address leads to registry history, not a conclusion of common control. Before calling an award incompatible with a ban, retrieve the operative decision, release conditions, amendments, stays and exceptions.

For a UK cohort, do not import World Bank rules or apply the new procurement regime retrospectively. The Procurement Act regime began on 24 February 2025; [Official start-date guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-short-guides/the-procurement-act-2023-a-short-guide-for-senior-leaders-html) and [transitional guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-guidance-documents-plan-phase/guidance-transitional-and-saving-arrangements-html) confirm that transitional procurements require their own rules. Current debarment guidance distinguishes mandatory from discretionary grounds, treats connected/associated persons and subcontractors differently, and allows specified exceptions. [Official guidance, updated 17 August 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-guidance-documents-procure-phase/guidance-debarment-html).

**What an agent could add:** follow a transfer into a new company's filings, discover what was acquired, and then choose the award and legal-scope records needed to test the consequence. Scripts remain preferable for routine list joins and change alerts. The agent earns its place if those triggers produce consequential source discoveries and fewer unresolved identity mistakes.

**Abandon** the proposed jurisdiction if successor, subcontractor or award evidence mostly requires paid registries or private procurement files. A suggested future test is 30–50 sanctions from a fixed 12-month window, stratified before looking for scandal. No such test was executed here. Report sanctions effectiveness or an accountability gap when supported; do not promise proof of covert evasion.

## 3. Follow the conditions into actual operation

**The potential story:** a project began an activity before an explicit prerequisite was met, or continued despite a surviving prohibition. An environmental document missing from a development-programme website cannot establish that story. The record may be held by a different regulator, and a later permission may replace the one initially found.

TI's mining-approval study identifies weak substantive assessment verification. TI-Brazil's 2026 Novo PAC review instead measures information access across portals and acknowledges that environmental records may exist elsewhere. Neither licenses an inference of illegal operation from document absence. [Mining study, printed pp.65–68 and method pp.90–93](https://images.transparencycdn.org/images/2017_CombattingCorruptionInMiningApprovals_EN.pdf#page=66), [Novo PAC review, pp.8–10 and 17–18](https://docs.transparenciainternacional.org.br/biblioteca/transparencia-de-informacoes-do-novo-pac.pdf#page=8).

**What happens next:** a sector regulator announces commercial operation. Resolve the actual project, location, phase and operator, then retrieve the operative environmental permission and its predecessor decisions. A condition requiring an assessment leads to the environmental dossier and approval decision; a mitigation deadline leads to the dated plan, inspection and discharge record. An appeal or emergency route leads to the court order or exemption and its effective scope. Physical-change evidence is triage; it does not itself establish legal status or environmental causation.

Manaus I shows why this matters. The supplied probe found its RIMA outside the programme portal. It describes a 162.905 MW first phase and a later 83.147 MW addition. ANEEL's 1 September 2026 announcement describes 114.8 MW entering partial commercial operation, with 48.1 MW expected later: approximately the first-phase total. The apparent capacity mismatch is substantially explained. This does not establish discharge of every environmental condition. [RIMA, p.9](https://www.ipaam.am.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/RIMA-UTE-Manaus-I.pdf), [ANEEL operating announcement](https://www.gov.br/aneel/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2026-defeso-eleitoral/termeletrica-manaus-i-inicia-operacao-comercial-parcial-com-114-8-mw).

**Environmental exceptions are a consequential extension, with a separate gate.** Identify who obtained a waiver, what activity or safeguard it changed, which conditions survived, and what subsequently happened. The next documents would be the operative exception decision, project-specific implementing permission, amendments and dated inspection or outcome records. An exception's existence is not misconduct, and a subsequent adverse event is not proof it caused the harm. No beneficiary-to-exception-to-outcome chain was supplied or verified in this synthesis; this remains an access bet, not an extra finalist.

**What an agent could add:** recognise that the relevant source has moved from a programme portal to an environmental proceeding, and that the correct comparison has changed from total capacity to an authorised phase. Fixed GIS overlays, identifier joins and expiry alerts remain the baseline. The agent should retrieve superseding decisions and actively reconcile apparent violations.

**Abandon** a project branch when operative amendments are unavailable, site identity is uncertain, field measurement is essential or specialist modelling cannot be assessed from public inputs. A future 15-project test in one regulator should retain cleared phase/renewal cases. Check automatic-extension and renewal rules before treating a visible expiry as illegal operation.

## 4. Keep the mineral project in view when ownership changes

**The potential story:** a suspension or sale changed the company name but left an economically important right or public obligation unresolved. A reporter should ask what happened to the title, residual royalties, state revenue entitlement and rehabilitation responsibility, rather than whether two ownership percentages match.

NRGI's 2024 red flags discuss intermediaries, residual benefits and obligations. Its August 2026 Indonesia analysis explicitly identifies fragmented post-award information; renewed licensing or resumed operations described there are already reported context, not new illegal-operation findings. Indonesia's contract-access gap also makes it an uncertain cheap first jurisdiction. [NRGI post-award analysis, 19 August 2026](https://resourcegovernance.org/articles/what-happens-after-mining-license-granted-indonesia-first-rga), [source-reading audit](annexes/enforcement-blind-spots.md).

**What happens next:** a sale announcement leads to the completion notice, concession identifiers and transfer approval. A retained royalty leads to the transaction schedule and later accounts. A public revenue right leads to the original concession agreement, transfer/novation clause and any approved amendment; testing actual payment then requires attributable public receipts or audited reconciliation. Private retained royalties and government revenue rights must be recorded separately. No original public-revenue-rights chain was delivered in the cancelled third memo, and none was reconstructed here.

ResourceContracts and issuer archives provide plausible original-document routes. Missing annexes, split concessions, renamed projects, security interests and confidential tax records can break the chain. A lawfully retained royalty is not corruption, and a sale does not automatically extinguish—or preserve—every obligation. The agent's contribution would be choosing the next source as each transaction changes the question. “AI reads mining contracts” would reproduce established work.

**Keep this conditional** until one jurisdiction supplies freely readable orders, stable titles, transaction records and the operative obligations. A future 20-project cohort should be selected from a dated census of transfers/enforcement decisions. Stop any payment claim that depends on confidential company taxes or unresolvable pooled amounts. Title lineage could still support an opacity story, but it cannot substitute for proof of lost public revenue.

## Returned money: pursue the tranche only if receipt and expenditure can be followed

A returned-assets agreement creates a serious accountability question, but it is the beginning of a money trail. The independently readable Ibori annex specifies £4,214,017.66, NSIA implementation, mixed project funding, annual reporting and a final-report trigger 120 days after final expenditure. It does not prove that receipt, expenditure or that trigger occurred. [UK–Nigeria annex, paragraphs 3–5, 12–15, 35–40](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/return-of-stolen-assets-confiscated-by-the-uk-agreement-between-the-uk-and-nigeria/mou-between-uk-and-nigeria-on-the-modalities-for-return-of-stolen-assets-confiscated-by-the-uk-annex-1).

The next source is an acknowledged receipt or audited account; then the disbursement schedule, segment-level expenditure and monitoring report. An applicable reporting deadline must be dated before alleging it was missed. Bridge completion cannot allocate one tranche among several funding sources. The possible new finding is a specific prohibited recipient/use, incompatible official accounts of the same tranche, or a demonstrably unmet reporting obligation. TI's enforcement/compensation analysis supplies context, not these last-mile facts.

Keep this conditional until at least one receipt-to-expenditure link is public. Stop on sealed terms, untraceable pooling or inaccessible receipt evidence. Do not subtract differently defined global frozen/recovered/returned totals and call the remainder missing money. [TI tranche design and access limits](annexes/ti-monitoring-gaps.md).

## What to test first, and what success means

Start with conditional revolving-door approvals: it has a precise public restriction, a demonstrable public-act path and a clear scope trap. Run the excluded-business path next only after establishing usable awards and transfer records. The operating-permission path deserves parallel editorial interest once complete amendment histories are available. These are proposed tests, not authorization to run them in this task.

The supervision-register denominator is retained in the enforcement annex as a reserve. Discovering actual service activity and the contracting entity could improve coverage, but unresolved affiliation must not become a rogue-provider count. FCA's September 2026 page says existing arrangements continue and transfer is expected from late 2028, completing around 2030. [FCA current plan](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/aml-supervisory-reform).

A useful agent should close leads as well as open them. Compare against scripts, structured joins and code-assisted human research on the same frozen cohort. Measure independently verified consequential facts, sources newly found, unresolved cases, false-positive reasons and total reviewer minutes. Include Alexis scope and Manaus staging as non-adverse controls. A larger pile of suspicions is not success; neither is a lower token bill with higher review cost.

**External paid spend in this revision: US$0.** Subscription allocation, model usage cost within existing access, human research and legal/domain review costs are unknown. Proposed analyst-day ranges in the supplied annexes are estimates, not measured economics. No paid call, new credential, outreach, complaint, email or pilot execution occurred. The plausible economic hypothesis is that inexpensive agents reduce the labour of maintaining branching public-evidence investigations; it remains unmeasured.
