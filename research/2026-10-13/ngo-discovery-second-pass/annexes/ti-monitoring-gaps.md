# Transparency International: investigative monitoring gaps worth testing

Research date: 13 October 2026. This is a targeted rereading and access-probe memo, not an exhaustive review of TI's decade of publications. No paid calls, accounts, outreach, complaints, or accusations were made. Proposed workflows below are original designs; statements attributed to TI are separated from them.

## Judgment

Three opportunities survive an initial novelty and evidence-access test:

1. **Test the conditions attached to former officials' private work against what they actually do afterwards.** The unit of investigation is a dated act and a specific restriction, not a job change.
2. **Reconstruct the fate of a particular asset-recovery or compensation tranche after the enforcement announcement.** The unit is a traceable sum and its obligations, not an aggregate recovered-versus-returned ratio.
3. **Test the prerequisites and continuing environmental conditions of projects that actually start operating.** The unit is a physical project and operative permission, not a missing document on a programme portal.

None is a claim to have discovered corruption. None requires pretending that the underlying institutional failure is novel. The potentially novel contribution is a continuous, evidence-generating investigation that moves past the NGO's known failure mode and either identifies a specific unresolved contradiction or positively falsifies it.

## 1. Did a conditional revolving-door approval remain true?

### Corpus evidence and institutional mechanism

TI-EU's 2021 European Commission integrity study, printed pp.54–55 (PDF pp.55–56), describes a permission system whose monitoring depends on knowing what former officials subsequently do. General consulting descriptions conceal current and future clients; approvals can be ring-fenced to policy areas and require new requests when the area changes. The study says that without client detail it is “impossible to monitor whether former officials have respected the conditions” (13 words). DG HR is the appointing authority, consulting former departments and other Commission bodies. Its account also warns against equating ordinary post-public employment with wrongdoing. [Report](https://transparency.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/EUIS2_EC.pdf#page=55)

### Concrete access probe, including negative evidence

The [Commission's 2024 report](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/e8fc367d-1d98-47ca-8f77-dd0121be3d8e_en?filename=2024_Annual-report-activities-senior-officials-after-service_EN.PDF.pdf) contains person-specific conditions rather than merely job titles. PDF pp.6–7 specify Alain Alexis's conference topics and co-panellist restrictions for the 24 months after 31 December 2022. PDF pp.10–11 specify Wolfgang Mueller's restrictions on certain clients, former files and professional contacts after 31 August 2022. These dates make historical rather than current-window tests possible.

An [organiser profile](https://www.concurrences.com/fr/auteurs/alain-alexis) records a 6 March 2023 foreign-subsidies conference. A [participating law firm's contemporaneous account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/willkie-farr-%26-gallagher-llp_this-week-brussels-partner-jens-olrik-murach-activity-7039639130338680832-pGkl) identifies Alexis and DG COMP's Christof Schoser on a panel. This demonstrates that a dated public act can be found. It **does not establish a breach**: the subject was not obviously within the listed defence/space restrictions, the relevant responsibilities require verification, and particular permissions may exist.

[Gide's practice page](https://www.gide.com/en/practices/international-trade/) names Mueller and describes trade-defence services. That does **not** establish that he personally served a restricted client on a restricted matter during the restricted period. This is a failed inference, not a lead to publish.

The [2025 Commission report](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/ca839f72-fd27-44d6-b5e9-4dbda7981769_en?filename=C_2025_6426_COMMUNICATION_TO_COMMISSION_EN.PDF) provides a later cohort with explicit conditions on professional contacts, previously handled files and, in some cases, funding-proposal work. It also imposes public-communication qualifications in several cases. Use this cohort to test the design; do not infer absent disclosure from a single page.

### Autonomous investigative sequence

1. Enumerate approvals, all amendments and actual service-ending dates. Convert each into a predicate with a subject, prohibited act, covered institution/client/file, dates, exceptions and source span.
2. Separate public-verifiable restrictions from unknowable ones. Conference participation, published advocacy authorship, disclosed legal representation and named mandates can be public. Private advice and unidentified clients usually cannot.
3. Search an approved individual's *dated* employer/client announcements, event programmes, videos and transcripts, consultation submissions, litigation appearances and official meeting records. Query aliases and employer renamings only to resolve identity, not to manufacture connections.
4. For a candidate act, retrieve the underlying official dossier, former responsibility and exact institutional counterpart. Search for later permission, withdrawal, event substitution and statements identifying personal rather than employer capacity.
5. Produce a contradiction packet only when act, capacity, date and scope are independently supported. Otherwise label the result cleared, unresolved, or unobservable. A public programme proves planned participation; recording or post-event account is stronger evidence of actual participation.
6. Human/legal review and an authorised right-of-reply process precede publication. Do not silently turn an inference into a legal conclusion.

**Cheap baseline:** a permission-date spreadsheet plus structured register joins and event-name searches. **Agent increment:** deciding what evidence would resolve a particular clause, following a dossier across languages and institutions, checking exceptions and disproving superficial matches. If this collapses to keyword matching, use the baseline.

**Novelty boundary:** TI already mapped career paths in [Access All Areas, 2017](https://transparency.eu/access-all-areas/), and TI-UK examined 604 roles in [2023 research](https://www.transparency.org.uk/news/research-reveals-extent-revolving-door-corruption-risk-westminster). Another role-overlap database is not a discovery. Publicly demonstrated noncompliance with a particular permission would be substantively different, but none was established here.

**Pilot/stop rule:** 20 public approvals with precise conditions; approximately 3–5 analyst-days plus model/browser cost to be measured, not asserted. Stop an individual branch when identity, dated personal activity or permission scope cannot be established from public sources. No covert access or guess at confidential client rosters. Advance only if agent-assisted packets have materially more verified new acts per reviewer-hour than baseline.

## 2. Where did this returned-money tranche actually end up?

### Corpus evidence and institutional mechanism

TI's *Exporting Corruption 2022*, printed pp.15–22 and methodology pp.96–98, distinguishes enforcement from victims' compensation. Its scoring uses investigations, cases and sanctions, with expert/government/media inputs and export-share weighting; it is not a last-mile spending audit. One concrete seed is the Amec Foster Wheeler compensation arrangement. [Full report](https://images.transparencycdn.org/images/2022_Report-Full_Exporting-Corruption_English.pdf#page=16)

TI-UK's [Closing the Loop, 9 September 2026](https://www.transparency.org.uk/publications/closing-loop?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) argues that civil recovery and settlement can leave the original corruption unestablished in court, preventing responsible return. Its [publication announcement](https://www.transparency.org.uk/news/suspected-corrupt-wealth-uk-risks-never-being-returned-victims-new-research-warns) explicitly warns that £1.3 billion frozen/seized/recovered and £276 million returned use different definitions and sources. Do not subtract these totals or call the difference missing money. Full Closing the Loop PDF was not retrieved; this evidence is publication-page and announcement depth only.

### Concrete access probe

The [UK–Nigeria Amec compensation MOU](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mou-between-uk-and-nigeria-in-relation-to-a-compensation-payment-of-210610/mou-between-the-uk-and-nigeria-concerning-the-application-of-the-compensation-amount-derived-from-the-deferred-prosecution-agreement-between-the-serio) is publicly readable. A £210,610 compensation sum can therefore be followed separately from much larger penalties.

The [Ibori return annex](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/return-of-stolen-assets-confiscated-by-the-uk-agreement-between-the-uk-and-nigeria/mou-between-uk-and-nigeria-on-the-modalities-for-return-of-stolen-assets-confiscated-by-the-uk-annex-1) identifies £4,214,017.66, a named implementing authority (NSIA), project segments, monitoring arrangements, annual reporting and a final-public-report trigger tied to final expenditure. These are observable obligations. The three infrastructure projects also receive other returned and domestic money: a bridge opening is not proof that a particular tranche paid for it, and a repeated announcement is not another transfer.

### Autonomous investigative sequence

1. Seed from every public return/compensation agreement in a bounded country pair, not famous defendants. Extract amount, currency, principal/interest/cost deductions, order, appeal/finality status, release conditions, recipient account institution (not secret details), permitted uses and deadlines.
2. Build a tranche ledger whose edges require evidence: ordered → actually confiscated → remitted → acknowledged received → allocated → spent → independently verified output. Preserve unknown states.
3. Search both governments' budget documents, implementing-authority audited accounts, parliamentary answers and public monitoring reports. Resolve project renamings and payment-date versus accounting-period differences.
4. Where funds are pooled, seek schedule/segment attribution and independent audit statements. Mark fungibility explicitly; do not falsely allocate the whole project to every recovery source.
5. Only after establishing an applicable reporting/disbursement trigger, test overdue reporting or inconsistent amounts. Search subsequent amendments, delays, refunds, currency conversions and revised project scopes.
6. Escalate a specific contradiction: e.g. an acknowledged receipt allocated to an excluded use, an audited expenditure whose recipient conflicts with an explicit restriction, or two incompatible official accounts of one tranche. Mere inability to find a report is a disclosure gap, not diversion.

**Cheap baseline:** a spreadsheet of MOUs, deadlines and links. **Agent increment:** discovering the next relevant jurisdiction/source from the previous document, tracing changed project/financial identities and closing or falsifying a causal money trail over several accounting cycles.

**Novelty boundary:** responsible return, civil-society monitoring and victim compensation are old TI demands. The investigation must discover the disposition of a specific tranche beyond existing announcements. Neither a global dashboard nor recycled notorious-case summaries passes.

**Pilot/stop rule:** 10 tranches with public agreements and a named implementing agency; 4–6 analyst-days to test source feasibility. Stop on sealed settlement terms, no public receipt evidence, unresolvable pooling or a reporting trigger that cannot be dated. No inference that money was stolen again. No requests for bank access or external outreach in this scope. If no tranche can be traced beyond an announcement, recommend a human-records-access project instead of selling autonomy.

## 3. Did operating infrastructure meet the conditions that made it permissible?

### Corpus evidence and institutional mechanism

TI/TI-Australia's *Combatting Corruption in Mining Approvals* (2017), printed pp.65–68, documents weak substantive ESIA verification, including dependence on proponents' geographic/community information. Annex 2, pp.90–93, uses country-led mapping, interviews and validation; country scope and depth differ, so results are not a comparative index and absent risks are not evidence of safety. [Report](https://images.transparencycdn.org/images/2017_CombattingCorruptionInMiningApprovals_EN.pdf#page=66)

TI-Brazil's *Transparência de Informações do Novo PAC* (January 2026), pp.8–10, studies 99 indicators across three programme portals using 22–26 October 2025 observations. Pages 17–18 discuss missing environmental documentation and the possibility that documents exist elsewhere. This is an access/integration study, not proof that works lacked permissions. [Report](https://docs.transparenciainternacional.org.br/biblioteca/transparencia-de-informacoes-do-novo-pac.pdf#page=8)

### Concrete access probe: UTE Manaus I

The report's named example led to an independent regulator search. It found an [IPAAM prior licence, LP026/2022, first amendment](https://www.ipaam.am.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/300822-LP-No-026-2022-1a-Alteracao-Global-Participacoes-em-Energia-S.A..pdf), process2146/T/13, whose reverse conditions installation permission on EIA/RIMA approval. A [57-page RIMA](https://www.ipaam.am.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/RIMA-UTE-Manaus-I.pdf) is publicly available outside the programme portal. Page9 explains staged development:162.905 MW first phase and an additional83.147 MW later. An apparent total-versus-first-phase inconsistency is therefore substantially explained, not a scandal.

[ANEEL's 1 September 2026 announcement](https://www.gov.br/aneel/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2026-defeso-eleitoral/termeletrica-manaus-i-inicia-operacao-comercial-parcial-com-114-8-mw) confirms partial commercial operation of114.8 MW, with48.1 MW steam capacity expected in December and an earlier physical inspection. Those figures fit the first phase. Environmental condition discharge and successive operative permissions were not fully reconstructed here; no noncompliance is established.

### Autonomous investigative sequence

1. Start from a recent, verifiable operational event or physical expansion, not a search for missing PDFs. Resolve project geometry, operator, licence numbers, stage and dependencies such as pipelines/roads.
2. Retrieve the legally operative licence version and preceding/replacing decisions. Extract prerequisites and continuing conditions with triggers, responsible authority and acceptable proof.
3. Follow cross-references into environmental-agency proceedings, gazettes, sector-regulator inspections, mitigation plans, administrative appeals, court orders and amendments. The next search should be selected by an unresolved obligation.
4. For an ESIA factual claim, test against genuinely independent spatial/administrative sources and dated evidence. A community's absence from a government map is not proof it does not exist. Environmental specialists are needed for technical modelling judgments.
5. Construct chronological alternatives: properly authorised phased operation; revised permission; approved emergency/exemption route; different co-located project; or a real mismatch between activity and operative condition.
6. Report only contradictions that survive the alternatives. A newsroom-ready packet would show actual activity after a prohibition or before a required approval, backed by effective dates and a checked amendment history. Where all approvals reconcile, publish no accusation.

**Cheap baseline:** GIS overlays, licence-number joins, portal-specific alerts and rules for expiry/due dates. **Agent increment:** resolving project stages and jurisdiction, reading conditions as contingent rules, finding superseding records in another agency and actively disproving mismatches. The Manaus probe demonstrates this difference: naive document absence and capacity flags would both mislead.

**Novelty boundary:** TI already knows ESIAs can be weak, and infrastructure portals are fragmented. The new work would identify a particular permit prerequisite ignored in actual operation, or a materially false proponent claim missed by the reviewer. Finding the already-public RIMA is an access result, not investigative news.

**Pilot/stop rule:** 15 projects in one regulator/sector with recent operational announcements, 5–8 analyst-days including domain review. Stop where operative licences or amendments are inaccessible, site identity is ambiguous, the alleged breach requires field measurements, or technically disputed modelling cannot be assessed from published inputs. Use only free public imagery/data already available. Never equate an expired visible licence with illegal operation without checking renewal and automatic-extension rules.

## Ideas tested and downgraded

- **Colombia donor joins:** The chapter already cleaned/mined over615,000 Cuentas Claras records, cross-referenced other systems and published dashboards. Its report uses a November2024 cutoff and distinguishes potential irregularities from established offences. See PDF pp.4–5,9,13–14 of [Alertas de irregularidades](https://transparenciacolombia.org.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Alertas-Irregularidades-en-Financiamiento-Electoral-2025-v.final-1.pdf). Generic donor matching would replicate known work. A potentially different task is resolving whether amended filings cured an original allegation or merely obscured it, but bank/tax facts and human testimony can be decisive. Poverty-register status must not be treated as proof a donation is illicit or a basis for punitive decisions.
- **Procurement after award:** TI's 2022 Integrity Pacts guide already spells out milestone, physical-verification, payment and amendment monitoring, with access and specialist/onsite prerequisites, printed pp.75–78. [Guide](https://images.transparencycdn.org/images/IP-IN_THE-EU_publication_EN.pdf#page=75). AI reproducing this checklist is not a new opportunity; private monitoring access cannot be assumed available to an autonomous public-web agent.
- **Golden visas:** [European Getaway (2018)](https://images.transparencycdn.org/images/2018_report_GoldenVisas_English.pdf) was inspected selectively for scope and diligence mechanisms, not fully deep-read. Applicant files and intermediary checks are not established as public. There was no access-proven, fresh workflow sufficient to recommend this over the three above.
- **Chinese critical-mineral ownership:** [Digging Deeper (2024)](https://mining.transparency.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Digging-Deeper_Global-Chinese-Mining-Sector_2024.pdf) maps81 companies/180 overseas projects and says smaller/private-individual projects may be missing. Read summary, recommendations, grievance mechanism and limitations on coverage; no standalone methods section was found by text search. Rebuilding its ownership map would recreate the rejected shallow approach. Nationality or country CPI is not project wrongdoing.
- **Local planning conflicts:** [Permission Accomplished (2020)](https://www.transparency.org.uk/publications/permission-accomplished) is promising for changes in actual planning decisions, but its full report was inaccessible in this pass. Publication and chapter announcement depth is insufficient to promote a fourth recommendation.

### Limits on breadth

This pass spans underlying2016–2026 activity and publications2017–2026, but is not balanced year-by-year or continent-by-continent. Strongest chapter evidence is UK, EU, Australia, Brazil and Colombia. The2017 global mining report incorporates eighteen national chapters but that is not a substitute for reading all their national originals. Africa/Asia-specific national investigations remain under-read. No new wrongdoing is claimed, no full investigative opportunity is validated by a single access probe, and estimated pilot budgets are hypotheses to measure.

## Acceptance test for any next experiment

Before scale: define a fixed cohort, pre-register baseline and outcome labels, blind-review every proposed contradiction, include cleared cases, measure researcher minutes per independently verified new fact, and keep retrieval failures separate from evidence of absence. A project only earns further investment if it produces consequential, defensible information unavailable from a spreadsheet alert or existing NGO dashboard. The outcome can be a clean falsification; it must not be manufactured suspicion.
