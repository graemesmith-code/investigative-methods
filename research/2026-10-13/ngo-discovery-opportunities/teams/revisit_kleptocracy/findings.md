## RK-02: ownership narratives versus public subsidiary-equity tables

1. **Original bottleneck and date.** The Sentry, [Gaming the System](https://thesentry.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/GamingTheSystem-TheSentry-Dec2022.pdf), December 2022, printed p.17: **“Ivanhoe generally continued to describe the Western Foreland in its publicly available materials as ‘100%-owned’ or ‘wholly-owned’”** (20 words). Read p.5 methodological footnote, pp.9–17 surrounding transaction, permit and disclosure context, and selected endnotes on pp.32–33 and 42. The NGO already reported the named case; its allegation is not treated as a new discovery.

2. **Consequential, falsifiable proposition.** Investor-facing project-ownership narratives can diverge from the issuer's equity disclosures, or appear to do so because the units differ. For each issuer/project/reference date: if the issuer claims *all equity in a specified entity or expressly defined project perimeter*, and its same-period table specifies a lower equity interest in that same unit, flag a documentary discrepancy. If licence rights, consolidated control, a subproject, a changed perimeter or indirect holding-company interests explain the difference—or cannot be mapped—record explained/unresolved, not contradiction. The journalist's initial output is a list of specific questions about public disclosure consistency.

3. **Free inputs, stable joins and measurement equivalence.** Side A: issuer news releases, annual-report narrative and MD&A. Side B: issuer annual/interim financial-statement subsidiary and joint-venture equity tables. Both are original issuer claims, not independent proof of beneficial ownership. Keep issuer identifier, exact subsidiary legal name, incorporation country, project/permit membership evidence, percentage *type*, reference date and publication date. Entity equity, project economic interest, licence ownership and accounting control are distinct variables. Names alone cannot bridge a project to subsidiaries. Define one issuer's full subsidiary table and all associated project narratives as the discovery population; the famous NGO case is an access/control case only. Subsequent issuers must be selected from a fixed exchange/sector roster, not suspicion lists.

4. **Successful bounded originals and failures.** An original [65-page issuer 2020 audited financial report](https://ivanhoemines.com/site/assets/files/5195/ivn_q4_2020_financial_statements_final.pdf) opened successfully. Printed p.37 describes Western Foreland exploration licences as 100% owned. Printed p.53, Note 30, reports Magharibi Mining SAU equity at 90% in both 2020 and 2019 and Makoko SA at 90%/0%; its header supplies the units and year columns. Printed p.14 explains consolidation by control, which is not identical to 100% equity. An original [27 July 2020 release](https://www.ivanhoemines.com/news-stories/news-release/ivanhoe-mines-engaging-in-strategic-discussions-regarding-its-tier-one-assets-in-africa/) also describes the project as 100% owned, but gives Kipushi project ownership of 68% and Kamoa-Kakula of 39.6%. The financial table lists Kipushi Corporation at 68% and Kamoa Holding at 49.5%. These yield an agreement control and a holding-company/project scope trap. Two distinct 2019 filings and the 2020 AIF failed browser access. No alternative route to a failed item was attempted. Different accessible documents supply the pilot; the inaccessible 2019 records remain failures.

5. **AI versus code/human.** Code parses all percentages and company-table rows, dates, exact entities and duplicate text, then creates candidate pairs. AI can classify what an ownership clause refers to, retain qualification and quantifier scope, and propose a project/entity bridge with exact documentary spans. Human review validates every retained pair. A semantic model must not turn a 90% subsidiary plus a 100% licence claim into a contradiction. `pilot.py` implements conservative numeric pairing and controls, providing a baseline rather than an asserted model result. An additional companion step compares human review with and without model-proposed interpretations on the same blinded pairs.

6. **Innocent explanations and missing evidence.** Strongest explanation is that a licence or project perimeter can differ from the economic equity in the legal holder; corporate control can permit consolidation below 100% ownership. Project scope may change over time. The July 2020 publicity and December 2020 financial table do not share an exact snapshot. The same financial report's p.37/p.53 pair removes the publication-date mismatch but still requires an original dated subsidiary-to-permit mapping. The NGO asserts membership; that attribution cannot substitute for an issuer or official bridge. Missing title/perimeter/equity instruments block a false-disclosure conclusion, not the extraction/abstention benchmark.

7. **Why now and measured criterion.** Ownership-table extraction and arithmetic are established methods. Cheap models offer a plausible way to interpret prose at scale, but savings are unmeasured. Do not call this newly possible in 2026. On at least 20 human-labelled pairs, require no false allegation flags on the scope traps, at least 95% field accuracy, and a 25% reduction in median total review minutes at equal or better precision versus code-assisted human review. Include correction time. Fail the AI component if the code/human baseline performs as well. This criterion permits a useful negative result.

8. **Prior coverage and proposed new work.** The Sentry already identified the Western Foreland discrepancy. Reproducing it is a positive/access control, not a story. New work would be a portable, issuer-wide scope-aware screen nominating previously unexamined matched-unit contradictions or showing that apparent ones are explainable. No new contradictory subject is claimed here.

9. **Pilot, stop/go and costs.** Use the 2020 report's entire subsidiary table (no hand-picked suspicious rows) and the issuer's dated public narratives around the same reporting period. Produce 20 labelled pairs, including the documented Kipushi match, Kamoa scope trap and Western Foreland unresolved bridge. Then preregister a 10-issuer sector roster and apply the passing method only if authorised. Stop if documents or exact same-unit joins are unavailable; report missingness by issuer. Estimate 10–20 hours human work for the initial pilot, separately costed; 200–500 pages × 1,000 tokens/page = 0.2–0.5 million input tokens if expanded. Local model on already owned hardware: cash can be $0, compute/electricity unknown. Optional future model allowance $25 and cash stop $200 are design caps, not calls or permission. All present checks and baseline runs cost $0. No paid requests or outreach.

## RK-01: implicit GPU configuration and variant interpretation

1. **Source passage.** C4ADS [Covert Compute](https://c4ads.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Covert-Compute-C4ADS.pdf), 9 September 2026, printed pp.7–8; exact 22-word p.8 bottleneck is retained in `../kleptocracy/findings.md` and linked from this revisit inventory. Re-read the methodology, limitations, pp.29–31 variant examples, and printed p.32 endnote 13. **Baseline correction:** C4ADS made 371,442 government documents text-searchable; endnote 13 says GPU-specification and controlled-chip keywords were also tried. Announcement-only search is an artificially weak baseline and is withdrawn from the proposed comparison.

2. **Falsifiable test.** Given whole-document OCR and a comprehensive GPU alias/specification lexicon, can a semantic configuration reader recover explicit but nonliteral configured variants, conditional clauses, quantities and tender-versus-award status that the baseline misses? Require original documentary spans; reject generic compatible server options and any inferred installed GPU without explicit configuration evidence.

3. **Public data and joins.** Original Chinese procurement notices/contracts/attachments and dated manufacturer configuration/specification documents; join by project ID and attachment provenance, then exact SKU, memory/form factor and revision. The manufacturer's specification maps capability only. Preserve announcement/contract/reference dates and change notices. Do not infer actual shipment, foreign-product-rule jurisdiction, export date or licensing status from public procurement.

4. **Sample result.** Prior two original CCGP intention opens returned 502 and the UCAS contract timed out. None was retried or routed around. Prior NVIDIA original PDF specification table passed a bounded check; Dell's proposed server page redirected without a configuration. This side does not establish a public procurement/configuration pair. Readiness remains conditional, even though the benchmark is worthwhile.

5. **Work allocation.** Code/OCR/fulltext aliases and table parsing are mandatory baseline. AI interprets configured-versus-optional variants, multilingual conditional language and scanned layout; human verifies the bridge. Restriction classification and legal conclusions are outside the model.

6. **Innocent explanation/missing evidence.** Cancelled procurement, a permitted variant, lawful domestic old stock, an export licence/exception or non-delivery can all explain a notice. Original configured award/contract, acceptance and historical export/licence evidence are missing. Modified aftermarket memory is not proof of a manufacturer's stock SKU or illegal export.

7. **Why now.** Low model cost may help interpretation; no demonstrated 2026 capability discontinuity. Require 95% field precision, at least 25% saved total reviewer minutes at comparable recall, and incremental valid configurations beyond whole-document OCR+aliases on 50 source-selected contracts. No positive result is asserted.

8. **Prior/new.** The NGO already includes bundled servers and modified GPU variants and searched specification terms. Only demonstrated residual semantic recovery would be new; a richer alias list or reading the whole PDF is ordinary method improvement, not an AI novelty claim.

9. **Bounded plan/cost.** Fixed publisher/month, first 50 server contracts in publication order; ten human-labelled configurations; code baseline then local-model companion. Stop without lawful original access or exact configured-SKU spans. Estimate 500 pages/0.5 million tokens and 10–20 review hours; $0 local cash on available owned hardware, infrastructure unknown, optional $25 model cap/$200 cash stop not used. Investigative export allegations require a separate evidence gate.
