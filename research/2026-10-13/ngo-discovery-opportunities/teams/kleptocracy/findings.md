# OCCRP, The Sentry and C4ADS: selective opportunity review

**No finalist passes the public-data access gate in this tranche.** The most promising parked design would read equipment configurations buried in Chinese public procurement attachments. It could recover documentary facts missed by literal chip-name searches. It would establish what institutions advertised or contracted to buy; it would not establish smuggling, delivery, absence of an export licence, or actual beneficial control.

## Parked design K-01: recover GPU configurations behind server names

Source: C4ADS, [Covert Compute](https://c4ads.org/reports/covert-compute/), 9 September 2026; [original PDF](https://c4ads.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Covert-Compute-C4ADS.pdf), printed pp.7–8, with acquisition context pp.11–14, appendix pp.25–31 and endnotes pp.32–35 examined. Exact bottleneck, p.8: **“It does not capture shipments in which controlled chips are embedded in systems labeled under a different server brand”** (22 words). Its procurement evidence is referenced to holdings available on request, not linked to an openly downloadable original corpus. The report already investigates public contracts. Extending its keyword screen to configurations embedded in attachments would be the proposed new method, rather than repeating its named cases.

**Falsifiable proposition:** a prospectively selected sample of public server procurement files contains explicit GPU configurations that chip-name searches of the announcement alone miss. If a notice lacks a chip name but its same-project attached specification identifies a GPU and quantity, count one recovered configuration. If the attachment merely lists compatible options, count zero. Any eligibility comparison must use the actual configured SKU and the rule effective on the relevant transaction date; an award date cannot establish export date.

**Inputs and bounded checks:** Chinese Government Procurement Network procurement intentions, awards and contracts; university-original procurement platform records; manufacturer-issued dated SKU datasheets. Search-index results identified two intention records, with stable `projId` values, and a UCAS contract with project number `4000005791760423001225` and contract number `251190124757ZY`. Direct browser opens of the two intentions returned 502; UCAS timed out. These are failed original checks, not acquired records. An actual three-page NVIDIA manufacturer PDF was opened successfully, including its p.2 technical specification table and Sep24 revision. The requested Dell XE9680 product URL redirected to a generic homepage, so the server-to-installed-GPU bridge was not established. See `checks.json` for exact URLs and outcomes.

**Join discipline:** join announcement to contract/attachment through publisher project ID; join configuration to manufacturer SKU, form factor, memory and revision, never a server family name alone. Distinguish procurement intention, tender, award, signed contract, acceptance, export and delivery. Retain original Chinese passages and spans. Preserve historical rule versions, effective dates and exceptional/licensed transactions. No name-based expansion to supposed military end-users.

**AI contribution:** translate and extract configuration clauses from heterogeneous Chinese tables and scanned attachments, distinguish minimum requirements from awarded equipment, and propose SKU mappings with quoted spans. Code handles downloading permitted records, deduplication, ID joins, quantities and date arithmetic. Humans confirm every retained configuration, OEM equivalence and legal relevance. A vendor brochure proves possible configuration; it does not prove configuration purchased. A simple regex baseline must be tested first; if it performs as well, AI is unnecessary.

**Strongest innocent explanations:** a plan was cancelled; a contract used a permitted GPU option; chips were domestic stock legally obtained before restrictions; a licence or exception applied; equipment was never delivered. Required missing evidence is original contract/configuration and acceptance, plus export date and licensing provenance for any prohibited-export conclusion. No inference of criminality follows from this screen.

**Why now:** cheap multilingual extraction makes reviewing attachments potentially affordable, but no evidence here establishes that the task first became possible in 2026. C4ADS' own 2026 project demonstrates large-scale text search was already used; its report also cites a 2024 procurement investigation. This is a possible labour-saving increment. It remains unvalidated until the attachment retrieval and extraction benchmark succeeds.

**Reproducible proposed pilot, not executed:** fix a month and one publisher before searching; select the first 50 server procurement notices in publication order. Archive only permitted originals outside Git, with hashes and metadata. Human-label intention/tender/award/configuration fields and GPU types in ten files; compare literal-keyword extraction with a local/open model. Stop if original attachments cannot be obtained lawfully, if no exact project join exists, or if reviewer-confirmed precision is below 95%. Go only if at least three clear configurations are recovered beyond the announcement-only baseline, with all claims limited to documentary procurement facts. Do not contact authorities as a substitute for missing online evidence.

**Projected costs (estimates):** $0 source fees if documents remain freely accessible; 50 × 10 pages = 500 pages, approximately 500,000 extracted tokens assuming 1,000 tokens/page. Local-model cash can be $0 on already owned hardware; compute/electricity and hardware availability are unknown. Human validation estimated 10–20 hours, separately costed and not represented as free. An optional future model budget cap of $25 and total cash stop at $200 are design limits, not authorization or tested prices. No paid calls were made.

## Coverage and limitations

The inventory is deliberately honest about discovery versus reading. Official Sentry Report Central exposed 94 links including translations. 88 distinct landing-page URLs were recovered across bulk browsing and contextual follow-up; nine identified translation mirrors map to originals. Some catalog targets failed, including occasional redirects/external pages. Dates are unverified for most Sentry landing records, and several catalog entries are policy briefs or series indexes whose substantive eligibility needs checking. The two PDF versions are not additional works.

OCCRP also has one independently opened methodological project page (2022-11-30) context-read. Its archive was purposively sampled at archive pages 1, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80 and 90. The catalog parser recorded 108 title/date targets; 88 distinct report URLs were recovered. Those 88 dates were joined by exact normalized title to the official catalog. Page numbering and totals differed across cached page responses; this is not an exhaustive decade census, and no numerical total for the entire OCCRP archive is asserted. Much of the sample depends on leaked financial evidence; 20 batch segments contained text, but their body-to-header attribution is unreliable; none counts as an acquired source or context-screened report here.

C4ADS' current report archive exposed nine titles. Its advertised archive pages 2–6 returned 404 through the browser; its XML sitemap was inaccessible. Four purposive official-site searches for 2017–24 supplemented the first archive page. Twenty distinct landing works were recovered, with 12 dated source-version records (including the PDF version) verified in-window. Search results are not counted as fulltext reading.

Across this tranche: 200 source-version records; 188 canonical works discovered (many Sentry dates/eligibility remain unverified); ten independently context-verified rendered excerpts/partial texts acquired; zero original fulltext files downloaded; four sources' selected fulltext sections context-screened and deeply read; six relevant landing-page abstract contexts read; zero finalists. Original PDF pages are reported from the browser; original bytes/hashes remain null. Recorded non-null hashes and bytes cover browser segments only. Batch segments have uncertain source attribution and are separately labelled; contextual follow-ups verify the six relevant landing pages. Copyrighted caches remain in `/tmp` and are excluded from Git. Root aggregation should use the explicit fields in `counts.json`, not call 199 reports “read.”

Shell requests to NGO sites, robots files and sitemap candidates returned 403. Aggregate browser batches sometimes associated an adjacent body with a different article header. All such batches were downgraded to attribution-unverified and excluded from acquisition counts. No denial or TLS issue was bypassed. Browser access remained available. Scope is 13 October 2016–13 October 2026, but an unknown date is not silently treated as within scope. No paid API calls, no outreach, no allegations and **actual authorized cash spend $0**.
