# Secrecy, ownership and extractive payments: selective review

**No unconditional finalist from these three organizations.** The closest reusable journalism method is a multilingual audit of whether extractive-payment disclosures describe the same payment before comparing numbers. Both sides of a recent DRC sample are downloadable, but a necessary reconciliation annex is separately referenced and absent from the published workbook. An already published discrepancy is not a fresh lead.

## Parked method NRGI-PTG-01: nominate payment descriptions that fail a documented comparison

Oil and mining companies publish payments to government. EITI reports publish companies' declarations and government receipts. A journalist could systematically identify which payments remain unexplained after reconciling the rules, instead of starting with a notorious company. The consequential proposition is narrowly this: **a stated company payment and a stated government receipt concern the same obligation, legal payer, recipient, amount basis and financial period, yet their unexplained residual exceeds the published measurement tolerance.** This tests disclosure consistency; it does not itself establish missing money, theft or tax evasion.

The exact original bottleneck is NRGI's *DRC’s Mining Revenues: Increasing Accountability by Analyzing Payments to Governments Reports*, 18 December 2020, printed p.29: “This single category appears to be insufficient for capturing the DRC’s different taxes and parafiscal tax-like payments.” [Original report](https://resourcegovernance.org/sites/default/files/documents/drc-mining-revenues-increasing-accountability-by-analyzing-payments-to-governments-reports.pdf). The report's p.10 methodology cleans reporting errors and duplicates; pp.17–18 distinguish projects from legal entities; pp.29–31 explain scope and classification differences; pp.35–36 provide a fiscal-term/payment-category/recipient codebook. Its case-study gaps expressly are not evidence of misreporting.

**Falsifiable test:** If a canonical payment tuple `(payer legal ID, project/license set, recipient agency ID, obligation code, cash/in-kind basis, currency method, period)` exists on each side, code computes a residual after applying only documented mappings. If the residual disappears when applying the stated consolidation, cash basis, recipient split, scope exclusions, amendments or FX method, the contradiction is falsified. If any element cannot be bridged, label it incomparable and stop. Do not compare a statutory rate with a payment absent its lawful base, deductions and timing.

**Public sides and successful bounded acquisition:** [DRC's official 2023 report and annex catalog](https://www.itierdc.net/all-repports/?cat=RAPPORT_ITIE_RDC&page=1) supplied a 191-page report (4,231,967 bytes), a 7,329,282-byte annex workbook and a 406,708-byte summary workbook. [Ivanhoe's official 2023 ESTMA report](https://www.ivanhoemines.com/wp-content/uploads/20231231-00000-Ivanhoe-Mines-Ltd-ESTMA-Reporting-template-vF.pdf) supplied its actual payment tables and explanatory notes. All four received HTTP 200, original files were parsed, and hashes are in `drc_pilot_acquisition.json`. The 2024 company report was also checked, but it is not compared with 2023 EITI data. This is stronger than a catalog check, weaker than a successful same-measurement reconciliation.

**Concrete bridge facts and failed gates:** Both selected reports concern calendar 2023. ESTMA uses USD, rounds to the nearest USD 10,000, declares 100% of Kamoa-Kakula joint-venture payments rather than Ivanhoe's 39.6% share, and uses a stated year-end CDF/USD rate of 2,662.70. Its amendment corrects a South African community-payee classification, illustrating why original-versus-amended reports matter. The independent audit identifies Kamoa's local tax ID `A0901048A` and license records, but a parent/project label is not yet an independently verified subsidiary/payment crosswalk. EITI's Annexe 52 directs readers to a separate Excel document; the downloadable annex has only five rows for this sheet and contains no public hyperlink to the missing reconciliation. Do not access a private external-workbook reference. EITI's p.139 already discusses the Kamoa final company-state discrepancy. A bare royalty-total comparison would ignore unproved tax composition and FX equivalence. See the separate independent audit for row-level evidence.

**AI's bounded role:** propose candidate French/English aliases from table headings and surrounding notes; extract consolidation, accounting, in-kind, rounding, conversion and amendment conditions with exact page/cell references; classify which obligations a broad label includes; identify ambiguity for human review. Code must handle downloads, table parsing where reliable, deduplication, joins on verified identifiers, conversions from documented rules and arithmetic. A reporter or fiscal specialist must approve entity/project and obligation mappings and resolve legal interpretation. A model must never decide that similar names represent one entity or that an unexplained difference is wrongdoing.

**Strongest innocent explanation:** the company and EITI deliberately report different scopes or valuation/timing conventions, with legitimate provincial taxes and government entities classified differently. NRGI documents such differences. Required missing evidence includes the detailed reconciliation workbook, exact current obligation definitions, legal payer/project crosswalk, government agency apportionment, transaction-currency conversion method and cash/in-kind cutoff. Hidden bank transactions remain unavailable.

**Why now and what is new:** the 2023 EITI report was published on 31 December 2025; it makes a more recent cohort available. NRGI performed these comparisons manually in 2019–20, so neither cross-disclosure checking nor basic extraction originated in 2026. Cheap language models might make repeatable bilingual footnote/codebook extraction affordable across new years; that is an untested cost-saving hypothesis, not a demonstrated capability breakthrough. Sherpa's 2016–17 work independently described fragmented, non-machine-readable payments and warned about measurement mismatches. Repeating NRGI's examples or EITI's published discrepancy has no novelty. The potential new work would be an evidence ledger for *previously unexamined later-year payments*, selected by failed comparability gates, followed by original document verification.

**Small reproducible pilot and stop/go:** enumerate one country's latest complete EITI year and all matching company disclosures; take a deterministic ten-report sample, including a known amended report and three negative controls with documented scope differences. Extract the canonical tuple, require exact evidence spans for every mapping and manually verify all candidate inconsistencies. Compare a code/rules baseline with a model-assisted extractor without selecting famous suspects. GO only if at least 90% of required comparison fields are evidenced, all ten-report numbers match manual transcription, the negative controls produce no allegation-like false positives, and at least one residual concerns a fully bridged, previously unreported obligation. STOP if the reconciliation annex remains missing, human review exceeds its budget, or the AI arm adds no measurable accuracy/time benefit. The current DRC sample fails the missing-annex and novelty gates, so is HOLD.

**Estimated pilot affordability, not actual spend:** ten reports/roughly 300 pages, up to 300,000 input and 30,000 output tokens, one extraction plus one independent check, with retry overhead budgeted at 2×. Model cash allowance USD 25 and optional local OCR/compute allowance USD 25 are conservative budget envelopes, not vendor quotes. Exact token pricing belongs in the parent's current official-pricing annex. Human bilingual/fiscal review: estimated 8–20 hours, hourly cost unknown; source-access fees zero for the checked files, but unavailable annexes are not priced into existence. Actual spend: USD 0; no model API calls, outreach or paid services.

The shared 2025 PSC report counts once as a work and once under each co-author organization. Organization rows therefore cannot simply be added for unique fulltext work counts. Acquired web sections do not mean an entire PDF was downloaded or read; metadata hashes in those rows refer to the extracted landing representation, not original PDF bytes. Fulltext-screened means substantive surrounding passages were actually reviewed, not that every page was read. Deep review means relevant methods, limitations, cases and supporting sections were read, not a claim to page-by-page exhaustive reading.

NRGI: a fresh sitemap enumerated 5,169 URLs, including 455 canonical English publication URLs. 452 landing responses were acquired; three returned 403. Verified publication dates put 299 within the decade and 151 outside it; two acquired pages lacked a usable date (five unresolved records including failed pages). Sitemap modification times were not substituted for publication dates. All non-English publications and substantial investigations appearing only under `/articles/` remain a coverage gap. This is a broad English catalog census, not a complete NRGI decade review.

TJN: the official report catalog exposes 110 links from 24 October 2016 through 11 September 2026. All canonical report landing links and catalog dates were resolved. Submissions and policy comments remain in the census with distinct type/eligibility fields, rather than padding report counts. Index methodologies, blog investigations and non-catalog reports may be absent. Direct report/site downloads returned 403; the legitimate web retrieval tool supplied selected public report sections. No access denial was bypassed.

Open Ownership: all 13 English publication catalog pages were visited. 123 candidate cards resolved to 109 distinct publication landing URLs; stale/dynamic pagination duplicated some cards. The card filter excludes short tool/navigation labels and may omit unusually short publication cards. Standalone technical tools and consultations were marked separately. Cross-language versions were not separately counted. Founding-to-date coverage is through the available English catalog (oldest resolved publication 29 March 2018), leaving any earlier publications and omitted short-title cards as explicit gaps.

No organization was given a quota of finalists. These results are selective research design, not a live investigation or finding against any person or company.
