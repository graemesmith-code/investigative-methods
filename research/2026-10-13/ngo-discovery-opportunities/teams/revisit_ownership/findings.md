# Revisited: public rights and ownership-history interpretation

**Pilot-ready: interpret the rights and effective dates already disclosed in corporate filings, then compare only evidenced ownership declarations.** This is a small document-reading experiment, not a concealed-control finding. The initial review was too restrictive in requiring methodological novelty or demonstrated AI improvement before allowing a pilot. Neither is a prerequisite for testing a public, bounded extraction task.

## RIGHTS-HISTORY-01 — pilot_ready

1. **Original bottleneck and source.** The TJN/Open Ownership report *Insights from the United Kingdom's People with Significant Control register*, 13 May 2025, printed pp.7–8 explains share/vote/appointment interests and its structured-data methodology; pp.19–21 explain deduplication, time changes and percentage limitations. Its printed p.20 names the problem “range-based values”. This is not proof that a named owner has an exact 100% stake. [Original](https://taxjustice.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/oo-tjn-research-paper-uk-psc-register-2025-05.pdf). Current [Companies House guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/people-with-significant-control-pscs), updated 30 July 2026, expressly directs companies to check their constitution/articles and shareholder records for voting and other rights. The [2026 significant-influence guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/people-with-significant-control-2026-company-statutory-guidance/statutory-guidance-on-the-meaning-of-significant-influence-or-control-over-companies-in-the-context-of-the-register-of-people-with-significant-contr) also distinguishes first-three-condition reporting from fourth-condition influence; applying exceptions requires the actual facts.

2. **Consequential proposition and test.** Some public statements describe share classes, voting or appointment rights that cannot be inferred from the PSC graph alone. If a named legal holder's shares and operative rights are explicitly disclosed for the same period, classify the evidenced PSC condition/range and compare it with that period's filed declarations. If a later amendment, different class, delayed effective date, excluded entity, indirect holding or incomplete member register explains the difference, the contradiction is falsified or the case becomes incomparable. The output is an evidenced question about disclosure consistency; never an inference that a different person secretly controls the company.

3. **Two free public sides and joins.** Side A is Companies House's public PDF filing history: incorporation/IN01 prescribed particulars, articles/resolutions, capital statements, shareholder-update CS01s and PSC change forms. Side B is the public current/ceased PSC page and dated PSC notification/cessation filings. These are two reporting views from the **same registry**, not independent claimants: the company is the original declarant on both. Exact eight-character company number joins corporate records; share class, number and operative document/effective dates join the rights claim to its declaration. Person names match only within the same company's complete disclosed event chain, with alternative people explicitly disambiguated; no cross-company person identity inference is allowed. A same-company identifier is not sufficient to resolve a person outside that chain. Filing date, statement date and transfer/notification effective date are separate fields. “No updates” does not mean a complete new shareholder list.

4. **Successful bounded record check.** IDs `11800000` and `11800001` were selected as sequential seed controls before reading names. Four public history/PSC pages returned HTTP 200. Four PDFs totaling 17 pages were downloaded from links on those official pages; three were image-only and free local Tesseract OCR recovered text. No access denials or TLS warnings occurred on these sources. `public_filing_samples.json` records exact URLs/bytes/hashes. For 11800000, incorporation PDF p.4 describes one ordinary share with full voting/dividend/distribution rights; updated CS01 p.2 records that share's 31 March 2019 transfer; PSC01 p.2 records the ownership condition and its effective date. Those documents establish usable evidence spans, **not a legally verified missing-condition finding**. The second seed's 2026 CS01 explicitly has no updates and supplies no shareholder table; it is a negative control that the extractor must not turn into an empty shareholder population. Its PSC page supplies separately labelled share, voting and appointment interests. No individuals are named in this report.

5. **AI versus code and human.** Code acquires the files, parses/OCRs them, lists amendments, extracts dates/numbers, preserves exact company IDs and calculates thresholds after rights are established. A small model can propose a structured interpretation of prose prescribed particulars/articles (non-voting shares, conditional voting, veto/appointment rights), distinguish present from contingent rights, connect a superseding amendment to its prior clause and identify abstention-worthy ambiguity. Regex can detect words but cannot reliably resolve arbitrary clause qualifications cheaply. This is a testable hypothesis; no model was run. A legal-capable human validates operative rights, current legislation, exceptions and whether the filing series is complete. Private shareholder agreements and actual informal influence stay unavailable unless independently public.

6. **Strongest innocent explanation and missing evidence.** Apparent differences may arise from different PSC conditions, the lawful suppression of a redundant condition, amended rights, incomplete shareholder snapshots, nominee/indirect arrangements, ceased declarations, image OCR errors or filing delays. Do not assume all disclosed voting rights are currently exercisable. The sample's one-share rights/transfer chain still requires the entire intervening amendment/capital/PSC history and the precise applicable reporting rules before any inconsistency claim. For public complex rights not fully documented, abstain. A documents-only screen cannot certify true ownership.

7. **Why now.** The economic opportunity is routine machine-readable/OCR acquisition followed by low-cost prose interpretation at a scale a reporter can audit. The underlying public filings and PSC tests are established. The 2025 report already publishes Python graph analysis; the 2026 government guidance is an updated rule source, not evidence that this method was impossible earlier. Cheaper multilingual/document models make a modest interpretation experiment plausible; improvement remains to be measured.

8. **Prior coverage and incremental work.** TJN/OO have already analyzed share/vote distributions and ownership outliers. This pilot does not rebrand those results. Its increment is a reproducible evidence ledger connecting *specific disclosed rights clauses and later amendments* to dated declarations, using a data-selected cohort. No prior-coverage sweep sufficient to establish a scoop was conducted. Publishable novelty is a later reporting gate, not a prerequisite for the interpretation benchmark.

9. **Reproducible pilot, baseline, stop/go and costs.** Run `pilot.py` for the two bounded transport controls. It uses Python stdlib, `pdftotext`, `pdftoppm` and Tesseract; original documents stay in `/tmp`. A future five-company sample should come from a fixed company-ID/date cohort, retain unsuccessful draws and include ordinary, non-voting, contingent-vote and “no update” controls. Collect the full relevant history before interpreting. Label at most 100 pages for operative rights, event dates, overrides and “insufficient evidence”; compare regex/rules+human versus OCR+model+human. GO to discovery only if every selected numeric assertion matches the document image, the model supplies correct page-bound evidence for at least 90% of interpretable clauses and no negative control becomes an ownership allegation. Otherwise revise or stop. These thresholds govern the experiment, not pilot_ready classification. Estimated cash cap USD 25 for model extraction/checks and USD 10 optional compute, assuming 100 pages, 150,000 input/20,000 output tokens, two passes and 2× retry overhead; exact current official prices are in the parent's pricing annex. Human review estimated 6–12 hours; hourly cost unknown. Actual cash spend USD 0, no paid API calls.

## LAND-EXEMPTION-01 — conditional_blocked

The 26 January 2026 Open Ownership land-register report, [Scottish/BC chapter](https://www.openownership.org/en/publications/bridging-the-gap-for-effective-asset-transparency/registers-of-beneficial-ownership-of-land/), distinguishes legal ownership, tenancy and controlled-interest relations. It explains that a recorded person under a recognized transparency regime can be exempt while an associate under such a regime does not exempt a different recorded person. The [official RoS exemption guidance](https://kb.ros.gov.uk/rci/do-i-need-to-register/transparency-regimes), updated 1 July 2026, verifies that ROE registration does not by itself remove an overseas entity's RCI duty. Legal interest/role and exact foreign company identifiers, not similar names, would be the units of comparison.

A useful potential test is: for RCI entries with an explicit company number and a declared transparency regime, does the cited regime actually cover the recorded person's legal role at the association date? AI could interpret role/exemption prose and dates; code could join verified company numbers and legislated regime lists; a human would resolve statutory applicability. The strongest innocent explanation is that the actor is an associate rather than recorded person, the company is exempt in its particular capacity, or the public snapshot is later than the association. Missing RCI absence is not proof of a duty violation because the legal owner universe also requires evidence. A 20-entry pilot could cost an estimated USD 25 cash plus 8–16 review hours, but is **blocked here**: the official RCI search returned HTTP 403; no alternative route was attempted. Scottish paid title-by-title ownership searches are not authorized for this task. No two-sided RCI entry sample passed access, so this is not pilot_ready. The 2026 paper and RoS guidance already describe the exemption issue; AI benefit and future reporting novelty are unmeasured.

## CONTRACT-AMENDMENT-01 — conditional_blocked

NRGI's *Tying Their Hands?*, 27 September 2021, printed p.3 describes its all-English 34-contract sample; pp.4–8 distinguish fiscal/non-fiscal stabilization, force majeure and arbitral provisions and warn against definitive enforceability interpretations. A modest multilingual extension could extract the exact stabilization carve-outs, duration, later amendments and preserved obligations from public original contracts, compare them with dated public rules, and ask whether a specific legal obligation is expressly excluded or preserved. A model would propose translated clause meanings with original spans; code would track document versions/dates and exact project/contract identifiers; a human lawyer would resolve operative instruments and conflicts. Clauses alone do not prove corruption or a damages entitlement. Strongest innocent explanation: a superseding amendment or limited fiscal clause excludes the relevant regulation. Missing amendments and governing law are decisive.

No original-contract pair was acquired in this revisit. The initial official ResourceContracts website/API probes returned 403. They were not retried through a mirror, CDN or alternative transport. The report itself is readable, but a report's quotations are not a successfully checked paired contract dataset. A ten-contract/200-page pilot might fit an estimated USD 50 cash plus 12–25 legal/language-review hours only after authorized free documents are accessible. Existing English clause analysis is not a reason to reject a multilingual pilot; the current access and completeness failures are. Legal enforceability remains a separate reporting step.

## Still rejected

Generic graph-degree/absent-PSC checks with no document interpretation remain an ordinary-code baseline rather than an AI opportunity. Hidden-control inference from unavailable internal agreements remains blocked by evidence access. Universal land/register mismatch scoring without first classifying interests, legal thresholds, exemptions and event dates remains rejected as a measurement error.

This revisit adds no new NGO work to the original census. It rereads targeted sections of three previously screened works and adds contextual reading of six previously enumerated works. Its genuinely new sample evidence is four public corporate PDFs, four public registry pages, current government guidance and a failed RCI access check. There is one pilot_ready interpretation design, two conditional_blocked designs and no live allegation.
