## 1. Shareholder statements versus dated beneficial-control declarations

**Verdict: promising bounded method; hold as an evidence-ready AI finalist.** Global Witness, *The companies we keep*, official landing date 1 December 2020, analyzes a 1 March 2018 snapshot. PDF physical p30/printed p29 proposes shareholder cross-checks. Original passage (18 words): “Cross-checking data against other government datasets, in particular shareholder data, which would help weed out inconsistencies.” [Original PDF](https://globalwitness.org/documents/466/The_companies_we_keep.pdf). This is expressly an existing method, not evidence that it became possible in 2026.

Independent free retrieval succeeds for an access-only positive control: company number **16545784**, incorporation 27 June 2025. The [filing-history document](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/16545784/filing-history/MzQ3MTY3NDMyNWFkaXF6a2N4/document?download=0&format=pdf) is 290,829 bytes, SHA256 `1fdea69eff656f454b862d98878ca4f6bd233810108351b8687606dc8c17f0d7`, 11 PDF pages. Physical p4 gives 100 ordinary shares with full voting/distribution rights; p5 gives one natural-person subscriber holding all 100 shares; pp6–8 declare the same person's contemporaneous 75%+ share/voting control. The [separate PSC register page](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/16545784/persons-with-significant-control) returns 200 and notification date 27 June 2025, agreeing with the incorporation statement. No conflict was found. This company was selected from a generic filing search purely to test access; it was not nominated by a production dataset and is not a suspect. Both records ultimately originate in company self-report, not independent verification of beneficial ownership.

**Falsifiable test.** In a registry-nominated cohort, a natural-person subscriber owning strictly more than 25% of a single ordinary share class with corresponding full voting rights at incorporation should have an applicable dated PSC declaration unless a documented statutory treatment explains otherwise. A “no registrable PSC” declaration in that same initial filing would nominate an internal reporting contradiction. Exactly 25% does not cross the threshold. A contradiction proves inconsistent declarations, not concealment, criminality or the truth of either declaration.

**Minimum matching gates.** Company number, incorporation/effective date, complete capital denominator, share class and rights, each subscriber's shares, PSC effective/notification dates, and no corporate-subscriber/nominee/trust/exemption ambiguity. Later comparisons require full shareholder baseline plus every relevant change and dated PSC changes. Current PSC snapshots cannot stand in for historical PSC states. [Current CS01 guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/confirmation-statement-form-cs01-new-version) says Part4 supplies changes and CS01 cannot update PSC: a single CS01 is not a full holdings table. Missing change history means abstain. A company moving to strike-off is also not evidence of beneficial-ownership wrongdoing.

**AI marginal value audit.** The successful NEWINC control is searchable text. `pdftotext` plus ordinary label/number rules extracts the necessary fields; AI is unnecessary here. AI might cheaply interpret genuinely scanned, rotated, irregular historic forms and classify share-rights language, but no such difficult sample, extraction-quality benchmark, free-local-model measurement or actual cost comparison has passed. Code should choose the cohort, calculate holdings, rebuild chronology, enforce thresholds and deduplicate. Human review must assess rights, nominees, scope and every contradiction. AI must abstain rather than invent missing shareholders. A low-cost pilot can begin with 30 deterministic registry-nominated incorporation records and compare rules/OCR against local AI on the difficult subset. Go only with complete denominators, at least 95% independently reviewed field precision, no missing-time silent inference and demonstrated savings on the difficult subset; otherwise use code alone or stop. Numerical targets are proposed design gates, not achieved performance.

**Innocent explanation / missing evidence.** A mismatch may be filing error, amendment timing, nominee/legal-owner versus beneficial-owner difference, separate class rights, indirect control, or applicability rules. The missing evidence is the operative legal/beneficial ownership arrangement and complete contemporaneous changes. These cannot be generated by a model. Prior coverage is already explicit in Global Witness's 2018 analysis; no comprehensive prior-publication novelty search has passed in this audit. Do not claim “overlooked” or “new in 2026.”

**Cost.** Proposed 30-record pilot cash US$0 with public downloads and local tools, assuming existing hardware; hardware/power and human legal review are unmeasured. No hosted-model pricing claim or paid authorization is implied. A larger historical scan has substantial acquisition, OCR and review costs that remain unmeasured.

## 2. DRC company payments versus government recipient reconciliation

**Verdict: hold. Acquisition succeeds; the numerical allegation design fails measurement and novelty gates.** NRGI, *DRC's Mining Revenues: Increasing Accountability by Analyzing Payments to Governments Reports*, December 2020 ([original report](https://resourcegovernance.org/sites/default/files/documents/drc-mining-revenues-increasing-accountability-by-analyzing-payments-to-governments-reports.pdf)), printed p10, gives a precise methodological warning (23 words): “The gaps illustrated by these case studies are not indicative of misreporting by any party.” The same page describes harmonizing company/revenue names, reallocating categories and correcting machine-conversion errors. It says missing contracts prevent a comprehensive assessment. This explicitly establishes substantial prior published use of the proposed comparison.

The team retrieved the 2023 DRC EITI report (191 pages), a 7.3MB annex workbook, a summary workbook and Ivanhoe's **same calendar-year 2023** amended ESTMA report. Original URLs/hashes are in [the team acquisition manifest](../../teams/secrecy_ownership/drc_pilot_acquisition.json). Independent review below uses those acquired bytes, not a new third-party fetch. Reproduce with the adjacent script. Ivanhoe original URL: <https://www.ivanhoemines.com/wp-content/uploads/20231231-00000-Ivanhoe-Mines-Ltd-ESTMA-Reporting-template-vF.pdf>; SHA256 `eee06780c86f61b94632a92904446679884b1da6dde1b7ff5d41737bd5f6ff5a`.

**What actually joins.** EITI annex0 row 14 identifies KAMOA COPPER SA (ex.BARBADOS), NIF `A0901048A`. Annex3 rows 610–612 gives permits 12873, 13025, 13026. These are stable local entity/title identifiers. Ivanhoe ESTMA physical p5 supplies a Kamoa-Kakula *project* total US$516,580,000 and royalty US$66,350,000. Notes explicitly cover **100% of JV payments**, not Ivanhoe's39.6% interest; round to US$10,000; translate CDF at year-end CDF / USD 2662.70. EITI annex49 row 7 reports Kamoa mining royalty receipts US$77,543,916 split across DGRAD/province/local authority/FOMIN. Merely subtracting the project royalty from this entity-level total is invalid until entity-project scope, tax definitions, recipient inclusion, reporting threshold and exchange/recognition conventions are bridged. No successful equivalence bridge has passed.

**What is already known.** EITI printed p139, table 67, already reports Kamoa's final company declaration US$524,199,416 versus state US$502,285,805: unreconciled US$21,913,611. This public reconciliation cannot be presented as a newly discovered gap. EITI printed p138 identifies bundled customs receipts lacking individual-operator allocations and payroll-tax payments combining different agencies; printed p145 lists timing, duplicate, advance-payment and category corrections. AI cannot reconstruct the missing allocation key from totals.

**Critical availability failure.** The acquired annex workbook's `Annexe 52` is only five rows. Row5 says the company reconciliation sheets are in a separate Excel document. That separate workbook has not been acquired. XLSX relationships were inspected: no public annex52 download hyperlink appears; one external workbook reference points to personal SharePoint and was not accessed. A catalog's “annexes” label does not prove possession of the needed tax-by-recipient/company evidence.

**Narrow viable pilot question.** Does a *new* externally reported same-year payment to a named government recipient fall outside EITI's documented scoping/materiality universe, after matching legal entity, project, recipient, fiscal category, cash date, currency and exclusions? This tests completeness of disclosure, not theft. Start with all mutually eligible issuer-years rather than a famous company. AI could propose French-English agency aliases and map explanatory narrative to a reviewed taxonomy, but ordinary code handles XLSX extraction, arithmetic, temporal filters and crosswalk enforcement. Current free-source evidence does not establish AI's cost advantage over code/dictionary review.

**Stop/go.** Stop numerical comparison if detailed recipient reconciliation, period/currency bridge, tax scope, project ownership bridge or adjustment notes are absent. Stop novelty claim if EITI/NGO has already explained the row. Go only for a residual, newly documented scope omission after all bridges and original disclosure provenance are preserved, with a clean negative control and human sign-off. Private bank records, receipt allocations or undisclosed contracts remain inaccessible evidence regardless of model price.

**Cost.** Proposed three issuer-years / one jurisdiction first gate, public downloads and local tools, cash US$0 assuming existing hardware. Review/translation/tax-law labor is unmeasured. An optional later hosted-model pilot under US$200 would require explicit authorization and current official pricing, neither obtained here. No current pricing was cited and no paid calls were made.
