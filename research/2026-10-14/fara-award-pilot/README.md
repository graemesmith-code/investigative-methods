# FARA and federal awards: overlap is not a disclosure violation

## Editorial stopping note — 15 October 2026

**Status: parked at the editor's request.** The pilot found overlaps between FARA relationships and federal award recipients; it did **not demonstrate a conflict of interest or disclosure violation**. Serving multiple clients is a lead-selection signal, not proof of misconduct.

The main unresolved question is the **content of the work**: what the firm was doing for each client, on which issues and dates, and whether those duties actually conflicted. A substantive paired reading was not completed. It would require dated foreign-client mandates and activity reports alongside federal statements of work, deliverables, and the relevant conflict-of-interest, disclosure and waiver clauses. The first pass failed to retrieve four FARA originals (HTTP 403); catalogue links in the dossiers are discovery pointers, not documents reviewed.

The separate, deeper foreign-principal/ownership branch also remains incomplete: 32 source-name groups were untested, and no false SAM declaration was established. That unfinished branch must not be presented as a completed negative finding.

**Why stop here:** establishing a publishable story would now require document-intensive, case-by-case work beyond the inexpensive archive join. The editor decided that further work was not worth pursuing now. This is a resource-allocation decision, not exoneration or a legal conclusion. The original findings and limitations below remain as the historical record; no further research or outreach was undertaken for this stopping note.


**No disclosure violation was established.** This $0 pilot identifies some FARA registrants and named foreign principals in federal award records. Canadian Commercial Corporation dominates the US action count. The other corroborated US keys are law/communications firms whose ordinary federal services can coexist with unrelated foreign-client engagements. Canadian funding disclosures are already present in the strongest organizational example. Grants and Canadian procurement coverage remain incomplete.

## Newsroom findings

- **CCC accounts for 2,184 of 2,310 expanded US actions (94.5%).** Its matched FY2025 net obligation changes are US$1,397,519,226.86. That is an obligation measure, not cash paid or a lobbying budget. CCC is the named principal and recipient; its separate FARA agent is not treated as the contractor.
- **The 11 other US keys are registrants, not evidence that their foreign clients received contracts.** Three have only zero-obligation matched actions; two have only deobligations. Ordinary dual engagements are a plausible explanation, not a concealed-control finding.
- **Three names match active Canadian lobbying records:** The Porter O’Brien Agency Inc., Burke Consulting LLC and Nuclear Waste Management Organization. NWMO appears in two FARA roles, giving four role/registration keys, not four organizations.
- **NWMO already discloses funding:** C$3,547,322.81 from AECL and C$1,876,192.54 from NRCan for the financial year ended 31 December 2025. These are self-reported amounts, not independently verified grant payments. A consultant’s client funding is not the consultant’s fee income.
- **There is no full grants result.** Three US FY2025 grant-name queries returned no rows; the grants corpus was not screened. Canadian all-federal grants and contracts files returned 403. The accessible CanadaBuys history is a bounded PSPC-managed slice; no temporal FARA overlap was corroborated in it. This is not a comprehensive Canadian negative.

## Two scopes, not two additive totals

This baseline’s conservative direct exact-name, building/city and date check yields **14 address groups, 79 actions and 37 award/IDV IDs**. Carrying an address-confirmed identity only to the **same exact legal name and recipient UEI** yields **21 groups, 12 recipient/role/registration keys, 2,310 actions and 1,719 award/IDV IDs**. **The 79 actions are included in 2,310. Do not add them.** FARA supplies no UEI: the bridge is derived within the award corpus. **An address mismatch is unresolved, not a rejection.** Overseas headquarters and recipient operating offices can differ; distinct subsidiaries need their own dated identity/control checks. This baseline does not claim to exhaust all direct-principal or subsidiary ownership leads. A broader resolution review is separate from these published results.

## Coverage and the missing story test

All three FARA CSVs were read: 7,090 registrants, 17,765 principal relationship rows and 154,309 catalogue rows; 15 principal rows were quarantined. Cached FARA active status is evaluated **6 October 2026**, not certified live on publication day. Every US archive CSV was read: **6,639,176 FY2025 actions**, 1 October 2024–30 September 2025, and **101,379 distinct nonblank raw recipient names**, not unique legal entities.

Canada covers all 177,371 primary registration-version rows, 218,466 funding rows and 2,083 bounded CanadaBuys history rows. Latest OCL effective/posted date: 9 October 2026. History award dates: 1 April–25 September 2026. Other Canadian ZIP members were not analyzed for ownership/activity. Original FARA PDFs were inaccessible and not read. [The technical annex](TECHNICAL-ANNEX.md) gives all stage denominators, methods and gaps.

For a US ownership inconsistency, obtain the actual dated offer/SAM immediate/highest-level owner representation, applicable award-year clause and a contrary sourced control fact for that same entity. FARA status alone is not that fact. Award-specific lobbying requires its own payment/activity evidence and operative clause/SF-LLL. See [FAR 52.204-17](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.204-17), [52.203-11](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.203-11), [52.203-12](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.203-12) and [DOJ’s FARA FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions).

For Canada, compare actual qualifying grant/nonrepayable-contribution receipts to the registration’s completed financial year and amendments. **Procurement is excluded from lobbying government-funding disclosures**, so procurement omission is not an inconsistency: [OCL registry](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/guest), [Lobbying Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/L-12.4/FullText.html). Consultant contract-award lobbying and in-house activity differ; a consultant’s FARA engagement does not establish control of its client.

X04 needs the exact LD-1 and Exhibit A control facts. X06 needs itemized campaign financing and an exact ad/placement bridge. Federal obligations supply neither. Inaccessible representations remain untested, not absent or false. No candidate passes a source-backed violation gate.
