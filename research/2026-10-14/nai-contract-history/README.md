# NAI Logistics: orders after designation and a later termination

**Public-records follow-up, 14 October 2026. No sanctions violation has been established.**

Official procurement records show **eight delivery orders with positive obligations dated 13–14 December 2023**, after Treasury designated NAI Logistics B.V. on 11 December. Those actions recorded **$36,309.46** in positive obligations. Five reducing modifications offset **$15,662.32**, leaving **$20,647.14 net**. Four orders were fully deobligated. These amounts describe reported contractual obligations; they do not establish payments or completed fuel deliveries.

The original DLA modification **P00002** terminated vehicle **SPE60523D9402 in its entirety**, effective **11 March 2025**, expressly because NAI was placed on the OFAC sanctions list. Its embedded notice makes a more specific claim: the government had been restricted by “OFAC\ DFAS” for transactions because there was “no applicable general license or exemption” for this engagement. That is the contracting officer's stated determination, and its underlying basis remains unresolved. [Original modification and embedded notice](https://dibbs2.bsm.dla.mil/Downloads/Awards/22NOV22/SPE60523D9402_P00002.PDF); [readable notice text](evidence/documents/termination-notice.txt).

An official-government-business authorization was already present in **31 CFR 583.509 in 2023**, effective from 21 December 2022. It was renumbered **583.510 on 12 March 2024**. Its existence does not establish that it applied to these orders, payments or blocked property. The DLA notice does not explain its applicability analysis. Neither the general provision nor the notice alone establishes legality or a violation for any particular transaction. [Historical 2023 CFR](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title31-vol3/pdf/CFR-2023-title31-vol3-part583.pdf), [2022 rule](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/930146/download), [2024 rule](https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/2024-03/20240311_global_magnitsky_sanctions_regulations.pdf).

## Payments and remaining questions

The seven linked federal-account funding records for this vehicle report **null gross outlays**. Summary zeroes and absent rows therefore cannot establish that no payment occurred. No post-designation disbursement voucher, settled invoice, bank transfer, supplier receipt, delivery ticket or acceptance certificate was recovered. The notice says transactions were restricted, but gives no restriction start date or payment ledger. It conditionally promises payment for accepted deliverables, subject to law and set-off; that clause does not establish an actual acceptance or payment.

Four pre-designation December 8 orders and the eight December 13–14 orders have reported performance periods after designation. A scheduled performance period does not prove delivery. Four complete deobligations, no later positive procurement obligations and DLA's restriction statement are material counterevidence to a claim of 15 months of uninterrupted purchasing or payment.

The remaining questions concern actual delivery and acceptance, the reasons for the reducing modifications, the start of OFAC/DFAS restrictions, unpaid or blocked invoices, the legal basis for DLA's no-applicable-authorization determination, and the timing of the final termination. No outreach or records request was made.
