# Route records requests by custodian

Method note — 1 November 2026.

A useful records inquiry separates scientific measurements, regulatory decisions and purchased operational reports. Different offices may hold each category. A referral identifies another place to ask; it does not establish that the records exist or what they contain.

Put plainly, the task is to find the paper trail showing who knew what and when. Getting another email address is progress in finding that trail, not evidence of the underlying story.

## Practical method

1. Use the original correspondence to confirm the intended recipient, record categories, date range and historical identifiers.
2. Include former and current identifiers for the same asset, explicitly avoiding duplicate treatment.
3. Describe unverified timestamps as search references and ask the custodian to check them.
4. Ask for original records, attachments, revisions, timestamps and time zones, including notes of verbal instructions where relevant.
5. Ask the recipient to identify the appropriate custodian and access procedure for material held elsewhere.
6. Keep existing paid orders separate from new inquiries. An order acknowledgement establishes receipt of an order, not delivery of records.
7. Record draft, sent, acknowledged and fulfilled as separate states. Verify a saved draft by reading it back; do not describe it as a submitted request.

## Technical annex

Suggested tracking fields: record category; custodian; verified contact source; requested date range; current and historical identifiers; draft or sent state; order reference where applicable; acknowledgement date; delivery status; unresolved questions.

Validate that former identifiers map to the intended asset and retain uncertainty about the relationship between an event and an operation. A failed or empty search does not prove records are absent.

This general note contains no private correspondence, account identifiers, source links, case-specific well identifiers, event timestamps or draft text. It is a reusable procedure, not an investigative finding.
