## Verified ownership and financing facts

### Ownership and conversion

S06 identifies Framestone Limited, Cyprus registration **HE315582**, as the sole shareholder immediately before the 29 June 2016 transaction. **€365,400 of its existing claim** was waived in return for **36,540 additional shares at €10 each**. Total capital became **€465,400**, divided into **46,540 shares**.

This establishes direct corporate ownership on that date. It does not identify Framestone's ultimate human owners, its total creditor exposure, the origin of the claim or the financing of a named Italian asset. The instrument refers to part of a claim; principal and interest must not be collapsed into one inferred cash transfer.

## Extraction and verification method

The review used native PDF text extraction, page rendering, optical character recognition with Tesseract, and visual comparison of critical pages. Optical character recognition means converting an image of text into searchable text.

Native extraction alone was inadequate. Some pages were scanned images; others returned nonempty but garbled text because of font encoding. Material notes would have been missed by a rule that applied optical character recognition only to pages with zero extracted characters.

Three parallel document reviews covered governance papers, early accounts and later accounts. Material ownership and loan figures were checked against page images. Parallel reviewers were assistants, not independent external auditors. There was no blinded or held-out assessment.

A reproduction from the matching originals should:

1. Check every file's SHA-256 fingerprint against the source manifest.
2. Extract text page by page while retaining original page numbers.
3. Inspect readability, not only character counts; render and recognize scanned or garbled pages.
4. Read the account heading, period, balance-sheet side and loan-table column labels before extracting a relationship.
5. Check critical names, identifiers, amounts and financial direction against the image.
6. Reconcile principal totals and separate accrued interest.
7. Preserve contradictory printed values rather than silently harmonizing them.
8. Apply the editorial stopping rule in the [readable account](README.md#the-lesson-for-automated-journalism).

The structured ledger preserves reviewed claims rather than pretending to be a complete automatic extraction of every line. Full prompts, model settings, exact call costs and complete review-time logs were not captured. Exact replay of the assistant interaction is therefore unavailable.

## Source inconsistencies that an automated system must preserve

- S06's English narrative contains “167263,” while the filing cover, registration form and French counterpart give **B167623**. Use the corroborated identifier and retain the discrepancy.
- Framestone's loan maturity is printed **9 February 2022** in S09 p. 12 and **8 February 2022** in S11 p. 12. Visual checks confirm a source discrepancy.
- S07 p. 15 prints **€271,000** as the prior-year Framestone principal comparison, while S05 p. 15 records **€271,700**. The original year's figure is used above; this review does not explain the mismatch.
- S11's narrative describes the €100,000 asset as a security deposit “received” by the Swiss branch. Preserve that wording; do not invent a winery borrower or silently reverse its meaning.

These issues are reasons for careful extraction. They are not, by themselves, evidence of deception.

## Editorial evaluation and stopping rule

**Observed technical result:** named relationships and financial amounts recovered and checked from the supplied filings.

**Observed editorial result:** no consequential new finding established; this lead parked after the user's assessment of weak reporting value.

**Not measured:** recall, precision, inter-reviewer agreement, incremental discovery over a human baseline, time saved, total cash cost, cost per useful lead or performance on a larger corpus. Unknown cost must not be reported as zero.

Before another network-expansion round, require a specific reporting question, a plausible public consequence, one decisive obtainable record or comparison, and an explicit stopping condition. This is an editorial rule proposed from this case, not a validated classifier. It is broader than searching only for legal violations; a consequential finding need not establish wrongdoing.

The successful extraction and the weak editorial return must remain separate in future summaries. No claim of “never reported” is supported: novelty was not exhaustively tested.
