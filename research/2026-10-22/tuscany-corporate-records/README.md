# Tuscany company records: useful extraction, weak reporting return

**Decision: park this lead, 22 October 2026.**

Eleven company filings yielded verifiable ownership and financing facts, but no consequential new finding about Russian interests in Tuscany. This was a useful exercise in reading difficult records. It did not establish that expanding the company network, or obtaining bulk access to Aleph, would produce a worthwhile story.

The decision concerns this round of work on Luxembourg Beverage Supply Company and its possible relevance to Bertinga. It is an editorial decision about the evidence in hand. It does not establish that the wider Tuscany investigation is exhausted, or that any company has acted lawfully or unlawfully.

## What we learned

A June 2016 deed identifies **Framestone Limited of Cyprus, registration HE315582**, as the sole shareholder of Luxembourg Beverage Supply Company, registration **B167623**. Framestone converted **€365,400 of an existing claim into shares**, increasing the company's capital from €100,000 to €465,400. This was a debt conversion, not a fresh cash investment on that date. [S06, PDF pp. 4–7](TECHNICAL-ANNEX.md#source-register)

Earlier accounts record financing from Framestone. Later accounts record loans in the opposite direction: at March 2017 the Luxembourg company was owed **€523,168.70 by Framestone** and **€1,099,344.17 by IPCOM Limited**. By March 2018 it reported **€1,780,822.96 in principal owed by four affiliated businesses**. Those disclosures identify counterparties to investigate; they do not trace the money to a Tuscan acquisition. [S05 p. 15; S07 p. 15; S09 p. 12; S11 p. 12](TECHNICAL-ANNEX.md#source-register)

The records also describe a substantial beverage-trading operation through a Swiss branch established in 2012. Revenue reached approximately **€52.97 million** in the year ending March 2018. Revenue is not the value of a vineyard or the amount invested in one. [S02 p. 14; S11 pp. 6, 12–14](TECHNICAL-ANNEX.md#source-register)

The connection between the later Swiss company and Bertinga was already reported by [The Insider and IRPImedia in August 2025](https://theins.co/en/inv/284032). The present work did not independently establish the Italian ownership chain. None of the eleven filings names Bertinga, Riecine or Aiola. No identified Tuscan purchase price, acquisition payment or ultimate human owner emerged from this set.

## Why we stopped

The filings produced more companies and transactions to investigate, but no clear public consequence, substantiated contradiction or other finding strong enough to justify continuing. Related-company lending and a cross-border ownership structure do not by themselves establish concealment, improper funding or a false sale.

The initial assistant assessment gave too much editorial weight to additional corporate connections. A later, deliberately enthusiastic retelling amplified that impression without adding evidence. The user's challenge prompted a correction: **a growing network was being mistaken for a stronger story**.

This is a failure to establish sufficient reporting value from this selected set. It is not a measured failure rate for corporate-record investigations generally.

## The lesson for automated journalism

Before expanding a company network, write down:

1. **The reporting question:** the specific claim or relationship that needs to be established.
2. **The consequence:** what would change for readers, public accountability or the people affected if it were true.
3. **The decisive evidence:** the document or comparison that could distinguish competing explanations.
4. **The stopping condition:** what result would make further collection a poor use of time.

After one bounded review, decide whether the evidence has made that question more answerable or more consequential. Another company name, director, address or loan balance is not sufficient by itself.

Record extraction quality and editorial value separately. This review recovered facts from scanned and garbled-text documents. It produced no story-ready finding. It did not measure time saved, extraction accuracy across an unseen sample, discovery yield or cost at scale.

## What would justify reopening?

Reopen only if new, verifiable evidence creates a clear reporting question and a feasible decisive check—for example, a material contradiction between a declared sale and documented continuing control, or primary records linking the financing to a consequential, previously unresolved transaction.

An additional connected company, easier archive access or an interesting-sounding summary is not sufficient. The historical Italian ownership record and the missing corporate transfer records remain evidence gaps; this note does not initiate their acquisition.

## Access and experiment status

The work used eleven supplied filings, previous catalogue review and a small amount of public-source context. It did **not** run a bulk Aleph collection or establish permission for automated processing. No outreach to the Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project was sent in this round. A catalogue listing is not evidence that Aleph contains the particular missing documents.

No new data source was independently validated for addition to the Rabbit catalog.
