# Rabbit catalogs: choosing somewhere to look

The Rabbit catalogs are a reference for finding public-record systems to try on a reporting beat. They are separate from the experiments: appearing in a catalog does not mean a source was tested, is accessible today, or has produced a useful lead.

## Start with a reporting question

Choose one thing you want to understand: a spending change, a rule and its enforcement, a company's ownership, or the evidence behind a public claim. Find a source that records that activity, then read a few ordinary entries before designing a larger experiment.

For each source, ask:

1. **What does a row or document describe?** A payment, a maximum contract value and a request for information mean different things.
2. **What is left out?** Check dates, redactions, missing attachments, reporting thresholds and update delays.
3. **Can I check the original?** Keep the official link and date. Test a known example before trusting a search that returns nothing.
4. **What could I do next?** Prefer a source that leads to an obtainable document and a specific question over a large list of unexplained alerts.
5. **What will a small trial cost?** Include access restrictions, reading time and any paid requests; a listed price is not a current quote.

The [public-source trial](../experiments/02-public-source-pilot.md) shows how to check access. The [ATI trial](../experiments/07-canadian-ati.md) shows why a summary index and the documents behind it must be treated separately. Use the [run record](../templates/run.md) to keep your test comparable with someone else's.
