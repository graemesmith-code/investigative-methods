# DATA1 + RULE + DATA2 = WORTH CHECKING

**Methodology options recorded 14 October 2026. These are proposals, not a new experiment or a demonstrated detection method.**

Two datasets become more useful when a dated rule explains why their relationship deserves attention. The emerging lesson is:

**DATA1 + RULE + DATA2 = WORTH CHECKING**

This is a way to choose a reporting question. It is not an equation that produces proof. A stable company number, well identifier or location can connect records correctly while the comparison still uses the wrong date, activity, jurisdiction, units or exception. A discrepancy is a reason to inspect the underlying evidence and an ordinary explanation.

## What the two investigations taught us

**Sanctions:** DATA1 is a dated OFAC designation and verified identity. RULE is the applicable sanctions program and rule version, jurisdiction, exceptions and authorizations. DATA2 is the recipient's dated contract actions and, when the question concerns payment or performance, actual payment or delivery records. The NAI reconstruction found eight reported orders after designation, reducing modifications and a later termination notice. It did not establish delivery, payment or a violation. A zero-dollar termination does not answer the earlier-order question; the existence of a general authorization does not settle its applicability. [NAI report and parked decision](../research/2026-10-14/nai-contract-history/README.md).

**Alberta seismic activity:** DATA1 is dated seismic observations, with magnitude type, catalogue version and uncertainty. RULE is the applicable location, formation, magnitude, notification, shutdown and conditional restart requirements, effective at the event time. DATA2 must include actual stage-level pumping, stop and restart records. Those operational records remain missing. Reporting windows, monthly totals, current licence labels and proximity do not establish pumping after an event, historical rule applicability or an unauthorized restart. [Existing findings](../research/2026-10-06/alberta-seismic-injection/README.md), [dated rules and limits](../research/2026-10-06/alberta-seismic-injection/RULES.md).

The useful result can be an exact missing-record question or a decision to stop. It need not be an allegation.

## Name the kind of constraint first

| Kind | What it can support | What must be checked |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal obligation** | A duty or prohibition applying to a specified actor and activity | Exact official text, jurisdiction, effective version, scope, conditions and exceptions; evidence the triggering conduct occurred |
| **Accounting constraint** | A reconciliation within a defined set of accounts | Cash versus accrual, gross versus net, consolidation boundaries, units, period, adjustments and restatements |
| **Physical constraint** | A bounded balance or limit in a specified system | System boundary, incoming/outgoing flows, opening/closing stocks, conversions, losses, measurement error and uncertainty |
| **Contractual commitment** | A signed party's promise under the operative agreement | Actual document, parties, amendments, conditions, performance/acceptance evidence and remedies; a database status is insufficient |
| **Plausible expectation** | A hypothesis about what might normally happen | Evidence supporting the expectation and credible alternatives; label it as an expectation, never a binding rule |

Neither a model suggestion nor a surprising correlation creates a legal obligation. A physical imbalance in an incompletely observed system is not proof of wrongdoing.

## Five cheap options for Rabbit

Rabbit already distinguishes systems from access points, preserves source provenance and warns that catalogued routes are not necessarily working APIs. Existing reviewed source-pair recipes also record identity, time, missing evidence and ordinary explanations. The following options build on that small existing material; they do not require re-parsing the whole catalog. [Catalog guide](../catalogs/README.md), [provenance](../references/rabbit/README.md), [existing pair review](../research/2026-10-04/source-pair-completeness/README.md).

| Option | Small first step | Benefit | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Add rule cards to existing pairs** | Take eight already reviewed pairs. Add the constraint kind, exact rule source/version, trigger, exceptions and missing factual test. | Quickly exposes pairs that were joined correctly but cannot answer their proposed question. | Human review is still needed for each rule and its applicability. |
| **2. Make a reusable rule-family index** | Draft five family cards, such as disclosure duties, permit conditions, spending reconciliations, physical balances and contract milestones. Route only a few known sources to them. | Reuses questions and verification checklists without inventing a rule for every possible pair. | A family is a discovery aid; its members can have different jurisdictions, versions and exceptions. |
| **3. Let a model discover candidate connections, verify rules exactly** | Give an existing model a small fixed set of source descriptions and a small verified-rule bank. Ask for many conditional triples and ordinary explanations; the reporter need not specify each investigation. Keep suggested obligations unverified until exact official text is checked. | Broadens ideas before spending much collection effort. | A plausible citation or model-invented obligation must never be treated as law. Verification, not generation, is usually the expensive step. No model calls were made for this note. |
| **4. Work backward from documented failures** | For a future pilot, select a few published official enforcement decisions or audits that already document a failure. Identify the rule and record relationship used to establish it. | Provides known examples against which a proposed comparison can be tested. | Do not generalize a case-specific duty to everyone or select only successful cases; include ordinary and exception cases. No new cases were researched here. |
| **5. Compare specific attributable commitments** | On the small subset, propose connections between a dated, attributable promise and independently observed records of the promised outcome. Preserve the speaker, exact statement, deadline, scope and qualifications. | Offers a separate cheap accountability route without first proving a legal prohibition. | Establish whether this is a binding contract, another specific public commitment or only a vague aspiration. Amendments, conditions and actual outcome evidence matter; a broken promise is not automatically illegality. |

These options can be combined: source descriptions support broad generation, a small reusable verified-rule bank helps route candidates, and comparison cards preserve skeptical review. Legal, commitment and physical/accounting tracks remain distinct. None has been executed as part of this note. There were no paid calls, new collection runs, full-catalog parsing or outreach.

## Generate broadly; rank importance separately

Allow broad candidate generation on the supplied small subset, including reasons a pair might fail. For each candidate, keep separate assessments of:

- **Potential importance if substantiated:** possible public harm, money, people affected or institutional significance, with the basis stated.
- **Access:** whether the needed original records can actually be obtained and reused.
- **Comparability:** whether entity, scope, units and timing align well enough to test the proposition.
- **Verification effort:** the human work needed to establish the rule, exceptions, identity and actual conduct.
- **Novelty:** what is already reported and what remains unknown; use “not assessed” until checked.

Do not blend these into one “scandal score.” An important but inaccessible question can be parked; an easy comparison can be low consequence. Importance is an editorial assessment conditional on evidence, not a probability of wrongdoing. Record uncertainty separately rather than letting a model's confidence fill a missing fact.

## Recommended bounded pilot and stopping rule

**Recommend a discovery-led pilot combining a small description set, a reusable rule bank and skeptical review.** Select **12 existing source descriptions** from the already reviewed material before looking for apparent discrepancies, using a stated selection rule and retaining both accessible and blocked examples. Supply up to **five small rule-family cards** based on already verified official material; a family card is a routing aid and does not establish case applicability. The user does not need to invent or manually specify each investigation.

In one existing-session model generation pass, ask for **up to 30 DATA1 + RULE + DATA2 candidate triples**, including legal, specific-commitment and bounded accounting/physical tracks, ordinary explanations and exact missing facts. Require the model to distinguish verified bank entries from suggested rules. A candidate can suggest a rule to verify, but a model-invented obligation never becomes law. If that generation route is unavailable within the agreed budget, stop or record the fallback rather than quietly buying calls.

Keep two separate lists: **potential importance if substantiated** and **evidence readiness**. Readiness records access, comparability and verification effort separately; novelty stays separate and “not assessed” until checked. Select the **strongest five feasible candidates** for exact primary-rule/commitment verification, recording which higher-importance candidates were parked and why. Reject proposals requiring substantial harmonization at this stage, unsupported rates/disparities, invented duties, or inference of payment/pumping from administrative events. A rate or disparity requires a defensible exposed/eligible denominator, consistent numerator and coverage period; a raw count difference is insufficient.

Set a cap of **two hours of human review**, **two primary documents per shortlisted candidate**, no new bulk collection, and **zero external paid calls**. These are proposed limits for a later authorized pilot; the pilot has not started. The five cards should establish the exact constraint and scope or say why it remains unverified, preserve counterevidence and exceptions, and identify the decisive missing fact.

Nominate **at most one inexpensive, feasible decisive check** for later authorization. Nomination does not execute the check. Compare the five cards with the existing source-pair descriptions; record corrected rejections as well as surviving questions. Continue only if at least **two cards materially improve the existing recipes** and one candidate offers a cheap decisive check without substantial harmonization. This would justify another bounded trial, not prove discovery accuracy or investigative novelty.

Stop at 30 candidates, five source checks or two hours, whichever limit applies. Stop an individual candidate when the exact rule/version or applicability cannot be established within its limit, identity/units/time cannot be aligned cheaply, a defensible denominator is unavailable, or decisive evidence is inaccessible. Park it with the missing fact and a reopening trigger. If the pilot fails its improvement/feasible-check condition, record that result and do not expand to the full Rabbit catalog.

## Minimum standard for a defensible comparison

Identify the **entity, activity and scope; units; observation and reporting times; jurisdiction; effective rule version; conditions and exceptions; independently observed evidence; and explicit uncertainty**. Keep original evidence and the rule text separately from the join or model output. Two mirrors of one feed are not independent corroboration. Independently produced records can still share an error or describe different events.

A good output reads: “These records may meet this dated condition; this specific missing fact would resolve it.” It does not turn a join, catalogue absence, scheduled period or model-invented obligation into a finding.
