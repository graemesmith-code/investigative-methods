# Check transcripts before summarizing meetings

A reusable procedure for journalists and assistants.

## The reporting purpose

A useful meeting summary tells the reader what was discussed, what was agreed, and what needs to happen next. Each of those statements needs support in the recorded conversation. Check the transcript before relying on a calendar title, generated summary or task field.

The most useful output is a short recap followed by an action list with clear owners, conditions and deadlines. Preserve uncertainty in the speaker's words: "I might", "probably", and "if" change what a reader should expect.

## Classify actions clearly

| Status | Evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| Explicit commitment | A speaker clearly agrees to do something. |
| Tentative offer | A speaker raises a possibility without committing. |
| Suggested next step | A useful action proposed by the journalist or assistant after reviewing the conversation. |
| Completed | Separate evidence shows that the promised action happened. |

An agreement to make an introduction does not establish that it was sent. A calendar invitation does not establish attendance. A scheduled meeting window does not establish how much conversation was recorded. Leave an owner or deadline unspecified when the evidence does not supply it.

## Technical annex: repeatable checking procedure

1. **Define the scope.** Select the meeting records the summary covers. Distinguish the time of the meeting from the time its notes were last modified.
2. **Check completeness.** Read each transcript through its final available range. Follow continuation markers when retrieval is truncated. Record access and completeness limits.
3. **Check what was captured.** Compare the calendar title and invitee list with the actual conversation. Identify setup audio, unrelated playback and gaps before describing the meeting.
4. **Extract commitments.** Read for spoken actions even when structured task fields are empty. Preserve the supporting passage in the authorized source record.
5. **Resolve ownership.** Identify who spoke and who accepted each task. Keep unidentified speakers unidentified until evidence resolves them.
6. **Preserve conditions.** Classify each action using the table above. Do not infer a deadline from the meeting date or treat a promise as completion.
7. **Attribute claims.** Report substantive assertions as what the speaker said until independent reporting verifies them. Check disputed wording against original audio when available.
8. **Deliver a readable summary.** Lead with decisions and useful next actions. Provide source links that the intended reader can access and show times in the reader's local time zone.

For a reproducible run, retain the retrieval date, record identifiers, transcript ranges checked, speaker-identification basis, supporting passages and any unresolved gaps in an appropriate access-controlled workspace. A public methods note can describe the procedure without reproducing source material.

## Practical judgment

The recorder can help you remember, but you still have to decide what the conversation supports. Ask three plain questions: Was this actually said? Who agreed to do it? What would prove it happened? Those questions make a summary useful enough to act on.
