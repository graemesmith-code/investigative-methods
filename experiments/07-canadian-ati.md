# 07. Which Canadian ATI releases should a reporter read next?

**Question:** Can inexpensive ranking and a small number of news searches turn Canada's completed access-to-information (ATI) summaries into useful document choices? The 1 October 2026 live run ranked **1,234 summaries**, checked ten shortlisted topics and obtained **one seven-page release**. It identified two specific files to seek next. **No new allegation was verified.**

## What the source contains—and what it does not

The government index describes completed requests: department, request number, wording, disclosure status and pages released. The saved set contains 441 Global Affairs Canada and 793 National Defence summaries for closure year 2026. Their closure months stop at July and August respectively, so it is not a complete up-to-the-minute feed.

A summary tells you what someone asked for; it does not prove the request's premise. “Partly disclosed” does not tell you how useful the surviving pages are. We scored request summaries, **not 1,234 underlying document packets**. A request number must be paired with its department because different departments can use the same number.

## A recipe you can follow

1. Start with the [saved summary spreadsheet (CSV)](../research/2026-10-01/ati-cloud-preflight/corpus/records.csv), which opens in common spreadsheet software. Choose a beat and a small set of rows. Keep the department and request number together.
2. Read for a specific event and an obtainable document. Check pages and disclosure status; do not mistake alarming request wording for evidence.
3. Group repeated requests about the same subject. Pick a short list and keep a few lower-ranked examples to check what you may have missed.
4. Search for the exact event and read the results. Separate news reporting from official statements and general background. Try a known-covered example too.
5. Read an actual release wherever one is available. Decide whether to obtain a named file, ask a sharper question, or stop.

The live run used Jev to help rank text and assess passages, Exa to search, and further review to make the final decisions. You can try the same reading sequence manually on a small sample.

## What happened: two useful next documents

**Nanisivik storage tanks — obtain the ten-page file.** DND request A-2025-02587 names a briefing note about an environmental protection compliance order. Searches found reporting about the naval facility's delays and transition out of operational use, but did not establish the precise order's resolution. DND's public explanation cites other operational and construction issues. **It does not connect the tank order to the transition.** The release, order and completion record are needed to learn what correction was required and whether it was made. A routine technical correction remains possible.

**Biometric training — obtain the 95-page file.** DND request A-2025-00181 concerns authority for collecting Canadian personnel's data during training. Existing public privacy material and historical rules supply context, but do not establish the policy governing this 2025 matter. The release is needed to distinguish a proposal, an authorization and actual collection, then examine consent, retention and supervision. **No unlawful collection or privacy breach was established.**

Both packets remained unread. These are files worth obtaining next, not findings about what their contents prove.

## A useful decision to stop

The mental-health policy request, DND A-2025-01384, led to an article that already named the request and linked its seven-page release. We obtained the image-only scan and checked its text against the page images. It contains Military Police Policy Manual Chapter 323, *Mental Health Intervention*, including limits on emergency detention. The publisher associates the scan with the request; an official response letter was not independently obtained.

The decision was **already covered; stop unless a specific implementation case raises a new question**. This was the only actual release packet obtained in the run.

## All ten shortlisted topics

Four topics had Canadian coverage of the public story or policy. The other six had no exact match established in these limited searches; they are **not six proven unreported stories**. Internal files can remain unread even where the public story is covered.

|---|---|---|
| Leopard tanks | Additional tanks and aid package already reported | Four-page internal recommendation might add detail |
| China nuclear-related permits | Rules and unrelated number matches, no identified transaction | Five-page note to identify goods, parties and decision |
| Ukraine budget remainder | Public aid announcements provide context; separate funding envelopes can be confused | Two internal notes to identify the exact remainder |
| Fifty armoured vehicles | Programme and delivery already reported | Read the later recommendation before claiming anything new |
| GAC assignment eligibility | Staffing-cut coverage gives background, not the precise decision | Nine-page file and eligibility bulletin |
| Israel / Maple reactions | The very article named in the request was found | Internal reactions would require a distinct question |
| Nanisivik | Facility coverage, unresolved exact order/remedy | Ten-page compliance file |
| Mines allegation | Related injury/clearance reports do not substantiate the separate deployment allegation | Read the one-page release before repeating the allegation |
| Military mental-health policy | Exact request already covered; seven-page scan obtained | Stop without a concrete new implementation question |

The [detailed account](../annexes/ati-report.md#all-ten-shortlisted-topics-in-plain-english) links the coverage and records. Each topic initially received one search returning ten results. Only three received an expansion search; only Nanisivik received a French query. This was not a comprehensive review of Canadian media.

## What failed or remains uncertain

A lower-ranked **medical-fairness check** accidentally used Global Affairs' unrelated summary with the same request number. The correct DND record is A-2025-02382, 303 partly disclosed pages. Its ten model assessments and previous low-priority conclusion are **invalid; a corrected rerun is pending**. Correcting identity did not validate those assessments. All ten shortlist identities and the other checked cases were reviewed and are unaffected. [Correction and original evidence](../research/2026-10-01/ati-live/correction-identity.md).

The known-covered C22 pistol example recovered the original CBC investigation. One successful example does not show that search finds all relevant coverage. A highly ranked request about the death penalty had “no records” status, illustrating why a dramatic topic need not offer evidence. No measured comparison against simple rules or check of all missed stories was completed.

## What it cost and what to do next

Estimated Jev usage was **about 6 US cents across 1,402 calls**; Exa reported **about 12 US cents for 17 searches**. Together that is **about 18 US cents**, excluding setup and reporting time. Human review time was not measured. The [exact accounting](../docs/costs-and-limitations.md) separates estimates from provider-reported costs; this is not an audited bill.

Read the two named releases before making substantive claims. The invalid medical-fairness assessment still needs a corrected rerun. The requested OpenAI comparison **has not run: no API calls, no result and USD0 spent**. [Technical continuation requirements](../annexes/ati-files.md#continuation-and-the-unrun-comparison) explain the remaining setup. No new ATI calls or document requests were made for this rewrite.
