# 03. Can a headline filter find a known news story?

**Question:** Can contract headlines help narrow a reading list and identify earlier coverage? A prototype was published on 22–24 September 2026; its current operation has not been tested here.

## What the source contains

Headlines give a short description of a contract announcement. They often omit the particular action, changed terms or context that makes it worth reporting. A news search returns only what its index and query can retrieve, not everything ever published.

## A recipe you can follow

1. Choose a few contract events you already know were covered. Save the actual article links and dates.
2. Search for each using the contract identifier, then the company, action and programme name.
3. Check whether the known article appears. If it does, read it to see whether it covers this exact action.
4. If it does not, record that the search missed a known example before using it on unknown cases.

## What happened

A GDELT search reportedly failed to retrieve a known Wall Street Journal article about THAAD, a missile-defence system. The exact article reference, query, date settings and response log were not recovered. The failure therefore remains a reported result; we cannot say whether the cause was the index, the query or another restriction.

The prototype's publication is a milestone, not a measure of accuracy. **A search that misses a known article cannot support a claim that another event was unreported.**

## Cost, limits and the next useful step

Build, hosting, model, search and review costs are unknown. Recover the exact WSJ reference and original search settings, then try a small group of known articles and record every result. No recovery rate has been established yet.
